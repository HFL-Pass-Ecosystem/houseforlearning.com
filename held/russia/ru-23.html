Credit and Debit Counselling

Learn to help people understand their money, read credit and debt, build a budget and plan repayment, while staying within the rules and one's authority.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner32 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to help people understand their money, read credit and debt, build a budget and plan repayment, while staying within the rules and one's authority.

  • Understand Explain interest, repayment schedules, fees and the effect of debt on a household.
  • Apply Build a monthly budget and a debt list from a client's papers.
  • Analyse Compare repayment options by total cost and time.
  • Evaluate Judge when to refer a client to a licensed professional or the competent body.
  • Create Build a clear action plan and a record of the session.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a structured money-counselling session and produce a budget, debt list and action plan.

Condition

In 6 weeks, with sample client files, a calculator, a spreadsheet and a session record template.

Standard

Budget balances to 0 RUB; interest and payoff calculations within 1 percent; plan has dated steps; referral rule applied correctly; pass mark 8 of 10.

Who it is for: Money advisers, social workers and bank or community staff in training. Requirements: Arithmetic and spreadsheet basics; respect for privacy. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Financial counselling and debt work are regulated in part. Giving credit advice, handling client money, negotiating with creditors or advising on insolvency may need a licence or registration under the relevant federal rules from the competent Russian authority; this course does not grant one. Do not promise debt will be cancelled and do not take fees for outcomes you cannot deliver. Protect personal data. Refer legal and insolvency questions to a qualified lawyer. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Money and credit basics 4 hUnderstand

Credit is the use of someone else's money now in return for paying more later. Clients often know the instalment but not the full cost, which includes interest, fees and insurance. Learn the terms principal, rate, term, instalment and fee, and the difference between secured debt, backed by an item, and unsecured debt. Explain every figure in plain words before comparing options.

  • Define principal, interest rate, term, instalment and fee.
  • Separate nominal rate from the full cost.
  • Know secured and unsecured debt.
  • Describe overdrafts, cards, loans and instalment plans.
  • Explain credit history in plain words.
Worked example. Task: find the cost of a 120,000 RUB loan at 18 percent a year over 12 months. Step 1: monthly rate = 18 / 12 = 1.5 percent. Step 2: instalment = 120,000 x 0.015 / (1 - 1.015^-12) = about 11,000 RUB. Step 3: total paid = 11,000 x 12 = 132,000 RUB, so the cost of credit is 12,000 RUB. Check: 12,000 is 10 percent of the principal, plausible for 18 percent a year on a balance that falls to zero (average about 55 percent: 0.18 x 0.55 = 9.9 percent).

Activity. Calculate the instalment and cost of three loans.

Standard to meet. Within 1 percent of the model.

Module 2 Interviewing and ethics 4 hApply

A client tells the truth when they feel safe, so the first minutes matter more than any calculation. State your purpose, how data is kept and what you cannot do, then ask open questions and listen. Record facts, not judgments, and never pressure the client or promise results. Shame about debt is common; a calm, respectful manner brings out the full picture of debts and income.

  • Open with purpose, privacy and limits of your role.
  • Ask open questions and listen.
  • Record facts, not judgments.
  • Explain what you cannot do.
  • Never pressure or promise results.
Worked example. Opening line: 'I can help you list your money and options. I cannot cancel debts or give legal advice.' Step 1: ask for monthly income and note 60,000 RUB. Step 2: ask for papers on every debt and note a card of 40,000 RUB and a loan of 80,000 RUB. Step 3: read the figures back and ask the client to confirm. Check: 40,000 + 80,000 = 120,000 RUB matches the papers, and the record shows facts only, with role limits and privacy stated.

Activity. Role-play an opening and record it.

Standard to meet. Role limits stated; privacy covered.

Module 3 Budgeting 5 hApply

A budget compares what comes in with what must go out, and shows what remains after needs. Build it from papers, not memory: pay slips, bank statements, bills and loan schedules. List income by source and date, then essentials, then debt payments, then other spending. Irregular costs such as annual fees should be divided by twelve. Test the plan over three months before trusting it.

  • List income by source and date.
  • List essentials: rent, food, transport, utilities.
  • List debt payments.
  • Find the surplus or deficit.
  • Test the plan over three months.
Worked example. Task: balance a monthly budget with income of 60,000 RUB. Step 1: essentials: rent 22,000 + food 15,000 + transport 4,000 + utilities 5,000 = 46,000 RUB. Step 2: add debt payments of 10,000 RUB: spending 56,000 RUB. Step 3: surplus = 60,000 - 56,000 = 4,000 RUB. Step 4: stress case: income down 10 percent to 54,000 RUB gives -2,000 RUB, so a cut is needed. Check: 56,000 + 4,000 = 60,000, so the budget balances to 0 RUB.

Activity. Build a balanced budget from a sample file.

Standard to meet. Budget balances to 0 RUB.

Module 4 Debt list and priorities 4 hAnalyse

Not all debts are equal. Priority debts carry the worst consequences if unpaid, such as housing, utilities and secured items, and they come first. Among the rest, rank by interest rate, then balance. Build a list with creditor, balance, rate, instalment and arrears, check for double fees or errors, and keep every letter and statement in order, because dates and papers are the evidence.

  • List each debt: creditor, balance, rate, instalment, arrears.
  • Mark priority debts: housing, utilities, secured items.
  • Rank others by rate.
  • Check for double fees or errors.
  • Keep every letter.
Worked example. Task: choose where extra money goes. Debts: card 40,000 RUB at 36 percent a year, loan 80,000 RUB at 15 percent. Step 1: card interest = 40,000 x 0.36 = 14,400 RUB a year. Step 2: loan interest = 80,000 x 0.15 = 12,000 RUB. Step 3: the card costs more on half the balance, so extra money goes first to the card while the loan gets its normal instalment. Check: 14,400 / 40,000 = 36 percent and 12,000 / 80,000 = 15 percent, matching the stated rates.

Activity. Build a ranked debt list.

Standard to meet. Fields complete; ranking explained.

Module 5 Repayment options 4 hAnalyse

Repayment options differ in time and total cost. Paying only the minimum is slowest and dearest; a fixed higher amount shortens the term; the snowball method clears small balances first for motivation, while the avalanche method clears the highest rate first to save most interest. Always compute months to payoff and total interest, and beware of restructures that lower the instalment only by lengthening the term and adding fees.

  • Compare paying minimum, fixed amount, snowball and avalanche.
  • Compute months to payoff.
  • Compute total interest.
  • Check whether a restructure lowers cost.
  • Beware of fees and longer terms.
Worked example. Task: payoff of 40,000 RUB at 3 percent a month, paying 4,000 RUB monthly. Step 1: first month interest = 40,000 x 0.03 = 1,200 RUB, so 2,800 RUB reduces the balance. Step 2: months = -ln(1 - 1,200 / 4,000) / ln(1.03) = 0.357 / 0.0296 = about 12.1. Step 3: total paid about 12.1 x 4,000 = 48,300 RUB, so interest is about 8,300 RUB. Check: after 12 payments about 260 RUB remains, so the thirteenth payment is small.

Activity. Compare two options for a sample debt.

Standard to meet. Months and interest computed within 1 percent.

Module 6 Working with creditors 4 hCreate

Clear, written, factual contact with creditors helps the client and protects the adviser. A short letter states the balance, what the client can pay, from what date, and asks for written confirmation. Keep copies and dates of everything. Negotiate only with the client's written consent and only within your authority; where the matter needs a licence or a lawyer, escalate. Never promise outcomes you do not control.

  • Write a short letter with facts and an offer.
  • Keep copies and dates.
  • Know what you may do under your authority.
  • Do not negotiate without client consent.
  • Escalate to a licensed professional where needed.
Worked example. Task: offer a payment plan on a 40,000 RUB balance when the budget surplus is 4,000 RUB. Step 1: offer 3,000 RUB a month, leaving 1,000 RUB reserve. Step 2: months to clear = 40,000 / 3,000 = 13.3, so 14 payments, ignoring added interest. Step 3: letter: 'Balance 40,000 RUB. I can pay 3,000 RUB each month from 5 December. Please confirm in writing.' Check: 14 x 3,000 = 42,000 RUB covers the balance, and 3,000 is below the 4,000 surplus, so the offer is affordable.

Activity. Draft a creditor letter.

Standard to meet. Facts, offer, date and request to confirm.

Module 7 Referral and the rules 4 hEvaluate

Knowing the edge of your role protects the client. Refer legal, insolvency and court matters to a qualified lawyer, distress to proper support, and work that needs a licence to people who hold it. Learn to spot predatory offers and scams, especially promises to cancel debt for an upfront payment. Check licence needs with the competent authority, record the referral and its date, and explain to the client why you are referring.

  • Refer legal, insolvency and court matters.
  • Refer mental distress to appropriate support.
  • Spot predatory offers and scams.
  • Never take payment for promised cancellation.
  • Check licence needs with the competent authority.
Worked example. Task: triage a court claim of 300,000 RUB against a client earning 60,000 RUB a month. Step 1: copy the date on the claim and any reply deadline in the papers. Step 2: this is a court matter outside advice limits, so refer to a qualified lawyer the same day. Step 3: list income and debts to hand to the lawyer. Check: 300,000 / 60,000 = 5 months of income, so urgency is high; the record shows the referral, its date and the reason.

Activity. Sort ten cases into advise, refer or decline.

Standard to meet. Nine of ten sorted correctly.

Module 8 Action plan and follow-up 3 hCreate

A plan is a short list of dated steps the client can actually do, usually three to five. Each step has an owner and a date, the first step is small and soon, and a check-in is set. Use the budget surplus to size payments, and name what to do if income falls. Record the client's agreement and store records safely under the personal data rules.

  • Write three to five steps.
  • Give each a date and an owner.
  • Set a check-in.
  • Record agreement.
  • Store records safely.
Worked example. Task: write a plan for a client with a 4,000 RUB monthly surplus. Step 1: by 10 Dec client and adviser send letters to creditors. Step 2: by 15 Dec client sets a pay-day transfer of 4,000 RUB, with 3,000 RUB to the card and 1,000 RUB to a reserve. Step 3: by 5 Jan review the budget. Check: 3,000 + 1,000 = 4,000 RUB equals the surplus, each step has an owner and a date, and a 40,000 RUB card clears in about 14 months.

Activity. Write an action plan for a sample client.

Standard to meet. Steps dated and measurable.

assessment

Evidence: budget, debt list, payoff comparison, action plan and session record. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Instalment for 120,000 RUB over 12 months at 18 percent?

Model answer: About 11,000 RUB.

Q2. Which debts come first?

Model answer: Those with the worst consequence, such as housing.

Q3. Budget: 60,000 income, 56,000 spending.

Model answer: 4,000 RUB surplus.

Q4. May you promise debt cancellation?

Model answer: No.

Q5. Why record facts, not judgments?

Model answer: To keep an accurate, fair file.

Q6. When do you refer?

Model answer: Legal, insolvency, court or distress matters.

Q7. Avalanche method means?

Model answer: Pay the highest-rate debt first.

Q8. Why write to creditors?

Model answer: To keep a dated record.

Q9. What data rule applies?

Model answer: Protect personal data under the relevant federal rules.

Q10. Who sets licence needs?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority.
