Credit and Debit Counselling

Learn to help people understand their money, read credit and debt, build a budget and plan repayment, while staying within the rules and one's authority.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner32 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to help people understand their money, read credit and debt, build a budget and plan repayment, while staying within the rules and one's authority.

  • Understand Explain interest, repayment schedules, fees and the effect of debt on a household.
  • Apply Build a monthly budget and a debt list from a client's papers.
  • Analyse Compare repayment options by total cost and time.
  • Evaluate Judge when to refer a client to a licensed professional or the competent body.
  • Create Build a clear action plan and a record of the session.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a structured money-counselling session and produce a budget, debt list and action plan.

Condition

In 6 weeks, with sample client files, a calculator, a spreadsheet and a session record template.

Standard

Budget balances to 0 RUB; interest and payoff calculations within 1 percent; plan has dated steps; referral rule applied correctly; pass mark 8 of 10.

Who it is for: Money advisers, social workers and bank or community staff in training. Requirements: Arithmetic and spreadsheet basics; respect for privacy. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Financial counselling and debt work are regulated in part. Giving credit advice, handling client money, negotiating with creditors or advising on insolvency may need a licence or registration under the relevant federal rules from the competent Russian authority; this course does not grant one. Do not promise debt will be cancelled and do not take fees for outcomes you cannot deliver. Protect personal data. Refer legal and insolvency questions to a qualified lawyer. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Money and credit basics 4 hUnderstand

Credit is cost over time. Understanding the cost is the first step to control.

  • Define principal, interest rate, term, instalment and fee.
  • Separate nominal rate from the full cost.
  • Know secured and unsecured debt.
  • Describe overdrafts, cards, loans and instalment plans.
  • Explain credit history in plain words.
Worked example. A loan of 120,000 RUB at 18 percent a year over 12 months has an instalment of about 11,000 RUB; total paid 132,000 RUB, cost 12,000 RUB.

Activity. Calculate the instalment and cost of three loans.

Standard to meet. Within 1 percent of the model.

Module 2 Interviewing and ethics 4 hApply

A client tells the truth when they feel safe. Listening comes before advice.

  • Open with purpose, privacy and limits of your role.
  • Ask open questions and listen.
  • Record facts, not judgments.
  • Explain what you cannot do.
  • Never pressure or promise results.
Worked example. Opening line: 'I can help you list your money and options. I cannot cancel debts or give legal advice.'

Activity. Role-play an opening and record it.

Standard to meet. Role limits stated; privacy covered.

Module 3 Budgeting 5 hApply

A budget shows what is left after needs.

  • List income by source and date.
  • List essentials: rent, food, transport, utilities.
  • List debt payments.
  • Find the surplus or deficit.
  • Test the plan over three months.
Worked example. Income 60,000 RUB; rent 22,000; food 15,000; transport 4,000; utilities 5,000; debt payments 10,000: total 56,000 RUB, surplus 4,000 RUB.

Activity. Build a balanced budget from a sample file.

Standard to meet. Budget balances to 0 RUB.

Module 4 Debt list and priorities 4 hAnalyse

Not all debts are equal. Priority is by consequence, then cost.

  • List each debt: creditor, balance, rate, instalment, arrears.
  • Mark priority debts: housing, utilities, secured items.
  • Rank others by rate.
  • Check for double fees or errors.
  • Keep every letter.
Worked example. Debts: card 40,000 RUB at 36 percent, loan 80,000 RUB at 15 percent. Extra money first to the card, which costs 14,400 RUB a year against 12,000 RUB.

Activity. Build a ranked debt list.

Standard to meet. Fields complete; ranking explained.

Module 5 Repayment options 4 hAnalyse

Options differ in time and total cost.

  • Compare paying minimum, fixed amount, snowball and avalanche.
  • Compute months to payoff.
  • Compute total interest.
  • Check whether a restructure lowers cost.
  • Beware of fees and longer terms.
Worked example. 40,000 RUB at 3 percent a month, paying 4,000 RUB monthly: about 12.5 months and about 8,700 RUB of interest.

Activity. Compare two options for a sample debt.

Standard to meet. Months and interest computed within 1 percent.

Module 6 Working with creditors 4 hCreate

Clear, written, factual contact helps.

  • Write a short letter with facts and an offer.
  • Keep copies and dates.
  • Know what you may do under your authority.
  • Do not negotiate without client consent.
  • Escalate to a licensed professional where needed.
Worked example. Letter: 'Balance 40,000 RUB. I can pay 3,000 RUB each month from 5 December. Please confirm in writing.'

Activity. Draft a creditor letter.

Standard to meet. Facts, offer, date and request to confirm.

Module 7 Referral and the rules 4 hEvaluate

Know the edge of your role. Referral protects the client.

  • Refer legal, insolvency and court matters.
  • Refer mental distress to appropriate support.
  • Spot predatory offers and scams.
  • Never take payment for promised cancellation.
  • Check licence needs with the competent authority.
Worked example. A client facing a court claim of 300,000 RUB: refer to a qualified lawyer at once and note the date of the claim.

Activity. Sort ten cases into advise, refer or decline.

Standard to meet. Nine of ten sorted correctly.

Module 8 Action plan and follow-up 3 hCreate

A plan is a short list of dated steps the client can do.

  • Write three to five steps.
  • Give each a date and an owner.
  • Set a check-in.
  • Record agreement.
  • Store records safely.
Worked example. Plan: by 10 Dec send letters; by 15 Dec set up transfer of 4,000 RUB; by 5 Jan review the budget.

Activity. Write an action plan for a sample client.

Standard to meet. Steps dated and measurable.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: budget, debt list, payoff comparison, action plan and session record. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Instalment for 120,000 RUB over 12 months at 18 percent?

Model answer: About 11,000 RUB.

Q2. Which debts come first?

Model answer: Those with the worst consequence, such as housing.

Q3. Budget: 60,000 income, 56,000 spending.

Model answer: 4,000 RUB surplus.

Q4. May you promise debt cancellation?

Model answer: No.

Q5. Why record facts, not judgments?

Model answer: To keep an accurate, fair file.

Q6. When do you refer?

Model answer: Legal, insolvency, court or distress matters.

Q7. Avalanche method means?

Model answer: Pay the highest-rate debt first.

Q8. Why write to creditors?

Model answer: To keep a dated record.

Q9. What data rule applies?

Model answer: Protect personal data under the relevant federal rules.

Q10. Who sets licence needs?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority.
