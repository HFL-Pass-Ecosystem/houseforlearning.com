Bitcoin Mining (Legal-Compliance-First Analysis)

Learn to analyse a digital asset mining operation in the right order: first the legal and licensing check, then power, hardware, cooling, records, tax reporting and risk. No result is promised and nothing here helps avoid any rule.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to analyse a digital asset mining operation in the right order: first the legal and licensing check, then power, hardware, cooling, records, tax reporting and risk. No result is promised and nothing here helps avoid any rule.

  • Understand Explain how proof-of-work mining works and what hash rate, difficulty, block reward and fees mean.
  • Apply Complete a rules check that records which activity is allowed, by whom, where and with what registration or reporting.
  • Analyse Calculate energy use, cost per day and cost per unit of hash rate for a given machine and tariff.
  • Evaluate Judge whether a project passes a go or no-go decision on legal status, power, heat, noise, records and risk.
  • Create Design a compliance and operations file for a small, lawful site with records, monitoring and an exit plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a go or no-go file for one proposed small mining site (written case), with a rules check, power and heat calculation, cost model, records plan and risk register.

Condition

Open-book; 6 hours; calculator and templates; no machines bought or run; no wallet keys or real funds used.

Standard

Rules check completed before any cost model; energy and heat calculations error-free; assumptions labelled as illustrative; five risks with actions; decision justified in one page; pass mark 80 percent.

Who it is for: People who want to understand mining as an energy-using, regulated activity, and advisers or owners who must judge whether a project is lawful and sensible. Requirements: Adult learner; basic arithmetic and spreadsheet skills. No prior crypto knowledge needed. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: The legal status of mining and of digital assets varies by country and changes often. In the Russian Federation, mining may be restricted, registered, reported or taxed under federal rules, and some regions or periods may limit it; electricity connection, building, fire and noise rules also apply. Before any step, check with the competent Russian authority and a qualified local lawyer and tax adviser. This course teaches analysis only. It never helps to evade a rule, hide activity, avoid reporting or use stolen power. Prices are volatile and returns are not promised; do not borrow to mine.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How mining works 4 hUnderstand

Mining is a computing process that secures a network and, when it succeeds, earns a reward. Understanding the mechanism makes the cost and risk clear.

  • Miners compete to find a valid block using computation; the network sets a difficulty so blocks arrive about every 10 minutes.
  • Hash rate is computations per second. A machine of 100 TH/s does 100 trillion per second.
  • A miner's expected share of rewards roughly equals its share of the total network hash rate. Luck causes short-term variation, which pools smooth.
  • Rewards come from the block subsidy and fees; the subsidy halves about every four years, so economics change on a schedule.
Worked example. Illustrative only: if the network had 600 EH/s (600,000,000 TH/s) and a machine had 100 TH/s, its share is 100 / 600,000,000 = 0.000000167 (about 1 in 6 million). With 144 blocks a day this share is tiny, so mining at small scale usually runs through a pool.

Activity. Write a half-page explanation for a lay reader of hash rate, difficulty and a pool, with one diagram.

Standard to meet. All terms correct; diagram shows block, difficulty and reward flow.

Module 2 The rules check comes first 5 hApply

Before any purchase, find out if the activity is lawful for this person, in this place, in this way. A project that fails this step stops here.

  • List the questions: Is mining allowed for this type of person or company? Is registration, reporting or licensing needed? Are there regional limits? How are income and assets taxed?
  • Check the site: ownership or lease, permitted use of the building, electricity contract type, fire and noise rules, neighbours.
  • Write each answer with its source (the competent authority or a qualified adviser), the date and the person who gave it. Rules change; recheck every 6 months.
  • If any answer is unclear, the result is "not yet" and no money is spent.
Worked example. Rules check sheet of 12 questions: 9 answered with source and date, 3 unclear. Result: stop at "not yet" until the 3 unclear items are answered in writing.

Activity. Complete a rules check for a written case. Mark each answer: confirmed, unclear or not allowed.

Standard to meet. All 12 questions covered; sources and dates present; decision follows the rule that unclear means stop.

Module 3 Power: the main cost 5 hAnalyse

A miner turns almost all electricity into heat. Power cost and capacity usually decide whether a site makes sense.

  • Energy use (kWh) = power (kW) x hours. A 3,000 W machine uses 3 kW x 24 = 72 kWh a day.
  • Efficiency = watts divided by TH/s, shown as J/TH. Lower is better.
  • Use a legal electricity contract and a certified electrician. Check the circuit rating, cable size and protection; machines run at full load day and night.
  • Never use unmetered, stolen or borrowed power.
Worked example. 3,000 W machine at 100 TH/s = 30 J/TH. Daily energy 72 kWh. At a tariff of 5 RUB per kWh: 360 RUB per day, 10,800 RUB for 30 days. At 6 RUB per kWh: 12,960 RUB. A 16 A circuit at 230 V supports about 3.7 kW, so one machine fits with little margin.

Activity. Calculate daily and monthly energy cost and the circuit load for three machines and two tariffs.

Standard to meet. All calculations correct; circuit limit checked; efficiency compared in J/TH.

Module 4 Heat, noise and site safety 4 hApply

Every watt becomes heat, and fans are loud. Poor cooling and wiring are fire risks.

  • Heat output equals electrical input: 3,000 W gives about 3 kW of heat, similar to a room heater on full.
  • Plan fresh air in and hot air out; keep temperatures within the maker's range and measure them.
  • Keep distance from combustibles, use fire detection and an extinguisher, and follow building rules. Check noise limits for neighbours and workplaces.
  • Place machines on stable, non-flammable shelves with space for airflow.
Worked example. 10 machines of 3 kW = 30 kW of heat. A room of 50 cubic metres would warm quickly, so ventilation of several thousand cubic metres an hour is needed. A professional should size it.

Activity. Draw a site layout with airflow, power feed, fire equipment and a noise estimate for 4 machines.

Standard to meet. Heat total correct; airflow and exit paths shown; fire plan listed.

Module 5 Cost model with honest assumptions 5 hAnalyse

A cost model is a tool for questions, not a forecast. It must state assumptions and show what changes the answer.

  • List costs: hardware, power, cooling, space, network, staff time, repairs, fees and taxes.
  • Compute cost per day and the price per coin at which the day's cost is covered (break-even price). This tells you how exposed you are.
  • Test sensitivity: power cost up 20 percent, difficulty up 10 percent, coin price down 30 percent. Show each separately.
  • Include an end-of-life value for hardware that is low, and never assume the future price.
Worked example. Daily cost: power 360 RUB + cooling and space 60 RUB + repair reserve 30 RUB = 450 RUB. If the machine yields 0.00015 coin a day (illustrative), break-even price = 450 / 0.00015 = 3,000,000 RUB per coin; the project loses money at any price below that.

Activity. Build a cost model for one machine with 5 sensitivity cases.

Standard to meet. Break-even price calculated; all assumptions labelled illustrative; sensitivities shown one at a time.

Module 6 Records, wallets and reporting 4 hApply

Good records show income, costs and compliance. Security protects funds from theft.

  • Record each reward received: date, amount, value in RUB at that date, wallet address and source.
  • Keep costs: invoices for hardware, electricity bills, repairs, with dates.
  • Reporting and tax: follow the rules the competent authority and your adviser confirm. Do not move assets to hide them.
  • Wallet security: use hardware wallets or well-reviewed secure storage, keep backups of recovery phrases offline, never share them, and use two-factor access.
Worked example. Month with 3 reward payments: 0.00002 coin each valued at 3,000,000 RUB per coin on the day = 60 RUB, 3 payments = 180 RUB. Ledger lines: date, amount, rate, RUB value, wallet.

Activity. Fill a 10-line ledger and write a one-page security checklist.

Standard to meet. RUB values correct; recovery phrase rules stated; no mixing of personal and business wallets.

Module 7 Risk, fraud and scams 4 hEvaluate

The field has many scams and hard risks. Knowing them protects money and reputation.

  • Beware promises of fixed returns, cloud mining sold with guarantees, requests for upfront fees and pressure to act fast.
  • Buy hardware from the maker or a traceable seller. Check warranty, delivery times and import rules.
  • Main risks: price fall, difficulty rise, hardware failure, rule change, power outage, theft, fire. Give each a likelihood, effect and action.
  • Never borrow to buy machines; plan for a stop and sale of hardware.
Worked example. Risk score = likelihood (1 to 3) x effect (1 to 3). Rule change 2 x 3 = 6; fire 1 x 3 = 3; price fall 3 x 2 = 6. Actions: recheck rules every 6 months; fit detection; keep a reserve of 3 months of costs: 450 RUB x 90 = 40,500 RUB per machine.

Activity. Review three advertised offers for scam flags and write a risk register of 5 entries.

Standard to meet. All flags found; risk scores calculated; each risk has an action and owner.

Module 8 Decision file and exit plan 4 hCreate

The final product is a plain, honest decision with its evidence, including how to stop.

  • One page: decision (go, not yet, no-go), reasons, conditions to meet, review date.
  • Attach the rules check, power and heat sheet, cost model, ledger plan and risk register.
  • State triggers to stop: rule change, power tariff above a limit, unmet safety item, or running below break-even for 60 days.
  • Write the exit plan: switch off, record, sell or recycle hardware lawfully.
Worked example. Example: decision "not yet": three rule answers missing; circuit margin only 0.7 kW; conditions: written answers, a certified electrician's report, review date in 30 days. Stop trigger: tariff above 7 RUB per kWh.

Activity. Write the go, not yet or no-go file for your case with attachments.

Standard to meet. Decision matches the rules check; triggers measurable; exit plan lawful.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: rules check sheet, power and heat calculation, cost model with stated assumptions, records plan, risk register and a one-page go or no-go decision. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What comes first in this course, before costs?

Model answer: The legal and licensing rules check.

Q2. What does an unclear answer in the rules check mean?

Model answer: Stop and spend nothing until it is answered in writing by the competent source.

Q3. A machine uses 3 kW for 24 hours. What is the energy?

Model answer: 72 kWh.

Q4. At 5 RUB per kWh, what is that cost?

Model answer: 360 RUB per day.

Q5. What is J/TH?

Model answer: Efficiency: energy per unit of hash; lower is better.

Q6. Where does the electricity go?

Model answer: Almost all becomes heat, so cooling and ventilation are needed.

Q7. What is a break-even price?

Model answer: The coin price at which a day's revenue covers that day's cost.

Q8. Name two scam flags.

Model answer: Guaranteed returns and demands for upfront fees or fast action.

Q9. Why must power be metered and lawful?

Model answer: Unmetered or stolen power is unlawful and unsafe.

Q10. Name two stop triggers.

Model answer: A rule change and a power tariff above the agreed limit.
