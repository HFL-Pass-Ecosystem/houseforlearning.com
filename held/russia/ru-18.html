Furniture (design, make, retail)

Design a simple piece, plan its materials and cutting, build it to a safe standard, cost it and prepare it for retail.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner30 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Design a simple piece, plan its materials and cutting, build it to a safe standard, cost it and prepare it for retail.

  • Understand Explain how joints, grain direction and moisture decide the strength and life of a piece.
  • Apply Build a small table or cabinet from a drawing to a tolerance of 1 mm.
  • Analyse Calculate board use, waste and cost from a cutting list.
  • Evaluate Judge a finished piece for stability, finish and safety against a checklist.
  • Create Design a product sheet, price and retail plan with warranty and care terms.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design, build, cost and prepare for sale one small piece of furniture.

Condition

In 6 weeks in a workshop with hand and power tools, a tape, a square, a moisture meter and a spreadsheet.

Standard

Piece within 1 mm of drawing on key sizes; timber moisture below 12 percent when assembled; costing within 5 percent of actual; pass mark 8 of 10 on the check.

Who it is for: Hobbyists and small makers who want to build and sell furniture. Requirements: Basic workshop skill; access to tools and a ventilated space. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Woodworking carries risk of cuts, dust, noise and fire. Use guards, push sticks, eye, ear and dust protection, and extraction; do not use machines without training. Finishes can be flammable and toxic; follow the maker's data sheet. Consumer products must meet the relevant federal rules for safety and labelling; check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before selling at scale.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Design and ergonomics 4 hUnderstand

Furniture fits a body and a room. Standard dimensions are a starting point for a design that works.

  • Know typical sizes: table height about 750 mm, seat about 450 mm, desk about 720 mm.
  • Sketch in plan, front and side views with sizes in mm.
  • Check clearance: 900 mm for walking routes, 600 mm behind a chair.
  • Choose a style and limit to one main material.
  • Build a cardboard mock-up to test proportion.
Worked example. A dining table for four: top 1200 x 750 mm, height 750 mm. Chair space needs 600 mm each side, so the room needs at least 2400 x 1950 mm free.

Activity. Draw a table or cabinet with sizes and make a mock-up.

Standard to meet. Three views with sizes; mock-up tested for clearance.

Module 2 Timber, boards and moisture 4 hUnderstand

Wood moves with moisture. Using the right material and drying avoids cracks and warping.

  • Compare solid wood, plywood, MDF and particle board for strength, cost and finish.
  • Read grain: keep long grain along load, mark faces and edges.
  • Measure moisture with a meter; aim for 8 to 12 percent indoors.
  • Allow for movement: solid wood shifts across the grain, not along it.
  • Stack boards flat with spacers to acclimatise for at least 1 week.
Worked example. A solid top 600 mm wide can change about 0.2 percent per 1 percent moisture shift across the grain. A 3 percent change gives 600 x 0.002 x 3 = 3.6 mm of movement.

Activity. Measure moisture in three boards and record it.

Standard to meet. Readings recorded; any board above 12 percent set aside.

Module 3 Cutting list and costing 4 hAnalyse

A cutting list turns a drawing into parts. Good planning cuts waste and shows the true cost.

  • List each part with length, width, thickness and quantity.
  • Add 3 mm per cut for the saw blade and 5 mm trim allowance.
  • Nest parts on a board of 2440 x 1220 mm to reduce waste.
  • Add hardware, finish, consumables and time.
  • Include 10 percent for waste and mistakes.
Worked example. Parts total 1.9 m2 of 18 mm board; a sheet is 2.98 m2. With 15 percent waste you need 2.19 m2, so one sheet at 3,800 RUB. Hardware 900 RUB, finish 600 RUB, 20 hours at 500 RUB = 10,000 RUB; total 15,300 RUB.

Activity. Produce a cutting list and cost sheet for your design.

Standard to meet. List complete; board use calculated; total adds up.

Module 4 Joints and assembly 5 hApply

A joint carries load. The right joint matches the part and the tools available.

  • Compare butt with screws, dowel, mortise and tenon, dovetail and pocket hole.
  • Mark out with a knife and square; cut to the waste side of the line.
  • Dry-fit before glue; check square by measuring diagonals.
  • Clamp for the glue time the maker states, usually 30 to 60 minutes.
  • Wipe squeeze-out and let the glue cure 24 hours before load.
Worked example. A frame 800 x 500 mm is square when both diagonals are equal: sqrt(800 squared + 500 squared) = 943 mm. A difference over 2 mm means adjust before the glue sets.

Activity. Build the carcass or frame and record diagonals.

Standard to meet. Diagonals within 2 mm; joints tight with no gaps over 0.5 mm.

Module 5 Finishing 3 hApply

A finish protects and shows the grain. Preparation matters more than the product.

  • Sand through grits 80, 120, 180, 240; remove dust between steps.
  • Choose oil, wax, lacquer or varnish for the use.
  • Apply thin coats and sand lightly between them.
  • Ventilate; keep rags flat to dry because they can ignite.
  • Test on an offcut first.
Worked example. Three coats of lacquer at 80 mL per m2 on a 1.5 m2 piece use 360 mL. At 1,200 RUB per litre that is 432 RUB.

Activity. Finish the piece and one test offcut.

Standard to meet. Even surface with no runs; offcut shows the same result.

Module 6 Safety and quality check 3 hEvaluate

Safe furniture does not tip, trap or shed splinters. A checklist makes the check repeatable.

  • Test stability: a table should not tip under a 100 N side load at the edge.
  • Round sharp edges to at least 2 mm.
  • Check for pinch points at hinges and drawers.
  • Use fixings rated for the load; anchor tall units to a wall.
  • Record the results with date.
Worked example. A cabinet 1800 mm high and 400 mm deep should be fixed to the wall with two brackets rated for 50 kg each.

Activity. Run the checklist on your piece.

Standard to meet. All checks recorded; failures fixed and rechecked.

Module 7 Retail and customers 4 hCreate

Selling needs a clear product sheet, fair terms and care advice.

  • Write a product sheet: size, material, finish, weight, lead time.
  • Set a price using cost plus margin and a market check.
  • State warranty and return terms honestly.
  • Give a care card: avoid direct sun, wipe with a damp cloth.
  • Meet the relevant federal rules for consumer sale, labelling and tax.
Worked example. Cost 15,300 RUB with a 40 percent margin on price: price = 15,300 / 0.6 = 25,500 RUB.

Activity. Write a product sheet, price and care card.

Standard to meet. Sheet complete; price worked out; terms in plain words.

Module 8 Review and scale 3 hEvaluate

A first piece teaches the process. A review shows whether it can become a line.

  • Compare planned and actual hours and cost.
  • List three changes that save time or waste.
  • Test demand with a small run of three pieces.
  • Plan tool and space needs for a batch.
  • Set dates for the next build.
Worked example. Planned 20 hours, actual 24: variance 20 percent. A jig that saves 3 hours per piece cuts a batch of 3 by 9 hours.

Activity. Write a variance report and a batch plan.

Standard to meet. Variance computed; three changes named; batch plan dated.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: drawing, cutting list, costing sheet, build log, finished piece with photos, product sheet and care card. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is a typical table height?

Model answer: About 750 mm.

Q2. Which moisture level suits indoor furniture?

Model answer: About 8 to 12 percent.

Q3. Why dry-fit before gluing?

Model answer: To check fit and square before the glue sets.

Q4. How do you check a frame is square?

Model answer: Measure both diagonals; they must be equal.

Q5. What is the waste allowance in costing?

Model answer: About 10 to 15 percent.

Q6. Why keep oily rags flat to dry?

Model answer: They can heat and ignite.

Q7. Calculate the price at 40 percent margin on cost 15,300 RUB.

Model answer: 15,300 / 0.6 = 25,500 RUB.

Q8. Why anchor tall cabinets?

Model answer: To prevent tipping.

Q9. Which direction does solid wood move most?

Model answer: Across the grain.

Q10. Who do you ask about product rules?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a local adviser.
