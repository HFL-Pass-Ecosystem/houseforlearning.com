Ceramic Studios

Learn to make, dry, fire and glaze pots safely, run a small studio, cost work and sell it.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner33 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to make, dry, fire and glaze pots safely, run a small studio, cost work and sell it.

  • Understand Explain clay body, shrinkage, drying and firing stages.
  • Apply Throw or hand-build six pieces and fire them to a set schedule.
  • Analyse Calculate shrinkage, clay use and firing cost.
  • Evaluate Judge pieces for faults and cause.
  • Create Build a studio plan with safety, costing and a sales range.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Make, fire and finish six pieces and cost a small production run.

Condition

Over 8 weeks in a studio with clay, a wheel or hand-building tools, glazes and a kiln under supervision.

Standard

Six sound pieces; shrinkage measured within 1 percent; firing log complete; costing adds up; pass mark 8 of 10.

Who it is for: Hobbyists and starting studio owners. Requirements: Access to a studio and supervised kiln. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Clay dust (silica), glaze chemicals and kilns are hazardous: use wet cleaning, respirators where needed, ventilation, heat gloves, and keep flammables away. Only trained people load and fire a kiln. Food-contact ware needs glazes that are safe for it; check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser about product safety, electrical and fire rules and sale. Do not claim glazes are food safe without proof.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Clay and tools 4 hUnderstand

Clay bodies differ in texture, plasticity and firing range, and the body you choose decides which glazes, temperatures and forming methods will work. Earthenware is open and forgiving, stoneware dense and strong, porcelain fine but prone to slumping. Moisture, air pockets and mixed batches cause most early failures, so learn to test, wedge, store and label clay before you make anything.

  • Compare earthenware, stoneware and porcelain by firing temperature.
  • Know grog and its effect on strength.
  • Wedge to remove air.
  • Keep clay damp and sealed.
  • Set up tools and a clean station.
Worked example. Task: pick a clay for a 1240 C firing. Step 1: stoneware fires about 1200 to 1280 C, so 1240 C is inside its range; earthenware (about 1000 to 1100 C) would slump or bloat. Step 2: a 10 kg bag costs 1,500 RUB, so 1,500 / 10 = 150 RUB per kg. Step 3: a 350 g mug uses 0.35 x 150 = 52.5 RUB of clay. Check: 52.5 / 0.35 = 150, which matches the bag price. A stoneware glaze fired at 1000 C would stay unmelted.

Activity. Test three clays and note feel and firing range.

Standard to meet. Notes complete and station clean.

Module 2 Forming 5 hApply

Even walls make strong pots, and the forming method should suit the shape: throwing for round symmetrical forms, coiling and pinching for irregular ones, slabs for flat or boxy work. Uneven thickness dries and shrinks unevenly, which cracks ware in the kiln. Learn to centre, check thickness with a needle gauge, score and slip every join, and compress bases to prevent cracks.

  • Throw: centre, open, pull, shape.
  • Hand-build: pinch, coil, slab.
  • Keep wall thickness 5 to 8 mm.
  • Score and slip joins.
  • Cut a base with a wire.
Worked example. Task: buy clay for six mugs, each 9 cm tall, 8 cm wide with 6 mm walls, about 350 g thrown. Step 1: 6 x 350 g = 2,100 g = 2.1 kg. Step 2: add 15 percent for trimmings and test pieces: 2.1 x 1.15 = 2.4 kg, so buy one 2.5 kg block. Step 3: at 150 RUB per kg the clay costs 2.5 x 150 = 375 RUB. Check: 375 / 6 = 62.5 RUB per mug, close to the 52.5 RUB bare clay cost plus waste.

Activity. Make six pieces in one form.

Standard to meet. Even walls within 2 mm; joins sealed.

Module 3 Drying and shrinkage 4 hAnalyse

Water leaves clay from the surface first, so thin rims and edges dry before thick bases and handles, and the difference pulls a piece apart as cracks. Slow, even drying under loose plastic, away from draughts and heaters, keeps moisture balanced. Shrinkage is the other half: clay shrinks as it dries and again as it fires, so every wet size must be planned from the finished size wanted.

  • Dry slowly under loose plastic, then open air.
  • Know leather-hard and bone-dry stages.
  • Measure shrinkage with a test tile.
  • Plan a bigger wet size.
  • Dry thick parts longest.
Worked example. Task: size a mug to fire at 9 cm. Step 1: a 10 cm line on a test tile measures 8.7 cm after firing, so shrinkage = (10 - 8.7) / 10 = 13 percent. Step 2: fired size is 87 percent of wet size, so wet height = 9 / 0.87 = 10.3 cm. Step 3: for an 8 cm width, 8 / 0.87 = 9.2 cm. Check: 10.3 x 0.87 = 8.96 cm and 9.2 x 0.87 = 8.0 cm, within 1 percent of the targets.

Activity. Make and measure a 10 cm test tile.

Standard to meet. Shrinkage computed within 1 percent.

Module 4 Bisque firing 4 hApply

The first firing turns dry clay into hard, porous ceramic by driving out water and burning off organic matter. Trapped water turns to steam near 100 C and chemically bound water leaves around 500 to 600 C, so ware must be bone-dry and heated slowly through those zones or it can burst and damage other pieces. Stack ware without touching, log every firing, and cool with the kiln closed.

  • Make sure ware is bone-dry.
  • Stack without touching.
  • Follow a schedule: slow to 600 C, then faster to 950 C.
  • Log start, peak and time.
  • Cool closed until below 100 C.
Worked example. Task: plan a bisque to 950 C. Step 1: ramp at 100 C per hour from 0 to 600 C: 600 / 100 = 6 hours. Step 2: ramp at 150 C per hour from 600 to 950 C: 350 / 150 = 2.3 hours. Step 3: total heating 8.3 hours, then about 12 hours cooling until below 100 C. Step 4: at 30 kWh per firing and 6 RUB per kWh, energy costs 180 RUB. Check: 6 + 2.3 = 8.3 and 30 x 6 = 180, so schedule and cost agree.

Activity. Plan and log a bisque firing.

Standard to meet. Schedule and log completed.

Module 5 Glazes 4 hApply

Glaze is a thin layer of glass fused onto clay, and the result depends on recipe, thickness and peak temperature. Too thin looks dry and rough, too thick runs onto the shelf or crawls. Test every new glaze on tiles before your pieces, record the recipe and thickness, and keep glaze chemicals labelled. Never state that ware is food safe without proper proof of testing.

  • Dip or brush evenly.
  • Wipe the foot clean.
  • Test on tiles first.
  • Record recipe, thickness and result.
  • Wear protection; do not eat from ware without safety proof.
Worked example. Task: glaze 12 mugs. Step 1: a dip in glaze at 1.4 specific gravity gives a coat of about 0.5 mm; too thick runs on the shelf. Step 2: each mug takes about 40 g of dry glaze, so 12 x 40 = 480 g. Step 3: one 1 kg batch at 400 RUB covers it, costing 480 / 1000 x 400 = 192 RUB, or 16 RUB per mug. Check: 480 g is below 1,000 g, and 192 / 12 = 16 RUB. Fire a test tile first.

Activity. Glaze three test tiles and your pieces.

Standard to meet. Even coat; feet wiped; record kept.

Module 6 Glaze firing and faults 5 hEvaluate

The glaze firing melts the coating to glass at the glaze's peak temperature, often with a short hold to smooth it. Faults are information: crawling points to dust or a thick coat, pinholes to escaping gas, crazing to a body and glaze that shrink differently, warping to uneven walls or fast heating. Keep a fault log linking each defect to a cause and one change to test.

  • Fire to the glaze cone; log peak.
  • Find causes: crawling (dust or thick coat), pinholes, crazing, warping.
  • Fix by changing prep, thickness or schedule.
  • Do not open the kiln hot.
  • Check shelves are wadded.
Worked example. Task: diagnose a firing. Result: 4 of 6 mugs show crazing and 1 of 6 shows pinholes. Step 1: crazing rate = 4 / 6 = 67 percent, pinholes = 1 / 6 = 17 percent. Step 2: crazing on most pieces suggests glaze and body shrink differently, so try slower cooling or a different glaze. Step 3: change one thing and refire one tile. Check: if no mug has both faults, 4 + 1 = 5 faulty and 1 sound of 6, matching the log.

Activity. Write a fault report for your firing.

Standard to meet. Each fault has cause and fix.

Module 7 Studio safety and care 3 hEvaluate

Fine silica dust and heat are the two main studio hazards, and both are controlled by housekeeping and habits. Dry sweeping lifts invisible dust that damages lungs over years, so use wet cleaning and a respirator for mixing. Kilns need ventilation, clearance from walls and heat gloves. Keep glaze chemicals labelled and stored safely, and know where the fire extinguisher and first aid kit are.

  • Wet-mop; never dry sweep.
  • Ventilate the kiln room.
  • Keep a safe distance from hot surfaces.
  • Store chemicals labelled.
  • Plan fire and first aid.
Worked example. Task: check a kiln room. Step 1: room 2.4 m x 2.0 m x 2.5 m = 12 m3. Step 2: if the maker requires 10 air changes per hour, extraction must move 12 x 10 = 120 m3 per hour; use the maker's own figure. Step 3: a 6 kW kiln at 230 V draws 6,000 / 230 = 26 A, so a qualified electrician must confirm the supply. Check: 26 x 230 = 5,980 W, about 6 kW; keep 30 cm clearance from walls as the maker states.

Activity. Complete a studio safety checklist.

Standard to meet. All items checked; actions listed.

Module 8 Costing and selling 4 hCreate

A fair price covers every cost: clay, glaze, kiln energy, your hours, space, packaging and breakage, not only materials. Hours are usually the largest cost of handmade ware. Divide each firing's cost by the pieces in the load, allow for pieces lost to faults, and add a margin for tools and growth. Test the price with a small range, and follow the rules on sale and taxes.

  • Add clay per piece, glaze, firing share, hours and space.
  • Divide firing cost by pieces per load.
  • Add a margin.
  • Test market with a small range.
  • Follow rules on sale and taxes.
Worked example. Task: price one mug. Step 1: kiln share: 30 kWh x 6 RUB = 180 RUB per firing; two firings = 360 RUB over 30 mugs = 12 RUB each. Step 2: clay 0.35 kg x 150 = 52.5 RUB; glaze 20 RUB. Step 3: 1.5 hours at 500 RUB = 750 RUB. Step 4: cost = 12 + 52.5 + 20 + 750 = 834.5 RUB; with a 30 percent margin 834.5 x 1.3 = 1,085, so price 1,100 RUB. Check: 1,100 - 834.5 = 265.5 RUB, about 32 percent over cost.

Activity. Cost one product and set a price.

Standard to meet. Cost adds up; price includes margin.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: six pieces, a shrinkage test tile, a firing log, a glaze record and a costing sheet. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Typical stoneware firing range?

Model answer: About 1200 to 1280 C.

Q2. Why wedge clay?

Model answer: To remove air and even the texture.

Q3. Shrinkage of 10 cm to 8.7 cm?

Model answer: 13 percent.

Q4. Why dry ware slowly?

Model answer: To prevent cracks.

Q5. Why wipe glaze from the foot?

Model answer: So the ware does not stick to the shelf.

Q6. What is bisque?

Model answer: First firing that hardens the clay.

Q7. How should you clean the floor?

Model answer: Wet mop; never dry sweep.

Q8. Cause of crazing?

Model answer: Glaze and body shrink differently.

Q9. Who loads the kiln?

Model answer: Only trained people.

Q10. May you call ware food safe without proof?

Model answer: No.
