Personal Assistants for the Elderly

Learn to plan and deliver safe, respectful non-medical support for older adults at home: daily routines, mobility, meals, reminders, errands, communication and knowing when to call for help.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner36 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to plan and deliver safe, respectful non-medical support for older adults at home: daily routines, mobility, meals, reminders, errands, communication and knowing when to call for help.

  • Understand Explain the limits between personal assistance, nursing and medical care, and why each limit exists.
  • Apply Carry out a safe transfer, a meal and hydration routine and a home safety check using a written checklist.
  • Analyse Assess one older person's needs, risks and preferences and rank them by urgency.
  • Evaluate Judge an incident and decide whether to act, record, escalate or call emergency services.
  • Create Design a four-week support plan with visit times, tasks, records and a review date.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a four-week support plan and a first-visit assessment for one older person (a role-play client or a consenting volunteer), with a home safety check, a daily routine, a record sheet and an escalation sheet.

Condition

Open-book; 6 hours over one week; templates, a pen, a tape measure and a calculator; a role-play client or a written case; no medical advice given.

Standard

All 5 documents complete; at least 8 of 10 risks found in the case home; every task inside the non-medical boundary; escalation contacts named; pass mark 80 percent on the rubric.

Who it is for: People who want to work as a personal assistant, companion or home helper for older adults, and family members who care for a relative. Requirements: Adult learner; patience and respect for privacy; basic reading and arithmetic. A clean record check and a health check may be required by employers or by the competent authority. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: This course is not nursing or medical training. A personal assistant does not diagnose, change treatment, give injections or decide doses. Social-care services may need registration, a contract and checks under the relevant federal and regional rules; check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before offering paid care. Always call emergency services for chest pain, stroke signs, serious falls, choking or unresponsiveness.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Role, boundaries and dignity 4 hUnderstand

A personal assistant supports daily living. Clear limits protect the older person and the assistant, and respect keeps the person in charge of their own life.

  • Separate three roles: personal assistance (company, meals, errands, reminders), nursing care (wound care, injections) and medical care (diagnosis, treatment). Only the first is yours.
  • Ask for consent before every task, even a small one: "May I help you with your coat?" Consent can be withdrawn at any time.
  • Keep personal information private. Share it only with the client, named family and the supervisor, and only what is needed.
  • Write a one-page job description with tasks you will do, tasks you will not do and who you call for the rest.
Worked example. A client asks you to adjust her blood pressure tablet because she feels dizzy. Correct response: do not change anything, sit her down, check she is safe, call her doctor or the family contact, and record the time and what she said. A 20-minute call beats a wrong dose.

Activity. Sort 12 tasks (for example "cut toenails of a diabetic client", "fetch bread", "give an injection") into do, do with training, and never do. Explain two choices aloud.

Standard to meet. At least 11 of 12 tasks sorted correctly; each "never do" has a named person to call.

Module 2 Needs and risk assessment 5 hAnalyse

Good support starts by listening and observing. An assessment records what the person can do, wants to do and finds hard.

  • Use a simple domains list: mobility, eating and drinking, washing and dressing, memory, mood and company, home, money and errands.
  • Observe and ask, never assume. Record "walks 10 m with a stick, rests twice" rather than "weak".
  • Rate each risk as likelihood (1 to 3) times effect (1 to 3). A score of 6 or more needs action in the first week.
  • Record what matters to the person: favourite meal, daily rhythm, hobbies, who they want to see.
Worked example. Case: Mr A, 82, lives on the 4th floor without a lift. Falls risk likelihood 3, effect 3 = 9 (action now). Missed meals likelihood 2, effect 2 = 4 (monitor). Loneliness 3 x 2 = 6 (action in week one: two social visits per week).

Activity. Complete the assessment form for a written case or a role-play client. Score at least 8 risks and rank them.

Standard to meet. All seven domains covered; scores calculated correctly; top three risks have a named action and a date.

Module 3 Safe movement and the home 5 hApply

Falls are among the most serious everyday risks for older people. Most can be reduced with simple changes and safe technique.

  • Check the home room by room: loose rugs, cables, poor light, wet floors, missing handrails, unreachable shelves, steep thresholds.
  • Never pull a person by the arm. Ask them to move forward on the seat, feet flat, lean forward, and stand on a count of three while you guard.
  • Keep a clear route of at least 90 cm to the toilet and fit a night light. Place often-used items between knee and shoulder height.
  • After a fall, do not lift at once. Check for pain, head injury or confusion, keep the person warm, and call for help if in doubt.
Worked example. Hallway of 80 cm with a rug and a 60 W bulb. Actions: remove the rug (0 RUB), clear shelf items to give 90 cm, fit a 9 W LED lamp bright enough to read by (about 250 RUB), tape cables. Risk score falls from 9 to 3.

Activity. Run a 20-item home safety checklist in a room of your own home. List 6 changes, their cost in RUB and their order.

Standard to meet. At least 15 of 20 items checked; six changes ranked by risk; one safe stand-up demonstrated.

Module 4 Food, drink and reminders 5 hApply

Older people can lose appetite and thirst. Regular meals, drinks and agreed reminders keep strength up, and the assistant stays inside the medication boundary.

  • Offer a drink with every visit and every meal. A common general target is about 1.5 litres of fluid a day unless a doctor has set a limit; ask the family for the limit.
  • Plan three meals and two snacks a day, soft or small portions if chewing is hard, and check food dates and storage (fridge at 0 to 5 degrees Celsius).
  • Reminders: you may remind a person to take tablets from a pre-filled organiser set up by the person, family or pharmacist. You do not choose, split or change doses.
  • Record what was eaten and drunk in simple units (cups, portions) and report a big change within the day.
Worked example. Target 1,500 ml a day. A cup is 200 ml: 7 cups reach 1,400 ml; add a 200 ml soup to pass 1,500 ml. A weekly shop for one person planned at 2,400 RUB = about 343 RUB per day.

Activity. Write a 3-day menu with drinks for a client who has poor teeth and a low appetite, plus the shopping list and cost.

Standard to meet. Fluid plan reaches the target; menu fits the stated difficulty; reminder rules written with the boundary.

Module 5 Communication, memory and mood 4 hApply

How you speak decides whether help is accepted. Calm, clear and unhurried communication reduces fear and conflict.

  • Face the person, speak slowly, use short sentences and one idea at a time. Check hearing aids and glasses are in use.
  • With memory problems, do not argue about facts. Acknowledge the feeling, redirect gently and keep routines the same.
  • Watch for low mood: poor sleep, loss of interest, not eating, talk of being a burden. Report to family or doctor; any talk of self-harm needs urgent help the same day.
  • Offer choices of two ("tea or compote?") rather than open questions.
Worked example. Client repeats the same question 6 times in an hour. Poor response: "I already told you." Better: answer calmly each time, write the answer on a card by the chair, then start a favourite activity for 15 minutes.

Activity. Role-play three scenes (refusal of a bath, repeated question, sadness) for 5 minutes each with a peer who scores you.

Standard to meet. Peer score of 4 out of 5 or better on calm tone, choice offered and no argument.

Module 6 Errands, money and paperwork 4 hApply

Handling someone else's money or papers is a trust task. Clear rules and receipts protect everyone.

  • Use a written authority for errands, an agreed spending limit and a receipt for every purchase. Keep your money and theirs apart.
  • Never accept gifts of value, loans, a will role or access to bank cards and PIN numbers. Tell the family if offered.
  • Warn against telephone and doorstep scams. A caller who needs money or codes quickly is a red flag; hang up and ring the family.
  • Help with forms by reading and explaining, not by deciding. Keep copies in a labelled folder.
Worked example. Float of 3,000 RUB given on Monday. Receipts: bread 120, milk 95, pharmacy item 640, vegetables 410 = 1,265 RUB. Change returned 1,735 RUB. Sheet signed by both.

Activity. Fill in a spending ledger for a week of 8 purchases, with receipts and change, and write a three-line scam script.

Standard to meet. Ledger balances to the ruble; no cash mixing; scam script names the person to call.

Module 7 Emergencies, safeguarding and escalation 5 hEvaluate

You will meet worrying signs. Knowing what to do in the first minutes, and whom to tell, is the most important skill in the job.

  • Call emergency services at once for stroke signs (face drooping, arm weakness, speech trouble), chest pain, breathlessness, choking, unresponsiveness or a serious fall.
  • Learn the signs of neglect or abuse: unexplained bruises, fear of a person, missing money, no food. Report to your supervisor and the competent authority; do not confront.
  • Keep an emergency sheet by the phone: address with entrance and floor, diagnoses, allergies, medicines list, doctor and family numbers.
  • Record facts, times and words used, not opinions. Sign and date every record.
Worked example. You find a client on the floor at 10:05, awake but unable to stand. Timeline: 10:05 safe check and call; 10:07 emergency call, address given with entrance code; 10:09 keep warm; 10:25 crew arrives; 10:40 family informed; 11:00 record written.

Activity. Judge five short incident cards: choose call-now, same-day report or next-visit note, and justify each in one line.

Standard to meet. At least 4 of 5 judged correctly; every call-now case names what you say on the phone.

Module 8 Design a support plan 4 hCreate

A plan joins everything: needs, visits, tasks, records and review. It should be clear enough for a stand-in to follow.

  • Set visit times to match the person's day, not yours. Add a task list for each visit with time estimates.
  • Cost the plan honestly: hours, travel time and expenses. Show the person and family the cost before starting.
  • Include a review at week two and week four, and a list of changes that trigger an early review (a fall, a hospital stay, a new medicine).
  • Add a handover page so another assistant could cover a visit.
Worked example. Plan: 3 visits a week x 2 hours = 6 hours; at a posted rate of 450 RUB per hour = 2,700 RUB per week, 10,800 RUB for 4 weeks, plus about 300 RUB per week travel = 12,000 RUB. Review dates on day 14 and day 28.

Activity. Build the four-week plan, the cost sheet, the record sheet and the handover page for your case.

Standard to meet. All tasks within boundary; cost adds up; two review dates and three early-review triggers present.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name the three roles that must stay separate.

Model answer: Personal assistance, nursing care and medical care. A personal assistant does only the first.

Q2. A client wants you to double a tablet dose. What do you do?

Model answer: Do not change it. Make her safe, contact her doctor or family, and record the request and time.

Q3. How do you stand a person up safely?

Model answer: Feet flat, move forward on the seat, lean forward, stand on a count of three, guard without pulling the arm.

Q4. What is a general daily fluid target and its caveat?

Model answer: About 1.5 litres a day unless a doctor has set a limit.

Q5. Score a risk with likelihood 3 and effect 2. What action level?

Model answer: 3 x 2 = 6. This needs action in the first week.

Q6. What do you do when a client repeats the same question many times?

Model answer: Answer calmly, write it on a card, redirect to an activity, and do not argue.

Q7. List two scam warning signs.

Model answer: Pressure to act quickly, requests for money, codes or card details by phone or at the door.

Q8. When must you call emergency services at once?

Model answer: Stroke signs, chest pain, breathlessness, choking, unresponsiveness or a serious fall.

Q9. What belongs on an emergency sheet?

Model answer: Address with entrance and floor, diagnoses, allergies, medicines, doctor and family numbers.

Q10. Why do you cost the plan before starting?

Model answer: So the person and family agree to the hours, price and expenses, which avoids disputes.

jobs this leads to

Where this takes you

  • Personal assistant to an older person. Provides non-medical daily support in the client's home.
  • Companion carer. Offers company, outings and routine support.
  • Care coordinator assistant. Schedules visits, keeps records and liaises with families.
  • Home safety adviser (older adults). Checks homes and suggests low-cost safety changes.
  • Day-centre support worker. Helps run activities and meals for older people.
