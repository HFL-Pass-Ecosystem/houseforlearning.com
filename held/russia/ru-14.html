Cleaning Business

Start and run a cleaning service: scoping jobs, products and equipment, safe methods, pricing, quality checks, staff and client contracts.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner27 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Start and run a cleaning service: scoping jobs, products and equipment, safe methods, pricing, quality checks, staff and client contracts.

  • Understand Explain cleaning, disinfecting and sanitising and which one a job needs.
  • Apply Calculate time, labour and price for a cleaning job from floor area and task rates.
  • Analyse Compare chemicals and methods for risk, cost and surface compatibility.
  • Evaluate Judge a finished job against a written checklist and decide what to redo.
  • Create Build a job scope, quote, risk sheet and quality checklist for a client.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Scope and quote a cleaning job for a 120 square metre office, with a risk sheet and a quality checklist.

Condition

Desk exercise with floor plan, rate table and spreadsheet; 4 hours.

Standard

Time and price correct to 5 percent, every chemical has a safe-use note, checklist covers all areas, and at least 90 percent of rubric points.

Who it is for: People starting a cleaning service, supervisors in cleaning teams and facilities staff. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. Physical fitness is useful for real work. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Cleaning chemicals can burn skin, damage lungs and react dangerously if mixed: never mix products, always read the maker label and safety sheet. Staff need training, protective equipment and safe lifting. Work in medical, food or hazardous sites needs special rules. Registration, tax, employment, insurance and waste rules are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before you start, and never clean biohazard or asbestos areas without qualified authorisation.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Clean, disinfect, sanitise 3 hUnderstand

Cleaning removes dirt; disinfecting kills germs on a surface; sanitising reduces them to a safe level. Dirt must be removed first.

  • Define each term with an example.
  • Choose the right level for an office, kitchen and bathroom.
  • Learn contact time: a disinfectant needs time on a wet surface.
  • Work from clean to dirty and top to bottom.
Worked example. A disinfectant label says 5 minutes contact. Spray at 10:00, wipe at 10:05 or later. Wiping at 10:01 gives no assurance of disinfection.

Activity. Write a plan listing which surfaces need cleaning only and which need disinfection.

Standard to meet. Each choice has a reason and a contact time where relevant.

Module 2 Chemicals and safety 4 hEvaluate

Chemicals do the work but can harm. Read the label and safety sheet, dilute correctly and never mix.

  • Keep products in labelled containers.
  • Wear gloves and eye protection as the label advises.
  • Never mix bleach with acids or ammonia products.
  • Ventilate and store out of reach of children.
Worked example. Concentrate diluted 1:40. For a 10 L bucket: 10 000 mL / 41 = 244 mL concentrate plus 9.76 L water. Mark the bucket at the dose.

Activity. Complete a safe-use table for five products.

Standard to meet. Dilutions correct and incompatible pairs flagged.

Module 3 Equipment and methods 3 hApply

Good tools save time and protect surfaces. Colour coding prevents cross-contamination.

  • Colour code cloths: for example red for toilets, blue for general.
  • Match method to surface: damp mop, scrub, vacuum with filter.
  • Maintain and wash tools after use.
  • Use wet-floor signs.
Worked example. Cloth set: 4 colours x 6 cloths = 24 cloths; wash at 60 C. Mop heads: replace after 40 washes. Daily 5 mop heads: replacement due after 8 working days of use per head, tracked on a log.

Activity. Design a colour-code plan for a small office.

Standard to meet. No cloth used across toilet and food areas.

Module 4 Scoping and timing a job 4 hApply

Time estimates decide profit. Use production rates per area and task, and add set-up and travel time.

  • Walk the site and measure areas.
  • Use rates such as m2 per hour for each task.
  • Add 15 percent for set-up, breaks and travel.
  • Record special tasks such as windows.
Worked example. Office 120 m2: vacuum and mop at 60 m2/h = 2.0 h; toilets 2 x 0.5 h = 1.0 h; desks and bins 0.75 h. Subtotal 3.75 h + 15 percent = 4.3 h.

Activity. Time a 150 m2 job from given rates.

Standard to meet. Total within 5 percent of the model.

Module 5 Pricing and quoting 3 hCreate

A quote covers labour, products, equipment wear, travel, insurance, tax and profit.

  • Compute hourly cost per worker including social charges as set by law.
  • Add materials and overhead.
  • Add a margin.
  • State scope, frequency and exclusions in writing.
Worked example. 4.3 h x 400 RUB = 1,720 RUB labour; products 120 RUB; travel 150 RUB; overhead 10 percent = 199 RUB; total cost 2,189 RUB. At 25 percent margin: 2,189 / 0.75 = 2,919 RUB per visit. For 12 visits a month = 35,028 RUB.

Activity. Write a quote with scope and exclusions.

Standard to meet. Price correct to 5 percent and exclusions stated.

Module 6 Quality checks and complaints 3 hEvaluate

Quality is what the client sees on arrival. A checklist and photos prevent disputes.

  • Build a room-by-room checklist.
  • Score each item pass or redo.
  • Photograph before and after where agreed.
  • Fix complaints within 24 hours and log the cause.
Worked example. Checklist of 40 items; inspection finds 3 fails: 92.5 percent pass. Target 95 percent, so two items are retrained.

Activity. Create a 30-item checklist and score a sample job.

Standard to meet. Checklist covers all rooms; scoring is correct.

Module 7 Staff, contracts and legal basics 3 hEvaluate

People are the service. Training, fair pay, insurance and clear contracts protect everyone.

  • Train staff on chemicals, lifting and site rules.
  • Check the employment or contractor rules with the competent authority.
  • Write a service agreement with scope, price, access, liability and notice.
  • Keep keys and client data safe.
Worked example. New staff induction of 4 hours covers 8 topics. Test of 10 questions, pass mark 8. Key log: each key signed out and in, any gap reported same day.

Activity. Draft an induction outline and agreement headings.

Standard to meet. Outline covers chemicals, lifting, keys and privacy.

Module 8 Launch and growth plan 4 hCreate

Start with a few reliable clients, price for profit and keep records.

  • Define target clients and area.
  • Set weekly hours and capacity.
  • Track hours, income, cost and repeat clients.
  • Review monthly and change one thing.
Worked example. 3 regular offices, 12 visits each month = 36 visits. Income 36 x 2,919 = 105,084 RUB; costs 36 x 2,189 = 78,804 RUB; gross profit 26,280 RUB before tax and fixed costs.

Activity. Build a 3-month plan with capacity and review dates.

Standard to meet. Plan matches hours to capacity.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: a job scope, a time and price calculation, a chemical safety table, a quality checklist and a client agreement outline. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Marking rubric

Timing and priceOver 5 percent off.Within 5 percent with exclusions.Also models margin by client.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is the difference between cleaning and disinfecting?

Model answer: Cleaning removes dirt; disinfecting kills germs and needs a clean surface and contact time.

Q2. Why never mix bleach with other products?

Model answer: It can release toxic gas.

Q3. How much concentrate for 10 L at 1:40?

Model answer: About 244 mL.

Q4. What is contact time?

Model answer: The time a disinfectant must stay wet on a surface.

Q5. Why colour-code cloths?

Model answer: To prevent cross-contamination.

Q6. Why add 15 percent to a job time estimate?

Model answer: For set-up, breaks and travel.

Q7. What goes in a quote?

Model answer: Scope, frequency, price and exclusions.

Q8. A checklist of 40 has 3 fails. Pass rate?

Model answer: 92.5 percent.

Q9. Can you clean a biohazard area untrained?

Model answer: No; it needs qualified authorisation.

Q10. Why keep a key log?

Model answer: To protect client property and show who had access.
