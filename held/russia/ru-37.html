Oil and Gas

Learn the oil and gas value chain from reservoir to pipeline: geology basics, drilling, production measurement, process safety, pipelines and storage, environment and the licensing and compliance duties that surround every task.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner36 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn the oil and gas value chain from reservoir to pipeline: geology basics, drilling, production measurement, process safety, pipelines and storage, environment and the licensing and compliance duties that surround every task.

  • Understand Explain the stages of the oil and gas value chain and the role of each discipline.
  • Apply Convert and calculate volumes, rates, water cut and mass using correct units and a well-test record.
  • Analyse Interpret a production record and find the likely cause of a decline or a measurement error.
  • Evaluate Judge a work plan against permit-to-work, gas detection, hot-work and isolation rules and decide if it may start.
  • Create Design a field operating routine with safety checks, measurements, records and reporting.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare an operating pack for one written oil and gas production site: a flow and unit calculation, a safety and permit analysis, a monitoring routine and an incident and spill response sheet.

Condition

Open-book; 7 hours; calculator, conversion table and templates; written case only; no field work or live equipment.

Standard

Unit conversions error-free; mass balance within 2 percent; permit analysis finds all 6 planted gaps; response sheet lists first actions within 10 minutes; pass mark 80 percent.

Who it is for: People who plan to work or train in oil and gas operations, service companies, supply chains, safety or environment roles. Requirements: Adult learner; basic mathematics and science. Site entry requires medical fitness, site induction and employer-approved competence. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Oil and gas work is highly regulated and dangerous: fire and explosion, toxic gas such as hydrogen sulphide, high pressure, confined spaces, heights, and environmental harm. Exploration, production, pipelines, storage and transport need licences, permits, trained and certified staff, industrial safety declarations and state supervision. Check with the competent Russian authority and a qualified adviser. This course is education only and does not authorise anyone to work on equipment; always follow the site's own procedures and stop work if unsure.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The value chain and the rules around it 4 hUnderstand

Oil and gas move through upstream (finding and producing), midstream (treating, storing, transporting) and downstream (refining and selling). Every stage has permits and supervision.

  • Upstream: surveys, exploration drilling, appraisal, development, production. Midstream: separation, treatment, pipelines, storage, loading. Downstream: refining and distribution.
  • Subsurface rights, land use, environmental approvals, industrial safety and technical supervision are separate permissions; none replaces another.
  • Local communities, workers and the environment can be harmed, so impact assessment, monitoring and reporting are required duties.
  • Keep a compliance register: permit, holder, scope, expiry date and the person who checks it.
Worked example. Register row: pipeline operation permit; holder: operator; scope: 12 km, 0.3 m diameter; expiry: 30 June; check: quarterly by the compliance lead; status: valid, renewal started 90 days ahead.

Activity. Draw the value chain and fill a compliance register of 8 permissions for a written site.

Standard to meet. All stages shown; 8 permissions with owner and expiry; no gap in the checks.

Module 2 Geology and reservoir basics 4 hUnderstand

Hydrocarbons are held in porous rock under a seal. Porosity and permeability decide how much is stored and how easily it flows.

  • A reservoir needs source rock, a porous reservoir rock, a seal and a trap. Missing any one means no accumulation.
  • Porosity is the pore volume as a percent of rock volume; permeability is the ease of flow.
  • Oil in place (stock-tank) = area x thickness x porosity x (1 - water saturation) divided by formation volume factor.
  • Recovery factors vary widely; use only measured or approved values, never hopeful ones.
Worked example. Area 2,000,000 m2, thickness 10 m, porosity 0.18, water saturation 0.30, volume factor 1.2: 2,000,000 x 10 x 0.18 x 0.70 / 1.2 = 2,100,000 m3 in place.

Activity. Calculate in-place volume for two cases and explain which input matters most.

Standard to meet. Both results correct; inputs listed with units.

Module 3 Wells and drilling 5 hUnderstand

Wells connect the reservoir to the surface. Drilling is risky; barriers keep pressure under control.

  • A well has casing strings, cement, a wellhead and a completion; each is a barrier or part of the barrier system.
  • Drilling mud density balances formation pressure; too low risks an influx (kick), too high risks losses.
  • Well control is a trained, certified function. Warning signs (pit gain, flow with pumps off) must lead to immediate shut-in under the site procedure.
  • Record depth, mud weight, pressures and events on the daily drilling report.
Worked example. Hydrostatic pressure = density x gravity x depth. Mud 1,200 kg/m3 at 2,500 m: 1,200 x 9.81 x 2,500 = 29.4 MPa. If formation pressure is 30 MPa, the well is under-balanced; raise the mud density under the site procedure.

Activity. Calculate hydrostatic pressure at three depths for two mud densities and compare with a formation pressure.

Standard to meet. Calculations in MPa correct; balance state named; warning signs listed.

Module 4 Production measurement and units 5 hApply

You cannot manage what you do not measure. Correct units and test routines make reports reliable.

  • Well test gives total liquid rate, water cut and gas rate. Oil rate = liquid rate x (1 - water cut).
  • Mass = volume x density. Typical crude is about 0.85 t per m3; 1 m3 = 6.29 barrels.
  • Gas-oil ratio = gas volume divided by oil volume at standard conditions.
  • Check calibration dates on meters and the allocation method between wells and the sales meter.
Worked example. Test: liquid 120 m3/day, water cut 30 percent, gas 9,000 m3/day. Oil = 84 m3/day = 71.4 t/day = 528 barrels/day. GOR = 9,000 / 84 = 107 m3 of gas per m3 of oil.

Activity. Process a 5-well test table: oil rate, mass, water cut change and GOR for each.

Standard to meet. All figures correct; units written on every line.

Module 5 Process safety, gas and hot work 6 hEvaluate

Most major incidents come from loss of containment and ignition. The safeguards are simple and strict.

  • Permit-to-work: a written permit defines the task, hazards, isolation, gas test, people and time. Work does not start without it.
  • Gas detection: flammable gas and toxic gas (hydrogen sulphide) must be monitored with calibrated detectors and personal alarms; leave at the first alarm and follow muster.
  • Isolation: lock, tag and test before breaking a line. Verify zero pressure.
  • Hazardous area equipment, grounding, and no ignition sources in classified areas. Hot work needs a gas test and a fire watch.
Worked example. Hot work on a skid: gas test 0 percent of the lower explosive limit before work, repeated every 60 minutes; fire watch for 30 minutes after; isolation by double block and bleed, locked and tagged; permit valid 8 hours.

Activity. Review three permits and find the 6 planted gaps.

Standard to meet. All gaps found; each fix is stated in one line.

Module 6 Pipelines, tanks and flow 4 hApply

Moving fluid safely means knowing flow, pressure, corrosion and leaks.

  • Flow rate Q = area x velocity. A pipe of 0.3 m inner diameter has an area of 0.0707 m2.
  • Corrosion, erosion and third-party damage are the main failure causes; inspection and cathodic protection manage them.
  • Tank measurements: level, temperature and water cut convert to net volume; check high-level alarms.
  • Use leak detection by pressure and mass balance; investigate any imbalance over the set limit.
Worked example. v = 1.5 m/s: Q = 0.0707 x 1.5 = 0.106 m3/s = 382 m3/h. Inlet 382 m3/h, outlet 372 m3/h: imbalance 10 m3/h = 2.6 percent, above a 2 percent alarm; start the leak investigation.

Activity. Calculate flow and imbalance for 3 pipeline cases and decide whether to investigate.

Standard to meet. Flows and percent correct; decision matches the limit.

Module 7 Environment, spills and flaring 4 hEvaluate

Operators must limit harm to air, water and soil, and report incidents to the competent authority.

  • Prevent: secondary containment, drip trays, inspection rounds, tank overfill protection.
  • Respond: stop the source if safe, raise the alarm, contain, report, recover and clean under a plan with trained staff.
  • Flaring and venting are monitored and limited under the relevant rules; measure and record volumes.
  • Keep waste records and send waste only to licensed handlers.
Worked example. Spill of 2 m3 on a bunded pad of 40 m2: depth if uncontained 5 cm; bund holds 15 m3, so the spill is contained. Actions: stop the leak, absorb, recover 2 m3, record, report as required, test soil.

Activity. Complete a spill response sheet with first actions in 10 minutes and the reporting steps.

Standard to meet. Actions in order; contact roles named; waste route lawful.

Module 8 Design an operating routine 4 hCreate

Combine measurement, safety and compliance into a daily routine that others can follow.

  • Daily round: gauges, leaks, alarms, tank levels, safety equipment and records.
  • Weekly: calibration checks, permit audit, drills. Monthly: production reconciliation and compliance register review.
  • Define stop-work authority: anyone can stop work for safety.
  • Set reporting lines and times for incidents, near misses and deviations.
Worked example. Reconciliation: wells total 520 t/day; sales meter 508 t/day; tank change +6 t/day; losses 6 t/day (1.2 percent), within the 2 percent limit.

Activity. Write a one-page routine and a reconciliation for a written 4-well site.

Standard to meet. Reconciliation within 2 percent; routine covers daily, weekly and monthly checks.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name the three stages of the value chain.

Model answer: Upstream, midstream and downstream.

Q2. What four elements must a reservoir have?

Model answer: Source, reservoir rock, seal and trap.

Q3. How is oil rate found from a well test?

Model answer: Liquid rate x (1 - water cut).

Q4. Convert 84 m3 of oil to tonnes at 0.85 t/m3.

Model answer: 71.4 tonnes.

Q5. What does a permit-to-work contain?

Model answer: Task, hazards, isolation, gas test, people and valid time.

Q6. What do you do at a gas alarm?

Model answer: Stop, leave by the safe route and go to the muster point per the procedure.

Q7. Why is hydrogen sulphide special?

Model answer: It is toxic at very low levels and can disable the sense of smell; detection and escape rules are essential.

Q8. A pipeline imbalance is 2.6 percent and the limit is 2 percent. What next?

Model answer: Start the leak investigation and follow the procedure.

Q9. Who may stop work for safety?

Model answer: Anyone on site.

Q10. Who checks permits and permissions?

Model answer: The compliance lead against the register, and the competent authority through supervision.
