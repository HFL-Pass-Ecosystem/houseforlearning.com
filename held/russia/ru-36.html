HELD · RU-36

Drones

Learn to plan and fly small unmanned aircraft lawfully and safely for inspection, mapping and imaging: rules, risk assessment, flight planning, image-quality calculations, data handling and records.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to plan and fly small unmanned aircraft lawfully and safely for inspection, mapping and imaging: rules, risk assessment, flight planning, image-quality calculations, data handling and records.

  • Understand Explain the parts of a small unmanned aircraft and how weight, wind and battery limit a flight.
  • Apply Complete a pre-flight check, a flight log and a post-flight check without missing a step.
  • Analyse Calculate ground sample distance, image footprint, line spacing and flight time for a mapping job.
  • Evaluate Judge a planned flight against airspace, people, weather and equipment limits and decide go or no-go.
  • Create Design a mission plan with a risk assessment, permissions list, emergency actions and data plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan one inspection or mapping mission over a written site (about 20 hectares), with a legal and airspace check, risk assessment, flight plan calculations, checklist set and data plan.

Condition

Open-book; 6 hours; map, calculator, templates; no live flying is required for the written assessment; any practice flight is on a permitted site with a supervisor.

Standard

Permissions list complete; GSD, spacing and flight time calculated within 5 percent of the key; risk assessment covers 8 hazards; go or no-go decision justified; pass mark 80 percent.

Who it is for: People who want to use small drones for survey, inspection, farming, media or public safety work, and managers who commission such work. Requirements: Adult learner; basic arithmetic and map reading. Pilots may need registration, training or certification as the rules require. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Unmanned aircraft flights are regulated. Registration, pilot qualification, permission to use airspace, restricted and prohibited zones, flights over people, privacy and data protection, and rules for carrying loads all apply, and they change. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before every type of flight. Never fly near airports, crowds, critical sites or beyond the rules. This course does not authorise any flight.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Rules, airspace and permissions 5 hUnderstand

The first question for any flight is whether it is allowed. Rules depend on aircraft mass, location, purpose and the pilot.

  • Sort aircraft by mass category as set in the relevant federal rules, because registration and permission needs depend on it.
  • Check airspace: controlled zones, restricted and prohibited areas, nearby airports, power lines and critical sites.
  • Obtain permission to use the airspace where it is required, and the landowner's consent to take off and land.
  • Respect privacy: do not film people or private property without lawful grounds; follow data rules.
Worked example. Permissions list for one job: aircraft mass 0.9 kg (category per rules), registration done, airspace request submitted 3 days before, landowner letter signed, insurance valid to 31 December, area 2.4 km from the nearest airfield boundary.

Activity. Build a permissions list for a written site with 10 items and mark each as done, to do or not allowed.

Standard to meet. All 10 items checked; any not-allowed item stops the plan.

Module 2 Aircraft, power and the physics of flight 4 hUnderstand

A multirotor lifts by thrust against weight; a fixed wing flies on lift. Know the main parts and limits.

  • Parts: frame, motors, propellers, flight controller, sensors, radio link, battery, camera or payload.
  • Thrust-to-weight ratio of at least 2 gives control margin. A 1.2 kg aircraft needs at least 2.4 kg of total thrust.
  • Battery energy (Wh) = voltage x capacity in Ah. Flight uses 70 to 80 percent of capacity; keep a reserve.
  • Cold, altitude and wind all reduce flight time; payload mass reduces it more.
Worked example. Battery 15.2 V x 5.0 Ah = 76 Wh. Average draw 220 W gives 76 / 220 = 0.345 h = 20.7 min total; usable 75 percent = 15.5 min. A 10 percent loss in cold cuts this to 14 min.

Activity. Calculate the flight time of three aircraft and set a land-by time for each.

Standard to meet. Energy and times correct; reserve applied.

Module 3 Weather and site conditions 4 hEvaluate

Wind, rain and visibility decide go or no-go as much as the aircraft does.

  • Use the maker's limits: for small aircraft often about 10 m/s of wind at height. Take gusts into account and stay well below the limit.
  • No flight in rain, fog or poor light unless the aircraft and rules allow it. Keep visual line of sight where required.
  • Walk the site: obstacles, trees, wires, people, roads, take-off and landing area, emergency landing spots.
  • Record weather at the start of the mission: wind, temperature, cloud and visibility.
Worked example. Forecast wind 7 m/s gusts 11 m/s; aircraft limit 10 m/s. Decision: no-go until gusts fall below 8 m/s. Re-check in 60 minutes.

Activity. Make go or no-go calls for five weather cases and justify each in one line.

Standard to meet. At least 4 of 5 correct; gusts and limits compared.

Module 4 Risk assessment and emergencies 5 hAnalyse

A written assessment finds what can go wrong and what you will do. It shapes the flight plan.

  • List hazards: loss of link, low battery, GPS error, bird strike, collision, flyaway, fire from battery, person approaching.
  • Score likelihood (1 to 3) x effect (1 to 3) and add a control for scores of 4 or more.
  • Set return-to-home height above the tallest obstacle plus 20 metres, and test failsafe settings.
  • Handle battery damage safely: swollen cells are removed, not charged, and stored in a non-flammable container.
Worked example. Hazard: loss of link over a road. 2 x 3 = 6. Controls: fly away from the road, return-to-home at 60 m (tallest tree 35 m + 20 m = 55 m, rounded up), safety observer on the road side.

Activity. Complete a risk table of 8 hazards for the case site.

Standard to meet. Eight hazards scored; every 4+ has a control; return height calculated.

Module 5 Mission planning for mapping 5 hApply

Mapping needs overlapping images at a known height. The numbers decide the quality and the battery plan.

  • GSD (cm per pixel) = sensor width (mm) x height (m) x 100 divided by focal length (mm) x image width (px).
  • Image footprint width = GSD x image width in pixels. Line spacing = footprint x (1 - side overlap).
  • Use about 75 percent front and 65 to 70 percent side overlap for survey.
  • Flight time = total line length divided by speed, plus turns and take-off and landing; split into batteries.
Worked example. Sensor 13.2 mm, focal 8.8 mm, 5472 px, height 100 m: GSD = 13.2 x 100 x 100 / (8.8 x 5472) = 2.74 cm/px. Footprint 150 m. At 70 percent side overlap the spacing is 45 m. Site 200,000 square metres: line length about 4,444 m; at 8 m/s = 556 s = 9.3 min, one battery.

Activity. Compute GSD, footprint, spacing and flight time for a 40 ha site at 80 m, and the number of batteries.

Standard to meet. Each value within 5 percent; battery count follows the land-by time.

Module 6 Inspection and imaging work 4 hApply

For roofs, towers, pipelines and crops, the plan changes: smaller distances, angles and safe stand-off.

  • Keep a safe stand-off from structures, lines and people; do not fly near energised lines without the owner's approval and a competent person.
  • Choose the camera angle and distance so a defect of 5 mm appears at least 3 pixels wide; this sets the maximum distance.
  • Capture overlapping photos with consistent light, and log location, time and image number for each.
  • Mark and report defects by severity with photos, location and suggested follow-up by an expert.
Worked example. Camera gives 0.2 mm per pixel at 2 m. To see a 5 mm crack at 3 pixels needs 1.67 mm per pixel; allowed distance up to about 2 x 8.3 = 16 m, but wind and safety limit it to 5 m.

Activity. Plan an inspection route for a chimney or roof with distances, angles and a defect log template.

Standard to meet. Stand-off shown; resolution maths correct; log template complete.

Module 7 Data, records and privacy 4 hEvaluate

Images and logs are valuable and sensitive. Keep them in order and protect people.

  • Name files with date, site and flight number. Back up to two places the same day.
  • Keep flight logs: pilot, aircraft, battery, times, weather, issues and permissions used.
  • Blur or remove people and vehicles when the purpose does not need them; follow the data rules and the client's terms.
  • Quality-check maps with at least 5 ground control points; error should be within 2 times the GSD.
Worked example. 1,200 images of 20 MB = 24 GB per flight; two backups = 72 GB. A map with GSD 2.74 cm has an error limit of about 5.5 cm.

Activity. Create a file naming plan, a log template and a backup and privacy note for a job.

Standard to meet. Naming works for 3 flights; backup rule and privacy step stated.

Module 8 Design the mission file 4 hCreate

Bring everything into one pack that a supervisor or client can review before you fly.

  • Cover sheet: purpose, area map, date, pilot, aircraft, observer, contacts.
  • Attach permissions, risk table, calculations, checklists, weather rule and emergency plan.
  • State a stop rule: any new person, wind above limit, link loss or low battery means land.
  • Add a short debrief form to record lessons after each flight.
Worked example. Pack for a 20 ha mapping job: 3 flights of 9 minutes, 2 batteries, 1 observer; decision: go if wind < 8 m/s and visibility > 5 km; stop rule written; debrief after landing.

Activity. Assemble the mission file for the case site.

Standard to meet. All parts present; decision rule is measurable; calculations match the plan.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: permissions list, risk assessment, mission plan with calculations, three checklists, flight log sample and a data plan. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is the first question before any flight?

Model answer: Whether the flight is allowed, covering registration, airspace, permission and the pilot's status.

Q2. A battery is 15.2 V and 5.0 Ah. What is its energy?

Model answer: 76 Wh.

Q3. Why use only 70 to 80 percent of battery capacity?

Model answer: To keep a reserve for return, cold, wind and ageing.

Q4. What does a thrust-to-weight ratio of 2 give?

Model answer: Control margin to climb and manoeuvre safely.

Q5. Define GSD.

Model answer: The ground distance represented by one image pixel, in cm per pixel.

Q6. If footprint is 150 m and side overlap is 70 percent, what is line spacing?

Model answer: 45 m.

Q7. What do you do with a swollen battery?

Model answer: Stop use, do not charge, and store in a non-flammable container.

Q8. How is return-to-home height chosen?

Model answer: Tallest obstacle plus a safety margin, such as 20 m.

Q9. Why blur people in images?

Model answer: To protect privacy when the job does not need identifiable people.

Q10. When is a no-go correct?

Model answer: When wind, gusts, visibility, airspace, people or equipment limits are not met.
