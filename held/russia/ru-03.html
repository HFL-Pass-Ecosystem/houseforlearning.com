HELD · RU-03

Metallurgy and Fabrication

Understand metals and how they are cut, formed, joined and finished in a fabrication workshop, and plan a small job from drawing to costed, quality-checked part. Workshop safety is part of every lesson.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner36 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Understand metals and how they are cut, formed, joined and finished in a fabrication workshop, and plan a small job from drawing to costed, quality-checked part. Workshop safety is part of every lesson.

  • Understand Explain the properties of common steels and non-ferrous metals and how heat and working change them.
  • Apply Calculate material weight, cutting lists, nesting and offcut waste for a fabricated part.
  • Analyse Compare cutting, forming and joining methods for a given job on cost, accuracy and risk.
  • Evaluate Judge a weld or fabricated part against the drawing and a quality checklist.
  • Create Build a job pack with drawing review, cutting list, process route, cost estimate and inspection record.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a costed job pack for one fabricated steel part, including a cutting list, process route and inspection record.

Condition

Individual work over 6 weeks with a drawing, tape and calliper, a calculator and the course templates; practical work only under a qualified supervisor.

Standard

Weights and cutting list correct to within 2 percent; process route safe and complete; estimate reconciles; 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Workshop assistants, apprentices, small fabricators, maintenance staff and people planning to start a small workshop. Requirements: Basic arithmetic and the ability to read a simple drawing; practical work needs a supervised workshop. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Hot work, cutting, grinding, lifting and electrical equipment cause serious injury and fire. Practical work must be done under a trained supervisor with protective equipment, ventilation, fire precautions and safe lifting. Welding of pressure parts, lifting equipment and structural work needs qualified, certified people and inspection under the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. This course gives no legal advice and no substitute for such qualification. Check which licences, certificates and inspections apply with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Metals and their properties 4 hUnderstand

Choice of metal sets strength, weight, corrosion resistance, cost and how a part can be made.

  • Compare mild steel, alloy steel, stainless steel, cast iron, aluminium and copper alloys.
  • Define strength, hardness, toughness and ductility in plain terms.
  • Explain how heat treatment and cold working change properties.
  • Read a material certificate and match it to the job.
Worked example. Steel has a density of about 7,850 kg/m3; aluminium about 2,700 kg/m3. A 1 m x 1 m x 10 mm plate weighs about 78.5 kg in steel and 27 kg in aluminium.

Activity. Choose a metal for three parts with reasons.

Standard to meet. Choices are justified by load, environment and cost.

Module 2 Reading drawings and measuring 5 hApply

A drawing carries sizes, tolerances, welds and finishes. Measuring correctly is the base of quality.

  • Read views, sections, dimensions and weld symbols.
  • Use tape, rule, square, vernier calliper and level; know each reading error.
  • Apply tolerance: a part 100 mm +/- 1 mm passes between 99 and 101 mm.
  • Mark out and check before cutting.
Worked example. A bracket is 250 mm long with a +/- 1 mm tolerance. Measured pieces: 249.4, 250.9, 251.4 mm. The third is out of tolerance and fails.

Activity. Check five dimensions on a sample part against a drawing.

Standard to meet. All dimensions recorded and pass or fail judged correctly.

Module 3 Cutting, nesting and material cost 5 hApply

Material is often the largest cost. Good nesting and cutting lists reduce offcuts.

  • Weight = volume x density; use tables for sections and plates.
  • Prepare a cutting list with part sizes, quantity and kerf allowance.
  • Nest parts to reduce waste; calculate yield percent.
  • Compare methods: shear, saw, flame, plasma, laser, abrasive; note accuracy and heat-affected zone.
Worked example. A 2,000 x 1,000 mm sheet, 5 mm steel, weighs 78.5 kg. Parts use 1,640,000 mm2 of 2,000,000 mm2 = 82 percent yield. At 85 RUB/kg the sheet costs 6,672.5 RUB and waste is 1,201 RUB.

Activity. Make a cutting list and nesting plan for ten parts and compute yield.

Standard to meet. Yield and cost within 2 percent of the mentor key.

Module 4 Forming and bending 4 hApply

Bending, rolling and pressing change shape without removing material. Springback, minimum radius and grain direction matter.

  • Estimate bend allowance from thickness and radius.
  • Respect minimum bend radius to avoid cracking.
  • Allow springback and test on offcuts.
  • Choose tooling to suit thickness and length.
Worked example. Bending 3 mm sheet to 90 degrees with 3 mm inside radius; bend allowance is about pi x (R + K x t) x 90/180 with K near 0.33: 3.14 x (3 + 1) / 2 = 6.3 mm.

Activity. Calculate flat length for a two-bend channel.

Standard to meet. Flat length within 1 mm of the key.

Module 5 Joining: welding, bolting and finishing 5 hAnalyse

Joining choices change strength, cost and inspection need. Welding needs trained, qualified people.

  • Compare MMA, MIG/MAG, TIG and spot welding, bolts and rivets.
  • Explain distortion, preheat, filler choice and joint preparation in plain terms.
  • List typical weld faults: porosity, lack of fusion, undercut, cracks.
  • Choose finishes: paint, galvanising, powder coat; prepare surfaces.
Worked example. A frame has 12 m of fillet weld at 6 mm leg. Time at 3 m/hour = 4 hours. At 400 RUB/hour labour the weld cost is 1,600 RUB plus consumables.

Activity. Pick joining methods for three jobs and note inspection needs.

Standard to meet. Method fits the job and the qualification need is stated.

Module 6 Workshop safety and hot work 5 hEvaluate

Safety is a condition of work. Most injuries come from lifting, cuts, burns, eyes, noise, fumes and electricity.

  • List protective equipment per task: eyes, face, hearing, hands, feet, respiratory.
  • Control hot work: clear combustibles, fire watch, extinguisher, permit if required.
  • Lift safely: weight limits, slings, crane signals, no work under load.
  • Keep guards on machines, isolate before maintenance.
Worked example. Hot work near 20 litres of solvent: the permit stops the job until the solvent is moved 11 m away and a fire watch is set for 30 minutes after work.

Activity. Complete a risk assessment for one task.

Standard to meet. Hazards, controls and a responsible person are stated.

Module 7 Inspection and quality records 4 hEvaluate

Inspection proves the part matches the drawing and standard. Records protect both customer and workshop.

  • Visual check, dimensional check and, where needed, non-destructive tests by qualified staff.
  • Record material batch, welder, date and result.
  • Control non-conforming parts: tag, separate, decide rework or scrap.
  • Calibrate measuring tools on a schedule.
Worked example. Of 50 parts, 3 fail dimensional check: a reject rate of 6 percent. At 1,200 RUB per part, rework of 3 parts at 300 RUB costs 900 RUB.

Activity. Prepare an inspection sheet and judge ten parts.

Standard to meet. Sheet records every check; judgements agree with the key for at least 9 of 10.

Module 8 Job pack and costing 4 hCreate

A quote joins material, labour, machine time, finish and margin.

  • Total material cost with waste allowance.
  • Estimate labour hours per operation.
  • Add machine, consumables, finish, transport and overhead.
  • Quote a price and check margin; agree changes in writing.
Worked example. Material 6,672 RUB, labour 8 h x 400 = 3,200 RUB, consumables 600 RUB, finish 1,500 RUB, overhead 15 percent of 11,972 = 1,796 RUB. Cost 13,768 RUB. A 20 percent margin gives a quote of 16,522 RUB.

Activity. Build a complete job pack for one part.

Standard to meet. Pack is consistent and arithmetic within 2 percent.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: weight and cutting list, nesting plan, process route, cost estimate, inspection record. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is the approximate density of steel?

Model answer: About 7,850 kg/m3.

Q2. How do you find plate weight?

Model answer: Volume times density.

Q3. What is kerf?

Model answer: The width of material removed by the cut.

Q4. What is springback?

Model answer: The partial return of a bent part toward its original shape.

Q5. Name two common weld faults.

Model answer: For example porosity and lack of fusion (or undercut, cracks).

Q6. Who may weld pressure or lifting parts?

Model answer: Only qualified, certified people under the relevant rules and inspection.

Q7. What must be cleared before hot work?

Model answer: Combustibles, with a fire watch and an extinguisher ready.

Q8. A part is 250 mm +/- 1 mm. It measures 251.4 mm. Pass or fail?

Model answer: Fail; it is outside the tolerance.

Q9. Why calibrate measuring tools?

Model answer: So readings can be trusted.

Q10. What do you do with a non-conforming part?

Model answer: Tag and separate it, then decide rework or scrap.
