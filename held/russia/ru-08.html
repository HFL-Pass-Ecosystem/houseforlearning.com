HELD · RU-08

Cooking Gas Distribution

Understand the lawful, safe handling and distribution of cooking gas (LPG cylinders and similar supplies): properties, cylinder handling, storage, transport, customer safety, records and the licensing limits. Safety and compliance come first.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner36 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Understand the lawful, safe handling and distribution of cooking gas (LPG cylinders and similar supplies): properties, cylinder handling, storage, transport, customer safety, records and the licensing limits. Safety and compliance come first.

  • Understand Explain the properties and hazards of liquefied petroleum gas and the layers of control around it.
  • Apply Calculate cylinder fill, cylinder counts, delivery loads and unit margins.
  • Analyse Compare delivery and storage arrangements on safety, cost and customer service.
  • Evaluate Judge a storage area, a vehicle or a customer installation against a safety checklist.
  • Create Build a compliance-first operating plan with checklists, records, an emergency plan and a cost model.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a compliance-first operating plan for a small cooking gas distribution point, with safety checklists, records, an emergency plan and a unit cost model.

Condition

Desk-based training over 6 weeks using case studies, checklists and a spreadsheet; no handling of gas or cylinders is part of this course.

Standard

Checklists cover storage, transport, delivery and emergency; unit cost model correct to within 2 percent; plan states each licence or approval to confirm; 9 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Delivery staff, depot assistants, small distributors, retail staff and people considering this business. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. No prior experience is needed. No practical work with gas is part of the course. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Liquefied gas is flammable and can explode; handling, storage, filling, transport and sale are licensed, inspected and strictly regulated under the relevant federal rules, which cover premises, vehicles, equipment, trained and certified staff, cylinder testing and insurance. This course is desk-based awareness and planning, not training to handle gas. Do not fill, repair, move or store cylinders without lawful authority, certified training and the required approvals. Before any step, confirm the licence and rules with the competent Russian authority and a qualified local adviser. In a leak, move people away, avoid sparks and call the emergency service. No income is promised.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Properties and hazards of LPG 5 hUnderstand

LPG is stored as a liquid under pressure and becomes a heavier-than-air gas on release. It is flammable in a narrow range of mixture with air and can collect in low places.

  • Explain why LPG is heavier than air and collects in pits and cellars.
  • Describe flammable range, ignition sources and why a small leak is serious.
  • Explain that cylinders must never be filled completely, to allow for liquid expansion.
  • Name the odorant and why it is added.
Worked example. A 50 L cylinder is filled to about 85 percent: 42.5 L of liquid; LPG liquid is about 0.54 kg/L, so about 23 kg. Overfilling to 100 percent would leave no room for expansion with heat. Check: 50 x 0.85 = 42.5 L and 42.5 x 0.54 = 22.95 kg. This is a desk exercise only; filling is done solely by licensed, certified fillers.

Activity. Build a hazard table: hazard, cause, effect, control, and the person responsible for each control, using the figures above.

Standard to meet. Table has at least 6 hazards and a control and owner for each; the fill and mass figures are correct to within 2 percent; no step tells a learner to handle gas.

Module 2 The legal and licensing frame 5 hEvaluate

Distribution of gas is a regulated activity. The first task is to know what is needed and who decides.

  • List the kinds of approval to check: business licence or permission, premises approval, vehicle and driver rules, staff certification, equipment inspection, insurance.
  • Separate what a seller may do from what only a licensed filler may do.
  • Keep a compliance register with the status and date of each item.
  • Check with the competent Russian authority and a qualified adviser before any step.
Worked example. A register lists 10 items; 7 confirmed, 3 open. With 3 items open the plan states "no operation until all 10 are confirmed". Check: 7 + 3 = 10, so 70 percent are confirmed, but the stop rule applies until the share is 100 percent. Each open item has an owner, a date to ask the competent authority, and a note of the answer received.

Activity. Build a compliance register with owners, dates, source of each rule and status, and write the stop rule at the top.

Standard to meet. At least eight approval types are listed with a status, an owner and a date; the stop rule is written; every item names the competent authority or adviser who confirms it.

Module 3 Cylinders: types, marks and checks 4 hApply

Each cylinder has marks for owner, capacity, test date and valve type. A cylinder past its test date or damaged must not be used.

  • Read the stamped marks: capacity, test date, next test.
  • Inspect for dents, rust, valve damage and leaks by trained staff.
  • Quarantine and tag suspect cylinders; return them to the owner for testing.
  • Keep cylinder records: serial, owner, location, date.
Worked example. Of 200 cylinders in a check, 6 are past test date and 4 are dented: 10 of 200 = 5 percent are quarantined and 190 stay in use.

Activity. Fill in a cylinder inspection sheet for ten sample cylinders (photos).

Standard to meet. Defects found and quarantine decisions correct.

Module 4 Storage and handling rules 5 hEvaluate

Storage is designed for safe distances, ventilation, no ignition and fire response. Only approved places may be used.

  • Store upright with valve caps on, secured against falling.
  • Keep distances to buildings, drains and ignition sources as the rules require.
  • Ventilate the area; ban smoking and open flame; use approved electrical fittings.
  • Keep fire extinguishers and signs; limit quantity to the approved amount.
Worked example. An approved store allows 100 cylinders x 23 kg = 2.3 tonnes. A delivery of 120 would exceed the limit by 20 cylinders (460 kg); the extra 20 are not accepted. Check: 120 - 100 = 20 and 20 x 23 = 460 kg, so the delivery would be 20 percent over the approved limit. The driver keeps the extra cylinders on the vehicle or returns them, and the refusal is logged with date and quantity.

Activity. Audit a storage case study against a 12-point checklist covering distances, ventilation, ignition, fire cover, signs and quantity, and rate each fault by risk.

Standard to meet. All 12 points are marked pass or fail; each fault is rated high, medium or low with a reason; every high fault has a corrective action and a date; the quantity limit is checked.

Module 5 Transport and delivery 5 hApply

Vehicles, loading and driver training are controlled. Cylinders are secured, upright and not left in a hot closed vehicle.

  • Check the vehicle is approved and equipped for the load.
  • Secure cylinders upright with valve protection; never exceed the load limit.
  • Plan routes; avoid tunnels or crowded places if the rules require.
  • Hand over with a leak check by a trained person and a record.
Worked example. A truck is approved for 1,000 kg of gas product. Each full cylinder weighs 23 kg gas plus 22 kg steel = 45 kg; the maximum is 22 cylinders (990 kg). Check: 23 + 22 = 45 kg, 1,000 / 45 = 22.2 so 22 whole cylinders, and 22 x 45 = 990. A route of 20 customers with one cylinder each is 900 kg, 90 percent of the limit, leaving 100 kg unused. Only a trained, authorised driver may deliver.

Activity. Plan a delivery route and load for 20 customers with weights, stop order, handover checks and the record kept at each stop.

Standard to meet. Total load is within the approved limit and shown in kg; the route lists all stops; each handover has a leak check by a trained person and a signed record; weather and heat controls are stated.

Module 6 Customer safety and installation 4 hApply

The customer must know how to use and store cylinders. Installation work is done only by qualified people.

  • Give a short safety briefing: ventilation, hose condition, leak check with soapy water, shut-off.
  • Never advise customers to carry out repairs.
  • Keep a complaint and incident log.
  • Offer a hose and regulator replacement schedule from a qualified supplier.
Worked example. A hose is 5 years old against a 3-year replacement limit set by the maker: it is replaced, and the sale of a new hose at 400 RUB is recorded. Check: 5 - 3 = 2 years overdue. The seller does not fit the hose; a qualified person does, and the date is noted so the next replacement falls due in 3 years.

Activity. Write a one-page customer safety card covering ventilation, hose age, a soapy-water leak check, shut-off and who to call, in plain words.

Standard to meet. The card is correct, fits one page, uses words a customer understands, tells no customer to repair equipment, and was read back by a peer with at least 4 of 5 points recalled.

Module 7 Emergency response and incidents 4 hEvaluate

Plans must be written, practised and posted. Staff know who to call and what not to do.

  • Leak: stop the work, remove ignition sources, move people away upwind, call the emergency service.
  • Fire: do not fight a cylinder fire unless trained; evacuate.
  • Write an incident report and tell the authority as required.
  • Practise a drill twice a year.
Worked example. A drill clears 12 staff and 3 customers from the depot in 90 seconds against a 120-second target; the time and any issues are logged. Check: 12 + 3 = 15 people cleared, and 90 s is 30 s inside the 120 s target, a margin of 25 percent (30 / 120). Even so, note issues such as a blocked exit and fix them before the next drill in six months.

Activity. Write an emergency plan for a depot with roles, emergency numbers, leak and fire steps, assembly point, incident report form and a drill record.

Standard to meet. The plan has named roles, numbers, steps and a drill record; the drill time is compared with its target; the plan says staff do not fight a cylinder fire unless trained; it is reviewed twice a year.

Module 8 Unit cost and operating plan 4 hCreate

The plan joins compliance, safety, cost and service. Cost per cylinder must include the cost of safety and compliance.

  • List costs per cylinder: purchase, transport, storage, labour, inspection, insurance, losses.
  • Add compliance costs such as training and equipment checks.
  • Compute margin and break-even deliveries per month.
  • State the stop rule: no operation without confirmed approvals.
Worked example. Purchase 900 RUB, transport 60, storage 20, labour 70, compliance and insurance 50: cost 1,100 RUB. Sell at 1,300 RUB: margin 200 RUB (15.4 percent). Fixed cost 60,000 RUB per month needs 300 cylinders to break even. Check: 900 + 60 + 20 + 70 + 50 = 1,100; 1,300 - 1,100 = 200; 200 / 1,300 = 15.4 percent; 60,000 / 200 = 300. If compliance cost rises by 30 RUB, margin is 170 RUB and break-even becomes 353 cylinders (60,000 / 170), so safety cost must never be cut.

Activity. Build the operating plan and cost model with unit cost, margin, break-even, a price or volume test and the stop rule on the first page.

Standard to meet. Costs add up and are within 2 percent; compliance cost is included; the test is shown; the stop rule is written; every licence to confirm is listed with the authority that decides.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why does LPG collect in low places?

Model answer: It is heavier than air, so a leak sinks and gathers in pits and cellars, where one spark can ignite it.

Q2. Why is a cylinder never filled completely?

Model answer: Liquid expands with heat, so the cylinder needs free space to avoid dangerous pressure; only licensed fillers set the fill level.

Q3. What do you do with a cylinder past its test date?

Model answer: Quarantine it and return it to the owner for testing.

Q4. Who may fill cylinders?

Model answer: Only a licensed and approved filler with certified staff.

Q5. How is a customer leak check done?

Model answer: With soapy water by a trained person, never a flame.

Q6. What should happen in a gas leak?

Model answer: Stop work, remove ignition sources, move people away and call the emergency service.

Q7. Why keep a compliance register?

Model answer: To track every licence, approval and check, and stop work if one is open.

Q8. How do you find a truck's maximum cylinders?

Model answer: Divide the approved load by the weight of one full cylinder.

Q9. Should a seller advise customers to repair regulators?

Model answer: No; only qualified people do that.

Q10. Who confirms licence rules?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a qualified local adviser.
