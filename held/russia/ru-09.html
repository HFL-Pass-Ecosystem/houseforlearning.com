HELD · RU-09

Transportation

Plan, cost and run safe road freight and passenger-style transport jobs: routes, vehicle checks, driver hours, load security, trip costing and delivery records.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner28 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Plan, cost and run safe road freight and passenger-style transport jobs: routes, vehicle checks, driver hours, load security, trip costing and delivery records.

  • Understand Explain how a trip is planned from order to proof of delivery and which records each step produces.
  • Apply Calculate trip distance, fuel use, driver time and cost per kilometre for a given route and vehicle.
  • Analyse Compare route and load options and identify the cheapest option that still meets the delivery window and safety limits.
  • Evaluate Judge a vehicle, driver and load for legal and safe departure using a written checklist.
  • Create Build a one-week dispatch plan with costing, compliance records and an incident response sheet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare and cost a one-week dispatch plan for three vehicles and eight deliveries, with pre-trip checks, driver hours and delivery records.

Condition

Desk exercise with a route table, vehicle data sheet, load list and spreadsheet; 4 hours; no live driving.

Standard

Costs within 3 percent of the model figure, no driver-hours breach, every load has a securing note, and at least 90 percent of rubric points met.

Who it is for: Dispatchers, small fleet owners, new drivers who want to plan, and anyone moving into logistics coordination. Requirements: Basic arithmetic and spreadsheet use. A driving licence is not needed for the course but is needed to drive in real life. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Road transport is regulated. Carrying goods or passengers for hire, dangerous goods, driver hours, vehicle inspection and insurance are all governed by the relevant federal rules and the competent Russian authority. This course teaches planning and records, not legal advice. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before you start operating, and never plan a trip that breaks driver-hours, weight or load-securing limits.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How a trip flows 3 hUnderstand

Every trip is a chain: order, plan, check, load, drive, deliver, record. A gap in the chain is where late, damaged or disputed deliveries come from.

  • Name the seven steps and the record each one leaves behind.
  • Separate the order (what, where, when) from the plan (which vehicle, which driver, which route).
  • Explain why proof of delivery protects both carrier and customer.
  • Show where hand-offs happen and who signs.
Worked example. Order: 1,200 kg of packaged goods from depot to a shop 180 km away, window 08:00 to 12:00. Plan: 3.5 t van, depart 05:30. Records: order, checklist, load note, delivery note signed at 09:40.

Activity. Draw the seven-step chain for one real or invented delivery and list the record at each step.

Standard to meet. All seven steps present, each with a named record and a named owner.

Module 2 Routes, distance and time 4 hApply

A route is judged on distance, time, road type and risk, not on distance alone. Time includes loading, rest and unloading.

  • Use average speeds by road type to estimate driving time, then add stops.
  • Add a buffer of 10 to 15 percent for weather and queues.
  • Plan return legs so the vehicle is not empty where possible.
  • Check bridge, weight and access limits on the route before departure.
Worked example. Route 180 km: 120 km at 80 km/h = 1.5 h, 60 km at 50 km/h = 1.2 h, total 2.7 h. Add 15 percent buffer = 3.1 h. Add 45 min load and 30 min unload = 4.35 h door to door.

Activity. Plan three routes of different length from one depot and compute door-to-door time for each.

Standard to meet. Times within 10 minutes of the model and a buffer shown on each route.

Module 3 Vehicle checks and upkeep 4 hEvaluate

An unsafe vehicle is a legal and human risk. A short, written daily check finds most faults before they become failures.

  • Build a walk-round list: tyres, lights, brakes, fluids, mirrors, load area, documents.
  • Define which faults stop the trip and which can be reported for repair.
  • Keep a service schedule by kilometres and by date.
  • Record every defect, who found it and when it was fixed.
Worked example. Van with service due every 15,000 km. Odometer 87,400 km, last service at 75,000 km: 12,400 km driven, 2,600 km left. At 400 km a week the service is due in 6.5 weeks, so book it in week 5.

Activity. Write a one-page pre-trip checklist and run it against a sample fault report; mark go or no-go.

Standard to meet. Every no-go fault is caught and the service due date is correct to within 1 week.

Module 4 Driver hours and fatigue 3 hApply

Tired drivers cause crashes. Driving and rest limits exist in federal rules and must shape the schedule, not be fixed afterwards.

  • Check the current driving, break and rest limits with the competent authority and write them on the plan.
  • Build breaks into the schedule, not into the buffer.
  • Keep a simple log: start, breaks, end, kilometres.
  • Refuse a plan that needs the driver over the limit; split the trip or add a driver.
Worked example. Planned day: 9 h of driving would exceed a 9 h daily limit once loading is added to duty time. Option: split into two days or relay with a second driver at the 5 h point, giving 4.5 h each.

Activity. Take an 11-hour driving plan and rebuild it so each driver stays inside the limit you have confirmed.

Standard to meet. No driver over the confirmed limit and breaks are shown in the schedule.

Module 5 Load planning and securing 3 hApply

Weight limits and load securing are about stability and axle load. A load that moves in braking is a danger to everyone.

  • Check payload against the vehicle rating, never the gross figure alone.
  • Place heavy items low and forward; spread weight across the floor.
  • Use straps, blocking and anti-slip mats and note the number of tie-downs.
  • Photograph the secured load before departure.
Worked example. Payload rating 1,500 kg. Load: 24 crates of 38 kg = 912 kg plus 6 drums of 55 kg = 330 kg; total 1,242 kg, margin 258 kg (17 percent). Use 6 straps, 4 over the crates and 2 over the drums.

Activity. Plan the load for a given list of items, show total weight and margin, and write a securing note.

Standard to meet. Total within payload, heavy items low and a securing method noted for each group.

Module 6 Costing a trip 4 hAnalyse

Price must cover fuel, driver, vehicle wear, insurance, tolls, overhead and a margin for risk. Cost per kilometre is the base number.

  • Split costs into fixed (per month) and variable (per kilometre).
  • Calculate fuel from consumption per 100 km and the pump price.
  • Add driver time, wear and tolls, then overhead.
  • Compare the quote with cost and find the break-even load.
Worked example. Trip 180 km one way, 360 km return. Fuel 12 L per 100 km x 360 = 43.2 L at 62 RUB = 2,678 RUB. Driver 10 h at 350 RUB = 3,500 RUB. Wear 6 RUB/km x 360 = 2,160 RUB. Tolls 400 RUB. Total variable 8,738 RUB; cost per km 24.3 RUB.

Activity. Cost three trips and set a quote for each with a 12 percent margin.

Standard to meet. Each total within 3 percent of the model and the margin shown separately.

Module 7 Compliance, insurance and incidents 3 hEvaluate

Operating for hire needs the right permissions, documents and insurance. When something goes wrong, calm and record keeping decide the outcome.

  • List the documents that travel with the vehicle and driver and who checks them.
  • Ask the competent authority and an adviser which permit applies to your kind of transport.
  • Write a three-step incident sheet: make safe, call, record.
  • Photograph, note witnesses and keep the load documents.
Worked example. Minor collision, no injury: stop, hazard lights, call the emergency service if needed, photograph 6 views, note the time and place, inform the dispatcher within 15 minutes and the insurer the same day.

Activity. Write an incident sheet and a document checklist for your own planned operation.

Standard to meet. Sheet has make-safe, call and record steps; checklist names a checker for each document.

Module 8 Dispatch plan and review 4 hCreate

Bring it together: a week of work with cost, compliance and a review of what went wrong.

  • Allocate vehicles and drivers to jobs by capacity, location and hours.
  • Attach checklist, load note and cost to each trip.
  • Track on-time delivery and cost per kilometre.
  • Hold a weekly review and change one thing.
Worked example. Week: 8 deliveries, 3 vehicles, 1,920 km, total cost 46,700 RUB = 24.3 RUB/km. 7 of 8 on time = 87.5 percent. Change: start the late route 30 minutes earlier.

Activity. Build the full one-week dispatch plan from the data pack.

Standard to meet. Plan passes all checks above and the review names one measurable change.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: costed trip sheets, a pre-trip checklist, a driver-hours log, a load securing note, a delivery record and an incident sheet. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name the seven steps of a trip.

Model answer: Order, plan, check, load, drive, deliver, record.

Q2. Why add a buffer to the driving time?

Model answer: Weather, queues and stops are normal; a 10 to 15 percent buffer keeps the delivery window realistic.

Q3. A vehicle has a payload of 1,500 kg and a load of 1,242 kg. What is the margin?

Model answer: 258 kg, about 17 percent.

Q4. Who decides the driver-hours limit you plan to?

Model answer: The relevant federal rules and the competent authority; confirm the current figures before planning.

Q5. What is the first step after a minor collision?

Model answer: Make the scene safe, then call for help if needed and record.

Q6. Fuel 12 L/100 km over 360 km at 62 RUB per litre costs what?

Model answer: 43.2 L x 62 = 2,678 RUB (rounded).

Q7. Why photograph a secured load?

Model answer: It is evidence the load was secured at departure.

Q8. What is the difference between fixed and variable cost?

Model answer: Fixed cost is per month whatever you drive; variable cost rises with kilometres.

Q9. A pre-trip check finds a brake fault. Go or no-go?

Model answer: No-go until repaired and re-checked.

Q10. What does proof of delivery protect?

Model answer: Both carrier and customer against disputes about time, quantity and condition.

jobs this leads to

Where this takes you

  • Dispatcher. Plans trips, allocates drivers and keeps delivery records.
  • Fleet coordinator. Tracks maintenance, documents and costs for a small fleet.
  • Transport planner. Designs routes and schedules to meet service windows at lowest cost.
  • Owner-driver (with the right permits). Runs one vehicle as a small business and keeps own compliance records.
  • Warehouse and loading supervisor. Plans and checks load securing and hand-offs.
