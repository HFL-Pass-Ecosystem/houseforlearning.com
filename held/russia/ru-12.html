Sporting Goods

Select, fit, sell, maintain and advise on sporting goods: equipment sizing, safety standards, stock seasonality, repairs and honest advice for different sports.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner28 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Select, fit, sell, maintain and advise on sporting goods: equipment sizing, safety standards, stock seasonality, repairs and honest advice for different sports.

  • Understand Explain how sport, user level and body size decide the right equipment.
  • Apply Measure and fit a customer for a common item such as footwear, a helmet or a bicycle.
  • Analyse Compare products on safety, durability, fit and price for a stated user.
  • Evaluate Judge when a used or damaged item is unsafe and must not be sold or used.
  • Create Build a seasonal range plan with stock, display and an advice sheet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Advise three customers on equipment, build a seasonal range plan for a small sporting goods shop, and write a safety and returns sheet.

Condition

Desk exercise with product data, a measuring guide and a spreadsheet; 4 hours.

Standard

Fit and size calculations correct, safety limits stated, range plan within a 600,000 RUB buying budget, and at least 90 percent of rubric points.

Who it is for: Sports shop staff, club equipment managers, online sellers and parents or coaches who buy for others. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. Interest in sport is helpful. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Sporting goods can cause injury if wrong, damaged or misused: helmets, climbing and water-safety gear, protective equipment and children products need special care. Product conformity, labelling and consumer rules are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. This course gives general guidance, not medical or coaching advice. Check with the competent Russian authority, the maker instructions and a qualified coach. Never sell or advise use of safety gear that is damaged, expired or past its life.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Sport, user and need 3 hUnderstand

The right item depends on who uses it, how often and where. A beginner and a racer need different things.

  • Ask about sport, level, frequency, place, budget and goals.
  • Separate need from want.
  • Note health, age and size limits.
  • Record the answers on a simple form.
Worked example. Customer: adult, beginner runner, 3 runs per week, 5 km, road. Need: cushioned road shoes, budget 6,000 RUB. Not needed: trail spikes or a 15,000 RUB racing model.

Activity. Fill the needs form for three different customers.

Standard to meet. Each form leads to a clear item type and budget band.

Module 2 Sizing and fit 4 hApply

A poorly fitted item causes blisters, strain or injury. Measure, do not guess.

  • Measure foot length in the afternoon, standing.
  • Leave about 10 to 12 mm of space at the toe for running shoes.
  • Size helmets by head circumference; it must sit level and not move.
  • Size bicycles by rider height and inside leg.
Worked example. Foot length 265 mm plus 12 mm toe space = 277 mm internal length. Choose the size whose insole is about 277 mm, per the maker size chart.

Activity. Measure two people and choose a size from a chart you are given.

Standard to meet. Measurements correct to 2 mm and size choice justified.

Module 3 Safety and standards 4 hEvaluate

Protective gear works only if it meets the rules, fits and is undamaged. Check what the maker says.

  • Look for the conformity marking the competent authority requires.
  • Check manufacture date and maker life guidance.
  • Replace a helmet after a significant impact even if it looks fine.
  • Never advise gear beyond the maker stated use.
Worked example. Helmet bought 6 years ago, maker guidance suggests replacement after 5 years or after any hard impact. Decision: do not sell it as new, advise replacement.

Activity. Inspect a list of ten described items and mark safe, repair or retire.

Standard to meet. All unsafe items retired with a reason.

Module 4 Comparing products 3 hAnalyse

Compare on fit, safety, weight, durability, warranty and price per year of use.

  • List criteria that matter for the user.
  • Weight them.
  • Calculate cost per use or per season.
  • Be honest about trade-offs.
Worked example. Shoe A 5,500 RUB lasts 700 km = 7.86 RUB/km. Shoe B 8,000 RUB lasts 1,100 km = 7.27 RUB/km. B is cheaper per km but costs 2,500 RUB more now; advise by budget and mileage.

Activity. Compare three products for one customer in a table.

Standard to meet. Table has weights, cost per use and a clear recommendation.

Module 5 Seasonal stock and buying 4 hCreate

Sport sells in seasons. Buy early enough to have stock and late enough to sell it.

  • Map the sport seasons for your region.
  • Plan opening buy, reorder and markdown dates.
  • Limit risky lines to small buys.
  • Plan markdowns to clear stock without losing money.
Worked example. Winter budget 600,000 RUB: 55 percent core (330,000 RUB), 30 percent seasonal (180,000 RUB), 15 percent test lines (90,000 RUB). Markdown week 8 at 20 percent off for items with over 6 weeks cover.

Activity. Build a quarter range plan with budget split.

Standard to meet. Splits sum to the budget and markdown rules are dated.

Module 6 Care, repair and used goods 3 hApply

Good care extends life and keeps customers safe. Know what you can fix and what you must refer.

  • Clean, dry and store gear as the maker advises.
  • Do simple repairs only within your skill.
  • Refer safety-critical repairs to a qualified person.
  • Sell used goods only if safe and described honestly.
Worked example. Used bicycle: tyres 40 percent worn, brake pads 60 percent worn. Replace pads (safety critical) 700 RUB; tyres can wait 500 km; price drops 1,200 RUB.

Activity. Write a care sheet and a used-goods checklist.

Standard to meet. Checklist separates safety-critical from cosmetic faults.

Module 7 Advice, honesty and returns 3 hEvaluate

Trust brings customers back. Honest advice, clear returns and no medical claims.

  • Do not make health or performance promises.
  • Explain the return rules from the competent authority and your policy.
  • Record complaints and fix causes.
  • Refer injury questions to a health professional.
Worked example. Month: 240 sales, 12 returns = 5 percent. Cause: 7 size errors. Action: add a second fit check; target 3 percent.

Activity. Write a returns policy and an advice script.

Standard to meet. Policy is clear, fair and without false claims.

Module 8 Shop plan and review 4 hCreate

Combine range, fit, safety and records into a monthly plan.

  • Set sales and margin targets.
  • Schedule staff training on fitting.
  • Run a monthly safety audit of display and rental gear.
  • Review returns and stock.
Worked example. Monthly sales 420,000 RUB at 33 percent gross margin = 138,600 RUB. Fit training 4 h per person. Audit finds 3 items retired.

Activity. Build a monthly plan with targets and audit dates.

Standard to meet. Plan has targets, training, audit and review.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: a fitting record, a product comparison, a safety check sheet, a seasonal range plan and a returns procedure. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. How much toe space for running shoes?

Model answer: About 10 to 12 mm.

Q2. When should a helmet be replaced?

Model answer: After a significant impact or at the maker life guidance.

Q3. Cost per km of a 5,500 RUB shoe lasting 700 km?

Model answer: 7.86 RUB per km.

Q4. Why measure feet in the afternoon?

Model answer: Feet swell during the day.

Q5. Can you promise a medical benefit from a product?

Model answer: No; refer health questions to a professional.

Q6. A bicycle brake pad is 60 percent worn. Safety critical?

Model answer: Yes, brakes are safety critical; replace or refer.

Q7. Why use small buys for test lines?

Model answer: To limit risk on unproven products.

Q8. What goes on a needs form?

Model answer: Sport, level, frequency, place, budget and goals.

Q9. Why track returns?

Model answer: To find and fix causes such as size errors.

Q10. Who sets conformity rules for gear?

Model answer: The relevant federal rules and the competent authority.
