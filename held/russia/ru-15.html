Education (Teaching Practice and Multilingual Digital Delivery)

Plan, teach and assess lessons with clear outcomes, and deliver them online in more than one language: accessible materials, simple tools, feedback and safe handling of learner data.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner28 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Plan, teach and assess lessons with clear outcomes, and deliver them online in more than one language: accessible materials, simple tools, feedback and safe handling of learner data.

  • Understand Explain how learning outcomes, activities and assessment line up.
  • Apply Write a lesson plan with timed activities, checks for understanding and materials.
  • Analyse Compare learner results to find gaps and adjust teaching.
  • Evaluate Judge a digital lesson for accessibility, language clarity and learner safety.
  • Create Build a short multilingual online module with a rubric and a feedback plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design and teach a 45-minute lesson, then adapt it as a short online module in two languages with an assessment and rubric.

Condition

Desk exercise plus a short peer teaching slot; 6 hours; any common presentation and quiz tools.

Standard

Outcomes measurable, timings add up, every question maps to an outcome, text readable in both languages, and at least 90 percent of rubric points.

Who it is for: New teachers and tutors, trainers in companies, community educators and people moving lessons online. Requirements: Knowledge of one subject and the ability to write clearly in at least one language; a second language is needed for the multilingual part. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Teaching children and handling learner data is regulated. Licensing or qualification for formal teaching, child-safeguarding duties, background checks, data protection and accessibility duties are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. This course covers practice, not a licence to teach in a school. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Never record or share learners images or data without proper permission, and report child-safety concerns through the proper channel.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Outcomes that can be tested 3 hUnderstand

Start with what learners will do at the end. Good outcomes use action verbs and can be checked.

  • Write outcomes with verbs such as explain, calculate, compare.
  • Avoid vague words such as know or appreciate.
  • Match each outcome to an activity and a check.
  • Limit a lesson to 2 or 3 outcomes.
Worked example. Weak: Understand fractions. Strong: By the end learners add two fractions with unlike denominators correctly in 4 of 5 questions in 10 minutes.

Activity. Rewrite five weak outcomes as testable ones.

Standard to meet. Each outcome has a verb, a condition and a standard.

Module 2 Planning a lesson 4 hApply

A plan sets the time, steps, materials and checks. Leave room for questions.

  • Use a simple shape: start, teach, practise, check, close.
  • Time every step and total it.
  • List materials and room set-up.
  • Plan for fast and slow learners.
Worked example. 45 min lesson: start 5, teach 10, practise 15, check 10, close 5 = 45. Reserve 5 minutes spare by shortening practise to 12 minutes.

Activity. Write a 45-minute plan with timings that add up.

Standard to meet. Total equals 45 and each step maps to an outcome.

Module 3 Explaining and questioning 3 hApply

Short, clear explanations plus good questions keep learners thinking.

  • Explain with an example before a rule.
  • Ask open questions and wait 3 to 5 seconds.
  • Check understanding with quick tasks, not just Is that clear?
  • Correct errors kindly and specifically.
Worked example. Class of 24: ask a quick question to all, 5 answer aloud, 24 answer on mini boards. You see 24 answers instead of 5, which is 100 percent coverage.

Activity. Prepare 8 checking questions for one outcome.

Standard to meet. Questions test the outcome at different levels.

Module 4 Assessment and feedback 4 hEvaluate

Assessment shows who has learned and what to teach next. Feedback must be timely and specific.

  • Use a rubric with clear levels.
  • Mix quick checks and a final task.
  • Give feedback with one strength and one next step.
  • Keep scores private.
Worked example. Quiz of 10 questions, class of 24: mean 7.1. Q4 correct for 9 of 24 (37.5 percent). Reteach Q4 topic next lesson; do not change the others.

Activity. Analyse a sample set of results and write a reteach plan.

Standard to meet. Plan targets the weakest item with evidence.

Module 5 Inclusion and learners needs 3 hEvaluate

Learners differ in language, ability and background. Plan so all can take part.

  • Use plain language and short sentences.
  • Offer more than one way to show learning.
  • Provide large print or readable fonts where needed.
  • Ask the school or the competent authority how support needs are handled.
Worked example. Text at 14 pt, 1.5 line spacing, contrast ratio over 4.5:1. A glossary of 10 key words given in two languages before the lesson.

Activity. Adapt one lesson for three different learner needs.

Standard to meet. Adaptations are practical and respectful.

Module 6 Multilingual digital delivery 4 hCreate

An online module in two languages needs clear structure, consistent terms and careful translation, not machine output alone.

  • Keep short chunks: 5 to 8 minutes per unit.
  • Use the same term for the same idea in both languages.
  • Have a second reader check the translation.
  • Provide captions and text alternatives for media.
Worked example. Module: 6 units of 6 minutes = 36 minutes, 2 languages. Translation of 1,200 words reviewed in 2 hours; 14 terms in the glossary. Captions for 12 minutes of video.

Activity. Build a 3-unit module in two languages with a glossary.

Standard to meet. Terms consistent and every media item has captions or text.

Module 7 Online tools, privacy and safety 3 hEvaluate

Tools must be easy, accessible and safe. Learner data is private.

  • Choose tools that work on low bandwidth and basic phones.
  • Collect only the data you need.
  • Get permission before recording or sharing learner work.
  • Follow child-safeguarding duties and report concerns correctly.
Worked example. Class of 30 with 6 learners on slow connections: provide downloadable 2 MB files and a text version. Attendance data kept for 1 term, then deleted per the rules.

Activity. Write a tool and privacy checklist for your module.

Standard to meet. Checklist covers access, data, permission and reporting.

Module 8 Teach, review and improve 4 hCreate

Teach it, gather evidence and improve. A short cycle repeated often builds skill.

  • Teach to peers and collect feedback against the rubric.
  • Review results against outcomes.
  • Change one thing and test again.
  • Keep a short teaching log.
Worked example. Pilot with 12 learners: 9 met the standard = 75 percent. Target 80 percent. Change: add a worked example; re-test with 12 learners.

Activity. Teach a 10-minute segment and complete a review.

Standard to meet. Review names evidence and one change.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: a lesson plan, a rubric, a two-language online module, a question bank mapped to outcomes and a feedback and data-handling note. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What makes an outcome testable?

Model answer: An action verb, a condition and a standard.

Q2. How should a 45-minute lesson be timed?

Model answer: Steps add up to 45 minutes with a small spare.

Q3. Why wait 3 to 5 seconds after a question?

Model answer: It gives learners time to think.

Q4. What does a rubric do?

Model answer: It shows levels of quality so marking and feedback are consistent.

Q5. Q4 was right for 9 of 24. What percent?

Model answer: 37.5 percent.

Q6. Why check translation with a second reader?

Model answer: To catch meaning errors and keep terms consistent.

Q7. What minimum text contrast is a common guide?

Model answer: About 4.5:1.

Q8. Should you share a learner photo without permission?

Model answer: No.

Q9. Who sets teaching qualification rules?

Model answer: The relevant federal rules and the competent authority.

Q10. What should you do with a child-safety concern?

Model answer: Report it through the proper channel straight away.
