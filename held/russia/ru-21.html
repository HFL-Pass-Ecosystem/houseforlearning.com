Childcare

Learn to care for children safely and kindly: routines, development, play, health, safeguarding and clear communication with parents.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner31 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to care for children safely and kindly: routines, development, play, health, safeguarding and clear communication with parents.

  • Understand Explain stages of child development from birth to 7 years.
  • Apply Run a safe daily routine for a small group.
  • Analyse Identify hazards and rank them by likelihood and harm.
  • Evaluate Judge whether a child's behaviour or health needs action or referral.
  • Create Build an activity plan, a record sheet and a parent update.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and run a half-day session for a small group and keep proper records.

Condition

Over 6 weeks, under supervision of a qualified carer, with a checklist, record sheets and play materials.

Standard

Child to adult ratio kept at or below the limit set by the competent authority; all hazards on a 20-item checklist checked; records complete; pass mark 9 of 10 on safeguarding items.

Who it is for: Assistants, parents and new carers. Requirements: Minimum age and clearances as set by the competent authority; a supervising qualified carer. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Childcare is a regulated field. Working alone with children, running a setting, and giving first aid or medicine require checks, qualifications, licence or registration set by the competent Russian authority and the relevant federal rules. This course does not replace them. If a child is at risk, report to the person in charge and the competent body at once. Do not diagnose illness or give medicine without authority. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Child development 4 hUnderstand

Children grow in steps. Knowing the steps tells you what is fair to expect.

  • Describe physical, language, social and thinking steps from 0 to 7 years.
  • Note that each child differs.
  • Observe and record without labels.
  • Match play to stage.
  • Spot delays to raise with the supervisor.
Worked example. At 12 months many children say 1 to 3 words; at 24 months about 50 words. A child far below these may be flagged to the supervisor, never diagnosed.

Activity. Observe one child for 10 minutes and record neutral notes.

Standard to meet. Notes factual and dated.

Module 2 Safety and hazards 4 hAnalyse

Most harm is preventable. A risk check finds hazards before children do.

  • List hazards: water, heat, small objects, stairs, doors, plants, chemicals.
  • Rank by likelihood x harm on a 1 to 5 scale.
  • Apply controls: gates, covers, storage.
  • Check equipment daily.
  • Plan fire exit and practice.
Worked example. Cleaning spray within reach: likelihood 3 x harm 5 = 15. Move to a locked cupboard at 150 cm or higher, then recheck.

Activity. Complete a 20-item hazard checklist on a room.

Standard to meet. Every item rated and a control listed.

Module 3 Ratios and supervision 3 hApply

Enough adults, watching, is the first safety measure.

  • Know the ratio for each age set by the competent authority.
  • Count children at each change of place.
  • Keep sight lines open.
  • Never leave a child unattended with water or a risk.
  • Sign children in and out.
Worked example. With a limit of 1 adult to 8 children aged 3 to 7, a group of 18 needs at least 3 adults (18 / 8 = 2.25, round up).

Activity. Compute adults needed for four group sizes.

Standard to meet. All correct and rounded up.

Module 4 Daily routine and care 5 hApply

A steady day gives security. Care tasks are part of learning.

  • Plan arrival, play, snack, rest, outdoor time, tidy up.
  • Handwashing before eating and after toilet.
  • Meals and allergies: check the list daily.
  • Toilet and nappy changing with privacy and records.
  • Rest times by age.
Worked example. A 4-hour session: arrival 30 min, free play 60, snack 20, outdoor 60, story 30, activity 40. Total 240 minutes.

Activity. Write a timed routine for a half day.

Standard to meet. Times sum to the session; care tasks included.

Module 5 Play and learning 4 hCreate

Play is how children learn. Plans should be simple and flexible.

  • Choose a goal: language, movement, counting, sharing.
  • Prepare materials that are safe and age-fit.
  • Offer choice and follow interest.
  • Include quiet and active play.
  • Record what children did.
Worked example. Counting game with 10 blocks: each child sorts into groups of 2, 5 and 10. Goal: match number to amount up to 10.

Activity. Plan two activities with goals.

Standard to meet. Each has goal, materials, steps and safety note.

Module 6 Health, first aid and illness 4 hEvaluate

Know when to act and who to call. Only trained people give first aid.

  • Know signs of illness that need a parent call.
  • Handwashing and cleaning to limit spread.
  • Record accidents and tell parents.
  • Medicine only with written consent and authority.
  • Call emergency services for serious signs.
Worked example. A child has a temperature over 38 C and is lethargic: call the parent and supervisor, separate quietly, record the time of each step.

Activity. Fill an incident form for a sample fall.

Standard to meet. Time, place, injury, action and who told.

Module 7 Safeguarding and behaviour 4 hEvaluate

Children's safety comes first. Fair, calm guidance builds trust.

  • Recognise signs of harm: injuries, fear, change in behaviour.
  • Report to the person in charge and follow the route set by the competent authority.
  • Do not investigate yourself or promise secrecy.
  • Use praise, clear limits and redirection; never harm or shame.
  • Keep photos and data private.
Worked example. A child says a bruise came from an adult at home: listen, do not question, write exact words and time, and report the same day.

Activity. Role-play a report and write a safe record.

Standard to meet. Words recorded exactly; report made the same day.

Module 8 Working with parents 3 hCreate

Parents are partners. Clear, kind, timely news builds trust.

  • Greet and hand over with a short note.
  • Share good news as well as concerns.
  • Agree contact routes and pick-up people.
  • Handle complaints calmly and log them.
  • Respect privacy.
Worked example. Parent update: 'Today Mira built a tower of 8 blocks, ate her snack, and rested 40 minutes. Please bring spare socks.'

Activity. Write three parent updates.

Standard to meet. Each is factual, kind and under 60 words.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: hazard checklist, daily routine, activity plan, attendance and incident records, and a parent update. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why record observations without labels?

Model answer: To stay factual and fair.

Q2. Hazard score for likelihood 3 and harm 5?

Model answer: 15.

Q3. Adults needed for 18 children at 1 to 8?

Model answer: 3.

Q4. Who gives medicine?

Model answer: Only authorised staff with written consent.

Q5. What do you do if a child discloses harm?

Model answer: Listen, record exact words, report the same day.

Q6. Why count children at changes of place?

Model answer: To catch missing children at once.

Q7. Where store cleaning products?

Model answer: Locked and out of reach.

Q8. How long is a 240-minute session in hours?

Model answer: 4 hours.

Q9. Can you promise a child secrecy?

Model answer: No.

Q10. Who sets ratios and registration?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority.
