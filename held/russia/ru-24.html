Petcare Services

Learn to care for pets safely as a sitter, walker or groomer: handling, feeding, health signs, safety, records and running a small service.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner32 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to care for pets safely as a sitter, walker or groomer: handling, feeding, health signs, safety, records and running a small service.

  • Understand Explain basic needs of dogs and cats: food, water, exercise, rest and health checks.
  • Apply Run a safe visit or walk with a care record.
  • Analyse Identify risk to the animal, to people and to your business.
  • Evaluate Judge when a pet needs a vet.
  • Create Build a service plan, a contract and a price list.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and run care for a pet for a day with records, and cost the service.

Condition

Over 6 weeks, with consenting owners and animals, handling gear, a first aid kit for animals and a record sheet.

Standard

All visits logged with times and feeding; hazards checked on a 15-item list; price covers costs; pass mark 8 of 10 with all health-escalation items right.

Who it is for: Pet lovers starting as sitters, walkers or groomers. Requirements: Comfort with animals; written owner consent; proof of identity for clients. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Animals can bite, scratch and carry disease. Do not treat, medicate or diagnose; only a licensed vet may do so. Boarding, breeding, selling and transport of animals may need registration or permits under the relevant federal and local rules; check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Follow vaccination and animal welfare rules. Keep your own tetanus and similar cover as advised by a doctor. Insurance for the business is wise.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Animal needs 4 hUnderstand

A healthy pet has food, clean water, movement, rest, company and a clean space every day. Dogs need regular exercise and toileting; cats need clean litter, perches, hiding places and quiet. Food is set by weight, age and the owner's plan, and is never changed suddenly. Learn normal behaviour, appetite and toileting so that any change stands out early.

  • Dogs need daily exercise and toileting 3 to 5 times a day.
  • Cats need clean litter, perches and quiet.
  • Water must be fresh and always available.
  • Food by weight and age; follow the owner's plan.
  • Know normal behaviour so change stands out.
Worked example. Task: portion food for a 10 kg dog on the owner's plan of 25 g dry food per kg per day. Step 1: 10 x 25 = 250 g a day. Step 2: split into two meals of 125 g. Step 3: a 3 kg bag at 600 RUB lasts 3,000 / 250 = 12 days, costing 50 RUB a day. Check: 2 x 125 = 250 g, and 12 days x 50 RUB = 600 RUB, matching the bag price. Keep fresh water out and follow the owner's written plan.

Activity. Write a daily care plan for a sample dog and cat.

Standard to meet. Plan lists food, water, exercise and rest.

Module 2 Handling and body language 4 hApply

Calm, clear handling keeps you and the animal safe. Dogs and cats show stress before they bite: stiff body, raised hackles, low tail, ears back, lip licking, a turned head. Approach slowly and from the side, use a well-fitted lead and harness, and stop when the animal is stressed. Never separate fighting animals with bare hands, and keep your distance from unknown animals.

  • Read signals: stiff body, raised hackles, tail low, ears back.
  • Approach slowly and from the side.
  • Use a lead and harness properly.
  • Never separate fighting animals with bare hands.
  • Stop if the animal is stressed.
Worked example. Task: plan a safe passing distance on a 1.5 m lead. Step 1: keep at least 2 m between your dog and an unknown dog, shorten the lead to 1 m and stand on the far side of your dog. Step 2: if the other dog shows warning signs, cross the street, adding about 6 m of gap. Step 3: at 4.5 km per hour you walk 75 m per minute. Check: 4,500 / 60 = 75 m per minute, so 6 m takes under 5 seconds, a small delay for a large safety gain.

Activity. Practise leading and a calm greeting with a supervised dog.

Standard to meet. No pulling or panic; signals named.

Module 3 Intake and contract 3 hCreate

Good information at the start prevents trouble later. Collect owner contact, named vet, diet, medicines, fears, bite history, vaccinations and key access, and ask for written consent for emergency vet care. Agree visit times, fees and cancellation in a short contract. Keep personal data private and locked. Check proof of identity for new clients, and never accept a pet you cannot handle safely.

  • Collect owner contact, vet, diet, medicines, fears and key access.
  • Record vaccinations and any history of bites.
  • Agree times, fees and cancellation.
  • Get written consent for emergency vet care.
  • Keep data private.
Worked example. Task: price a one-week contract. Terms: 2 visits a day of 30 minutes, 900 RUB per visit, consent to call the named vet. Step 1: visits = 2 x 7 = 14. Step 2: fee = 14 x 900 = 12,600 RUB. Step 3: cancellation with less than 24 hours notice costs half a visit, 450 RUB. Step 4: the owner sets an emergency vet limit of 5,000 RUB in writing. Check: 12,600 / 14 = 900 RUB per visit, and the contract lists dates, fee, vet, consent clause and signature.

Activity. Draft an intake form and contract.

Standard to meet. Fields complete; consent clause included.

Module 4 Walks and visits 5 hApply

A structured visit gives safe, useful care. On arrival check the pet's eyes, gait and appetite, then walk a safe route with bags and water. Never leave a pet in a hot car or tied up alone, and lock doors and gates behind you. Shorten walks in heat and ice, and avoid busy roads. Log the time, toileting, food eaten and anything unusual for the owner.

  • Check the pet on arrival: eyes, gait, appetite.
  • Walk a safe route; carry bags and water.
  • Never leave a pet in a hot car or tied up.
  • Lock doors and gates behind you.
  • Log time, toileting and food eaten.
Worked example. Task: plan a walk distance. Step 1: 45 minutes at 4.5 km per hour = 0.75 x 4.5 = 3.4 km. Step 2: in 30 C weather cut to 20 minutes in shade: 20 / 60 x 4.5 = 1.5 km. Step 3: test the pavement; if you cannot hold the back of your hand on it for 5 seconds, it is too hot for paws. Check: 1.5 km is less than half of 3.4 km, and the log records start time, route, toileting, water and weather.

Activity. Run and log a walk or visit.

Standard to meet. Log has times, food and notes.

Module 5 Grooming and hygiene 4 hApply

Grooming prevents matting and skin problems and lets you spot lumps, parasites and sore ears early. Brush by coat type, bathe in lukewarm water with pet shampoo, never human products, and dry thoroughly. Trim nails only if trained, because cutting the quick causes pain and bleeding. Clean tools between animals to stop infection spreading, and stop if the animal is distressed or aggressive.

  • Brush by coat type.
  • Bathe with pet shampoo, never human products.
  • Trim nails only if trained.
  • Check ears, teeth, skin for signs.
  • Clean tools between animals.
Worked example. Task: plan coat care for a long-coated dog. Step 1: brushing 10 minutes daily = 70 minutes a week. Step 2: a bath every 4 to 6 weeks means 9 to 13 baths a year (52 / 6 = 8.7 and 52 / 4 = 13), in lukewarm water. Step 3: a 250 ml bottle of pet shampoo at 300 RUB gives 10 baths at 25 ml, so 30 RUB a bath. Check: 250 / 25 = 10 and 300 / 10 = 30 RUB; findings on ears, teeth and skin are noted for the owner.

Activity. Groom a supervised animal and record findings.

Standard to meet. No injuries; findings noted.

Module 6 Health signs and first aid 4 hEvaluate

You are not a vet. Your job is to notice, record and call. Learn the red flags: repeated vomiting, a bloated belly, blood, seizures, heat stress, difficulty breathing and collapse. For any red flag, call the owner and the named vet at once, keep the pet cool, calm and still, and give no human medicine. Write down what you saw and the time so the vet gets clear facts.

  • Know red flags: vomiting more than twice, bloated belly, blood, seizures, heat stress, difficulty breathing.
  • Call the owner and vet at once for red flags.
  • Keep the pet cool, calm and still.
  • Do not give human medicine.
  • Record what you saw and when.
Worked example. Task: act on heat stress. A dog is panting hard, drooling and weak after a 30-minute walk in 32 C. Step 1: move to shade at once and note the time, 15:10. Step 2: offer small amounts of cool water, not ice-cold, and wet the paws and belly with cool water. Step 3: call the vet immediately, then the owner, and follow the vet's transport advice. Check: the record shows 32 C, 30 minutes, the signs, the call at 15:12 and that no medicine was given.

Activity. Sort ten signs into watch, call owner or call vet.

Standard to meet. Nine of ten sorted correctly.

Module 7 Risk, safety and rules 4 hAnalyse

Risk covers animals, people and your business. List hazards for each route and home: traffic, other dogs, toxic plants, heat, ice, and escape through doors and gates. Score each by likelihood times harm, then add a control for the highest scores. Insure against injury and loss, learn registration needs from the competent authority, and keep clean records of incidents and near misses.

  • List hazards: traffic, other dogs, plants, heat, escape.
  • Rank by likelihood x harm.
  • Insure against injury and loss.
  • Know registration needs from the competent authority.
  • Keep clean records.
Worked example. Task: score an open-door hazard on scales of 1 to 5. Step 1: likelihood 3 and harm 5 (a dog on a road) give 3 x 5 = 15. Step 2: control: double-check locks and use a second lead clip, which lowers likelihood to 1. Step 3: new score = 1 x 5 = 5. Check: the reduction is 15 - 5 = 10 points, or 67 percent, and the control is written on the 15-item hazard list with the date it was checked.

Activity. Complete a 15-item hazard list for a route and a home.

Standard to meet. Items rated; controls listed.

Module 8 Pricing and running the service 4 hCreate

A price must cover time, travel, supplies, insurance and idle gaps between visits, not only the hour with the pet. Add these costs, set a price per visit and per walk, and plan a week schedule with travel gaps so that it is feasible. Handle reviews and complaints calmly, keep written records, and follow tax and registration rules from the competent authority.

  • Add time, travel, supplies and insurance.
  • Set a price per visit and per walk.
  • Plan a week schedule with travel gaps.
  • Handle reviews and complaints.
  • Follow tax and registration rules.
Worked example. Task: price a 45-minute walk. Step 1: 45 minutes plus 15 minutes travel = 1 hour. Step 2: labour 500 RUB per hour plus 60 RUB for bags and insurance share gives a break-even of 560 RUB. Step 3: add a 20 percent margin: 560 x 1.2 = 672 RUB, rounded to 700 RUB. Step 4: four walks a day for 5 days = 20 walks, 20 hours, 14,000 RUB. Check: 700 - 560 = 140 RUB per walk, 25 percent over cost, and 20 hours fits one working week.

Activity. Build a price list and a weekly plan.

Standard to meet. Prices cover costs; schedule is feasible.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: client intake form, care record, hazard checklist, contract and price list. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Food for a 10 kg dog at 25 g per kg?

Model answer: 250 g a day.

Q2. Who can diagnose or medicate?

Model answer: Only a licensed vet.

Q3. Name two signs of stress in a dog.

Model answer: Stiff body, ears back, low tail or raised hackles.

Q4. What do you do in heat stress?

Model answer: Move to shade, cool gently, call the vet at once.

Q5. Why get emergency consent?

Model answer: So you can get care quickly.

Q6. Hazard score: likelihood 3 x harm 5?

Model answer: 15.

Q7. Why log each visit?

Model answer: For proof of care and handover.

Q8. Which shampoo to use?

Model answer: Pet shampoo.

Q9. Never leave a pet where?

Model answer: In a hot car or tied up unattended.

Q10. Who to ask about permits?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a local adviser.
