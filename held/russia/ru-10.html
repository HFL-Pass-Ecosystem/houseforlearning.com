Language Translation

Translate and revise practical texts between two languages with accurate meaning, consistent terms, quality checks, confidentiality and a clear quote and delivery process.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner26 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Translate and revise practical texts between two languages with accurate meaning, consistent terms, quality checks, confidentiality and a clear quote and delivery process.

  • Understand Explain the difference between literal, faithful and adapted translation and when each is right.
  • Apply Translate a 500-word practical text with consistent terminology and correct numbers, dates and units.
  • Analyse Compare a draft with its source and classify errors by type and severity.
  • Evaluate Judge machine output and decide what must be corrected by a human before delivery.
  • Create Build a glossary, quote and delivery pack for a small client job.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Translate and revise a 500-word practical text, build a 20-term glossary and deliver it with a quote and a quality note.

Condition

Desk exercise with a source text, style notes and a spreadsheet; 4 hours; any dictionary or software tools you can use legally.

Standard

No meaning error in numbers or names, at most 2 minor style errors per 500 words, glossary used consistently, and at least 90 percent of rubric points.

Who it is for: Bilingual people starting paid translation work, office staff who translate and students of languages. Requirements: Strong reading ability in the source language and writing ability in the target language. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Some translations carry legal weight (for example documents for courts or official use): certification, sworn status and notarial rules are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. Do not claim a status you do not hold. Medical, legal and technical texts need subject checks. Respect client confidentiality and copyright, and do not upload private client text to services you have not checked. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser about certified work.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What translation is 3 hUnderstand

Translation carries meaning, tone and purpose across languages. The brief decides how close to the words you stay.

  • Define source and target text, purpose and reader.
  • Contrast literal, faithful and adapted styles with one example each.
  • List what must never change: names, numbers, dates, units.
  • Ask the client five brief questions before you start.
Worked example. Source sentence on a leaflet: a figure of 2.5 cm. In the target readers expect millimetres, so the brief decides: keep 2.5 cm or write 25 mm. The decision goes on the style sheet.

Activity. Write a brief of five questions and answer them for a sample job.

Standard to meet. Brief states purpose, reader, tone, format and deadline.

Module 2 Reading the source 3 hAnalyse

Most errors start in misreading. Read for the argument, the terms and the traps before translating.

  • Read the whole text once without translating.
  • Mark terms, idioms, names and numbers.
  • Flag ambiguous passages and ask the client.
  • Count the words to price and plan the job.
Worked example. Text of 500 words, 12 terms marked, 5 idioms, 9 numbers. Planned speed 350 words per hour: draft 1.4 h, revision 0.7 h, total 2.1 h.

Activity. Mark up a 300-word sample and list the five biggest risks.

Standard to meet. All numbers and names marked and risks ranked.

Module 3 Drafting 4 hApply

Write natural target-language sentences that keep the meaning. Do not translate word by word.

  • Restructure sentences to fit target-language order.
  • Convert units and formats only as the style sheet says.
  • Keep terms consistent across the text.
  • Leave a query note instead of guessing.
Worked example. Source date 03/04/2025 may mean 3 April or 4 March. Ask the client. If the answer is 3 April, write it as 3 April 2025 to avoid doubt.

Activity. Translate a 300-word text and annotate 5 decisions.

Standard to meet. No meaning errors in numbers or names and 5 decisions justified.

Module 4 Terminology and glossaries 3 hCreate

A glossary makes a long job or a repeat client consistent and saves time.

  • Collect terms with the chosen equivalent, source of the choice and note.
  • Check terms in reliable references and client material.
  • Keep one term per concept.
  • Share the glossary with the client for approval.
Worked example. Glossary of 20 terms: 14 from the client, 6 chosen by you with a reference. In a 500-word text 11 of the terms occur a total of 37 times; consistent use gives 37 of 37.

Activity. Build a 20-term glossary from a sample text and apply it.

Standard to meet. No term used two ways and each choice has a source.

Module 5 Revision and quality checks 4 hEvaluate

Revise in two passes: against the source for accuracy, then alone for reading quality. Count errors.

  • Check numbers, dates, names and units line by line.
  • Read the target aloud for flow.
  • Log errors by type: meaning, term, grammar, style.
  • Weight them: major, minor.
Worked example. Sample of 500 words. Found 1 major (wrong figure), 4 minor. Score: 1 x 5 + 4 x 1 = 9 points per 500 words. Target below 5; so another pass is needed.

Activity. Revise a flawed 300-word draft and log the errors.

Standard to meet. All major errors found and logged with a type.

Module 6 Machine tools and judgement 3 hEvaluate

Machine output saves time but can be fluent and wrong. You are responsible for the final text.

  • Check the confidentiality rules before using any online tool.
  • Compare output to source for omissions and invented facts.
  • Fix terms with the glossary.
  • Record when and how a tool was used if the client asks.
Worked example. Machine draft of 500 words: 6 meaning errors found in 30 minutes of checking. Editing time 40 min vs. translating from scratch 90 min; saving 50 min, but only if the checking is thorough.

Activity. Post-edit a machine draft and list the error types you found.

Standard to meet. All meaning errors fixed and none introduced.

Module 7 Confidentiality, certified work and ethics 3 hEvaluate

Clients trust you with private documents. Some texts need official certification you may not be able to give.

  • Sign or agree a confidentiality term before you receive private texts.
  • Do not claim certification or sworn status you do not hold.
  • Ask the competent authority which documents need certified translation.
  • Delete or return files as agreed at the end.
Worked example. A client asks for a translated diploma for an official body. You are not certified: you decline or refer the job to a person who holds the status, and you note it in writing.

Activity. Write a one-page confidentiality and scope statement for your service.

Standard to meet. States what you will and will not certify and how files are handled.

Module 8 Quote, delivery and growing a practice 3 hCreate

A clear quote and delivery note prevent most disputes.

  • Price per word or per hour with a minimum charge.
  • State the deadline, the rounds of revision and the format.
  • Deliver with a short quality note and the glossary.
  • Ask for feedback and keep a client list.
Worked example. Job of 500 words at 3 RUB per word = 1,500 RUB, minimum charge 1,000 RUB; rush rate +30 percent = 1,950 RUB. Your target is 9 RUB of income per minute worked: 2.1 h = 126 min gives 1,134 RUB, so 1,500 RUB passes.

Activity. Write a quote, a delivery note and a one-month plan for finding 3 clients.

Standard to meet. Quote has scope, price, deadline and revision terms.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the translation, a glossary, an error log, a quote and a delivery note with a quality statement. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
AccuracyMeaning error in a number or name.No meaning errors.Also explains choices for ambiguous passages.
TerminologySame concept translated two ways.Glossary used consistently.Also references sources for each term.
StyleWord-by-word, hard to read.Natural and fits purpose.Also adapts tone to the reader.
ProcessNo error log or brief.Brief, log and revision pass done.Also measures error rate over time.
EthicsClaims status not held.States scope and confidentiality.Also sets a file handling routine.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What must a brief state?

Model answer: Purpose, reader, tone, format and deadline.

Q2. Why ask about the date 03/04/2025?

Model answer: Date order differs by language; an error would change the meaning.

Q3. What is a glossary for?

Model answer: Consistent terms across a text and between jobs.

Q4. What are the two passes of revision?

Model answer: Against the source for accuracy, then alone for reading quality.

Q5. A machine draft reads well. Is it correct?

Model answer: Not necessarily; fluent text can omit or invent facts, so compare to the source.

Q6. Can you certify a translation for an official body?

Model answer: Only if you hold the status required by the competent authority; otherwise refer.

Q7. A 500-word job at 3 RUB per word costs?

Model answer: 1,500 RUB.

Q8. Which errors are major?

Model answer: Those that change meaning, such as wrong numbers or names.

Q9. Why leave a query note?

Model answer: It avoids guessing and lets the client decide.

Q10. What do you do with client files at the end?

Model answer: Delete or return them as agreed in writing.
