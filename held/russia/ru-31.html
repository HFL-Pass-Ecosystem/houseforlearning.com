Nursing Care Support

Learn the care support tasks that help a nurse or carer look after people: observation, hygiene, mobility, nutrition, infection prevention, safe records and communication. The course teaches support skills, not nursing practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner29 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn the care support tasks that help a nurse or carer look after people: observation, hygiene, mobility, nutrition, infection prevention, safe records and communication. The course teaches support skills, not nursing practice.

  • Understand Explain the limits of a care support role and when to call a nurse or doctor.
  • Apply Measure and record pulse, breathing rate and temperature correctly.
  • Analyse Compare observations over time and spot a change that needs escalation.
  • Evaluate Judge a care routine for dignity, safety and infection control.
  • Create Build a daily care plan sheet and a handover note for a care worker.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a daily care support sheet, an observation chart, a safe-moving plan, an infection-prevention checklist and a handover note for one person in a small care setting.

Condition

Given a case description, a blank chart, a spreadsheet and 5 hours of working time, with a qualified mentor to review.

Standard

All observations plotted correctly, every escalation trigger named, hand hygiene steps in order, and at least 8 of 10 check answers are right.

Who it is for: Care workers, family carers, volunteers and learners who may later train as nurses or nursing assistants. Requirements: Good health, basic arithmetic, willingness to work with people respectfully. No qualification is needed to study. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Nursing and care are regulated. What a support worker may do, medication handling, injections, wound care, licensing of care facilities and professional qualification are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. This course does not qualify anyone as a nurse and gives no medical advice. Never give medicines, diagnose or change treatment unless authorised and trained. In an emergency call the emergency services at once. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Role, limits and dignity 3 hUnderstand

A support worker helps with daily living and watches for change. Knowing the limits protects the person and the worker.

  • List what is inside the role: help with washing, eating, moving, comfort and observation.
  • List what is outside it: diagnosing, giving medicines or treatments without authority.
  • Show respect: knock, explain, ask consent, keep privacy.
  • Know whom to call: nurse in charge, doctor, emergency services.
Worked example. A resident refuses a wash. Instead of forcing it, the worker offers a choice of time, records the refusal, and tells the nurse. Respect and record, never force.

Activity. Sort 20 tasks into inside role, ask first, and outside role.

Standard to meet. At least 19 of 20 sorted correctly with reasons.

Module 2 Observation and measurement 4 hApply

Pulse, breathing and temperature are early signs. Accurate measuring and recording matter.

  • Count pulse for 30 seconds and double it, or for 60 seconds if irregular.
  • Count breathing for 60 seconds without telling the person.
  • Take temperature with a clean device and record the site.
  • Record time, value and your name on the chart.
Worked example. Pulse counted 36 in 30 seconds = 72 per minute. Breathing 15 per minute. Temperature 36.8 C. All within common adult ranges; a temperature of 38.5 C must be reported at once.

Activity. Take and chart a full set of observations on a partner.

Standard to meet. Counts are within 4 of the mentor reading and chart is complete.

Module 3 Hygiene, skin and comfort 4 hApply

Washing, mouth care and skin checks prevent infection and sores.

  • Prepare everything before starting; keep the person covered.
  • Wash from clean to dirty areas and dry well, especially skin folds.
  • Check skin for redness over bony places and report it.
  • Change position at the interval set in the care plan.
Worked example. A care plan says reposition every 2 hours: 12 changes in 24 hours. A missed night change leaves 4 hours on one side, which raises pressure sore risk, so it is reported.

Activity. Write a repositioning chart for a day and night.

Standard to meet. Times are even and each entry is signed.

Module 4 Safe moving and falls prevention 4 hApply

Poor lifting injures workers and patients. Use aids and follow the plan.

  • Assess the person, space and aids before any move.
  • Never lift all of a person's weight by hand; use hoists and sliding sheets.
  • Keep floors dry and clear, lights on, call bell within reach.
  • Report any fall and watch for delayed signs.
Worked example. A person weighs 70 kg. Lifting them by hand puts far more than a safe load on the carer; with a hoist, the carer pushes only a small force. Use the aid every time.

Activity. Write a moving plan for a bed-to-chair transfer.

Standard to meet. Plan names aids, number of staff and checks.

Module 5 Nutrition, fluids and elimination 4 hAnalyse

Enough food and drink keeps people well. Charts show problems early.

  • Offer fluids regularly; typical need is 1.5 to 2 litres a day unless a nurse says otherwise.
  • Seat the person upright to eat and drink safely.
  • Record intake and output on charts.
  • Report choking, vomiting, loss of appetite and changes in urine or stool.
Worked example. Intake in a day: 6 cups of 200 mL = 1,200 mL, below a 1,500 mL aim by 300 mL. Offer a drink every 2 hours and report if low for two days.

Activity. Total a fluid chart and note the gap.

Standard to meet. Totals correct and low intake reported.

Module 6 Infection prevention 3 hEvaluate

Hand hygiene is the single most important step to stop infection.

  • Clean hands before and after contact, before meals and after toilet.
  • Wash hands with soap for 20 seconds, covering all surfaces.
  • Wear gloves for body fluids and change between tasks.
  • Sort used items and sharps as the facility waste rules require.
Worked example. A worker with 15 contacts per shift cleans hands before and after each = 30 hand cleans. A missed one in 10 leaves 3 gaps, each a risk of passing infection.

Activity. Audit hand hygiene steps in order for a task.

Standard to meet. All steps in order and each gap has a fix.

Module 7 Recognise change and escalate 4 hAnalyse

Early reporting saves lives. Compare today with the person's usual.

  • Learn warning signs: new confusion, breathlessness, chest pain, fever, fall, bleeding.
  • Use a simple report: who, what you saw, when, what you did, what you need.
  • Call emergency services for severe signs without delay.
  • Record facts, not guesses.
Worked example. Pulse yesterday 72, today 108; temperature 37.0 then 38.2 C. The rise is 36 beats and 1.2 C, so report now using the format and do not wait for the next round.

Activity. Practise three reports with a partner.

Standard to meet. Each report is clear, factual and under 60 seconds.

Module 8 Records, handover and care plan 3 hCreate

Written records keep care continuous and show what was done.

  • Write the time, fact and your name; use plain words.
  • Keep records private and share only with those who need them.
  • Make a handover note: changes, needs, risks, to-do.
  • Follow the data protection rules of the relevant federal rules.
Worked example. Handover note for 6 residents in 10 minutes: 100 seconds each. Use the order name, change, risk, task so nothing is missed.

Activity. Write a handover note for one person from case notes.

Standard to meet. Note is accurate, brief and in order.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: observation chart, care support sheet, safe-moving plan, infection checklist and handover note. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is outside a support worker role?

Model answer: Diagnosing and giving medicines or treatments without authority.

Q2. Pulse 38 in 30 seconds. Rate?

Model answer: 76 per minute.

Q3. Why count breathing without telling the person?

Model answer: They may change their breathing if they know.

Q4. How long to wash hands?

Model answer: About 20 seconds with soap, covering all surfaces.

Q5. Why reposition at set times?

Model answer: To prevent pressure sores.

Q6. Give a safe moving rule.

Model answer: Use aids and follow the plan; do not lift full weight by hand.

Q7. A person has chest pain and breathlessness. Action?

Model answer: Call emergency services immediately.

Q8. What should a report include?

Model answer: Who, what you saw, when, what you did and what you need.

Q9. Why record facts, not guesses?

Model answer: Facts are reliable and support decisions.

Q10. A resident refuses a wash. What do you do?

Model answer: Respect the choice, offer another time, record and tell the nurse.
