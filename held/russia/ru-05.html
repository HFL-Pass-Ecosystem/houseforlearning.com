HELD · RU-05

Restaurants

Plan and run a small restaurant or cafe: concept, menu and recipe costing, kitchen and hygiene systems, staff and service, purchasing and cash control, and the permits and inspections that apply.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner36 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Plan and run a small restaurant or cafe: concept, menu and recipe costing, kitchen and hygiene systems, staff and service, purchasing and cash control, and the permits and inspections that apply.

  • Understand Explain how concept, location, menu and service fit together in a restaurant.
  • Apply Calculate recipe cost, menu price and food cost percent for a dish.
  • Analyse Compare menu items on contribution and popularity to improve the menu.
  • Evaluate Judge a kitchen against a food-safety and hygiene checklist.
  • Create Build an opening plan with menu, costings, hygiene system, staffing plan and daily cash report.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a restaurant opening plan with a costed menu, a hygiene system and a daily control sheet.

Condition

Individual work over 6 weeks with a spreadsheet, a kitchen scale, a probe thermometer and the course templates.

Standard

Recipe costs correct to within 2 percent; hygiene checklist with 15 or more points and corrective actions; daily sheet reconciles; 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Cooks, waiters, cafe owners, catering staff and people planning to open a restaurant or cafe. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. Kitchen experience helps but is not required. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Food service is strictly regulated: premises approval, food hygiene and staff health checks, allergen information, alcohol licensing where relevant, fire safety, waste handling, tax and registration. This course does not give legal advice. Before opening, confirm every permit and inspection with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Kitchens have burn, cut, slip and fire hazards; train staff and keep extinguishers and first aid ready. Restaurants fail often; no profit is promised.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Concept and location 4 hUnderstand

A clear concept names who you serve, what you offer, at what price and where.

  • Describe the customer and the occasion: quick lunch, family meal, evening out.
  • Count passers-by and competitors at the chosen place on different days.
  • Estimate seats, turns per day and average spend.
  • Check premises for ventilation, drains, fire exits and approval.
Worked example. 30 seats x 2 turns x 400 RUB average spend = 24,000 RUB per day at full use; at 60 percent use sales are 14,400 RUB.

Activity. Write a one-page concept and a seat-turn-spend estimate.

Standard to meet. Concept is clear and the estimate arithmetic is right.

Module 2 Menu design and recipe costing 5 hApply

Each dish needs a standard recipe, a yield and a cost. Menu price follows cost and market.

  • Write a standard recipe with weights and portion size.
  • Cost each ingredient per portion including trim loss.
  • Target food cost of 30 to 35 percent of selling price for many menus; test against the market.
  • Keep the menu short to reduce waste and speed service.
Worked example. Soup: 300 g vegetables at 60 RUB/kg = 18 RUB; meat 80 g at 500 RUB/kg = 40 RUB; others 12 RUB. Cost 70 RUB. At a 30 percent food cost, price = 70 / 0.30 = 233 RUB, rounded to 240 RUB.

Activity. Cost five dishes and set prices.

Standard to meet. Costs are within 2 percent and prices are rounded sensibly.

Module 3 Menu analysis 4 hAnalyse

Sort dishes by popularity and contribution margin to decide what to promote, reprice or drop.

  • Contribution = price minus food cost per portion.
  • Rank dishes by number sold and by contribution.
  • Classify as keep, promote, rework or drop.
  • Re-check after one month.
Worked example. Dish A: price 240, cost 70, contribution 170 RUB, 100 sold = 17,000 RUB. Dish B: price 400, cost 180, contribution 220 RUB, 30 sold = 6,600 RUB. A earns more in total though B has a higher margin per plate.

Activity. Analyse a five-dish sales sample and decide actions.

Standard to meet. Contribution and ranking are correct with reasons.

Module 4 Food safety and hygiene 6 hEvaluate

Food safety controls are built around temperature, time, cleanliness and separation.

  • Keep cold food at 5 degrees C or below and hot holding at 63 degrees C or above; cook to a safe core temperature.
  • Separate raw and cooked foods and use colour-coded boards.
  • Wash hands, wear clean clothing and stay off work when ill.
  • Keep cleaning schedules, temperature logs and supplier documents; list allergens on the menu.
Worked example. A fridge reads 9 degrees C at 08:00 against a 5 degrees C limit. Stock worth 18,000 RUB is checked: items above the limit more than 4 hours are discarded; the fault is logged and repaired.

Activity. Run a hygiene audit with a 15-point checklist.

Standard to meet. Faults are found, graded and each has a corrective action.

Module 5 Purchasing and stock 4 hApply

Order from par levels, check deliveries and rotate stock to reduce waste.

  • Set par levels for each item from average use.
  • Check temperature, quantity and quality on delivery.
  • Store first in, first out with date labels.
  • Count stock weekly and compute actual food cost.
Worked example. Opening stock 40,000 RUB + purchases 120,000 RUB - closing stock 38,000 RUB = 122,000 RUB food used. Food sales 380,000 RUB, so food cost is 32.1 percent.

Activity. Compute weekly food cost from stock counts.

Standard to meet. Result is correct and variances are explained.

Module 6 Kitchen flow and service 4 hApply

A good flow cuts waits and mistakes: prep, cook, pass, serve, clear.

  • Set mise en place before service.
  • Write order tickets clearly with table, items and allergies.
  • Set target times: starter in 8 minutes, main in 18 minutes.
  • Brief staff before and review after service.
Worked example. A table of four orders mains at 19:10; target out by 19:28. A ticket at 19:35 is 7 minutes late; the cause is logged.

Activity. Draw a service flow and time targets.

Standard to meet. Flow has clear stages and time targets.

Module 7 People, law and money 5 hEvaluate

Staff, permits and cash control hold the business up.

  • Plan staffing from busy hours and labour cost percent.
  • Keep staff training and health records as required.
  • Reconcile till takes with the daily sales report.
  • Confirm permits, inspections and alcohol rules with the competent authority.
Worked example. Weekly sales 380,000 RUB; labour 114,000 RUB = 30 percent; food 122,000 RUB = 32 percent; so food plus labour is 62 percent, leaving 38 percent for rent, energy and other costs and margin.

Activity. Build a weekly profit-and-loss sketch.

Standard to meet. Percentages reconcile and gaps are explained.

Module 8 Opening plan and review 4 hCreate

The plan joins concept, menu, safety and cash and sets a review rhythm.

  • List opening costs, equipment and working cash.
  • Plan a soft opening with limited menu.
  • Set daily, weekly and monthly checks.
  • Prepare for inspection day.
Worked example. Setup cost 1,800,000 RUB and monthly fixed costs 260,000 RUB; with a contribution of 60 percent of sales, break-even sales are 260,000 / 0.60 = 433,333 RUB per month.

Activity. Present the opening plan.

Standard to meet. Plan is consistent, with a break-even and checks.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: concept sheet, costed recipes, menu analysis, hygiene checklist, staff rota, daily control sheet. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is food cost percent?

Model answer: Food cost divided by food sales, times 100.

Q2. What cold-holding temperature is expected?

Model answer: Five degrees C or below.

Q3. What is a par level?

Model answer: The target stock level for each item used to decide orders.

Q4. Why use colour-coded boards?

Model answer: To keep raw and cooked foods apart.

Q5. How is contribution margin found?

Model answer: Selling price minus food cost per portion.

Q6. What do you do when a fridge is above its limit?

Model answer: Check and discard unsafe stock, log the fault and repair it.

Q7. Why list allergens?

Model answer: To protect customers with allergies, as the rules require.

Q8. What is break-even sales?

Model answer: Fixed costs divided by contribution share of sales.

Q9. Who confirms permits for a restaurant?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

Q10. Why keep temperature logs?

Model answer: To prove safe handling and spot faults early.
