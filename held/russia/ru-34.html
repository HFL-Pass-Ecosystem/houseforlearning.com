HELD · RU-34

Rentals Businesses (Vehicles, Tools, Machines)

Learn how to plan and run a small rental business for vehicles, hand and power tools or light machines: fleet choice, pricing, contracts, inspections, maintenance, insurance and damage handling.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn how to plan and run a small rental business for vehicles, hand and power tools or light machines: fleet choice, pricing, contracts, inspections, maintenance, insurance and damage handling.

  • Understand Explain how a rental business earns income and where it loses it: idle time, damage, late returns and downtime.
  • Apply Complete a check-out and check-in inspection with photographs and a signed condition report.
  • Analyse Calculate utilisation, cost per rental day and the break-even number of rental days per item.
  • Evaluate Judge a customer request for suitability, risk and required licence or training before agreeing.
  • Create Design a rental offer with a price list, deposit rule, contract terms and maintenance plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design a complete rental setup for a fleet of 5 to 10 items, with a price list, contract, inspection form, maintenance plan and monthly performance sheet.

Condition

Open-book; 6 hours; templates, calculator and sample items on paper; no real customer data used.

Standard

Price list covers cost plus margin; utilisation and break-even calculated without error; inspection form has at least 12 check points; contract names 8 required terms; pass mark 80 percent.

Who it is for: People who plan to rent vehicles, tools or machines in a small or home-based business, and staff of existing rental counters. Requirements: Adult learner; basic arithmetic. For vehicle rental, a lawful place to park and a business registration are needed. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Vehicle and machine rental can be regulated: business registration, vehicle roadworthiness, insurance, driver licence category checks, safe use of lifting or cutting machines, and consumer rights all apply. Some machines need trained operators. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser, and have a lawyer check the contract, before renting to anyone. Never rent out equipment you know to be unsafe.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How rental earns and loses money 4 hUnderstand

A rental item is an asset that earns only when it is out. The business is about keeping items safe, available and well used.

  • Income per item = rental days x daily rate. Costs are fixed (purchase, insurance, storage, registration) and variable (cleaning, fuel, wear, repair).
  • Utilisation = days rented divided by days available. Low utilisation means high cost per rental day.
  • Typical losses: idle stock, late returns, damage without evidence, unpaid deposits, and downtime for repairs.
  • Match fleet to demand: a small number of popular items beats a large number of rarely used ones.
Worked example. A power drill costs 9,000 RUB, expected life 3 years of 365 days. Daily ownership cost is about 8.2 RUB (9,000 / 1,095). If it is rented 60 days a year at 300 RUB, income is 18,000 RUB a year, utilisation 16 percent.

Activity. Choose five items and make a table of purchase cost, life, daily ownership cost and expected utilisation.

Standard to meet. Daily cost computed for all five; utilisation figures labelled as estimates.

Module 2 Fleet choice and cost per day 5 hAnalyse

Choose items your customers need and that you can maintain. Work out the true cost per rental day before pricing.

  • Survey local demand: ask 20 people which items they have hired or wished to hire, and for how long.
  • Prefer common models with spare parts available and simple servicing.
  • Cost per rental day = (yearly fixed + yearly variable cost) divided by days rented per year.
  • Add 10 to 15 percent for unplanned repair.
Worked example. Mini excavator: yearly fixed 120,000 RUB (depreciation, insurance, storage), variable 40,000 RUB, repair reserve 15 percent of 160,000 = 24,000 RUB. Total 184,000 RUB. At 80 rental days: 2,300 RUB per day cost.

Activity. Calculate cost per rental day for three items at 3 utilisation levels and choose the best two.

Standard to meet. Calculations correct to the ruble; repair reserve included; choice explained.

Module 3 Pricing, deposits and add-ons 4 hApply

A price list should cover cost, give a fair margin and make the terms clear. Deposits protect against loss.

  • Price tiers: hourly, daily, weekend, weekly. Longer rentals have lower daily rates.
  • Price = cost per rental day + margin, then check against what local customers accept.
  • Deposit equals a part of replacement value or a fixed sum, returned after inspection. State when and how it is refunded.
  • Add-ons: delivery, cleaning, fuel, insurance and accessories, each priced separately and shown at the start.
Worked example. Excavator cost 2,300 RUB per day; margin 30 percent gives 2,990 RUB, rounded to 3,000 RUB per day. Week rate 5 days charged for 7: 15,000 RUB. Deposit 20,000 RUB.

Activity. Build a tiered price list for two items with deposit and add-ons.

Standard to meet. Each tier covers cost; deposit and refund rule stated; no hidden fees.

Module 4 Contracts and customer checks 5 hEvaluate

A good rental contract is plain, fair and complete. A good customer check prevents most problems.

  • Required terms: parties, item, serial number, period, price, deposit, condition, use limits, return rules, damage and loss, insurance, late fee and contacts.
  • Check identity and, for vehicles, the right driver licence category and experience.
  • Refuse or ask for training when the customer cannot use the item safely.
  • Have a lawyer check the contract under the relevant federal rules. Keep a signed copy.
Worked example. Customer wants a petrol cutter saw for a roof job on a ladder. Judge: high risk, no guard or training; ask for operator training, protective equipment and a safe set-up, or decline.

Activity. Write a one-page contract for one item with the 13 terms. Judge three customer requests.

Standard to meet. All 13 terms present; three judgements justified with risk.

Module 5 Inspection, handover and returns 5 hApply

Evidence at the start and end of a rental settles disputes fast. Inspect every time.

  • Check-out: clean item, working, fuel or charge level, accessories, safety guards; photograph all sides with date and time.
  • Show the customer how to use the item and give a one-page safety sheet. Record that you did so.
  • Check-in: compare with the check-out photographs, test, and note hours or distance used.
  • Settle on the day: refund deposit minus agreed charges, with a written reason for each charge.
Worked example. Return of a van: 14:10 on the due date 12:00 = 2 hours 10 minutes late. Late fee rule: 150 RUB per started hour = 3 x 150 = 450 RUB. Fuel at 3/4 instead of full: 12 litres at 60 RUB = 720 RUB. Deposit 5,000 RUB - 1,170 RUB = 3,830 RUB refund.

Activity. Complete a 12-point inspection form for check-out and check-in on a sample item and settle a return.

Standard to meet. Photograph list complete; charges tie to the contract; refund correct.

Module 6 Maintenance and downtime 4 hApply

A broken item earns nothing and can hurt a customer. Planned maintenance is cheaper than failure.

  • Make a schedule for each item: after every rental (clean, quick check), monthly, and by hours or kilometres as the maker advises.
  • Keep a log: date, work done, parts, cost, who did it, and the next due date.
  • Take any item with a safety fault out of service and tag it.
  • Keep spare consumables such as blades, filters and oil to reduce downtime.
Worked example. Generator serviced every 100 hours. Rented 8 hours a day for 5 days = 40 hours. After 3 rentals (120 hours) it is over by 20 hours; service due. Downtime 1 day costs 1 x 1,200 RUB rate.

Activity. Create a service schedule and log for three items and mark which are overdue in a sample log.

Standard to meet. All service points set; overdue items found; fault tagging rule written.

Module 7 Insurance, damage and disputes 4 hEvaluate

Know who pays when something goes wrong, and handle disputes with evidence and fairness.

  • Check insurance for the item, third-party injury, theft and your business. Read the exclusions.
  • Separate fair wear from damage. Price repairs by quotes, not guesses, and send photos.
  • Offer a calm, written process for disputes with a response time of 3 working days.
  • Learn consumer rights that apply to services and keep within them.
Worked example. Dent on a hired trailer: quote 6,500 RUB; deposit 8,000 RUB; contract has a 1,000 RUB excess. Charge 6,500 RUB, refund 1,500 RUB, attach quote and photos.

Activity. Judge four damage cases as wear, damage, or fault of the owner, and compute charges.

Standard to meet. At least 3 of 4 correct; every charge matches a contract term.

Module 8 Design the rental business plan 4 hCreate

Bring it together in one pack and a monthly sheet that shows if the business is healthy.

  • Set monthly measures: utilisation, income per item, cost per rental day, damage cost, late rate and complaints.
  • Review the worst three items and decide: reprice, promote or sell.
  • Plan a growth step only when utilisation of existing stock is above your target.
  • Write a risk list with actions: theft, accident, price war, seasonality.
Worked example. Month: 10 items, 300 item-days available, 120 rented = 40 percent utilisation. Income 96,000 RUB, costs 70,000 RUB. Operating result 26,000 RUB. Target 50 percent.

Activity. Assemble the pack and a mock month sheet for your fleet.

Standard to meet. All parts present; calculations correct; one improvement action per weak item.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: fleet and cost sheet, price list, contract with required terms, inspection form with sample entries, maintenance schedule and a monthly performance sheet. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Define utilisation.

Model answer: Days rented divided by days available, as a percent.

Q2. A tool costs 9,000 RUB with a 3-year life. What is the daily ownership cost?

Model answer: About 8.2 RUB per day (9,000 / 1,095).

Q3. Why include a repair reserve?

Model answer: Because unplanned repair is common; 10 to 15 percent protects margin.

Q4. Name six required contract terms.

Model answer: Parties, item and serial number, period, price, deposit, return rules, damage and loss, late fee.

Q5. When should you decline a rental?

Model answer: When the customer cannot use the item safely or lacks the needed licence or training.

Q6. How does a deposit refund work?

Model answer: Refund after inspection minus agreed charges, each with a written reason.

Q7. What evidence do you collect at hand-over?

Model answer: Dated photographs, a condition report and a note that safety instructions were given.

Q8. What do you do with an item that has a safety fault?

Model answer: Take it out of service, tag it and repair before renting again.

Q9. A van is 2 h 10 min late with a rule of 150 RUB per started hour. What is the fee?

Model answer: 3 started hours x 150 = 450 RUB.

Q10. Why review the worst three items monthly?

Model answer: To reprice, promote or sell items that drag down returns.
