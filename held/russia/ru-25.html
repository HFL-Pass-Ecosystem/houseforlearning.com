HELD · RU-25

Construction

Learn how a small building project moves from brief to handover: quantities, cost build-up, programme, site safety, quality checks and records. You practise on a small house extension and a site office.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner31 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn how a small building project moves from brief to handover: quantities, cost build-up, programme, site safety, quality checks and records. You practise on a small house extension and a site office.

  • Understand Explain the stages of a building project from brief and design to permits, construction, inspection and handover.
  • Apply Calculate concrete, brick and reinforcement quantities for a simple structure, with a waste allowance.
  • Analyse Break a project cost into labour, materials, plant, overhead and contingency and find the largest cost risks.
  • Evaluate Judge a site against safety and quality checklists and decide which hazards must stop work.
  • Create Build a programme, a cost plan and a handover file for a small building job.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a quantity list, cost plan, 10-week programme, site safety plan and inspection log for a 6 m by 5 m single-storey extension.

Condition

Given a simple drawing, a local material price list in RUB, a spreadsheet, and 6 hours of working time, with a mentor available for questions.

Standard

Quantities within 5 percent of the mentor check, costs add up with no arithmetic error, every major hazard has a control, and at least 8 of 10 check answers are right.

Who it is for: Beginners, site assistants, small contractors and owners who want to manage a small build with understanding. Requirements: Basic arithmetic, a spreadsheet, and the ability to read a simple plan. No licence is needed to study. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Construction is regulated. Design, permits, builder licensing or membership of a self-regulating organisation, structural calculations, fire rules and site safety are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. This course teaches understanding only; it does not replace a licensed designer, engineer or safety officer. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before starting any real work. Work at height, excavation and electricity can kill: never rely on this course for safety decisions.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How a building project runs 3 hUnderstand

A project passes through clear stages, and each stage ends with a decision and a record. Missing a stage is the usual cause of delay and dispute.

  • Name the stages: brief, design, permit and approvals, tender or pricing, construction, inspection, handover.
  • Explain who does what: owner, designer, main contractor, subcontractors, supervisor and the inspecting authority.
  • Show why records matter: a hidden item such as buried reinforcement must be inspected before it is covered.
  • Distinguish a contract type: fixed price carries price risk for the contractor, cost-plus carries it for the owner.
Worked example. A 30 m2 extension: design 3 weeks, approvals 4 weeks, construction 10 weeks, handover 1 week. Total 18 weeks. If approvals slip by 2 weeks, handover moves from week 18 to week 20 unless work overlaps.

Activity. Draw the stage chain for the extension and mark where an inspection must occur before work is covered.

Standard to meet. All seven stages in order, with at least three hold points and one named record per stage.

Module 2 Reading drawings and measuring 4 hApply

Quantities come from drawings. Scale, units and levels must be read correctly or every later number is wrong.

  • Convert scale: at 1:50, 1 cm on paper is 0.5 m on site.
  • Read plans, sections and details, and list the dimensions you need: length, width, height, thickness.
  • Measure in metric units and keep one unit per line in the take-off sheet.
  • Check a drawing for conflicts, such as a door in a wall that is drawn as solid.
Worked example. A wall shown 12 cm long on a 1:50 plan is 6.0 m. At a height of 2.7 m and thickness 0.38 m the wall volume is 6.0 x 2.7 x 0.38 = 6.16 m3 before deducting a 0.9 m by 2.1 m door (0.72 m3).

Activity. Measure the wall volumes of the extension from a given plan and deduct two openings.

Standard to meet. Volumes within 5 percent of the mentor figure and every deduction listed.

Module 3 Quantities for concrete, masonry and steel 5 hApply

Bills of quantities turn geometry into orders. Waste and overbreak must be added honestly.

  • Concrete volume = length x width x depth; add 5 percent for spillage and uneven ground.
  • Block or brick count = wall area x units per m2, minus openings, plus 5 percent breakage.
  • Reinforcement: total bar length x mass per metre; add 10 percent for laps and cuts.
  • Order to the supplier unit, for example whole cubic metres or full pallets, and say how you rounded.
Worked example. A strip foundation 22 m long, 0.5 m wide, 0.4 m deep: 22 x 0.5 x 0.4 = 4.4 m3; plus 5 percent = 4.62 m3, order 4.7 m3. Reinforcement: 4 bars x 22 m = 88 m; at 0.89 kg/m = 78.3 kg; plus 10 percent = 86.2 kg.

Activity. Compute foundation, wall and slab quantities for the extension and produce an order list.

Standard to meet. Every line shows formula, allowance and rounding; total concrete within 5 percent of the check.

Module 4 Cost build-up and pricing 4 hAnalyse

A price is built, not guessed. Each rate has labour, material, plant and a share of overhead and risk.

  • Build a unit rate: material cost + labour hours x hourly rate + plant + waste.
  • Add site overhead (for example 8 percent), head-office overhead and contingency (for example 5 to 10 percent).
  • Separate fixed costs from quantity-driven costs so a change in scope can be priced.
  • Compare two quotes by line, not by total, to find what was left out.
Worked example. Block wall rate per m2: blocks 1.0 m2 at 520 RUB, mortar 85 RUB, labour 0.9 h x 450 RUB = 405 RUB. Subtotal 1010 RUB per m2. With 8 percent overhead and 5 percent contingency: 1010 x 1.08 x 1.05 = 1145 RUB per m2.

Activity. Price 40 m2 of block wall with a rate sheet and compare it with a second quote.

Standard to meet. Rate shows each component; total correct; at least two omissions found in the second quote.

Module 5 Programme and resources 4 hCreate

A programme sequences work and shows which delays move the finish date. The critical path is the longest chain of dependent tasks.

  • List tasks, durations and dependencies, for example walls cannot start before the foundation has cured.
  • Find the critical path by adding durations along each chain.
  • Level resources so a crew is not planned on two places at once.
  • Add float and weather allowance, and update the plan weekly.
Worked example. Tasks: excavate 3 days, foundation 4, cure 7, walls 12, roof 8, finishes 15. Chain total = 49 working days, about 10 weeks. A 3-day delay to the walls moves the finish by 3 days; a 3-day delay to a task with 5 days float does not.

Activity. Build a 10-week programme and mark the critical path.

Standard to meet. Dependencies are correct, the critical path total is right, and a weekly update note exists.

Module 6 Site safety and hazard control 4 hEvaluate

Construction has high rates of falls, collapses, electric shock and struck-by accidents. Controls are chosen from the most effective type downward.

  • Order of control: remove the hazard, replace it, guard it, organise work, then protective equipment as the last layer.
  • Work at height: edge protection, secured ladders, trained workers, no work in high wind or ice.
  • Excavation: shoring or safe slope, spoil kept back from the edge, a safe way out for people.
  • Daily checks: access, lifting gear, electrical leads, guards, first-aid kit and emergency route.
Worked example. A trench 1.8 m deep with 1.0 m of spoil placed at the edge is unsafe. Controls: shore the sides, move spoil at least 1 m back, ladder every 7 m of trench length, daily inspection signed by the supervisor.

Activity. Walk a photo or site case, list ten hazards and choose a control for each.

Standard to meet. All high-risk hazards have a control higher than protective equipment, and stop-work triggers are named.

Module 7 Quality, inspection and compliance 4 hEvaluate

Quality is checked against the drawing and the rules at set points. Compliance is proved by records, not memory.

  • Check tolerances: wall plumb within 10 mm over 3 m, floor level within 5 mm over 2 m, as the project specification states.
  • Test concrete: slump test on delivery and cube samples as the specification requires.
  • Keep hold-point records, delivery notes, test results and photographs with dates.
  • Know that permits, approvals and inspections are required by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority; ask a licensed adviser.
Worked example. A 3 m straightedge on a floor slab shows a 9 mm gap against a 5 mm tolerance: the item fails, is recorded, repaired and rechecked, and the recheck is signed and dated.

Activity. Complete an inspection log for three hold points and mark one failure with a corrective action.

Standard to meet. Each entry has date, item, result, inspector and action; failures are not hidden.

Module 8 Handover, cost control and records 3 hCreate

A job ends cleanly with a defects list, final account and a file the owner can keep.

  • Track budget against actual weekly and explain variances.
  • Control changes: price each variation and get written agreement before work.
  • Prepare the handover file: as-built drawings, test results, warranties, maintenance notes.
  • Hold back a retention amount until defects are cleared.
Worked example. Contract 1,200,000 RUB; variations +85,000 RUB; retention 5 percent of 1,285,000 = 64,250 RUB held until defects are fixed. Final payment on completion = 1,285,000 - 64,250 = 1,220,750 RUB less earlier payments.

Activity. Prepare a final account and handover file index for the extension.

Standard to meet. Arithmetic is right, every variation has a written record, and the file lists at least eight document types.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: quantity list, cost plan, programme chart, site safety plan, inspection log and handover file. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why is a hold point used before covering reinforcement?

Model answer: Because it cannot be inspected once covered; an inspector must check and sign first.

Q2. At scale 1:50, what real length is 8 cm on paper?

Model answer: 4.0 m.

Q3. A foundation needs 6.3 m3 of concrete. Give an order with 5 percent allowance.

Model answer: 6.3 x 1.05 = 6.615, order about 6.7 m3.

Q4. Name the order of hazard controls.

Model answer: Remove, replace, guard, organise work, then protective equipment.

Q5. What is the critical path?

Model answer: The longest chain of dependent tasks; any delay on it delays the finish.

Q6. Why compare quotes by line?

Model answer: To find omitted items and unequal assumptions that a total hides.

Q7. Which contract type puts price risk on the contractor?

Model answer: Fixed price.

Q8. A trench is 1.8 m deep. Give two controls.

Model answer: Shoring or safe slope, and spoil kept back from the edge with a safe exit.

Q9. Who should approve design and structural calculations?

Model answer: A licensed designer or engineer as required by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority.

Q10. Why price variations before work?

Model answer: To avoid disputes and to keep the budget under control.
