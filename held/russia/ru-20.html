Metals and Commodities

Learn how metals and other commodities are graded, priced, stored, traded and moved, and how to check quality and risk.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner32 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn how metals and other commodities are graded, priced, stored, traded and moved, and how to check quality and risk.

  • Understand Explain grades, assays, weights and delivery terms for common metals.
  • Apply Calculate the value of a lot from weight, purity and price.
  • Analyse Compare two supply offers on price, quality, delivery and risk.
  • Evaluate Judge a document set for gaps and mismatches.
  • Create Build a buy-store-sell plan with a margin and risk check.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Value, compare and document a physical commodity lot from purchase to delivery.

Condition

In 6 weeks with sample certificates, price tables, a calculator and a spreadsheet.

Standard

Valuation within 0.5 percent of model; document check finds all planted errors; margin plan sums correctly; pass mark 8 of 10.

Who it is for: Trainees in trading, procurement, recycling and logistics. Requirements: Arithmetic and spreadsheet basics. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Precious metals, scrap, strategic materials and some commodities are regulated: licences, registration, reporting, customs and origin rules apply, and handling can be hazardous (heavy loads, dust, radioactive or toxic scrap). Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before buying, holding, shipping or exporting. This course teaches analysis, not trading advice, and no price outcome is promised.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Commodity basics 4 hUnderstand

A commodity is a standard good that is bought by grade, not by brand.

  • Define ferrous, non-ferrous, precious and soft commodities.
  • Explain grade, purity and tolerance.
  • List the quoted units: tonne, kg, troy ounce (31.1035 g).
  • Distinguish spot and forward deals.
  • Know typical uses to see demand.
Worked example. A 2.5 kg bar of 99.9 percent purity holds 2.4975 kg of pure metal, or about 80.3 troy ounces (2,497.5 / 31.1035).

Activity. Convert five weights between kg, g and troy ounces.

Standard to meet. All conversions correct to 3 digits.

Module 2 Quality and testing 4 hUnderstand

Quality is checked by sampling and test, not by trust.

  • Explain sampling: representative, sealed, labelled.
  • Describe assay and spectrometer checks.
  • Read a certificate: lot, weight, grade, date, lab.
  • Know moisture and impurity deductions.
  • Keep chain of custody.
Worked example. A 20 t lot of scrap with 4 percent moisture and 2 percent dirt pays on 20 x 0.94 = 18.8 t.

Activity. Apply deductions to three sample lots.

Standard to meet. Net weights correct.

Module 3 Pricing and valuation 5 hApply

Value is weight x purity x price, adjusted for costs and terms.

  • Use a reference price and a premium or discount.
  • Convert currency only where the deal needs it; here values are in RUB.
  • Add freight, insurance and handling.
  • Compute the delivered cost per tonne.
  • Check against another source.
Worked example. 18.8 t at 210,000 RUB per tonne = 3,948,000 RUB; freight at 7,200 RUB per tonne = 18.8 x 7,200 = 135,360 RUB. Delivered: 4,083,360 RUB.

Activity. Value two lots to delivered cost.

Standard to meet. Within 0.5 percent of the model.

Module 4 Storage and handling 3 hApply

Metal and bulk goods lose value through loss, damage and mix-up.

  • Segregate by grade and lot.
  • Weigh in and out on a calibrated scale.
  • Control moisture, rust and theft.
  • Use lifting gear within rated load and trained operators.
  • Count stock regularly.
Worked example. A scale reads 20,040 kg in and 19,980 kg out of a pile with no sales: loss 60 kg or 0.3 percent, within a 0.5 percent tolerance.

Activity. Fill a stock sheet for a week.

Standard to meet. Opening plus in less out equals closing.

Module 5 Contracts and delivery terms 4 hAnalyse

Terms decide who bears cost and risk at each step.

  • Read price basis, quantity tolerance, payment and delivery point.
  • Compare ex-works and delivered terms.
  • Check inspection and claims windows.
  • Spot penalties and force majeure.
  • Use standard clauses; take advice on unusual ones.
Worked example. Offer A: 205,000 RUB/t ex-works plus 7,200 RUB freight = 212,200. Offer B: 211,000 delivered. B is cheaper by 1,200 RUB per tonne.

Activity. Compare two offers in a table.

Standard to meet. Total delivered cost for both; one risk noted each.

Module 6 Documents and compliance 5 hEvaluate

A lot is only as good as its papers. Mismatches stop shipments.

  • Check invoice, packing list, certificate and transport papers agree.
  • Verify origin and any required licence or permit.
  • Confirm seller identity and authority.
  • Record anti-money-laundering checks as the rules require.
  • Keep records for the required period.
Worked example. Invoice says 19.5 t, packing list 19.8 t, weighbridge 19.5 t. Flag the packing list for correction before payment.

Activity. Audit a set of five documents with planted errors.

Standard to meet. All errors found and listed.

Module 7 Risk and margin 4 hEvaluate

Price moves, currency, credit and quality risks eat margin.

  • Compute gross margin and per-tonne margin.
  • Stress price by plus and minus 5 percent.
  • Limit exposure per counterparty.
  • Use fixed price or matched buying and selling where possible.
  • Note storage days and finance cost.
Worked example. Buy 4,083,360 RUB, sell 4,300,000 RUB: margin 216,640 RUB or 5.0 percent of cost. A 5 percent fall in sell price gives 4,085,000, margin 1,640 RUB.

Activity. Stress-test a deal at plus and minus 5 percent.

Standard to meet. Three cases computed; decision stated.

Module 8 Plan and review 3 hCreate

A written plan links buying, storing, selling and checks.

  • Set volume, timing and limits.
  • List checks before each step.
  • Define who signs off.
  • Review results against plan.
  • Keep lessons learned.
Worked example. Plan: buy 40 t in two lots of 20 t, store up to 10 days, sell on a fixed price; stop if quality tests fail on either lot.

Activity. Write a one-page plan.

Standard to meet. Plan has volumes, checks, sign-offs and stop rules.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: valuation sheet, offer comparison, document check log and a buy-store-sell plan. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. How many grams in a troy ounce?

Model answer: 31.1035 g.

Q2. Net weight: 20 t, 4 percent moisture, 2 percent dirt.

Model answer: 18.8 t.

Q3. Why seal samples?

Model answer: To prove they were not changed.

Q4. What is a spot deal?

Model answer: Buying for near-immediate delivery at today's price.

Q5. Which documents must agree?

Model answer: Invoice, packing list, certificate and transport papers.

Q6. Why weigh on a calibrated scale?

Model answer: To trust weights and settle disputes.

Q7. Compute: 18.8 t x 210,000 RUB.

Model answer: 3,948,000 RUB.

Q8. Name two risks to margin.

Model answer: Price move and quality failure.

Q9. May precious metals be traded freely?

Model answer: No; licences and rules apply.

Q10. Who to check rules with?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a local adviser.
