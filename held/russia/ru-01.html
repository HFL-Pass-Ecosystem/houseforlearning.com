Fishing and Seafood Business

Plan and run a small, lawful fishing-product or seafood trading business: from catch or purchase records and cold-chain control to costing, customers and compliance. Built for people who handle fish and seafood, not for people who want to fish without permission.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner36 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Plan and run a small, lawful fishing-product or seafood trading business: from catch or purchase records and cold-chain control to costing, customers and compliance. Built for people who handle fish and seafood, not for people who want to fish without permission.

  • Understand Explain the main steps from catch or purchase to customer and where the permits, records and inspections sit in that chain.
  • Apply Calculate yield, landed cost per kilogram and gross margin for a batch of fish or seafood.
  • Analyse Compare cold-chain options and storage times for fresh, chilled, frozen and salted product and find the weak points.
  • Evaluate Judge a supplier or a sales channel against price, quality, paperwork and risk.
  • Create Build a one-year plan with a batch costing sheet, a cold-chain log, a compliance checklist and a cash plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a costed, documented plan for buying, storing and selling one fish or seafood product line, with a cold-chain log and a compliance checklist.

Condition

Individual work over 6 weeks, using a spreadsheet, a calculator, a probe thermometer log template and the course templates; sample prices are supplied or taken from local suppliers.

Standard

Batch costing correct to within 2 percent; cold-chain log complete with times and temperatures for 7 consecutive days; checklist covers every permit and record the learner confirmed with the competent authority; 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Small traders, cooperative members, processors, shop and market stall owners, and people planning to enter the seafood trade. Requirements: Basic arithmetic and a spreadsheet. No prior sector experience is needed. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Fishing, aquaculture and trade in fish products are regulated: catch rights and quotas, vessel and crew rules, veterinary and sanitary control, labelling and traceability all apply. This course does not give legal advice and does not tell anyone how to fish, land or sell without the required permissions. Before any real activity, check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser which permits, quotas and inspections apply to you. Work at sea and on ice is dangerous; follow qualified safety training. Margins vary and no profit is promised.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The seafood value chain 4 hUnderstand

Fish and seafood move from catch or farm to landing, primary handling, storage, processing, transport and sale. Each step changes value, risk and paperwork.

  • Name the steps: catch or harvest, landing, sorting, chilling, processing, packing, storage, transport, sale.
  • Separate the roles: fisher, farmer, processor, wholesaler, retailer, caterer. A small firm often plays two roles.
  • Mark where a record is kept at each step: catch or purchase document, batch number, temperature, destination.
  • Explain why product that cannot be traced to a source is a risk for every buyer.
Worked example. A trader buys 500 kg of whole chilled fish at 180 RUB/kg and sells it as fillets. Draw the chain with five steps and list one record needed at each.

Activity. Draw the chain for one product you know and mark records and inspection points.

Standard to meet. Chain has at least 6 steps and a record named at each; roles are separated correctly.

Module 2 Species, quality and freshness 4 hUnderstand

Quality is judged by simple sensory and temperature checks before any laboratory test. A buyer who can grade product protects both margin and customers.

  • Check fresh fish by eye, gill colour, smell, flesh firmness and clear eyes; record the result as pass or reject.
  • Know that chilled fish is held near 0 to 2 degrees C and frozen product at -18 degrees C or colder.
  • Describe how shellfish and some species carry higher risk and need extra handling rules.
  • Use a grade sheet with 5 criteria scored 0 to 2 and a clear reject score.
Worked example. A 20 kg box scores 9 of 10 on the grade sheet and passes at 8. Another box scores 6 and is rejected; at 180 RUB/kg the avoided loss on a 20 kg box is 3,600 RUB.

Activity. Grade 5 samples (real or photos) with the sheet and justify each reject.

Standard to meet. Grades agree with a mentor on at least 4 of 5 samples.

Module 3 Yield, waste and landed cost 5 hApply

Selling price must cover the true cost of usable meat, not of the whole fish. Yield and waste decide margin.

  • Yield percent = usable weight divided by purchased weight times 100.
  • Landed cost = purchase price plus transport, ice, packing and handling, divided by usable kilograms.
  • Track waste and by-products separately; some by-products can be sold or must be disposed of by rule.
  • Add overhead per kilogram: storage energy, labour, cleaning, losses.
Worked example. Buy 400 kg whole fish at 160 RUB/kg = 64,000 RUB. Transport and ice 4,000 RUB. Fillet yield 45 percent = 180 kg. Landed cost = 68,000 / 180 = 377.8 RUB/kg. At a sale price of 520 RUB/kg gross margin is 142.2 RUB/kg (27.3 percent).

Activity. Build a costing sheet for two products and compare their margins per kilogram.

Standard to meet. Both sheets are formula-driven and arithmetic is within 2 percent of the mentor key.

Module 4 Cold chain and storage 5 hAnalyse

Temperature control is the central safety and quality tool. Time above the limit is cumulative.

  • Log temperature at receiving, in storage, in transport and at handover, with time and initials.
  • Size ice or refrigeration: product weight, start temperature, journey time and outside heat.
  • Plan storage rotation: first in, first out, with date labels on every box.
  • Define corrective actions: product quarantined, supplier told, record kept.
Worked example. A chilled load of 300 kg travels 6 hours. The limit is 4 degrees C at delivery; the probe reads 7 degrees C. The batch is held and the carrier asked for the transport log; the 300 kg at 250 RUB/kg (75,000 RUB) is at risk and is not sold until cleared.

Activity. Keep a 7-day cold-chain log for a real or simulated store and mark two deviations with actions.

Standard to meet. Log complete for 7 days, deviations named, actions written and dated.

Module 5 Preservation and processing basics 4 hUnderstand

Salting, smoking, freezing and canning change shelf life and risk. Each needs its own control points.

  • Compare freezing, salting, drying, smoking and canning for time, cost and risk.
  • Explain why hot and cold smoking, and brining strength, need controlled and recorded values.
  • Keep batch records so a problem can be traced and recalled.
  • Note that processing premises and some processes need approval; ask the competent authority first.
Worked example. Salting 100 kg of fish with 8 kg of salt uses 8 percent salt by weight; at 25 RUB/kg salt costs 200 RUB, small against the fish value, so control is about safety and not cost.

Activity. Write a one-page batch record for one processed product.

Standard to meet. Record has batch number, dates, weights, temperatures and destination.

Module 6 Markets, customers and pricing 5 hAnalyse

Seafood is sold to shops, restaurants, markets, institutions and households. Each channel pays differently and pays at a different speed.

  • Map 4 channels with price, volume, payment days and paperwork.
  • Set price from cost plus target margin, then test against the local market.
  • Plan around seasons and weather; hold a cash reserve for slow weeks.
  • Judge a customer on payment record, not only on order size.
Worked example. A restaurant takes 40 kg/week at 520 RUB/kg paid in 14 days; a market stall takes 25 kg/week at 560 RUB/kg paid daily. Weekly sales are 20,800 and 14,000 RUB; the stall has better cash timing despite lower volume.

Activity. Compare two channels in a table including payment days and risk.

Standard to meet. Table covers price, volume, payment days and a stated choice.

Module 7 Compliance, permits and safety 5 hEvaluate

The rules around fishing and seafood are strict and enforced. A lawful business starts with knowing which rules apply before the first purchase.

  • List the kinds of permission to check: catch rights and quotas, vessel and crew rules, premises and veterinary approval, product labelling and traceability, tax and registration.
  • Keep supporting documents for every batch and keep them available for inspection.
  • Never buy product without a clear source document.
  • Identify safety hazards: cold, wet decks, ice, machinery, chemicals; use protective gear and training.
Worked example. A seller offers 1,000 kg at 100 RUB/kg, well below the 180 RUB/kg market price, with no source papers. Savings on paper are 80,000 RUB but the risk is seizure and loss of the whole stock, so the offer is declined.

Activity. Build a compliance checklist for one product and confirm each line with a named authority or adviser.

Standard to meet. Checklist has every permit and record type; each line has an owner and a confirmed answer or an open question.

Module 8 Plan, cash and review 4 hCreate

A plan joins costing, cold chain, sales and compliance into one view and one cash flow.

  • Build a 12-month cash plan with monthly sales, costs and a reserve.
  • Set 5 simple indicators: yield, margin per kg, rejects, days to pay and stock days.
  • Plan a 90-day review and list the decisions it can change.
  • State the main risks and a response for each.
Worked example. Monthly sales 300 kg x 520 = 156,000 RUB; cost of goods 300 x 378 = 113,400 RUB; overhead 20,000 RUB; surplus 22,600 RUB, which is the reserve builder and not guaranteed income.

Activity. Assemble and present the one-year plan.

Standard to meet. Plan ties costing, log, checklist and cash; a mentor finds no arithmetic error above 2 percent.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is yield percent?

Model answer: Usable weight divided by purchased weight, times 100.

Q2. What is landed cost?

Model answer: The full cost to bring usable product to the point of sale, divided by usable kilograms.

Q3. At what temperature is frozen fish normally stored?

Model answer: At -18 degrees C or colder.

Q4. Why is a batch number used?

Model answer: So product can be traced to its source and recalled if a problem is found.

Q5. A load arrives above the temperature limit. What do you do?

Model answer: Quarantine it, record the reading, tell the supplier and carrier, and do not sell it until cleared.

Q6. Why refuse stock with no source papers even at a low price?

Model answer: It may be unlawful, can be seized, and the loss can exceed the saving.

Q7. What does first in, first out mean?

Model answer: The oldest stock is sold first, using date labels.

Q8. Name two sensory freshness checks.

Model answer: Clear eyes and a fresh smell (also gill colour and firm flesh).

Q9. Who decides which permits apply to you?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority; check with it and a local adviser.

Q10. Why does a cash reserve matter in this trade?

Model answer: Catch, weather and demand vary, so slow weeks must be covered.
