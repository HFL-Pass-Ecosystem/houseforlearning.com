Mining

Learn how a small mining operation is planned and controlled: resource and grade, drilling and blasting basics, haulage, ventilation, safety management and records. The course teaches planning arithmetic and compliance thinking, not operation.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner29 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn how a small mining operation is planned and controlled: resource and grade, drilling and blasting basics, haulage, ventilation, safety management and records. The course teaches planning arithmetic and compliance thinking, not operation.

  • Understand Explain the mine life cycle from exploration to closure and the role of licences and inspection.
  • Apply Calculate tonnes, grade and metal content for a simple orebody block.
  • Analyse Break unit cost per tonne into drilling, blasting, loading, haulage and processing.
  • Evaluate Judge a hazard register for ground, ventilation, explosives and machinery risks.
  • Create Build a short-interval plan, a haulage cycle estimate and a safety and compliance file.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a block tonnage and grade estimate, a haulage cycle calculation, a hazard register and a compliance checklist for a small open-pit quarry.

Condition

Given a block model extract, equipment data, a spreadsheet and 6 hours of working time, with a mentor to review.

Standard

Tonnage and metal content within 3 percent of the key, cycle time arithmetic correct, every high hazard has a control, and at least 8 of 10 check answers are right.

Who it is for: Learners entering mining support, quarry technicians, planners and owners who want to understand the work. Requirements: Basic arithmetic, a spreadsheet and the ability to read a table. No mining experience is needed to study. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Mining is among the most regulated and dangerous sectors. Subsurface use rights, licences, environmental approval, explosives handling, mine rescue and inspection are controlled by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. This course does not authorise any person to work underground, handle explosives or run a mine. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before any real activity. Use only trained and certified persons for blasting, ventilation and ground control. Nothing here predicts a profit.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Mine life cycle and licensing 3 hUnderstand

A mine passes through exploration, assessment, development, production and closure, and each stage needs permission and records.

  • Name stages: exploration, resource estimate, feasibility, development, production, closure and rehabilitation.
  • Explain why subsurface use rights and approvals come before any works.
  • Distinguish a resource (geological estimate) from a reserve (economically and legally mineable part).
  • Explain why closure cost is planned from the start.
Worked example. A deposit has 2,000,000 t in the resource; after dilution and legal limits only 1,500,000 t are reserve, which is 75 percent. Planning on the larger number would overstate output by 33 percent.

Activity. Draw the life cycle and mark the permit or approval needed at each stage.

Standard to meet. All stages in order with at least four named approvals or records.

Module 2 Geology, sampling and grade 4 hApply

Grade is the share of valuable material in rock. A sample must represent the whole, or the estimate is wrong.

  • Define grade as grams per tonne or percent, and state which unit is used.
  • Take samples at set spacing and keep a chain of custody for each bag.
  • Compute a weighted average grade from lengths or tonnes.
  • Explain nugget effect and why a few samples can mislead.
Worked example. Three drill intervals: 2 m at 4.0 g/t, 3 m at 1.0 g/t, 1 m at 7.0 g/t. Weighted grade = (8 + 3 + 7) / 6 = 3.0 g/t.

Activity. Calculate weighted grade for a drill hole table of six intervals.

Standard to meet. Correct weighted average and units, with the sample record complete.

Module 3 Block tonnage and metal content 4 hApply

Tonnes are volume times density. Metal content is tonnes times grade, reduced by recovery.

  • Tonnes = volume (m3) x density (t/m3).
  • Metal (g) = tonnes x grade (g/t); recovered metal = metal x recovery percent.
  • Apply dilution and mining loss as percentages and keep them visible.
  • Reconcile plan against production each week.
Worked example. Block 50 m x 30 m x 10 m = 15,000 m3 at density 2.7 t/m3 = 40,500 t. At 3.0 g/t, metal = 121,500 g. With mining loss 5 percent and recovery 90 percent: 121,500 x 0.95 x 0.90 = 103,883 g.

Activity. Compute tonnes and recovered metal for three blocks and compare plan with actual.

Standard to meet. Within 3 percent of key; every factor shown.

Module 4 Drilling and blasting basics 3 hUnderstand

Blasting breaks rock but is the most dangerous task in the mine. Only certified persons may do it.

  • Explain burden and spacing, and why they set fragmentation.
  • Compute powder factor as explosive kg per tonne or m3 of rock.
  • Describe exclusion zones, warnings and misfire rules in general terms.
  • Recognise that storage, transport and use of explosives are strictly controlled by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority.
Worked example. A blast breaks 12,000 m3 using 3,600 kg explosive: powder factor = 0.30 kg/m3. Changing to 4,200 kg gives 0.35 kg/m3, a 17 percent increase in explosive cost.

Activity. Calculate the powder factor for three blast designs and compare cost.

Standard to meet. Correct factors; safe-distance rules named as set by the competent authority.

Module 5 Loading and haulage 4 hAnalyse

Haulage is often the biggest cost. A cycle time sets truck output.

  • Cycle = load + haul + dump + return + queue.
  • Trips per hour = 60 / cycle minutes; output = trips x payload x availability.
  • Match trucks to loader: too few trucks idle the loader, too many queue.
  • Check road grade, width and condition as a control on speed and safety.
Worked example. Cycle: load 4 + haul 9 + dump 1 + return 7 + queue 2 = 23 min. Trips/h = 2.6; payload 30 t; availability 85 percent: 2.6 x 30 x 0.85 = 66 t/h per truck. For 400 t/h need 6 trucks.

Activity. Compute truck numbers for a target of 350 t/h.

Standard to meet. Cycle, trips and fleet all correct and rounded up.

Module 6 Ground control, ventilation and dust 4 hEvaluate

Falls of ground, bad air and dust cause severe harm. Controls are inspection, support, ventilation and measurement.

  • Check slope or roof conditions at every shift start and after blasts or rain.
  • Explain airflow: air demand depends on people, diesel machines and gases.
  • Measure gas and dust with calibrated instruments and keep the records.
  • Prepare an emergency plan: alarms, refuge, rescue contacts and drills.
Worked example. A face needs 0.06 m3/s of air per kW of diesel engine. Two 150 kW machines = 300 kW x 0.06 = 18 m3/s required. A fan delivering 15 m3/s is short by 3 m3/s, so work must be reduced or the fan upgraded.

Activity. Review a hazard register and rate each hazard by likelihood and severity.

Standard to meet. High risks have engineering controls first; stop-work triggers are written.

Module 7 Cost per tonne and economics 4 hAnalyse

Unit cost shows where money goes. A cutoff grade is the lowest grade that pays for mining and processing.

  • Split cost: drill, blast, load, haul, process, overhead, closure provision.
  • Cutoff grade = cost per tonne / (price per g x recovery).
  • Run a sensitivity of 10 percent on price, grade and cost.
  • State that prices move and no profit is promised.
Worked example. Costs total 2,400 RUB per tonne. With price 3,500 RUB per g and recovery 90 percent: cutoff = 2,400 / (3,500 x 0.9) = 0.76 g/t. Rock below 0.76 g/t is waste in this model.

Activity. Calculate cutoff grade and test a 10 percent cost rise.

Standard to meet. Formula applied correctly; sensitivity table complete.

Module 8 Environment, closure and compliance file 3 hCreate

A mine must manage water, waste rock, dust and closure. A compliance file proves it.

  • List environmental duties: water discharge, waste storage, dust, land reclamation.
  • Keep monitoring logs and permit conditions in one register.
  • Plan closure: reshape, cover, revegetate and monitor.
  • Name who inspects and how often according to the relevant federal rules.
Worked example. Closure provision of 150 RUB per tonne on 500,000 t = 75,000,000 RUB set aside over mine life; ignoring it would hide a large future cost.

Activity. Build a compliance register with ten items, owner, frequency and record.

Standard to meet. Every item has an owner, a frequency and a named record.

assessment

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is the difference between resource and reserve?

Model answer: A resource is a geological estimate; a reserve is the part that is economic and legally mineable.

Q2. Block 20 x 20 x 5 m at density 2.5: how many tonnes?

Model answer: 2,000 m3 x 2.5 = 5,000 t.

Q3. Weighted grade of 1 m at 2 g/t and 3 m at 6 g/t?

Model answer: (2 + 18) / 4 = 5 g/t.

Q4. Define powder factor.

Model answer: Explosive mass used per unit of rock broken, for example kg per m3.

Q5. Cycle time is 20 min. How many trips per hour?

Model answer: 3.

Q6. Why shift-start ground inspection?

Model answer: Conditions change after blasts, rain and time; falls of ground are a leading cause of harm.

Q7. Who may handle blasting?

Model answer: Only certified persons authorised under the relevant federal rules.

Q8. Cutoff grade formula?

Model answer: Cost per tonne divided by price per unit times recovery.

Q9. Why plan closure cost early?

Model answer: It is a large future liability that must be provided for.

Q10. Does this course allow you to run a mine?

Model answer: No; licences and competent persons are required.
