HELD · RU-11

Retailing

Run a small shop or online store: choose stock, set prices and margins, control inventory, serve customers and keep daily sales records.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner28 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Run a small shop or online store: choose stock, set prices and margins, control inventory, serve customers and keep daily sales records.

  • Understand Explain markup, margin, stock turn and shrinkage and how they affect shop profit.
  • Apply Calculate selling price, margin and reorder quantity for a product range.
  • Analyse Compare product lines by sales, margin and stock turn and decide which to keep.
  • Evaluate Judge a shop layout, a price and a promotion against customer and cash results.
  • Create Build a 4-week trading plan with stock, staffing, daily cash-up and a weekly review.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Build a 4-week trading plan for a small shop with 30 product lines, including pricing, reorder points, cash-up and a weekly review.

Condition

Desk exercise with a product list, supplier price sheet and spreadsheet; 4 hours.

Standard

Margins correct to 0.5 percentage points, reorder points correct, daily cash-up reconciles to 0 RUB, and at least 90 percent of rubric points.

Who it is for: New shop owners, sales assistants moving to supervisor, online sellers and market stall holders. Requirements: Basic arithmetic and spreadsheet use. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Retail is regulated. Business registration, tax, cash and receipt rules, consumer protection, labelling, and special rules for food, alcohol, tobacco, medicines and children products are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authorities. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before you open or sell regulated goods. Do not sell restricted goods without the right permission, and never sell goods you cannot show were legally sourced.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The retail model 3 hUnderstand

A shop buys goods, adds value in service and convenience, and sells at a price that covers cost and leaves a profit.

  • Define gross profit, net profit, fixed and variable costs.
  • Compare a shop, a market stall and an online store.
  • Choose a customer and a range that fit.
  • List the records a shop must keep.
Worked example. Monthly sales 600,000 RUB, cost of goods 390,000 RUB: gross profit 210,000 RUB = 35 percent. Fixed costs 150,000 RUB leave 60,000 RUB before tax.

Activity. Draw the money flow of a shop from purchase to profit using your own numbers.

Standard to meet. Gross and net profit are calculated correctly.

Module 2 Choosing and buying stock 4 hAnalyse

Buy what customers will actually buy, in quantities that can be sold before cash runs out.

  • Use sales history, local demand and season.
  • Compare suppliers on price, minimum order and delivery.
  • Buy test quantities of new lines.
  • Check that goods are legally sourced and labelled.
Worked example. Supplier A: 80 RUB for 100 units, delivery 1 week. Supplier B: 76 RUB for 500 units minimum, 3 weeks. At 40 units per week, 500 units is 12.5 weeks of stock, tying up 38,000 RUB; choose A.

Activity. Compare two suppliers for three products and justify a choice.

Standard to meet. Comparison includes cash tied up and weeks of cover.

Module 3 Pricing and margin 4 hApply

Price must cover cost, expenses and profit, and also match what customers accept.

  • Markup = (price - cost) / cost; margin = (price - cost) / price.
  • Include sales tax rules as set by the competent authority.
  • Round to prices customers expect.
  • Test and adjust.
Worked example. Cost 120 RUB. Target margin 40 percent: price = 120 / (1 - 0.40) = 200 RUB. Markup = 80/120 = 66.7 percent.

Activity. Price 10 products to a target margin and round to a customer-friendly price.

Standard to meet. All margins within 0.5 points of target.

Module 4 Inventory control 4 hApply

Stock out loses sales; overstock ties up cash. Count regularly and set reorder points.

  • Reorder point = daily sales x lead time days + safety stock.
  • Count high-value lines weekly.
  • Record damage, theft and expiry as shrinkage.
  • Rotate stock first in, first out.
Worked example. Sales 12 units per day, lead time 5 days, safety stock 18 units: reorder point = 12 x 5 + 18 = 78 units. Counted 74: order now.

Activity. Set reorder points for 5 products from data.

Standard to meet. All five reorder points correct and a count schedule written.

Module 5 Customers and display 3 hEvaluate

Layout, service and honesty decide whether customers return.

  • Place high-demand items to draw customers through the shop.
  • Keep price labels clear and correct.
  • Greet, help and thank; handle complaints calmly.
  • Know the consumer return rules from the competent authority.
Worked example. Footfall 200 people per day, 50 buy: conversion 25 percent. Average basket 900 RUB. If better display lifts conversion to 28 percent, daily sales rise from 45,000 RUB to 50,400 RUB.

Activity. Review a shop layout and propose 3 changes with expected effect.

Standard to meet. Changes are linked to conversion or basket size.

Module 6 Cash, receipts and daily records 3 hApply

Cash discipline protects you from errors and theft. Follow the receipt and cash rules set by the competent authority.

  • Count the till at open and close.
  • Reconcile card, cash and receipts.
  • Bank takings on a schedule.
  • Keep records for the period required by law.
Worked example. Float 5,000 RUB. Cash sales 38,400 RUB. Expected till 43,400 RUB. Counted 43,250 RUB: shortage 150 RUB, to investigate.

Activity. Complete a daily cash-up form for a given day.

Standard to meet. Reconciles to 0 RUB or the difference is explained.

Module 7 Compliance and regulated goods 3 hEvaluate

Some goods need special permission, storage and labelling. Selling them without it risks serious penalties.

  • List the goods you plan to sell and whether any are regulated.
  • Ask the competent authority what registration, labelling and permission applies.
  • Keep supplier documents that show origin.
  • Refuse to sell what you cannot show is legal.
Worked example. Range of 30 lines: 22 general, 5 food, 3 regulated. The 3 regulated lines are held until the permission is confirmed in writing; that is 10 percent of lines and 18 percent of planned sales.

Activity. Audit a product range and flag every regulated line with a next step.

Standard to meet. Every regulated line flagged with a named authority to ask.

Module 8 Trading plan and weekly review 4 hCreate

A plan turns knowledge into a routine: order, display, sell, count, review.

  • Set weekly sales and margin targets.
  • Plan staffing around busy hours.
  • Review the top and bottom 5 lines.
  • Change one thing each week.
Worked example. Target week 150,000 RUB sales at 35 percent margin = 52,500 RUB gross. Actual 138,000 RUB at 34 percent = 46,920 RUB. Gap 5,580 RUB; action: reduce slow lines, raise price of 3 lines by 5 percent.

Activity. Build the 4-week plan and a weekly review sheet.

Standard to meet. Plan has targets, staffing, reorder and review steps.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: a price list with margins, a reorder sheet, a daily cash-up form, a stock count record and a weekly review. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is margin on a 200 RUB price with 120 RUB cost?

Model answer: (200-120)/200 = 40 percent.

Q2. What is markup on the same item?

Model answer: 80/120 = 66.7 percent.

Q3. How do you calculate a reorder point?

Model answer: Daily sales x lead time + safety stock.

Q4. What is shrinkage?

Model answer: Stock lost to damage, theft, expiry or error.

Q5. Why count the till at open and close?

Model answer: To reconcile cash and find errors early.

Q6. What does conversion mean?

Model answer: The share of visitors who buy.

Q7. A regulated product has no permission yet. What do you do?

Model answer: Do not sell it; ask the competent authority and wait for written confirmation.

Q8. Why compare weeks of cover for a supplier deal?

Model answer: A cheap unit price can tie up too much cash in slow stock.

Q9. What is FIFO?

Model answer: First in, first out: sell the oldest stock first.

Q10. Why change only one thing per week?

Model answer: So you can see which change caused the result.
