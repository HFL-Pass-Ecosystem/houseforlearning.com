Timber and Furniture

Supply, saw and work timber and run a small joinery and furniture business: species and grading, moisture, volume and yield calculations, cutting lists, joinery methods, finishing, costing and legal sourcing.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner37 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Supply, saw and work timber and run a small joinery and furniture business: species and grading, moisture, volume and yield calculations, cutting lists, joinery methods, finishing, costing and legal sourcing.

  • Understand Explain timber species, moisture content, drying and how wood moves.
  • Apply Calculate timber volume, sawing yield, cutting lists and cost for a furniture item.
  • Analyse Compare joinery methods and materials on strength, appearance and cost.
  • Evaluate Judge a timber lot or a made piece against grade and quality criteria.
  • Create Build a job pack with a drawing, cutting list, process route, cost and legal sourcing file.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a costed job pack for one furniture item with a cutting list, joinery plan, finish schedule and a legal timber sourcing file.

Condition

Individual work over 6 weeks with a tape, a moisture meter if available, a calculator and the course templates; practical work only under a trained supervisor.

Standard

Volumes and cutting list correct to within 2 percent; joinery plan suited to the load; costing reconciles; sourcing file shows origin documents for every board; 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Carpenters, joiners, sawmill and timber yard workers, furniture makers and people planning a wood business. Requirements: Basic arithmetic and the ability to read a simple drawing. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Forestry and timber trade are regulated: felling rights, transport and origin documents, timber accounting systems, protected species and areas, and fire rules all apply. Buying or selling timber without proof of lawful origin is unlawful and the stock may be seized. Saws, planers and lifting cause serious injury and dust is a health hazard. This course does not give legal advice. Check which permits, documents and registrations apply with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Use trained operators, guards and dust control. No income is promised.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Timber species and grades 4 hUnderstand

Species and grade decide strength, appearance, workability and price.

  • Compare softwoods (pine, spruce, larch) and hardwoods (birch, oak) for use.
  • Know grading by knots, cracks, wane and straightness.
  • Match species to use: frame, floor, table top, outdoor.
  • Describe wood structure: grain, heartwood, sapwood.
Worked example. Pine boards graded A cost 24,000 RUB/m3; grade C costs 14,000 RUB/m3. For hidden frame parts, grade C saves 10,000 RUB per m3. Check: 24,000 - 14,000 = 10,000. For a job needing 0.5 m3 of hidden parts the saving is 5,000 RUB, but a visible table top still needs grade A because knots and cracks would show and weaken the surface.

Activity. Choose species and grades for three items, such as a frame, a floor and a table top, and give a one-line reason and a price per m3 for each.

Standard to meet. Each choice fits the use, load and appearance; each has a reason; the saving or extra cost against grade A is calculated correctly in RUB per m3; no item uses a weak grade for a load-bearing part.

Module 2 Wood supply and legal origin 5 hEvaluate

Lawful origin documents are the base of the whole business. Without them stock and money are at risk.

  • List documents to ask for: origin, volume, species, transport, seller registration.
  • Match delivered volume and species to documents.
  • Never accept timber without a clear chain of papers.
  • Confirm rules for protected species and for export or transport with the competent authority.
Worked example. A load is documented as 12 m3 pine; measured at 14 m3 with some oak. The 2 m3 difference and the species mismatch mean the load is not accepted until the papers are corrected. Check: 14 - 12 = 2 m3, which is 2 / 12 = 16.7 percent over. Step 1: hold the load apart. Step 2: ask the seller for corrected papers. Step 3: log the decision with date and name.

Activity. Build a supplier file for one source listing documents required, checks at delivery, a decision rule and a record of each load.

Standard to meet. The file lists at least five documents; the rule sets a volume tolerance, for example 2 percent, and rejects any species mismatch; three sample loads are checked and logged; the competent authority is named as the source for rules.

Module 3 Measuring and volume 4 hApply

Volume of sawn timber is length x width x thickness. Round logs use a volume formula and a taper allowance.

  • Compute board volume in m3 from mm dimensions.
  • Compute log volume with the cylinder formula as an estimate.
  • Count packs and apply a stacking factor.
  • Record measurement method with each lot.
Worked example. Convert mm to m first. A board 3.0 m x 0.150 m x 0.025 m = 0.01125 m3. 80 boards = 0.9 m3; at 20,000 RUB/m3 the lot costs 18,000 RUB. Check: 0.01125 x 80 = 0.9 and 0.9 x 20,000 = 18,000. If the supplier invoices 1.0 m3 for the same lot, the overcharge is 0.1 m3 x 20,000 = 2,000 RUB, so measure every lot at delivery.

Activity. Calculate volumes and costs for three lots from measured dimensions and compare each with the invoice.

Standard to meet. All three volumes are within 2 percent of a recheck; units are converted correctly; each cost in RUB is shown; any invoice difference above 2 percent is flagged and the measuring method is recorded.

Module 4 Sawing, yield and drying 5 hAnalyse

Sawing pattern decides yield; drying decides stability.

  • Estimate sawn yield from logs; typical yield is 50 to 65 percent.
  • Allow saw kerf and trim.
  • Dry to a target moisture content for use: about 8 to 12 percent indoors.
  • Stack with stickers for airflow and protect from rain and sun.
Worked example. 2.0 m3 of logs at 55 percent yield gives 1.1 m3 sawn. Logs at 6,000 RUB/m3 cost 12,000 RUB, so sawn timber costs 10,909 RUB/m3 before labour and drying. Check: 2.0 x 0.55 = 1.1 and 12,000 / 1.1 = 10,909. If a better sawing pattern lifts yield to 60 percent, sawn volume is 1.2 m3 and the cost falls to 10,000 RUB/m3, a saving of 909 RUB per m3.

Activity. Compute yield and cost per m3 sawn from a log lot, then test one change in sawing pattern and drying plan and report the effect.

Standard to meet. Yield and cost per m3 are within 2 percent of a recheck; the comparison shows both patterns; the drying plan states a target moisture of 8 to 12 percent and a stacking method with stickers.

Module 5 Cutting lists and material planning 5 hApply

A cutting list turns a drawing into parts with sizes, grain direction and quantity.

  • List parts: name, length, width, thickness, quantity.
  • Add allowance for planing and trimming.
  • Nest parts on boards to limit waste.
  • Compute total board metres and waste percent.
Worked example. A table needs 4 legs 720 x 60 x 60 mm, 2 rails 1,100 x 90 x 25, a top 1,200 x 700 x 25. Volumes: legs 0.01037 m3, rails 0.00495 m3, top 0.021 m3; total 0.0363 m3; with 25 percent allowance 0.0454 m3. Check: 0.01037 + 0.00495 + 0.021 = 0.03632, and 0.03632 x 1.25 = 0.0454. Waste is 0.0454 - 0.0363 = 0.0091 m3, which is 20 percent of the purchase.

Activity. Prepare a cutting list and a waste figure for a cabinet with part names, sizes in mm, quantities, grain direction and a nesting sketch.

Standard to meet. The list is complete against the drawing; volumes and totals are within 2 percent; the allowance is stated; waste percent is calculated; grain direction is marked on every visible part.

Module 6 Joinery and assembly 5 hAnalyse

Joints must carry the load and allow for wood movement across the grain.

  • Compare mortise and tenon, dowel, dovetail, lap, screws and glue.
  • Allow for seasonal movement in wide panels.
  • Clamp, glue and dry as instructed; check square.
  • Test a sample joint for strength by a simple load.
Worked example. A solid pine panel 600 mm wide may move about 1 percent across the grain with large humidity change: about 6 mm. Fix with slotted holes. Check: 600 x 0.01 = 6 mm. A slot should allow at least 3 mm each side of the screw, so a fixed top on a cupboard would crack or split the frame. Choose mortise and tenon for chair legs because racking loads act on them.

Activity. Choose joints for a chair and a cupboard, give a reason for each joint, and test one sample joint with a simple safe load.

Standard to meet. Each joint suits the load and allows for movement across the grain; at least four joint types are compared; the movement figure is calculated; the test load, result and any failure are recorded with eye protection used.

Module 7 Finishing and safety 5 hEvaluate

Sanding and finish protect the wood and set appearance. Dust, solvents and machines need control.

  • Sand through grits (for example 80, 120, 180).
  • Compare oil, wax, varnish and paint on durability and care.
  • Control dust with extraction and masks; keep solvents away from heat.
  • Guard machines; lock out before blade change; wear hearing and eye protection.
Worked example. A varnish covers 10 m2/L per coat; a table with 3 m2 and 3 coats needs 0.9 L. At 700 RUB/L the finish costs 630 RUB. Check: 3 x 3 = 9 m2 of coverage, 9 / 10 = 0.9 L, 0.9 x 700 = 630. Allow drying between coats, sand lightly with 180 grit, and keep solvent rags in a closed metal container away from heat to prevent fire.

Activity. Write a finish schedule and risk list covering sanding grits, coats, drying times, quantities in litres and RUB, dust, solvent and machine controls.

Standard to meet. The schedule has coats, quantities and cost; at least five risks each have a named control; extraction, masks, hearing and eye protection and lockout are listed; quantities are correct.

Module 8 Costing, pricing and the job pack 4 hCreate

The job pack joins materials, labour, finish, overhead and margin.

  • Compute material cost from the cutting list.
  • Estimate hours per operation.
  • Add hardware, finish, delivery and overhead.
  • Set a price with a margin; agree changes in writing.
Worked example. Timber 0.0454 m3 x 20,000 = 908 RUB; hardware 400 RUB; finish 630 RUB; labour 12 h x 350 = 4,200 RUB; overhead 15 percent of 6,138 = 921 RUB. Cost 7,059 RUB. With a 30 percent margin, price = 7,059 / 0.7 = 10,084 RUB. Check: 908 + 400 + 630 + 4,200 = 6,138 and 6,138 + 921 = 7,059; margin = 10,084 - 7,059 = 3,025 RUB, 30 percent of price. Agree any change in writing before work.

Activity. Assemble the full job pack with sourcing file, cutting list, finish schedule, costing, price, timeline and a change record.

Standard to meet. The pack is consistent across documents; costs add up; price margin is correct; the sourcing file has matched origin papers; a risk list and a written change rule are included.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. How is board volume found?

Model answer: Length x width x thickness, in metres.

Q2. What is a typical indoor moisture target?

Model answer: About 8 to 12 percent.

Q3. Why must origin papers match the load?

Model answer: Mismatches may show unlawful timber and risk seizure.

Q4. What is sawing yield?

Model answer: Sawn timber volume divided by log volume, times 100.

Q5. Why use stickers when stacking?

Model answer: To allow air to flow and dry the boards evenly.

Q6. What is wood movement?

Model answer: Swelling and shrinking with humidity, mostly across the grain.

Q7. Name two ways to control dust.

Model answer: Extraction at the machine and masks (also cleaning methods).

Q8. What is a cutting list?

Model answer: A list of parts with sizes, quantity and grain direction.

Q9. Who can confirm timber permits?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

Q10. Why add an allowance to the cutting list?

Model answer: For planing, trimming and defects.
