HELD · RU-04

Garments and Accessories

Plan, make or source and sell garments and accessories as a small business: design brief, fabric and trim, pattern and fit, costing per item, quality control, labelling and sales channels.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner37 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Plan, make or source and sell garments and accessories as a small business: design brief, fabric and trim, pattern and fit, costing per item, quality control, labelling and sales channels.

  • Understand Explain how a garment or accessory moves from idea to pattern, sample, production and sale.
  • Apply Calculate fabric yardage, cost per item and a retail price for a garment.
  • Analyse Compare make-in-house, outsourced and bought-in options on cost, quality and speed.
  • Evaluate Judge a sample against a quality checklist for stitching, fit and finish.
  • Create Build a small collection plan with specification sheets, costing, quality plan and sales plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a small collection plan of three items with specification sheets, costing and quality checks.

Condition

Individual work over 6 weeks with a measuring tape, a spreadsheet, sample fabrics and trims and the course templates.

Standard

Fabric and cost calculations correct to within 3 percent; three specification sheets complete; quality checklist applied to two samples; 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Tailors, seamstresses, small label owners, market sellers and people planning to start a garment or accessory business. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. Basic sewing is useful but not required. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Garment sale is subject to rules on product labelling, fibre content, safety of children's clothing, tax and registration, and consumer rights. This course gives no legal advice. Check which labelling, conformity and registration rules apply to your product with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Do not copy protected designs or marks. Machines, irons and cutters cause injury; keep guards, light and ventilation. Earnings vary and none are promised.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 From idea to product 4 hUnderstand

A product starts with a customer and a need. The brief ties style, price, size range and season together.

  • Define the customer: age, use, budget, climate.
  • Write a brief: product, fabric, colours, sizes, target cost and price.
  • Know the steps: sketch, pattern, sample, fit, grade, cut, sew, finish, pack.
  • Check if the idea is original and does not copy protected designs.
Worked example. A brief for a winter work jacket: price 4,500 RUB, sizes 44 to 56, target cost 2,250 RUB (50 percent), made from 2.2 m of fabric.

Activity. Write a brief for one product.

Standard to meet. Brief has customer, price, sizes and target cost.

Module 2 Fabrics, trims and materials 4 hUnderstand

Fibre, weave, weight and finish decide feel, durability, care and cost.

  • Compare cotton, linen, wool, synthetics and blends; note weight in g/m2.
  • Choose lining, interfacing, zips, buttons, thread and labels.
  • Test shrinkage and colour fastness on a swatch.
  • Read fibre content and care symbols and keep them for labelling.
Worked example. A 10 cm x 10 cm swatch shrinks to 9.5 cm x 10 cm after wash: 5 percent in length. A 2.2 m pattern needs 2.2 / 0.95 = 2.32 m of fabric.

Activity. Test two fabrics for shrinkage and compare.

Standard to meet. Shrinkage is computed correctly and the fabric allowance adjusted.

Module 3 Pattern, fit and sizing 5 hApply

A pattern must fit the target body shapes. Grading creates other sizes from a base size.

  • Take body measurements: bust, waist, hip, length, arm.
  • Add ease and seam allowance (often 1 cm).
  • Fit a sample on a person or form and mark changes.
  • Grade by set steps between sizes; keep a size chart.
Worked example. Bust 92 cm with 8 cm ease gives a finished bust of 100 cm; each size step adds 4 cm to the bust, so the next size is 104 cm.

Activity. Create a size chart for three sizes.

Standard to meet. Measurements, ease and grade steps are consistent.

Module 4 Fabric use and costing 5 hApply

Cost per item = materials + labour + overhead. Marker efficiency decides fabric waste.

  • Lay patterns to cut as little waste as possible; compute marker efficiency.
  • Add trims, labels, packaging and thread.
  • Estimate sewing minutes and a labour rate.
  • Add overhead and a margin to set a price.
Worked example. Fabric 2.3 m x 600 RUB/m = 1,380 RUB; trims 220 RUB; labour 90 min at 8 RUB/min = 720 RUB; overhead 150 RUB. Cost 2,470 RUB. At a 40 percent margin on price, price = 2,470 / 0.6 = 4,117 RUB.

Activity. Cost three items and set prices.

Standard to meet. Costs reconcile within 3 percent.

Module 5 Cutting, sewing and finishing 5 hApply

Order of work and machine setup decide speed and quality. Standard seams and finishes give a clean product.

  • Prepare the machine: needle, tension, stitch length.
  • Sequence operations to reduce handling.
  • Finish edges with the right seam; press at each stage.
  • Keep a daily output and defect count.
Worked example. A jacket takes 12 operations of 90 minutes in total. In an 8-hour day (480 min) a sewer completes 5.3 jackets at best; planning for 4.5 allows for rework and breaks.

Activity. Plan the operations and time for one garment.

Standard to meet. Sequence is logical and the daily output estimate is realistic.

Module 6 Quality control and compliance 5 hEvaluate

Quality is checked at cutting, during sewing and at the end. Labelling and safety rules are part of quality.

  • Make an inspection sheet: stitch density, seam strength, symmetry, size, stains, thread ends.
  • Define acceptable quality level and what to do with defects.
  • Check labels: size, fibre content, care, maker details as required.
  • Check children's items for small parts, cords and flammability rules with the competent authority.
Worked example. In a lot of 100, 6 have defects: 6 percent. If the limit is 3 percent the lot fails; reworking 6 pieces at 120 RUB each costs 720 RUB.

Activity. Inspect two samples with the checklist.

Standard to meet. Defects found and graded correctly.

Module 7 Selling: channels and customers 5 hAnalyse

Market stalls, shops, online platforms, workshops and corporate orders each have different margin and effort.

  • Compare channels: price, fees, effort, payment speed.
  • Take good photos and write honest product descriptions.
  • Know consumer rights on returns and exchanges.
  • Set a minimum order and lead time for made-to-order items.
Worked example. Sell 50 items at 4,117 RUB direct = 205,850 RUB. Through a shop taking 30 percent you receive 144,095 RUB; the 61,755 RUB difference buys the shop's reach and time saved.

Activity. Compare two channels in a table.

Standard to meet. Table has price, fee, effort and payment time with a choice.

Module 8 Collection plan and cash 4 hCreate

A small collection plan keeps range, cash and workload in balance.

  • Limit styles; reuse fabrics across items.
  • Plan production batches by cash available.
  • Track stock turn and mark down slow items.
  • Review sales every month.
Worked example. Batch of 30 jackets costs 74,100 RUB. Selling 20 at full price brings 82,340 RUB; the other 10 at 20 percent off bring 32,936 RUB, so the batch ends with 41,176 RUB over cost.

Activity. Present the three-item collection plan.

Standard to meet. Plan has specs, costs, quality plan and a cash view.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: design brief, specification sheets, fabric and cost sheet, quality checklist, sales plan. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is ease?

Model answer: Extra room added to body measurements for comfort and movement.

Q2. How does shrinkage change fabric need?

Model answer: Divide the length by the shrink factor to add allowance.

Q3. What is marker efficiency?

Model answer: The share of fabric area used by pattern pieces.

Q4. Name three parts of cost per item.

Model answer: Materials, labour and overhead.

Q5. What is a specification sheet?

Model answer: A document with sizes, fabric, trims, seams and finish for a product.

Q6. Why test colour fastness?

Model answer: To avoid colour running in wash or wear.

Q7. What labels might be required?

Model answer: Size, fibre content, care and maker details; check with the competent authority.

Q8. Why avoid copying protected designs?

Model answer: It can break the law and harm others.

Q9. How do you set a price from cost and margin?

Model answer: Divide cost by one minus the margin share.

Q10. Why track defects by operation?

Model answer: To find and fix the step that causes them.
