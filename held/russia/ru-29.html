Social Media Marketing

Learn to plan, produce, publish and measure social media marketing for a small business: audience, content calendar, paid reach basics, community replies and honest reporting.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner28 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to plan, produce, publish and measure social media marketing for a small business: audience, content calendar, paid reach basics, community replies and honest reporting.

  • Understand Explain how audience, message and channel fit together in a small business plan.
  • Apply Build a four-week content calendar with formats, goals and posting times.
  • Analyse Calculate engagement rate, cost per click and cost per lead and find the weakest step.
  • Evaluate Judge posts and advertisements against honesty, consent and platform rules.
  • Create Build a monthly report with findings and the next test.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce an audience profile, a four-week calendar of 16 posts, one small paid test plan, a reply guide and a monthly report for a local small business.

Condition

Given a business profile, a spreadsheet, free design tools and 6 hours of working time, with a mentor to review.

Standard

Calendar covers all four weeks with a goal for every post, metric arithmetic is correct, every claim can be backed up, and at least 8 of 10 check answers are right.

Who it is for: Small business owners, marketing assistants, freelancers and community managers. Requirements: Basic arithmetic, a spreadsheet and access to the platforms the business uses. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Advertising, consumer protection, personal data, labelling of ads and platform terms are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority, and platform access and rules can change. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser, especially for regulated goods, health claims, finance and children. Never buy fake followers, invent reviews or promise guaranteed results. Reach and sales vary and this course promises none.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Goals, audience and message 3 hUnderstand

Marketing starts with a goal and a person, not a platform.

  • Set one goal with a number and a date, for example 30 enquiries in 8 weeks.
  • Describe the audience by need, place, age range and where they spend time.
  • Write a one-sentence promise that is true and checkable.
  • Choose one or two channels, not all of them.
Worked example. A bakery wants 30 enquiries for custom cakes in 8 weeks. Audience: parents aged 28 to 45 within 8 km. Promise: cakes made to order with 48 hours notice.

Activity. Write goal, audience and promise for a local business.

Standard to meet. Goal has a number and date; promise can be checked.

Module 2 Content planning and calendar 4 hApply

A calendar keeps posting steady and links each post to a goal.

  • Mix content: useful, proof, behind the scenes and offer, for example 40/20/20/20.
  • Plan 3 to 4 posts a week and the format of each: photo, short video, text, story.
  • Reuse one idea in several formats.
  • Batch production: make a week of content in one session.
Worked example. 16 posts in 4 weeks: 6 useful, 4 proof, 3 behind the scenes, 3 offers. A batch session of 3 hours for 4 posts is 45 minutes each.

Activity. Build the four-week calendar with goal per post.

Standard to meet. All 16 posts have format, goal and date.

Module 3 Creating clear, honest content 4 hCreate

Good posts are clear, specific and true.

  • Lead with the point in the first line.
  • Use real photos and plain words; add a single call to action.
  • Add captions to videos and alt text to images.
  • Mark paid or sponsored posts as the rules require; never fake reviews.
Worked example. Weak: Best cakes in town. Strong: Birthday cakes for 10 to 12 people, ready in 48 hours, from 2,400 RUB. One claim, one number, one action.

Activity. Write six captions and a call to action for each.

Standard to meet. Each caption has one point, one action and no unsupported claim.

Module 4 Community and replies 3 hApply

Replies build trust. Slow or rude replies lose customers.

  • Set a reply target, for example within 4 working hours.
  • Write short reply templates for price, delivery and complaints.
  • Move private details to a private message.
  • Handle criticism calmly and fix the real problem.
Worked example. Over a week 40 comments and 12 messages arrive. At 3 minutes each, replies take 52 x 3 = 156 minutes, about 2.6 hours. Templates cut it by about a third.

Activity. Write a reply guide with five templates.

Standard to meet. Templates are polite, accurate and avoid private data in public.

Module 5 Metrics and analysis 4 hAnalyse

Numbers show what works. Choose a few that tie to the goal.

  • Engagement rate = (likes + comments + shares) / reach x 100.
  • Click-through rate = clicks / views x 100.
  • Cost per lead = spend / leads.
  • Compare against your own past results, not other accounts.
Worked example. A post reaches 2,000 people with 90 reactions: 4.5 percent. A small paid test of 3,000 RUB gets 1,500 views, 45 clicks (3.0 percent) and 6 leads: 500 RUB per lead.

Activity. Compute four metrics from a given table and name the weakest.

Standard to meet. All arithmetic is correct and the weak step is found.

Module 6 Paid reach basics 4 hApply

A small paid test can show which message works. It must be capped and measured.

  • Set a budget cap per day and per test.
  • Test one variable at a time: image, line or audience.
  • Wait for enough results before choosing, for example 100 clicks per version.
  • Follow ad labelling and platform rules, and check restrictions with the competent authority.
Worked example. Two versions each get 1,500 RUB. A: 3,000 views, 60 clicks, 4 leads = 375 RUB per lead. B: 3,000 views, 36 clicks, 3 leads = 500 RUB per lead. A wins, but with so few leads repeat the test.

Activity. Plan a two-version test with a budget cap.

Standard to meet. Test changes one variable, caps spend and states the decision rule.

Module 7 Ethics, rules and risk 3 hEvaluate

Trust is the main asset. Rules protect users and your business.

  • Get consent before posting photos of people, especially children.
  • Do not collect or share personal data without a lawful basis under the relevant federal rules.
  • Disclose paid partnerships and never mislead about price or results.
  • Plan for a crisis: pause posts, check facts, reply once, correct openly.
Worked example. A post says 50 percent off but the base price rose 40 percent a week earlier. The real discount is small, so the post is misleading and must be corrected.

Activity. Audit six posts against a checklist and fix problems.

Standard to meet. All misleading or unconsented items are found and rewritten.

Module 8 Monthly report and next test 3 hCreate

A report turns numbers into one decision.

  • Show goal, result, and the three metrics that matter.
  • Explain why, using evidence from posts.
  • Name one test for next month with a hypothesis.
  • Keep the report to one page.
Worked example. Goal 30 enquiries; result 24 (80 percent). Best post type: proof posts with 6.1 percent engagement. Next test: double the proof posts from 4 to 8.

Activity. Write a one-page report from a given data table.

Standard to meet. Report has goal, result, reason and one test.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: audience profile, content calendar, paid test plan, reply guide, monthly report. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

CalendarPosts without goals.Four weeks with goals and formats.Also reuses ideas across formats.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why start with a goal, not a platform?

Model answer: The goal decides the audience, message and channel.

Q2. Engagement rate formula?

Model answer: (Likes + comments + shares) divided by reach, times 100.

Q3. 3,000 RUB spend, 6 leads: cost per lead?

Model answer: 500 RUB.

Q4. Why change one variable in a test?

Model answer: So you know which change caused the difference.

Q5. Should you buy followers?

Model answer: No; they are not real customers and break platform rules.

Q6. What is a good reply target?

Model answer: A set time such as within 4 working hours.

Q7. Why disclose paid posts?

Model answer: Honesty and the advertising rules require it.

Q8. A 50 percent off claim but price rose first. Problem?

Model answer: It is misleading; the real discount is lower.

Q9. Why batch content?

Model answer: It saves time and keeps posting steady.

Q10. Why consent for photos of people?

Model answer: They are personal data and children need special care.
