Insurance

Understand how insurance works, assess risk, read policies, handle claims and serve customers fairly within the rules.

about this course

Understand how insurance works, assess risk, read policies, handle claims and serve customers fairly within the rules.

  • Understand Explain risk pooling, premium, sum insured, excess and exclusions.
  • Apply Calculate a premium from a base rate and loading factors.
  • Analyse Compare two policies for cover, limits and exclusions.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Assess a customer's need, compare two policies and process a simple claim.

Condition

In 6 weeks using sample policies, a calculator and a record template.

Standard

Premium within 1 percent of the model answer; needs analysis covers 5 risk areas; claim file complete with decision reasons; pass mark 8 of 10.

Who it is for: Newcomers and junior staff in agencies, brokers and insurers. Requirements: Basic arithmetic; no prior insurance work. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Insurance is a regulated activity. Selling, advising or settling claims needs the licence, registration or employer authority the competent Russian supervisory authority requires; this course does not grant any of it. Do not give advice beyond your authority. Protect personal data under the relevant federal rules. Never promise a claim will be paid. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How insurance works 4 hUnderstand

Insurance moves a rare, costly loss from one person to a large pool of people who share it through premiums. It works because individual losses are unpredictable while the average across many similar risks is steady. Premiums fund claims, costs and a safety margin. Cover is only offered for risks that are accidental, measurable and outside the insured's control, and always inside the rules of the competent Russian supervisory authority.

  • Define risk pooling and the law of large numbers.
  • Define premium, sum insured, excess, limit and exclusion.
  • Separate insurable risks (accidental, measurable, not controllable) from uninsurable ones.
  • Explain insurable interest and utmost good faith.
  • Sketch the parties: insured, insurer, intermediary, regulator.
Worked example. A pool has 1,000 homes. Each faces a 0.2 percent yearly chance of a fire costing 2,000,000 RUB. Step 1: expected losses = 1,000 x 0.002 = 2 fires, or 4,000,000 RUB for the pool. Step 2: per home = 4,000,000 / 1,000 = 4,000 RUB. Step 3: costs and margin take 30 percent of the premium, so premium = 4,000 / 0.70 = 5,714 RUB. Check: 1,000 x 5,714 = 5,714,000 RUB; 70 percent of that is 4,000,000 RUB, which covers the expected claims.

Activity. Calculate expected loss and premium for a model pool.

Standard to meet. Expected loss and premium correct to 1 percent.

Module 2 Types of cover 4 hUnderstand

Cover differs by what is protected and who is protected. Life and health cover protect people, property and motor cover protect things, and liability cover pays for harm done to others. Some cover is compulsory under the relevant federal rules and some is voluntary. Indemnity cover restores the actual loss, while fixed-benefit cover pays an agreed sum. Reading each type's limits and common exclusions prevents false expectations.

  • List life, health, property, motor, travel and liability cover.
  • Match cover to risk: property cover to a building, liability to harm to others.
  • Explain compulsory versus voluntary cover as set by the relevant federal rules.
  • Describe indemnity versus fixed-benefit cover.
  • Know the common exclusions: war, wear and tear, intent.
Worked example. A flat is worth 4,500,000 RUB but insured for 3,000,000 RUB. Step 1: proportion insured = 3,000,000 / 4,500,000 = 0.667, so one third is uninsured. Step 2: a 600,000 RUB loss is paid at 600,000 x 0.667 = 400,000 RUB under the average rule, before any excess. Step 3: the customer bears 200,000 RUB. Check: 400,000 / 600,000 = 0.667, the same proportion as sum insured to value. Advice: raise the sum insured to the true value.

Activity. Fill a cover map for a sample family.

Standard to meet. Each risk matched to a cover type with reason.

Module 3 Risk assessment and pricing 5 hApply

A premium reflects the chance and size of loss, plus costs and tax. Underwriters start from a base rate for the class of risk, then adjust with loadings where risk is higher and discounts where it is lower. Each factor needs a reason that can be shown to the customer and the supervisor. Pricing must be fair, consistent between similar customers and checked for sense, never set by guesswork or pressure.

  • Start from a base rate for the class of risk.
  • Apply loadings for age, location, use and claims history.
  • Apply discounts for excess, security and no-claim periods.
  • Add tax and fees as the rules require.
  • Review the result for sense.
Worked example. Base rate for a motor class is 8,000 RUB. Step 1: city loading 1.25 gives 10,000 RUB. Step 2: young driver loading 1.10 gives 11,000 RUB. Step 3: no-claims discount 0.85 gives 9,350 RUB. Step 4: add any required tax or fee from the rate table. Check: multiply the factors first, 1.25 x 1.10 x 0.85 = 1.16875, and 8,000 x 1.16875 = 9,350 RUB. Both routes agree, and the result is about 17 percent above base, which is sensible.

Activity. Price three sample risks with a rate table.

Standard to meet. All three within 1 percent of the model.

Module 4 Reading policies 4 hAnalyse

Words decide who pays. A policy has a schedule naming the insured, period, sum insured and excess, definitions that fix the meaning of terms, cover clauses, exclusions and conditions the customer must meet, such as prompt notice of loss. Reading definitions before cover clauses avoids misreading. A comparison table shows gaps and overlaps, so a recommendation rests on wording and not on headline price.

  • Find the schedule, insured, period, sum insured and excess.
  • Read definitions before cover clauses.
  • Find conditions the customer must meet, such as notice of loss.
  • Compare two policies in a table.
  • Highlight gaps and overlaps.
Worked example. Policy A: excess 5,000 RUB, limit 1,500,000 RUB, flood covered, premium 12,000 RUB. Policy B: excess 2,000 RUB, limit 1,000,000 RUB, flood excluded, premium 10,500 RUB. Step 1: premium gap = 1,500 RUB. Step 2: for a flood-prone home with a 900,000 RUB flood loss, A pays 895,000 RUB and B pays 0. Step 3: B saves only 3,000 RUB of excess on other claims. Check: A costs 1,500 RUB more yet protects against the largest loss, so A is better.

Activity. Compare two sample policies in a table.

Standard to meet. Table covers limits, excess, exclusions and a recommendation.

Module 5 Needs analysis and fair advice 4 hCreate

Advice starts with the customer, not the product. A needs analysis asks about people, property, income, debts and liabilities, then ranks risks by likelihood and size. The adviser explains cost, limits and exclusions in plain words, states what is not known, and records the reasons for each recommendation. Records are kept for the period the rules require, and advice stays within the authority held.

  • Ask about people, property, income and liabilities.
  • Record answers and the reasons for a recommendation.
  • Explain cost, limits and exclusions in plain words.
  • Never mislead; state what you do not know.
  • Keep records for the period the rules require.
Worked example. A family has one earner and a 3,000,000 RUB mortgage. Monthly living costs are 40,000 RUB. Step 1: debt to clear = 3,000,000 RUB. Step 2: two years of costs = 40,000 x 24 = 960,000 RUB. Step 3: life cover needed = 3,000,000 + 960,000 = 3,960,000 RUB. Check: existing savings of 500,000 RUB would reduce the gap to 3,460,000 RUB. Record the figures, the reasons and the customer's decision.

Activity. Write a needs analysis for a sample customer.

Standard to meet. Five risk areas covered; reasons recorded.

Module 6 Claims handling 5 hEvaluate

A claim is a promise tested. Fair and prompt handling rests on evidence and policy wording. The handler logs the notice and its time, collects photos, reports and receipts, checks that cover was active and no exclusion applies, then calculates payment after excess and any average. The decision is written with reasons, and the customer is told how to complain. Never promise payment before checks are done.

  • Log the notice and time limits.
  • Gather evidence: photos, reports, receipts, statements.
  • Check cover, period, excess and exclusions.
  • Calculate payment; apply average if under-insured.
  • Write reasons, and tell the customer how to complain.
Worked example. Loss 120,000 RUB, excess 5,000 RUB. Case 1, full cover: 120,000 - 5,000 = 115,000 RUB. Case 2, sum insured is 60 percent of value: step 1, 120,000 x 0.6 = 72,000 RUB; step 2, less excess 5,000 gives 67,000 RUB. Check: the difference between the cases is 48,000 RUB, which equals 40 percent of 120,000, the under-insured share. Write the reasons and calculation in the file before telling the customer.

Activity. Process two sample claims with a file each.

Standard to meet. Decision correct with reasons; calculation shown.

Module 7 Fraud, ethics and data 3 hEvaluate

Fraud raises premiums for everyone and harms honest customers. Handlers learn common red flags such as late notice, inconsistent accounts and missing receipts, but a flag is a reason to look closer, not proof. Cases are escalated to the person in charge, not argued with the customer. Personal data is shared only as needed, conflicts of interest are disclosed, and vulnerable customers get extra care.

  • Spot red flags: late notice, inconsistent stories, no receipts.
  • Escalate, do not accuse.
  • Protect personal data; share only as needed.
  • Avoid conflicts of interest; disclose commission where the rules require.
  • Treat customers fairly, including vulnerable ones.
Worked example. A 'stolen laptop' claim of 90,000 RUB arrives 2 days after cover began, with no receipt. Step 1: list flags: new policy, no proof of purchase, high value. Step 2: do not refuse; ask for the serial number, a bank record and the police report. Step 3: escalate to the supervisor within 1 working day. Check: three flags are a pattern to review, not a verdict. Keep notes factual and decide only when the evidence is complete.

Activity. Review a case file and list red flags and next steps.

Standard to meet. Flags listed; escalation route stated.

Module 8 Compliance and review 3 hEvaluate

A licensed business works within the rules and tests itself. Staff must know exactly what authority the licence or employer gives them and stay inside it. Complaint logs show repeating causes to fix. Sample file audits against a checklist reveal gaps before a supervisor does. Measures such as claim handling time in days and complaint rate are tracked, and training is updated when rules or errors change.

  • Know what authority the licence or employer gives you.
  • Keep complaint logs and fix causes.
  • Check files in a sample audit.
  • Track key measures: claim time in days, complaint rate.
  • Plan training and updates.
Worked example. Audit 20 claim files against a 10-item checklist. Step 1: count complete files: 17. Step 2: completion rate = 17 / 20 = 85 percent. Step 3: a 95 percent target means at most 1 incomplete file in 20, so the gap is 2 files. Step 4: list the missing items, such as no reasons or no complaint notice, and assign fixes. Check: re-audit 10 new files in 4 weeks; 10 of 10 complete confirms the plan works.

Activity. Audit five files against a checklist.

Standard to meet. Results counted and an action plan written.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is risk pooling?

Model answer: Many people pay in so a few losses are shared.

Q2. Define excess.

Model answer: The part of a loss the insured pays.

Q3. What is average?

Model answer: Reducing a payment when the sum insured is less than the value.

Q4. Expected loss: 0.2 percent x 2,000,000 RUB.

Model answer: 4,000 RUB.

Q5. Why read definitions first?

Model answer: Terms set the meaning of cover.

Q6. Name two red flags of fraud.

Model answer: Late notice, missing receipts or inconsistent accounts.

Q7. May you sell insurance without authority?

Model answer: No; the competent authority's licence or registration is required.

Q8. What goes into a premium?

Model answer: Expected loss, costs, margin and tax.

Q9. Why record advice?

Model answer: To show it was suitable and to meet the rules.

Q10. Who do you check legal limits with?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a local adviser.
