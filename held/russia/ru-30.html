Automobile (Sales, Service, Workshop)

Learn how a car dealership and workshop run: vehicle sales steps, service intake, job cards, labour and parts pricing, workshop safety, quality checks and customer records.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner29 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn how a car dealership and workshop run: vehicle sales steps, service intake, job cards, labour and parts pricing, workshop safety, quality checks and customer records.

  • Understand Explain the flow of a vehicle from sale to delivery and from service intake to hand-back.
  • Apply Prepare a quote with vehicle price, extras and financing figures, and a job card with labour and parts.
  • Analyse Calculate workshop efficiency, gross margin and parts turnover and find weak points.
  • Evaluate Judge a workshop against safety, environmental and warranty checks.
  • Create Build a service process with checklists, records and customer follow-up.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a sales quote, a service intake form, two job cards, a workshop safety checklist and a weekly performance sheet for a small dealership with a workshop.

Condition

Given price lists in RUB, a labour rate, a spreadsheet and 6 hours of working time, with a mentor to review.

Standard

Quotes and job cards have no arithmetic error, every safety checklist item has a record, efficiency and margin are correctly calculated, and at least 8 of 10 check answers are right.

Who it is for: Sales trainees, service advisers, workshop assistants, parts clerks and small garage owners. Requirements: Basic arithmetic, a spreadsheet and interest in vehicles. No licence is needed to study. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Vehicle sale, registration, safety inspection, warranty, consumer protection, waste oils and batteries, and dealer and repair licensing are governed by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. Workshop work involves lifting, fire, fumes, electrics (including high-voltage systems) and moving parts. Only trained persons may carry out safety-critical repairs such as brakes and steering. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Do not certify a vehicle as safe without proper inspection.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Dealership and workshop flow 3 hUnderstand

A dealership has sales, finance, service, parts and bodywork. They share customers and records.

  • Name the departments and what each earns.
  • Trace a sale: enquiry, test drive, quote, contract, payment, registration, handover.
  • Trace a service: booking, intake, inspection, quote, approval, repair, quality check, hand-back.
  • Explain why records and customer approval protect both sides.
Worked example. A small site sells 20 cars and does 160 service jobs a month. If a typical car sale gives 90,000 RUB gross and a service job 4,000 RUB, service gives 160 x 4,000 = 640,000 RUB and sales give 20 x 90,000 = 1,800,000 RUB, so service is 640,000 / 2,440,000 = 26 percent of total gross and sales are 74 percent. Over 22 working days 160 jobs is about 7 jobs a day. Check: 640,000 + 1,800,000 = 2,440,000. Both departments need customer records, so one missing signature hurts both.

Activity. Draw both flows, mark where signatures and customer approvals are needed, and compute the share of gross from service for the figures given.

Standard to meet. All steps are in order and each has a record; at least 4 signature points are marked; the service share is computed correctly.

Module 2 Sales conversation and needs analysis 4 hApply

Good selling starts with questions about use, budget and trade-in.

  • Ask about use: daily distance, passengers, parking, budget.
  • Match two or three vehicles to needs and explain the differences.
  • Run a test drive with a checklist.
  • Be honest about condition, history and limits.
Worked example. A customer drives 60 km a day, five days a week. 60 x 5 x 48 weeks = 14,400 km a year. At 8 L/100 km and fuel 55 RUB per L, fuel is 14,400 x 0.08 x 55 = 63,360 RUB a year. A second car at 5.5 L/100 km uses 14,400 x 0.055 = 792 L, costing 792 x 55 = 43,560 RUB, so it saves 63,360 - 43,560 = 19,800 RUB a year. Use such figures to compare two options honestly.

Activity. Role-play a needs interview and record the answers, then match two options and compare their yearly fuel cost with the arithmetic shown.

Standard to meet. Needs are recorded, two options matched, facts stated accurately, and the fuel comparison is correct with distance, consumption and price all written down.

Module 3 Quotes, trade-in and finance arithmetic 4 hApply

A quote must show the full price and all extras clearly.

  • List base price, options, delivery, registration and insurance separately.
  • Value a trade-in using condition and market checks, and explain the figure.
  • Compute a simple loan payment and total cost of credit.
  • Show the total to pay and the monthly amount.
Worked example. Car 1,500,000 RUB, options 80,000 RUB, trade-in 400,000 RUB: to pay 1,180,000 RUB. Loan of 800,000 RUB over 36 months with total interest 190,000 RUB: total repaid 990,000 RUB, about 27,500 RUB a month (990,000 / 36). The credit costs 190,000 RUB, 23.75 percent of the loan. The customer also pays the rest in cash: 1,180,000 - 800,000 = 380,000 RUB. Show cash, loan, interest and monthly amount on separate lines.

Activity. Prepare a quote with a trade-in and a loan scenario, listing price, options, trade-in, cash needed, total repaid and monthly payment.

Standard to meet. All lines add up; total cost of credit shown plainly in RUB; the trade-in figure has a stated basis; the customer can read the total to pay in one place.

Module 4 Service intake and diagnosis 4 hApply

The job card starts with the customer's words and a careful check of the car.

  • Record mileage, fuel level, damage and warning lights with photos.
  • Write the customer complaint in their words.
  • Follow a diagnostic path: check, test, confirm, then quote.
  • Get written approval before extra work.
Worked example. Complaint: squeal when braking. Check: front pads 2 mm (limit 3 mm). Quote: pads 3,200 RUB plus labour 1.0 h at 2,000 RUB = 5,200 RUB. Approval recorded before work. If the technician then finds worn discs needing 6,500 RUB of parts and 1.5 h of labour (3,000 RUB), that adds 9,500 RUB and the new total is 14,700 RUB, so work stops until the customer approves again in writing.

Activity. Complete two job cards from case notes, each with complaint, mileage, findings, quote, approval and a second approval for one extra item.

Standard to meet. Complaint, findings, quote and approval are all recorded; extra work has its own written approval; quote arithmetic has no error.

Module 5 Labour, parts and workshop efficiency 4 hAnalyse

Workshops earn from labour hours sold and parts margin. Efficiency measures how well time is used.

  • Efficiency = hours sold / hours worked.
  • Utilisation = hours worked / hours available.
  • Parts margin = (sell price - cost) / sell price.
  • Turnover of stock = yearly parts cost / average stock value.
Worked example. A technician is present 40 h, works 34 h on jobs and sells 38 h of flat-rate time. Utilisation = 85 percent; efficiency = 38 / 34 = 112 percent; productivity = 38 / 40 = 95 percent. For parts: sell 5,000 RUB, cost 3,500 RUB, margin = 1,500 / 5,000 = 30 percent. Stock turnover: yearly parts cost 2,400,000 RUB / average stock 600,000 RUB = 4 turns a year, so stock lasts about 3 months.

Activity. Calculate four measures from a weekly table, explain in one line what each tells a manager, and name one improvement with the figure it targets.

Standard to meet. All measures correct to the nearest percent; each is tied to a decision; one improvement is named with the figure it should change.

Module 6 Workshop safety and environment 4 hEvaluate

Workshops have lifting, fire, chemical and electrical risks, and waste that must be handled correctly.

  • Check lifts and stands before use and mark the load limit.
  • Keep fire extinguishers, spill kits and clear exits.
  • Store oils, coolants, batteries and filters for licensed collection.
  • Only trained persons work on high-voltage vehicles.
Worked example. A lift rated 3,500 kg is used for a van of 3,200 kg plus 400 kg of load. Total 3,600 kg exceeds the rating by 100 kg, so the job must not proceed on that lift. Check: the lift has 3,500 - 3,200 = 300 kg of spare capacity, but the load is 400 kg. Move the job to a lift rated higher, or remove the load first. Record the decision and tell the supervisor.

Activity. Audit a workshop with a 20-point checklist covering lifts, fire, fumes, electrics and waste, and write an action for every failed item.

Standard to meet. All failures have an action, owner and date; at least 18 of 20 points are checked with a record; no job is allowed above a rated load.

Module 7 Quality, warranty and complaints 3 hEvaluate

A final check catches errors. Warranty and complaints need records and fairness.

  • Do a road test and a final checklist with sign-off.
  • Return old parts on request and explain the work.
  • Record warranty claims with date, mileage and findings.
  • Handle complaints with facts, a clear timeline and consumer rules from the competent authority.
Worked example. Out of 160 jobs a month, 8 come back within 14 days. Comeback rate = 5 percent. Target 2 percent, which is about 3 jobs, so 5 extra jobs per month need root-cause review. Cost check: 8 free repeat jobs at 4,000 RUB is 32,000 RUB of lost work; at the target of 3 it would be 12,000 RUB, so about 20,000 RUB a month is avoidable. Trace each comeback to intake, diagnosis, parts or the final check.

Activity. Calculate comeback rate, work out the cost of repeat jobs, and write a root-cause note that traces each case to one process step.

Standard to meet. Rate and cost are correct; the cause is traced to a step; one corrective action has an owner and a date.

Module 8 Customer records and follow-up 3 hCreate

A good record helps the next visit and keeps customers.

  • Record vehicle, history, next service date and consent for contact.
  • Send a reminder at the due date.
  • Ask for feedback after a visit.
  • Protect personal data according to the relevant federal rules.
Worked example. Of 500 customers, a reminder campaign brings back 90 (18 percent). At 4,000 RUB per job, 90 jobs give 360,000 RUB of work, which is 720 RUB of work per customer contacted (360,000 / 500). Only customers who gave consent may be contacted, so check the record before sending any reminder.

Activity. Design a reminder process and a feedback form, with the consent check, the timing of each message and the measure of return rate.

Standard to meet. Process has consent, timing and a measure; the return rate is computed correctly; the form has at most 5 questions and no unneeded personal data.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What goes on a service job card?

Model answer: Complaint in the customer words, mileage, findings, quote, written approval, work done and final sign-off, each with a date and a name.

Q2. Why get approval before extra work?

Model answer: It protects the customer from surprise bills and the workshop from disputes, because the agreed price and scope are written down.

Q3. Efficiency formula?

Model answer: Hours sold divided by hours worked; for 38 hours sold and 34 worked it is 112 percent, which shows time used well.

Q4. Lift rated 3,500 kg, vehicle 3,600 kg. Use it?

Model answer: No; the total exceeds the rating by 100 kg, so use a stronger lift or reduce the load and record the decision.

Q5. Loan 800,000 RUB, interest 190,000 RUB: total repaid?

Model answer: 800,000 plus 190,000 is 990,000 RUB, about 27,500 RUB a month over 36 months, and the customer must see this total.

Q6. Who may work on high-voltage systems?

Model answer: Only trained and authorised persons with the right tools and procedure; everyone else keeps clear and does not touch orange cables.

Q7. Comeback rate meaning?

Model answer: Share of jobs returning for the same fault soon after repair, such as 8 of 160 jobs, which is 5 percent, a signal to find the root cause.

Q8. Why show total cost of credit?

Model answer: Honesty and consumer rules require clear total costs, so the customer can compare offers and is not surprised by the interest paid.

Q9. How to store used oil?

Model answer: In labelled sealed containers kept away from drains and heat, then handed to a licensed collector, with the collection recorded.

Q10. Why photograph the car at intake?

Model answer: To record existing damage, mileage and warning lights, so any later dispute about who caused a mark can be settled with facts.
