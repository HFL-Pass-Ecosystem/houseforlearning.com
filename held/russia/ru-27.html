Medical Waste Disposal

Learn how medical waste is classified, separated, packed, stored, moved and recorded in a clinic or small hospital. The course teaches the management system and records, and stresses licensing and infection safety.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner27 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn how medical waste is classified, separated, packed, stored, moved and recorded in a clinic or small hospital. The course teaches the management system and records, and stresses licensing and infection safety.

  • Understand Explain the hazard classes of medical waste and why each class is handled differently.
  • Apply Sort a list of items into the correct class and container colour system of the facility.
  • Analyse Calculate waste generation per bed or per visit and identify the biggest source for reduction.
  • Evaluate Judge a storage room, a transport route and a waste log against a checklist.
  • Create Build a waste management plan with training, spill response and a record register.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a waste classification chart, a weekly generation estimate, a storage and handover checklist, and a spill and needle-stick response card for a 20-bed clinic.

Condition

Given a list of 30 items, a clinic profile, a spreadsheet and 5 hours of working time, with a mentor to review.

Standard

At least 28 of 30 items sorted correctly, weights and containers calculated without error, every checklist item tied to a record, and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Clinic staff, cleaners, facility managers and learners who want to work safely with waste. Requirements: Basic arithmetic and a spreadsheet. No medical qualification is needed to study the system. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Medical waste can carry infection, sharps injury, chemical and radiological risk. Classification, packaging, on-site treatment, transport and final disposal are regulated by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority, and carrying or treating waste usually needs a licence. This course does not authorise anyone to transport, treat or dispose of waste. Use only licensed contractors for hazardous classes. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. After a needle-stick or exposure, seek medical help at once.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Hazard classes and why they matter 3 hUnderstand

Waste is split by risk so that low-risk waste does not become expensive hazardous waste and dangerous waste is never mixed in.

  • Name the groups in general terms: non-hazardous, infectious, highly infectious, chemical and drug, and radiological waste.
  • Explain that the national rules fix the class system; the facility must use the current version.
  • Show the cost of mixing: one infectious item in a general bin turns the whole bin into hazardous waste.
  • List who is at risk: staff, cleaners, patients, drivers, the public.
Worked example. A clinic makes 50 kg of waste per day, of which 10 percent is infectious. If all is mixed, 50 kg is treated as infectious. If sorted, only 5 kg is. At 40 RUB per kg treatment this saves 45 x 40 = 1,800 RUB per day.

Activity. Match 15 items to hazard groups and note the rule that sets each group.

Standard to meet. At least 14 of 15 correct, with the reason stated.

Module 2 Segregation at the point of use 4 hApply

The safest sorting happens where waste is made. Bins, colours and labels must be close and clear.

  • Place the right container in each room: sharps box, infectious bag, general bin.
  • Never recap needles; drop the whole device into a puncture-proof sharps box.
  • Fill containers only to the line, usually three quarters, then close.
  • Label with date, room, and waste class.
Worked example. A sharps box of 5 L is closed at 75 percent = 3.75 L. If a ward uses 6 boxes per week, a stock of 10 per week with spare is needed.

Activity. Plan bins and labels for a consulting room, a procedure room and a ward.

Standard to meet. No room lacks a sharps box and each label has date and class.

Module 3 Packaging, labelling and on-site transport 3 hApply

Safe containers stop spills and injuries. Moving waste inside a building needs set routes and times.

  • Use leak-proof, sealed, labelled bags and rigid boxes.
  • Move waste on a set route, away from patients and food, using a closed cart.
  • Wear gloves and protective clothing and wash hands afterwards.
  • Do not compress or hand-sort sealed bags.
Worked example. A cart holds 6 bags of 8 kg = 48 kg. A route of 120 m at 0.8 m/s takes 150 s one way; 4 trips per day is about 20 minutes of staff time.

Activity. Design a route and timetable for a 20-bed clinic.

Standard to meet. Route avoids waiting areas and kitchens and times fit the shift.

Module 4 Storage and handover 4 hEvaluate

Waste is stored briefly in a locked, cool, cleanable room. Handover to a licensed contractor is recorded.

  • Store in a room with a washable floor, ventilation, a lock and a warning sign.
  • Keep storage within the maximum time and temperature set by the rules and the competent authority.
  • Check that the contractor holds a valid licence before first handover.
  • Sign a handover record each time: date, class, weight, container count, names.
Worked example. Weekly handover: 140 kg infectious in 20 boxes of 7 kg. A log entry records 140 kg, 20 boxes, driver, vehicle and licence number. Any later mismatch of more than 5 percent triggers a check.

Activity. Audit a storage room with a 15-point checklist.

Standard to meet. Every point is scored with evidence and failures have actions and dates.

Module 5 Waste quantity and cost 3 hAnalyse

You cannot manage what you do not measure. Weekly weights show where to cut waste.

  • Weigh each class weekly with a calibrated scale.
  • Compute kg per bed-day or kg per patient visit.
  • Compare classes and find the largest source.
  • Estimate savings from better sorting and fewer disposables.
Worked example. 20 beds, 75 percent occupancy = 15 bed-days per day. Waste 45 kg per day gives 3.0 kg per bed-day. Cutting infectious share from 25 to 15 percent saves 4.5 kg per day, 1,642 kg per year.

Activity. Work out kg per bed-day and a reduction target.

Standard to meet. Arithmetic is correct and the source of waste named.

Module 6 Infection control and injury response 3 hApply

A needle-stick or spill needs an immediate, practised response.

  • Wash the wound with soap and water, do not squeeze, report at once.
  • For a spill: restrict the area, wear protection, absorb, disinfect, bag, record.
  • Keep post-exposure contacts and steps on a laminated card.
  • Train and vaccinate staff as medical advice and the rules require.
Worked example. A 100 mL blood spill needs about 500 mL of disinfectant at the label strength and a contact time stated on the label, typically 10 minutes. Record time, person and area.

Activity. Write a one-page response card and test it in a drill.

Standard to meet. Drill completed in under 5 minutes and card reviewed by a clinician.

Module 7 Treatment, disposal and the licensed chain 3 hUnderstand

Different classes go to different treatment: disinfection, incineration or special handling. Each step needs licensed operators.

  • Explain the options in general terms: steam sterilisation, chemical disinfection, incineration.
  • Explain why on-site treatment equipment needs approval and monitoring.
  • Keep copies of the contractor licence and disposal certificates.
  • Never burn, bury or dump waste.
Worked example. A contractor collects 600 kg per month. Certificates must match: 600 kg collected equals 600 kg received. A shortfall of 30 kg (5 percent) must be explained in writing.

Activity. Build a chain-of-custody diagram from the bin to final disposal.

Standard to meet. Every step has a responsible party and a record.

Module 8 Management plan, training and audit 4 hCreate

A plan makes safe waste handling routine. It names owners, rules, training and checks.

  • Name a responsible person and a deputy.
  • Write procedures in plain words and train every new staff member before first duty.
  • Audit monthly and report results to management.
  • Review after any incident and update the plan.
Worked example. Training 24 staff for 1.5 hours each is 36 staff hours. At 300 RUB per hour internal cost, this is 10,800 RUB a year, small beside one injury claim.

Activity. Draft the plan with roles, training schedule and audit calendar.

Standard to meet. Plan names owners, dates and records and passes mentor review.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: classification chart, generation estimate, storage checklist, handover log, response card and training record. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why sort waste where it is made?

Model answer: It is safest, cheapest and avoids turning general waste into hazardous waste.

Q2. How full may a sharps box be?

Model answer: Up to the fill line, usually about three quarters, then close it.

Q3. Should needles be recapped?

Model answer: No; drop the whole device in a puncture-proof box.

Q4. What does a handover record contain?

Model answer: Date, class, weight, containers, names and licence details.

Q5. Who may transport hazardous medical waste?

Model answer: Only a licensed contractor, as set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority; clinic staff and learners do not carry it off site.

Q6. A clinic makes 3 kg waste per bed-day with 15 bed-days a day. Daily total?

Model answer: 3 x 15 = 45 kg per day, which is 315 kg per week; this is the baseline against which any reduction target is set.

Q7. First action after a needle-stick?

Model answer: Wash the wound with soap and water without squeezing, report at once, record the time and seek medical help using the post-exposure card.

Q8. May waste be burned on site informally?

Model answer: No; treatment needs approved equipment, monitoring and licences, and burning, burying or dumping waste is never allowed.

Q9. Why weigh waste weekly?

Model answer: To track trends and costs, find the largest source and show whether sorting changes actually reduce volume, for example kg per bed-day.

Q10. What shows a contractor is legitimate?

Model answer: A valid licence, checked and filed before first handover, plus disposal certificates whose weights match the handover log.
