HELD · RU-17

Art

Learn to plan, make, document, price and present original artworks as a working artist, with honest records and fair dealing with buyers and galleries.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner30 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to plan, make, document, price and present original artworks as a working artist, with honest records and fair dealing with buyers and galleries.

  • Understand Explain how medium, surface, composition and colour work together to carry meaning in a finished work.
  • Apply Produce a coherent series of six works from sketches, studies and a written concept.
  • Analyse Compare the cost of materials, time and overhead to set a defensible price for each work.
  • Evaluate Judge your own work and a peer's work against stated criteria and give usable feedback.
  • Create Build a portfolio, a condition-and-provenance record and an exhibition plan for a small show.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Make a series of six related works and prepare them for a small public show.

Condition

Over 8 weeks, using your own studio space, chosen medium, a camera or phone, a tape measure and a spreadsheet.

Standard

Six finished works, each with a one-page record (title, size in cm, medium, date, price in RUB, condition); photos at 3000 px on the long side; pricing sheet that adds up; pass mark 8 of 10 on the check and no rubric row at Not yet.

Who it is for: Self-taught or trained makers who want to sell or exhibit work in a structured way. Requirements: Basic drawing or painting skill in one medium; space and materials for it; a phone camera. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Art materials can be hazardous: solvents, pigments with heavy metals, resins, dust and kilns need ventilation, labelled storage and protective gear; follow the maker's data sheet. Export or sale of works that may count as cultural property can need permission from the competent Russian authority, and tax treatment of sales differs by status. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before selling or shipping abroad. No course can promise sales or fame.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Looking and concept 4 hUnderstand

Work begins with a question you can state. A concept in two sentences guides every later choice of size, colour and surface. Test the concept by asking whether a stranger could guess it from the title and one sketch; if not, narrow it before you buy materials.

  • Describe a work by its elements: line, shape, tone, colour, texture, space.
  • Write a concept in two sentences: subject plus the feeling or idea you want the viewer to reach.
  • Collect 10 references and note what you take from each (not copy).
  • Set limits for a series: one palette of 5 colours, one format, one theme.
  • Keep a dated sketchbook; it is your first record of authorship.
Worked example. A series on winter light: concept 'cold morning light on old brick'; palette of 5 pigments; format 40 x 50 cm; 12 thumbnail sketches of 6 x 8 cm done in 2 hours, of which 6 are chosen. Check: 12 thumbnails in 2 hours is 10 minutes each; choosing 6 of 12 keeps half, so the other half are dropped without regret.

Activity. Write your concept, palette and format, and draw 12 thumbnails. Time each thumbnail at about 10 minutes and mark the six you would develop.

Standard to meet. Concept in two sentences; palette of no more than 5 colours; 6 thumbnails chosen with a reason each. The total sketching time is under 2.5 hours.

Module 2 Materials and surfaces 4 hApply

The surface and the medium decide how a work ages. Choose them for permanence, not only for look. A cheap surface that yellows or cracks in five years damages your name and the buyer's trust, so spend on the support first and on the brush second.

  • Compare supports: canvas, board, paper, panel; note weight, absorbency and archival quality.
  • Match media to supports: oil on primed canvas, watercolour on 300 g/m2 paper, acrylic on board.
  • Prepare a surface correctly: size, prime, dry for the time the maker states.
  • Store pigments and solvents closed, labelled, away from heat.
  • Test colour mixes on a swatch card before the final work.
Worked example. A 50 x 70 cm canvas needs about 0.35 m2 of priming. Two coats at 120 mL per m2 give 84 mL; at 900 RUB per litre that is about 76 RUB of primer. Check: 0.5 x 0.7 = 0.35; 0.35 x 2 x 120 = 84 mL = 0.084 L; 0.084 x 900 = 75.6 RUB. Six works of this size need about 454 RUB of primer.

Activity. Prepare two surfaces and one swatch card of your palette. Note size, coats, drying time and primer cost for each surface.

Standard to meet. Surfaces primed evenly and dried to the stated time; swatch card labelled with each mix. Primer cost per surface is calculated within 5 RUB.

Module 3 Composition and colour 4 hAnalyse

A strong picture is organised. Composition guides the eye; colour sets mood and depth. Decide the value pattern before the colours, since a picture that works in three tones will survive almost any palette.

  • Test a design with thumbnails: rule of thirds, diagonals, centre weight, edge tension.
  • Plan a tonal map in three values: light, mid, dark, before adding colour.
  • Use warm and cool contrast to push forms forward or back.
  • Check a work in black and white on a phone to see if the value structure holds.
  • Judge balance by turning the work upside down.
Worked example. On a 40 x 50 cm work the focal point sits at 2/3 across: 26.7 cm from the left edge. A dark mass takes about 20 percent of the area, mid tones 50 percent, lights 30 percent. Check: 20 + 50 + 30 = 100 percent. On the 2,000 cm2 surface (40 x 50) the dark mass covers 400 cm2, mid tones 1,000 cm2 and lights 600 cm2.

Activity. Analyse three of your thumbnails with a value map and choose the strongest. Use squares of 10 x 10 cm so the percentages can be counted.

Standard to meet. Value map for each; choice justified in two sentences. The three values add to 100 percent and the focal point position is given in cm.

Module 4 Making the series 5 hApply

Six works in a row teach more than one perfect work. Plan time, make in stages and review. Keep the series moving by working on two or three pieces at once, so that one can dry while another is developed.

  • Break a work into stages: underlayer, blocking in, development, details, finish.
  • Schedule: for example 6 works at 12 hours each is 72 hours; at 9 hours a week that is 8 weeks.
  • Review each work after a day away and note one change.
  • Keep drying and varnishing times in the plan.
  • Stop when the concept is clear, not when every area is busy.
Worked example. Plan: 72 hours of making plus 8 hours for varnish, photos and records, 80 hours in total. At 10 hours a week the series takes 8 weeks. Check: 72 + 8 = 80 hours; 80 / 10 = 8 weeks. At 9 hours a week the same series needs 80 / 9 = 8.9 weeks, so allow nine.

Activity. Finish the six works with a stage log for each. Record hours per stage and any change from the plan.

Standard to meet. Six works complete; stage log dated; each work reviewed once. Total logged hours are within 10 percent of the 80 hour plan (72 to 88).

Module 5 Records, condition and provenance 3 hApply

A buyer trusts a work with a clear record. Records also protect you if a work is lost or disputed. Records are cheap to make while the work is fresh and almost impossible to rebuild years later, so make them the day the work is finished.

  • Make a one-page record: title, year, size in cm, medium, support, edition (if any), price in RUB, location.
  • Photograph the whole work, a detail and the signature, on a grey background with even light.
  • Note the condition at sale and give care advice: keep out of direct sun, humidity 40 to 60 percent.
  • Keep copies of invoices and agreements.
  • Number your works so each can be traced.
Worked example. Record numbering AB-2026-03 means artist code AB, year 2026, third work. A 50 x 70 cm oil on canvas priced at 54,000 RUB has a record, 3 photos at 3000 px and a care card. Check: numbering is unique because the final number counts up; six works get AB-2026-01 to AB-2026-06. Three photos of six works means 18 images to name and file.

Activity. Complete records and photos for all six works. Name each photo file with the record number.

Standard to meet. Each record has all fields; photos sharp and square on; numbering is unique. All 18 photos are filed and each file name matches its record.

Module 6 Pricing 3 hAnalyse

A price should cover costs and time and match the market for your level. Guesswork leads to loss or unsold work. Raise the price slowly and only when work sells quickly, and never undercut your own earlier price, which upsets earlier buyers.

  • Add materials, framing, your hours at a fair hourly rate, and overhead.
  • Compare with prices of similar work by artists at your stage.
  • Use a price per cm2 only as a check, not a rule.
  • Keep one price list; do not change it between buyers.
  • Account for gallery commission when selling through one.
Worked example. Materials 3,500 RUB + frame 4,000 RUB + 12 hours at 600 RUB = 7,200 RUB + overhead 10 percent. Subtotal 14,700 RUB, plus 1,470 RUB gives 16,170 RUB; with a 40 percent gallery share the retail price is 26,950 RUB. Check: 3,500 + 4,000 = 7,500; 12 x 600 = 7,200; 7,500 + 7,200 = 14,700; 14,700 x 0.1 = 1,470; 14,700 + 1,470 = 16,170; 16,170 / 0.6 = 26,950. The gallery keeps 40 percent, 10,780 RUB, and you receive 16,170 RUB.

Activity. Build a price sheet for all six works with the working shown. Include the retail price if a gallery takes 40 percent.

Standard to meet. Sheet adds up; comparison source stated; commission handled. Each retail price equals cost divided by 0.6 where a gallery is used.

Module 7 Presenting and selling 4 hCreate

Presentation turns a pile of work into a show. A clear statement and fair terms make a sale easier. Say the same thing in the statement, the labels and the sale agreement, so a visitor sees one confident voice.

  • Write an artist statement of 120 words: what, how, why.
  • Plan a hang: eye level centre at about 150 cm, 8 to 10 cm between frames.
  • Draft a simple sale agreement: price, payment, delivery, return terms.
  • Handle sales taxes and registration as the competent Russian authority requires.
  • Ship safely: corner protection, rigid box, insurance for value.
Worked example. A wall of 4 m fits three 50 cm works with gaps of 25 cm: 3 x 50 + 2 x 25 = 200 cm, centred with 100 cm free each side. Check: 3 x 50 = 150, 2 x 25 = 50, 150 + 50 = 200; 400 - 200 = 200, and 200 / 2 = 100 cm.

Activity. Write your statement, hang plan and agreement. Draw a scale plan on 1 to 20 paper for a 4 m wall.

Standard to meet. Statement is 100 to 140 words; plan to scale; agreement has price, payment and delivery. Every gap on the plan is measured in cm.

Module 8 Review and next steps 3 hEvaluate

A mature practice reviews itself. Honest critique and a plan keep the work moving. Ask the same five questions of every series, so you can compare one project with the next and see real progress.

  • Use criteria: concept, craft, composition, originality, finish.
  • Give peer feedback that is specific, kind and testable.
  • Compare time spent with income to see whether the practice is sustainable.
  • Set three goals for the next 6 months.
  • Keep a list of venues and calls for work.
Worked example. Series earned 53,900 RUB from 2 sales at 26,950 RUB against 80 hours and 11,000 RUB of costs: after costs 42,900 RUB, about 536 RUB per hour. Check: 2 x 26,950 = 53,900; 53,900 - 11,000 = 42,900; 42,900 / 80 = 536.25. This is before tax and without any unsold works.

Activity. Hold one peer critique and write a six-month plan. Include a table of hours, costs and income for the series.

Standard to meet. Critique uses the five criteria; plan has three dated goals. Hourly income is computed from hours and costs.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: sketchbook pages, six finished works with records, a pricing sheet, a photo set, an artist statement and an exhibition plan. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why write a concept before painting?

Model answer: It guides choices of size, colour and surface and keeps a series coherent. It also lets you explain the work to a buyer in two sentences.

Q2. Name three elements used to describe a work.

Model answer: Line, shape, tone, colour, texture or space. Naming them lets you compare works and give feedback that is specific.

Q3. Why test a work in black and white?

Model answer: To see whether the value structure works without colour. If the picture looks flat in grey, the light and dark masses need to be reorganised.

Q4. What must a record of a work include?

Model answer: Title, year, size, medium, support, price and location, with photos. Condition and care advice should be added at the time of sale.

Q5. Which factors go into a price?

Model answer: Materials, framing, hours at a fair rate, overhead and any commission. Add them line by line so the price can be explained to a buyer.

Q6. Calculate: 12 hours at 600 RUB plus 7,500 RUB materials.

Model answer: 7,200 + 7,500 = 14,700 RUB before overhead. Adding 10 percent overhead (1,470 RUB) gives 16,170 RUB.

Q7. Why keep a dated sketchbook?

Model answer: It records authorship and development. Dated pages help if ownership is questioned and show how a series grew.

Q8. How should solvents be stored?

Model answer: Closed, labelled, away from heat, with ventilation. Follow the maker's data sheet and keep them out of reach of children.

Q9. What is a safe humidity range for storing works?

Model answer: About 40 to 60 percent. Avoid direct sun and damp walls, which cause cracking, mould and fading.

Q10. Who should you consult before exporting a work?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Export of cultural property may need permission, so ask before you ship.
