HELD · RU-33

Salvaging

Learn to recover, grade, clean, test, price and resell used goods and materials safely and lawfully, and to keep the records that show where every item came from and where it went.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to recover, grade, clean, test, price and resell used goods and materials safely and lawfully, and to keep the records that show where every item came from and where it went.

  • Understand Explain the salvage chain from sourcing to resale and the legal and safety limits on each step.
  • Apply Grade a mixed lot by condition and material using a written grading key and weigh it with a calibrated scale.
  • Analyse Calculate landed cost, recovery rate and margin per kilogram or per item for a lot.
  • Evaluate Judge whether a lot is worth taking, needs a licensed handler, or must be refused.
  • Create Design a small salvage workflow with intake, sorting, storage, sales and a record book.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Take one mixed lot (real or written case of 200 kg to 500 kg), grade it, cost it, plan its disposal and resale routes, and complete the intake and sales records.

Condition

Open-book; 5 hours; gloves, scale, notebook, calculator and a grading key; hazardous items are identified on paper only, never handled.

Standard

Lot graded into at least 5 classes; weights sum to the intake weight within 2 percent; margin calculated to the ruble; every hazardous item routed to a licensed handler; pass mark 80 percent.

Who it is for: People who want to start or improve a small business recovering and reselling used goods, furniture, textiles, scrap metal, electronics parts or building materials. Requirements: Adult learner; ability to lift safely and use a calculator. A lawful place to store goods is needed. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Salvage can involve waste law, scrap-metal and secondhand-trade rules, stolen-goods risk, asbestos, batteries, refrigerants, electrical hazards and sharp or heavy items. Handling, storing and transporting waste may need a licence or registration; trade in some metals and goods is restricted or recorded. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser before you start. Never take goods without the owner's written permission, and never open or burn items of unknown content.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The salvage chain and the law 4 hUnderstand

Salvage turns discarded goods into value through a chain of lawful steps. Each step has a rule about ownership, waste status and records.

  • Map the chain: source, permission, collection, intake, sorting, cleaning or repair, storage, sale, remainder to disposal.
  • Ask who owns the goods and whether they are legally waste. Waste may need a licensed carrier and a transfer record under the relevant federal rules.
  • Never accept goods you suspect are stolen. Ask for a written release, keep copies of identity and note the date and vehicle.
  • Keep a record book: date, source, description, weight, price paid or free, and where each item went.
Worked example. A shop gives 300 kg of used shelving, steel and wood. You note: 180 kg steel, 90 kg wood, 30 kg plastic. Total 300 kg matches the weighbridge slip. The release letter names the shop and the date.

Activity. Draw the salvage chain for one product and mark the three points where a licence, permit or record may be needed.

Standard to meet. Chain shows all nine steps; three legal control points named; sample record line complete.

Module 2 Sourcing and permission 4 hApply

Good sources are steady, clean and legal. A written agreement saves arguments later.

  • Source types: shops closing, offices refitting, households clearing, building sites, repair shops, event organisers.
  • Agree in writing: what you take, when, who loads, who pays for transport, and what happens to leftovers.
  • Visit before accepting. Take photographs and estimate volume in cubic metres and weight in kilograms.
  • Say no to unsafe lots: unknown liquids, medical waste, broken glass heaps, wet insulation, items with damaged asbestos-like boards.
Worked example. An office clears 40 chairs and 12 desks. Estimated 40 x 6 kg + 12 x 25 kg = 540 kg and about 9 cubic metres. A 3-tonne van with 12 cubic metres needs one trip.

Activity. Write a one-page sourcing agreement and a site-visit checklist, and estimate weight and volume for a case.

Standard to meet. Agreement covers 5 items; estimate within 15 percent of the key.

Module 3 Safe handling and hazards 5 hEvaluate

Most salvage injuries and legal problems come from hazards that were not noticed in time.

  • Wear gloves, safety shoes and eye protection. Lift with a straight back; share loads above 20 kg.
  • Spot the hazards: batteries and capacitors, refrigerant circuits, mercury lamps, sealed cans, broken glass, mould, old paint, board or roofing that may contain asbestos.
  • Do not cut, drill or burn suspect material. Stop, isolate, photograph and call a licensed handler.
  • Keep a hazard routing sheet: item, hazard, action, who takes it, date.
Worked example. Lot of 20 old fridges. Each has a refrigerant circuit and compressor oil. They cannot be cut. Action: keep upright, intact, send 20 units to a licensed recycler. Resale of working units after test: 4 of 20.

Activity. Classify 15 items on a picture sheet as take, take with limits, or refuse. Explain 5 choices.

Standard to meet. At least 14 of 15 correct; every refusal names the safer route.

Module 4 Grading and sorting 4 hApply

A clear grading key makes value visible. Sort by material, condition and test result.

  • Use grades A (resell as is), B (clean or repair), C (parts), D (material recycling), E (disposal).
  • Weigh each class on a scale checked with a known 10 kg weight. Record to 0.1 kg.
  • Keep similar items together and label bins. Mixed bins lose value because buyers want a clean grade.
  • Check totals: class weights plus loss must equal intake weight within 2 percent.
Worked example. Intake 400.0 kg. A 60.0, B 120.0, C 90.0, D 110.0, E 14.0 = 394.0 kg. Difference 6.0 kg = 1.5 percent, within the 2 percent limit.

Activity. Grade a lot of 30 written items and balance the weights.

Standard to meet. Totals within 2 percent; each item has one grade and one reason.

Module 5 Cleaning, testing and repair 5 hApply

Light work can raise value a lot, but only where it is safe and legal. Electrical items need testing, not guessing.

  • Set a limit on time per item. If repair time times your hourly value is more than the gain, move the item to parts.
  • Clean safely with mild detergents. Never mix cleaners. Ventilate the space.
  • Electrical goods: visual check of cable and plug, insulation test by a competent person, and mark tested items. Sell untested ones as parts only.
  • Give honest descriptions: working, tested, parts only. False claims cause returns and disputes.
Worked example. A chair costs 0 RUB. Repair needs a 40 RUB screw set and 20 minutes. Valued at 500 RUB per hour, labour is 167 RUB. Sale 900 RUB. Gain = 900 - 40 - 167 = 693 RUB.

Activity. Calculate the repair-or-parts decision for 6 items using a table.

Standard to meet. Each decision shows time, material, labour value and gain; 5 of 6 correct.

Module 6 Pricing, channels and sales 5 hAnalyse

Price from cost, condition and what buyers pay. Match each grade to the right channel.

  • Channels: direct to customers, local shop or market stall, online listing, trade buyers by weight, and recyclers for material grades.
  • Cost = purchase + transport + labour + storage + fees. Set a floor price at cost plus the margin you need.
  • List accurately: photographs, size in centimetres, weight, defects, honest grade.
  • Track sell-through: items sold divided by items listed, and days in stock.
Worked example. Steel scrap grade D: 110 kg at 18 RUB per kg = 1,980 RUB. Transport 400 RUB, labour 2 hours at 300 RUB = 600 RUB. Net = 1,980 - 400 - 600 = 980 RUB.

Activity. Price 10 items and choose a channel for each with a reason.

Standard to meet. Floor price shown for each item; channel fits grade; net margin calculated correctly.

Module 7 Records, storage and compliance 4 hEvaluate

Records protect you in a dispute and prove goods were lawful. Storage rules protect people and goods.

  • Keep an intake record, a sales record and a waste transfer record with signatures and dates.
  • Store dry, off the floor, with aisles at least 1 metre wide and a clear exit. Keep flammables away and have an extinguisher.
  • Check tax and registration needs for sales and trade. Ask the competent authority and an adviser.
  • Review monthly: stock age, space used, losses, incidents and complaints.
Worked example. Monthly review: 2,000 kg in, 1,740 kg sold or recycled, 120 kg disposed, 140 kg still in stock; 2,000 = 1,740 + 120 + 140. Stock over 90 days: 35 kg, marked for price cut.

Activity. Complete a mock month of records for 12 lines and find the 2 planted errors.

Standard to meet. Both errors found; stock reconciles; compliance checklist complete.

Module 8 Design a salvage workflow 4 hCreate

Bring all the steps into a small workflow another person could follow.

  • Draw the floor plan with zones: intake, hazards hold, sorting, repair, storage, dispatch.
  • Write standard steps for intake, with a refuse list, weighing, labelling and photographing.
  • Set weekly targets: kilograms in, kilograms sold, recovery rate (sold divided by in), and incidents (aim for zero).
  • Set a review rule: stop a source if more than 20 percent of its weight is grade E.
Worked example. Weekly target: 600 kg in, 480 kg sold = recovery rate 80 percent. Source X sent 200 kg of which 50 kg was grade E = 25 percent, above 20 percent, so review the source.

Activity. Produce the floor plan, the intake procedure, the record sheet and the weekly target sheet.

Standard to meet. All four documents complete; recovery rate and source review rule worked correctly.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: sourcing agreement, intake record, grading sheet, hazard routing sheet, costing sheet, sales ledger and a workflow diagram. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why check who owns goods before you take them?

Model answer: To avoid handling stolen goods and to know whether the goods are legally waste.

Q2. What must be done with refrigerators before recycling?

Model answer: Keep them intact and send them to a licensed recycler; the refrigerant must not be released.

Q3. How do you check a grading balance?

Model answer: Class weights plus loss must equal intake within 2 percent.

Q4. A repair gains 693 RUB; another costs 800 RUB in time for 600 RUB sale. Which do you do?

Model answer: Do the first. The second loses money, so sell it for parts.

Q5. Name three hazards you must not cut or burn.

Model answer: Suspect asbestos board, sealed cans or pressurised items, batteries and fridge circuits.

Q6. What is recovery rate?

Model answer: Sold or recycled weight divided by weight in, as a percent.

Q7. Why photograph at intake?

Model answer: To prove condition and quantity, and to support the listing and any dispute.

Q8. What is the minimum clear aisle width in storage in this course?

Model answer: At least 1 metre.

Q9. How do you price an item?

Model answer: Add all costs, add the margin you need to set a floor, then compare with what buyers pay.

Q10. When do you refuse a lot?

Model answer: When it is unsafe, unlawful, of unknown origin or needs a licence you do not hold.
