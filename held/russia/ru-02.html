HELD · RU-02

Farming

Plan and manage a small crop and livestock farm as a business: land and season planning, input and yield calculations, animal and crop care, records and cash flow. Focuses on sound practice and legal basics.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Plan and manage a small crop and livestock farm as a business: land and season planning, input and yield calculations, animal and crop care, records and cash flow. Focuses on sound practice and legal basics.

  • Understand Explain the farm year and how soil, water, weather and labour shape what can be grown or kept.
  • Apply Calculate seed, fertiliser and feed quantities and a gross margin per hectare or per animal.
  • Analyse Compare crop and livestock options by cost, risk, labour and cash timing.
  • Evaluate Judge a farm record set for gaps in soil, input, health and sales information.
  • Create Build a one-year farm plan with a calendar, input budget, record system and cash plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a farm plan for one holding with a season calendar, input budget, gross margin and record system.

Condition

Individual work over 6 weeks with a calculator, spreadsheet, a plot map (sketch is enough) and sample local prices and yields.

Standard

Input quantities correct to within 5 percent; gross margins reconcile to the budget; record system covers inputs, health, harvest and sales; 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Smallholders, family farm members, farm workers, cooperative members and people planning to start farming. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. No prior farming experience is needed. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Farming is subject to rules on land use and tenure, plant protection products, fertilisers, animal identification and health, veterinary control, food safety and the environment. This course gives no legal advice. Confirm which permits, registrations and rules apply to your land and activity with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Machinery, pesticides, animals and heights cause serious injury; use trained operators and protective gear, and follow the label on every product. Yields and prices vary with weather; no income is promised.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The farm as a system 4 hUnderstand

A farm joins land, water, plants, animals, labour and money. A change in one part moves the others.

  • List the resources: land area, soil, water, labour days, equipment, buildings, cash.
  • Draw the flows: manure to field, feed from field, grain to market, cash back to inputs.
  • Name the main risks: weather, pests, disease, price, labour, access to market.
  • Set farm goals in measures: tonnes, litres, margin per hectare.
Worked example. A 5-hectare holding has 3 ha for grain, 1 ha for vegetables and 1 ha for fodder, keeping 4 cows. Fodder of 1 ha at 6 tonnes/ha dry matter feeds only part of the herd, so purchased feed is needed. Check: 4 cows x 18 kg x 200 days = 14,400 kg need; 6,000 kg grown covers about 42 percent; the 8,400 kg gap at 12 RUB/kg costs 100,800 RUB.

Activity. Draw the system diagram for a real or model farm and list three risks with responses.

Standard to meet. Diagram has all resources and flows; risks have a named response.

Module 2 Soil, water and climate 4 hUnderstand

Soil type, acidity, organic matter and water supply decide crop choice and input need. Short growing seasons demand early-maturing varieties.

  • Read a soil test: pH, organic matter, major nutrients; know what the numbers mean for the crop.
  • Describe how drainage, slope and frost pockets change plot use.
  • Plan water: source, amount per hectare, timing and storage.
  • Use local weather records to find the last frost, first frost and growing days.
Worked example. A soil test shows pH 5.2 for a crop needing 6.0 to 6.5. Liming at the rate advised by the test lab corrects acidity; a 5 ha field at 3 tonnes/ha needs 15 tonnes of lime.

Activity. Summarise a soil test and give a plot plan for two crops.

Standard to meet. Test values are read correctly and the plan matches the climate.

Module 3 Crop planning and rotation 5 hApply

A rotation breaks pest and disease cycles and spreads workload. Seed and fertiliser rates come from target yield and local advice.

  • Design a 4-year rotation including a legume or fodder break.
  • Calculate seed: area x seed rate (kg/ha); allow for germination.
  • Calculate nutrient need from target yield and soil supply.
  • Set sowing and harvest windows and match them to labour.
Worked example. Wheat at 200 kg/ha seed on 3 ha needs 600 kg. At 35 RUB/kg seed cost is 21,000 RUB. Target yield 3.5 t/ha gives 10.5 t; at 15,000 RUB/t revenue is 157,500 RUB.

Activity. Write a 4-year rotation and a seed and fertiliser list for year 1.

Standard to meet. Quantities are within 5 percent of the mentor key.

Module 4 Plant health and safe use of inputs 4 hEvaluate

Scouting, resistant varieties and rotation come before chemical control. Any plant protection product must be approved and used exactly as labelled.

  • Scout weekly and record pest and disease counts against action levels.
  • Read labels: dose, safe interval before harvest, protective gear, storage and disposal.
  • Store products locked, away from food and children, with a spill plan.
  • Keep a spray record: date, product, dose, area, weather, operator.
Worked example. A label gives 2 L/ha for 4 ha: 8 L total; a 2 L over-dose costs product, risks residues and breaks the label. The safe interval of 21 days before harvest fixes the last spray date.

Activity. Prepare a spray record and a storage checklist.

Standard to meet. Record has all fields; checklist matches the label rules.

Module 5 Livestock care and health 5 hApply

Animal welfare, feed, housing and health plans drive output. Disease control depends on prevention and veterinary guidance.

  • Plan feed by animal group using dry matter need and ration balance.
  • Provide shelter, clean water and space suited to the species and season.
  • Keep identification, movement and treatment records and follow veterinary instructions and withdrawal periods.
  • Report notifiable disease signs to the veterinary authority at once.
Worked example. A dairy cow eating 18 kg dry matter/day needs 18 x 200 = 3,600 kg over 200 winter days. For 4 cows that is 14.4 tonnes; at 12 RUB/kg this is 172,800 RUB of feed.

Activity. Calculate winter feed for a herd and prepare a health record template.

Standard to meet. Feed total within 5 percent; record has ID, treatment, dose and withdrawal date.

Module 6 Costs, margins and marketing 5 hAnalyse

Gross margin = output value minus variable costs. It lets you rank enterprises before you count fixed costs.

  • List variable costs: seed, fertiliser, feed, vet, fuel, hired labour.
  • Compute margin per hectare or per animal and compare options.
  • Choose sales routes: direct, cooperative, processor, market; compare price, volume and payment time.
  • Check break-even yield or price.
Worked example. Potatoes: yield 20 t/ha x 14,000 RUB/t = 280,000 RUB. Variable costs 190,000 RUB/ha. Margin 90,000 RUB/ha. Break-even yield = 190,000 / 14,000 = 13.6 t/ha.

Activity. Compute margins for three enterprises and rank them.

Standard to meet. Margins within 3 percent; ranking has stated reasons.

Module 7 Records, land and rules 4 hEvaluate

Good records support management, credit, inspection and any lawful support or subsidy a farm applies for.

  • Keep field, input, animal, harvest, sales and cost records in a single place.
  • Know the types of paper that matter: land tenure, registration, product and animal documents.
  • Check any support scheme rules and deadlines with the competent authority before relying on them.
  • Protect people: equipment guards, rest times, first-aid kit.
Worked example. A farm sells 10 t of grain at 15,000 RUB/t = 150,000 RUB. Missing weight tickets would leave 150,000 RUB of income undocumented, which is an avoidable audit and credit problem.

Activity. Audit a sample record set and list five gaps.

Standard to meet. At least five true gaps found, each with a fix.

Module 8 Whole-farm plan and cash 4 hCreate

The plan joins crops, animals, labour and cash with a calendar.

  • Build a month-by-month cash plan; mark when inputs are paid and when sales arrive.
  • Plan labour days per month and find peaks.
  • Prepare for a bad year: reserve, insurance where available and lawful, flexible costs.
  • Set a review after harvest and after winter.
Worked example. Input spend 300,000 RUB in April; sales 520,000 RUB in October. The gap needs 300,000 RUB of working capital for 6 months; at an interest rate of 15 percent per year the cost is about 22,500 RUB.

Activity. Present a one-year farm plan.

Standard to meet. Plan reconciles calendar, budget and cash with a stated reserve.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is gross margin?

Model answer: Output value minus variable costs.

Q2. How do you find the seed needed?

Model answer: Area times seed rate, adjusted for germination.

Q3. Why use a rotation?

Model answer: It breaks pest and disease cycles, spreads workload across the year, and balances nutrient use because legumes and fodder crops restore what cereals and roots remove from the soil.

Q4. What must you read on a plant protection label?

Model answer: Dose, safe interval before harvest, protective gear, storage and disposal.

Q5. What is a withdrawal period?

Model answer: The set number of days after a veterinary treatment before milk, meat or eggs from that animal may be sold or eaten, as stated on the product label.

Q6. Who do you tell about a suspected notifiable animal disease?

Model answer: The veterinary authority, at once.

Q7. What does a soil pH of 5.2 tell you for a crop needing 6.0?

Model answer: The soil is too acid and liming is likely needed, as advised by the test.

Q8. Why compute break-even yield?

Model answer: To see the lowest yield at which variable costs are covered at the expected price, so you can judge how much a poor season or a price fall can hurt.

Q9. Why keep spray and treatment records?

Model answer: For safety, traceability, inspection and good management.

Q10. Who confirms support scheme and permit rules?

Model answer: The competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

jobs this leads to

Where this takes you

  • Farm worker. Carries out field and animal tasks under instruction with safe practice.
  • Smallholder. Runs a family farm with a plan and records.
  • Farm records assistant. Keeps input, health and sales records for a farm or cooperative.
  • Cooperative coordinator. Pools produce and organises sales and shared equipment.
  • Farm advisor assistant. Supports advisers with soil, crop and herd data.
