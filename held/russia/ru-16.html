Photography and Videography

Plan, shoot, edit, deliver and price photo and video work: exposure, composition, light, sound, file handling, consent and client agreements.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner28 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Plan, shoot, edit, deliver and price photo and video work: exposure, composition, light, sound, file handling, consent and client agreements.

  • Understand Explain exposure, depth of field and frame rate and how each changes an image.
  • Apply Set exposure and compose shots for portraits, products and short video scenes.
  • Analyse Compare shots and edits and identify why one communicates better.
  • Evaluate Judge a shoot against a brief and check technical quality.
  • Create Build a shot list, edit workflow, client agreement and price quote for a small job.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and price a half-day product and portrait shoot with a short video clip, covering settings, shot list, edit time, file backup and a client agreement.

Condition

Desk exercise with sample brief, rate table and spreadsheet; 4 hours; shooting practice is encouraged but not required for assessment.

Standard

Settings reasoned for each shot type, edit time and price correct to 5 percent, backup plan with 3 copies, consent and usage terms stated, and at least 90 percent of rubric points.

Who it is for: Beginners turning a hobby into paid work, small shop owners making their own content and assistants to working photographers. Requirements: Access to any camera or phone is helpful; basic arithmetic. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Photography involves law and safety. Image rights, consent, privacy, work in public and private places, copyright and use of drones are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority; drone flying may need registration and permission. Do not photograph people where privacy is expected or children without proper consent. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser. Use safe practice with lights, cables, heights and vehicles.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Light and exposure 4 hUnderstand

A photograph is light captured on a sensor. Aperture, shutter speed and ISO balance exposure and decide look.

  • Aperture controls light and depth of field.
  • Shutter speed controls light and motion blur.
  • ISO raises brightness but adds noise.
  • One stop up or down doubles or halves the light.
Worked example. Handheld at 50 mm: use at least 1/50 s to avoid shake. Moving to f/2.8 from f/5.6 is 2 stops more light, so shutter can go from 1/50 to 1/200 s at the same exposure.

Activity. Set exposure for three scenes: bright outdoor, indoor window light, low light.

Standard to meet. Each setting is reasoned and avoids shake.

Module 2 Composition and framing 3 hApply

Composition guides the eye. Simple rules help until you know when to break them.

  • Use thirds, leading lines and clear backgrounds.
  • Leave space for the subject to look or move into.
  • Vary distance: wide, medium, close.
  • Check edges and horizons.
Worked example. Shot list for a portrait: 1 wide, 3 medium, 3 close, 2 detail = 9 frames, 3 variations each = 27 images, from which 10 are delivered.

Activity. Make a 10-shot list for a product and for a portrait.

Standard to meet. Shots vary in distance and each has a purpose.

Module 3 Working with people 3 hApply

People look best when comfortable. Clear directions and consent protect everyone.

  • Explain the plan and ask for consent in writing.
  • Give simple directions.
  • Respect privacy and cultural sensitivity.
  • Take extra care with children and ask for guardian consent.
Worked example. Shoot of 12 people: 12 signed release forms, 11 agree to web use, 1 refuses; that person is excluded from the web gallery.

Activity. Write a one-page consent and release form.

Standard to meet. Form states use, duration and withdrawal.

Module 4 Video basics and sound 4 hApply

Video adds motion and sound. Poor sound ruins good pictures.

  • Choose frame rate: 25 fps standard, 50 fps for slow motion.
  • Set shutter near double the frame rate.
  • Place the microphone close to the speaker.
  • Record 10 seconds of room tone.
Worked example. Clip at 25 fps: shutter 1/50. Interview 10 min at 100 MB per minute video = 1 GB; with a 4 GB card you can record 4 interviews.

Activity. Plan a 60-second video with scene list and sound plan.

Standard to meet. Frame rate, shutter and microphone choices are reasoned.

Module 5 Editing and delivery 4 hCreate

Editing selects, corrects and tells the story. Keep originals untouched.

  • Cull to the best frames.
  • Correct exposure, colour and crop gently.
  • Cut video to the story and balance sound.
  • Export in the formats and sizes agreed.
Worked example. 300 raw images: cull to 60 (20 percent) in 40 minutes; edit 60 x 2 min = 120 min; export 20 min. Total 3 h. Video 1 minute: 2 h edit.

Activity. Estimate edit time for a half-day shoot.

Standard to meet. Estimate within 5 percent of the model.

Module 6 Files, backup and organisation 3 hApply

Lost files lose clients. Use the 3-2-1 idea: 3 copies, 2 kinds of media, 1 off-site.

  • Name files by date and job.
  • Copy cards before reformatting.
  • Verify copies.
  • Keep a job log and delete only after delivery and an agreed time.
Worked example. Half-day shoot: 300 raw files of 30 MB = 9 GB, plus 2 GB video = 11 GB. Three copies need 33 GB; a 1 TB drive holds about 90 such jobs.

Activity. Write a backup plan with copy count and checks.

Standard to meet. Plan has 3 copies, 2 media types and 1 off-site.

Module 7 Rights, law and safety 3 hEvaluate

Copyright and image rights decide who can use what. Some places and flights need permission.

  • State who owns the copyright and what the client may use.
  • Ask the competent authority about image rights and drones.
  • Do not use music or others work without permission.
  • Keep cables, stands and locations safe.
Worked example. Agreement grants web and print use for 24 months, not resale. Extra use costs 30 percent of the original fee: 6,000 RUB x 0.30 = 1,800 RUB.

Activity. Write a usage clause and a site safety list.

Standard to meet. Clause states owner, uses, duration and extra use.

Module 8 Quote, client and business plan 4 hCreate

Price time and risk, not only the shoot. Include planning, edit and admin.

  • Count shoot, travel, edit, admin and equipment wear.
  • Set an hourly target and a minimum job fee.
  • State what is included, the number of images and revisions.
  • Follow up for feedback.
Worked example. Shoot 4 h + travel 1 h + edit 3 h + admin 1 h = 9 h. At 700 RUB/h = 6,300 RUB; equipment wear 400 RUB; total 6,700 RUB for 20 images and 1 video.

Activity. Write a quote and agreement for the half-day job.

Standard to meet. Quote total correct to 5 percent and includes scope and revisions.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: a brief, a shot list, exposure notes, an edit log, a backup record, a client agreement and a quote. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
TechnicalExposure not reasoned.Settings reasoned for each case.Also compares creative alternatives.
PlanningNo shot list.Shot list and sound plan.Also contingency for weather.
FilesSingle copy.3-2-1 backup.Also checksums and logs.
LegalNo consent or usage terms.Consent and usage clause.Also covers drone and location rules.
PricingTime missing.All time counted, within 5 percent.Also tests profit per hour.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What are the three exposure controls?

Model answer: Aperture, shutter speed and ISO.

Q2. What is a stop?

Model answer: A doubling or halving of light.

Q3. Why use 1/50 s at 50 mm handheld?

Model answer: To limit camera shake; the minimum is about 1 over the focal length.

Q4. What shutter fits 25 fps video?

Model answer: About 1/50 s.

Q5. What is the 3-2-1 backup idea?

Model answer: 3 copies, 2 media types, 1 off-site.

Q6. Why get a signed release?

Model answer: It shows the person agreed to the use.

Q7. Who owns copyright by default?

Model answer: Check the rules; state ownership and licence in the agreement.

Q8. Edit time for 60 images at 2 min each?

Model answer: 120 minutes.

Q9. Can you fly a drone anywhere?

Model answer: No; registration and permission may be needed from the competent authority.

Q10. Why record room tone?

Model answer: To fill gaps and match sound in editing.
