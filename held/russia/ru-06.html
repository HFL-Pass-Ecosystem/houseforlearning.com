HELD · RU-06

Educational Advisory Services

Set up and deliver an honest educational advisory service: understand learners and their goals, map pathways and entry rules, give clear unbiased guidance, protect personal data and keep sound records.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Set up and deliver an honest educational advisory service: understand learners and their goals, map pathways and entry rules, give clear unbiased guidance, protect personal data and keep sound records.

  • Understand Explain the main education pathways, entry steps and the role of an adviser.
  • Apply Conduct a structured guidance interview and record agreed actions.
  • Analyse Compare study options on fit, entry requirements, time, cost and risk.
  • Evaluate Judge an information source or offer for accuracy and honesty.
  • Create Build an advisory service pack with intake form, option sheet, action plan and data protection note.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Deliver a documented guidance session and produce an option comparison and an action plan for a learner case.

Condition

Individual and peer work over 6 weeks using case studies, interview templates and a spreadsheet; no real personal data is used in training.

Standard

Interview covers goals, strengths, constraints and options; comparison has at least 3 options and 5 criteria; plan has dated actions; 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Teachers, career advisers, school and college staff, training centre staff and people planning an advisory service. Requirements: Good communication; no formal qualification is required for the course. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Education services are regulated: institutions and programmes may need licences or accreditation, and advice about study abroad, recognition of qualifications and visas depends on current rules. An adviser must not promise admission, results, jobs or visas, and must not take hidden commissions without telling the learner. Personal data of learners, especially children, must be protected under the relevant federal rules. This course gives no legal advice. Confirm licensing, data and advertising rules with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The adviser role and ethics 4 hUnderstand

An adviser helps a learner decide; the learner owns the decision. Honesty and independence are the core duties.

  • State what you can and cannot do: inform, guide, refer; not guarantee.
  • Declare any payment from a school or provider.
  • Respect confidentiality and its limits, such as risk of harm.
  • Refer to specialists for needs beyond your skills.
Worked example. A learner asks, "Will I get in?" A clear answer: "I cannot promise that. Last year 120 applied for 40 places, so about 1 in 3; here is how to raise your chances."

Activity. Write a one-page code of practice.

Standard to meet. Code covers honesty, payment disclosure, confidentiality and referral.

Module 2 Education pathways and entry rules 5 hUnderstand

Learners move through stages: general education, vocational training, higher education and adult learning. Each has entry steps and dates.

  • Draw the pathway map with ages, qualifications and links between stages.
  • List entry routes: exams, tests, interviews, portfolios, prior learning.
  • Note key dates and documents for applications.
  • Check current rules with the official body before advising, since they change.
Worked example. A school leaver has 70 of 100 points required for a course that admits at 65, with 3 places per 10 applicants; the margin is 5 points, so a back-up option is needed.

Activity. Build a pathway map for one region.

Standard to meet. Map shows stages, entry routes and a verification source.

Module 3 Guidance interview 5 hApply

A good interview moves from rapport to goals, strengths, constraints, options and actions.

  • Open with purpose, time and confidentiality.
  • Use open questions and listen without leading the learner.
  • Summarise back and check understanding.
  • Agree 2 to 3 actions with dates.
Worked example. In a 45-minute session: 5 minutes opening, 20 minutes exploring, 10 minutes options, 10 minutes actions. Two actions: collect results by Friday; visit two programmes next week.

Activity. Run a role-play interview and record it.

Standard to meet. Structure is followed and actions are dated.

Module 4 Assessing needs and strengths 4 hAnalyse

Interests, strengths, values and constraints (money, location, health, family) all shape choices.

  • Use simple tools: interest list, skills list, values ranking.
  • Collect evidence such as grades and projects.
  • Spot barriers early: distance, cost, language, disability support.
  • Record only data needed for the advice.
Worked example. Budget for study: tuition 90,000 RUB, living 12,000 RUB x 10 months = 120,000 RUB, total 210,000 RUB per year against family support of 150,000 RUB; the gap is 60,000 RUB and options must address it.

Activity. Complete a needs profile for a case.

Standard to meet. Profile shows strengths, constraints and a gap analysis.

Module 5 Comparing options 5 hAnalyse

Compare at least three options against the same criteria and show the trade-offs.

  • Choose criteria: fit, entry chance, length, cost, location, outcomes.
  • Weight criteria by the learner's priorities.
  • Score and total; test the result by changing weights.
  • Discuss the risks of each option.
Worked example. Weights: fit 40, cost 30, entry chance 30. Option A scores 8, 6, 7; total = 3.2 + 1.8 + 2.1 = 7.1. Option B scores 7, 8, 5; total = 2.8 + 2.4 + 1.5 = 6.7. A is slightly ahead.

Activity. Build a weighted comparison table.

Standard to meet. Weights sum to 100 and totals are correct.

Module 6 Checking information and avoiding misleading claims 4 hEvaluate

Advice is only as good as its sources. Offers that promise results are a warning sign.

  • Check licence and accreditation of a provider through the competent body.
  • Ask for outcome data and its method.
  • Spot pressure tactics, guarantees and vague fees.
  • Keep a source log with date of check.
Worked example. A provider claims "100 percent of graduates get jobs" with no data. You request the numbers: of 60 graduates 48 answered a survey and 36 had a job, which is 75 percent of respondents and 60 percent of graduates.

Activity. Judge three provider claims.

Standard to meet. Each claim is rated with evidence.

Module 7 Data protection and records 4 hApply

Learner data is personal and often about minors. Collect little, store safely and share only with consent.

  • Explain the purpose and get consent before recording.
  • Store files in a locked place or protected system.
  • Keep records for a set time and then delete them.
  • Check the relevant federal rules with the competent authority.
Worked example. A service holds 400 files; if 25 percent are past the retention period, 100 files must be reviewed and deleted.

Activity. Draft a data protection note and a retention table.

Standard to meet. Note covers purpose, consent, storage, sharing and deletion.

Module 8 Service design and follow-up 4 hCreate

A service has intake, session, plan, follow-up and review.

  • Design the client journey in five steps.
  • Set response times and caseload.
  • Track outcomes: actions done, places gained, learner feedback.
  • Plan improvement from the data.
Worked example. An adviser sees 6 learners/day x 5 days = 30 sessions/week; with 45 min sessions plus 15 min notes that is 30 hours, so the weekly caseload is capped at 30.

Activity. Present the service pack.

Standard to meet. Pack joins intake, options, plan, data note and follow-up.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: intake form, interview record, option comparison, action plan, data protection note, follow-up log. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Who owns the decision in guidance?

Model answer: The learner.

Q2. May an adviser promise admission?

Model answer: No; they may inform and support, not guarantee.

Q3. What must be declared?

Model answer: Any payment or interest from a provider.

Q4. Why check a provider's licence?

Model answer: To confirm it may lawfully offer the programme.

Q5. What is an open question?

Model answer: One that invites a full answer, not just yes or no.

Q6. Why agree dated actions?

Model answer: So the learner knows what to do next and progress can be checked.

Q7. What is a weighted comparison?

Model answer: Scoring options on criteria weighted by the learner's priorities.

Q8. How should learner data be treated?

Model answer: Collected sparingly, stored safely, shared with consent and deleted on time.

Q9. Why verify entry rules?

Model answer: They change, and wrong advice harms the learner.

Q10. Name one warning sign of a misleading offer.

Model answer: A guaranteed result or pressure to pay quickly.
