HELD · RU-06

Educational Advisory Services

Set up and deliver an honest educational advisory service: understand learners and their goals, map pathways and entry rules, give clear unbiased guidance, protect personal data and keep sound records.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Set up and deliver an honest educational advisory service: understand learners and their goals, map pathways and entry rules, give clear unbiased guidance, protect personal data and keep sound records.

  • Understand Explain the main education pathways, entry steps and the role of an adviser.
  • Apply Conduct a structured guidance interview and record agreed actions.
  • Analyse Compare study options on fit, entry requirements, time, cost and risk.
  • Evaluate Judge an information source or offer for accuracy and honesty.
  • Create Build an advisory service pack with intake form, option sheet, action plan and data protection note.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Deliver a documented guidance session and produce an option comparison and an action plan for a learner case.

Condition

Individual and peer work over 6 weeks using case studies, interview templates and a spreadsheet; no real personal data is used in training.

Standard

Interview covers goals, strengths, constraints and options; comparison has at least 3 options and 5 criteria; plan has dated actions; 8 of 10 check answers right.

Who it is for: Teachers, career advisers, school and college staff, training centre staff and people planning an advisory service. Requirements: Good communication; no formal qualification is required for the course. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Education services are regulated: institutions and programmes may need licences or accreditation, and advice about study abroad, recognition of qualifications and visas depends on current rules. An adviser must not promise admission, results, jobs or visas, and must not take hidden commissions without telling the learner. Personal data of learners, especially children, must be protected under the relevant federal rules. This course gives no legal advice. Confirm licensing, data and advertising rules with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The adviser role and ethics 4 hUnderstand

An adviser helps a learner decide; the learner owns the decision. Honesty and independence are the core duties.

  • State what you can and cannot do: inform, guide, refer; not guarantee.
  • Declare any payment from a school or provider.
  • Respect confidentiality and its limits, such as risk of harm.
  • Refer to specialists for needs beyond your skills.
Worked example. A learner asks, "Will I get in?" A clear answer: "I cannot promise that. Last year 120 applied for 40 places, so about 1 in 3; here is how to raise your chances." Check: 40 / 120 = 0.33. Then name two steps, such as improving a weak subject by the next test and preparing a back-up option, and note in the record that no promise was made and that no payment from any provider was involved.

Activity. Write a one-page code of practice with at least six rules, and add two sample answers to common learner questions that avoid any promise.

Standard to meet. The code covers honesty, payment disclosure, confidentiality with its limits, and referral; at least six rules are written in plain words; both sample answers give a real figure and no guarantee.

Module 2 Education pathways and entry rules 5 hUnderstand

Learners move through stages: general education, vocational training, higher education and adult learning. Each has entry steps and dates.

  • Draw the pathway map with ages, qualifications and links between stages.
  • List entry routes: exams, tests, interviews, portfolios, prior learning.
  • Note key dates and documents for applications.
  • Check current rules with the official body before advising, since they change.
Worked example. A school leaver has 70 of 100 points against an entry line of 65, with 3 places per 10 applicants. Margin = 70 - 65 = 5 points, which is thin because last year the line moved by 4 points. Step 1: confirm the current line with the official body. Step 2: list two back-up courses with lower lines. Check: with 30 percent of applicants admitted, a back-up option is needed.

Activity. Build a pathway map for one region showing stages, ages, entry routes, key dates and the official body that confirms each rule.

Standard to meet. The map has at least four stages and three entry routes; every rule has a named verification source and a check date; two common learner journeys are traced on it from start to entry.

Module 3 Guidance interview 5 hApply

A good interview moves from rapport to goals, strengths, constraints, options and actions.

  • Open with purpose, time and confidentiality.
  • Use open questions and listen without leading the learner.
  • Summarise back and check understanding.
  • Agree 2 to 3 actions with dates.
Worked example. In a 45-minute session: 5 minutes opening, 20 minutes exploring, 10 minutes options, 10 minutes actions. Check: 5 + 20 + 10 + 10 = 45. Two actions: collect results by Friday; visit two programmes next week. The adviser asks mostly open questions, summarises twice, and the learner, not the adviser, states the final choice of next steps in the record.

Activity. Run a 45-minute role-play interview with a peer observer and record it on the interview template, including a summary and two dated actions.

Standard to meet. Timing stays within 5 minutes of plan; at least eight open questions are used; the record shows goals, strengths, constraints, options and two actions, each with a date and an owner.

Module 4 Assessing needs and strengths 4 hAnalyse

Interests, strengths, values and constraints (money, location, health, family) all shape choices.

  • Use simple tools: interest list, skills list, values ranking.
  • Collect evidence such as grades and projects.
  • Spot barriers early: distance, cost, language, disability support.
  • Record only data needed for the advice.
Worked example. Budget for study: tuition 90,000 RUB, living 12,000 RUB x 10 months = 120,000 RUB, total 210,000 RUB per year against family support of 150,000 RUB; the gap is 60,000 RUB and options must address it. Check: 90,000 + 120,000 = 210,000 and 210,000 - 150,000 = 60,000, or 5,000 RUB a month over 12 months. Options include a cheaper programme, part-time work or a local course.

Activity. Complete a needs profile for a case covering interests, strengths, values, constraints and a yearly budget with the gap in RUB.

Standard to meet. The profile lists at least three strengths with evidence and three constraints; the budget adds up; the gap is stated in RUB per year and per month; only data needed for the advice is recorded.

Module 5 Comparing options 5 hAnalyse

Compare at least three options against the same criteria and show the trade-offs.

  • Choose criteria: fit, entry chance, length, cost, location, outcomes.
  • Weight criteria by the learner's priorities.
  • Score and total; test the result by changing weights.
  • Discuss the risks of each option.
Worked example. Weights: fit 40, cost 30, entry chance 30. Option A scores 8, 6, 7; total = 3.2 + 1.8 + 2.1 = 7.1. Option B scores 7, 8, 5; total = 2.8 + 2.4 + 1.5 = 6.7. A is slightly ahead. Test: if cost weight rises to 40 and fit falls to 30, A = 2.4 + 2.4 + 2.1 = 6.9 and B = 2.1 + 3.2 + 1.5 = 6.8, so the lead is fragile and risks must be discussed.

Activity. Build a weighted comparison table with three options, five criteria and weights from the learner, then change the weights once and report what changes.

Standard to meet. Weights sum to 100; every total is correct to one decimal place; the sensitivity test is shown; a risk is stated for each option; the learner, not the adviser, picks the final weights.

Module 6 Checking information and avoiding misleading claims 4 hEvaluate

Advice is only as good as its sources. Offers that promise results are a warning sign.

  • Check licence and accreditation of a provider through the competent body.
  • Ask for outcome data and its method.
  • Spot pressure tactics, guarantees and vague fees.
  • Keep a source log with date of check.
Worked example. A provider claims "100 percent of graduates get jobs" with no data. You request the numbers: of 60 graduates 48 answered a survey and 36 had a job, which is 75 percent of respondents and 60 percent of graduates. Check: 36 / 48 = 0.75 and 36 / 60 = 0.60. The claim is not supported; note the response rate of 80 percent (48 / 60) and ask how a job was defined and when it was counted.

Activity. Judge three provider claims by asking for licence status, outcome data and method, and rate each claim as supported, partly supported or unsupported.

Standard to meet. Each rating cites at least two pieces of evidence; the source log gives name, date checked and result; one pressure tactic or vague fee is found and described in the notes.

Module 7 Data protection and records 4 hApply

Learner data is personal and often about minors. Collect little, store safely and share only with consent.

  • Explain the purpose and get consent before recording.
  • Store files in a locked place or protected system.
  • Keep records for a set time and then delete them.
  • Check the relevant federal rules with the competent authority.
Worked example. A service holds 400 files; if 25 percent are past the retention period, 100 files must be reviewed and deleted. Check: 400 x 0.25 = 100. At 10 files reviewed per hour the clean-up takes 10 hours, so schedule it in two half-days and record the deletion date in a log. Files of minors are handled first, and consent forms are checked before any sharing.

Activity. Draft a data protection note and a retention table that lists each type of record, its purpose, who may see it, where it is kept and when it is deleted.

Standard to meet. The note covers purpose, consent, storage, sharing and deletion in under one page; the table has at least five record types, each with a retention period marked as to be confirmed with the competent authority.

Module 8 Service design and follow-up 4 hCreate

A service has intake, session, plan, follow-up and review.

  • Design the client journey in five steps.
  • Set response times and caseload.
  • Track outcomes: actions done, places gained, learner feedback.
  • Plan improvement from the data.
Worked example. An adviser sees 6 learners/day x 5 days = 30 sessions/week; with 45 min sessions plus 15 min notes that is 30 hours, so the weekly caseload is capped at 30. Check: 45 + 15 = 60 minutes x 30 = 1,800 minutes = 30 hours. Leaving 10 hours for preparation, follow-up and training in a 40-hour week, the service reviews outcomes monthly and adjusts the cap if feedback or actions done fall.

Activity. Present the service pack with the five-step client journey, response times, caseload cap, outcome measures and an improvement plan.

Standard to meet. The pack joins intake form, option sheet, action plan, data note and follow-up log; caseload arithmetic is correct; at least three outcome measures have a target and a review date.

assessment

Evidence products: intake form, interview record, option comparison, action plan, data protection note, follow-up log. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Who owns the decision in guidance?

Model answer: The learner owns the decision; the adviser informs, challenges gently, compares options honestly and records what was agreed.

Q2. May an adviser promise admission?

Model answer: No; an adviser may inform, guide and support with honest figures and options, but admission is decided by the institution, so no guarantee may be given.

Q3. What must be declared?

Model answer: Any payment or interest from a provider.

Q4. Why check a provider's licence?

Model answer: To confirm it may lawfully offer the programme.

Q5. What is an open question?

Model answer: One that invites a full answer, not just yes or no.

Q6. Why agree dated actions?

Model answer: So the learner knows what to do next and progress can be checked.

Q7. What is a weighted comparison?

Model answer: Scoring options on criteria weighted by the learner's priorities.

Q8. How should learner data be treated?

Model answer: Collected sparingly, stored safely, shared with consent and deleted on time.

Q9. Why verify entry rules?

Model answer: They change from year to year, and wrong advice about dates or documents can cost the learner a place, so always check the official source.

Q10. Name one warning sign of a misleading offer.

Model answer: A guaranteed result or pressure to pay quickly.
