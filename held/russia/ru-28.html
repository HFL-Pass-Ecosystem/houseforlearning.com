Mobile App Services

Learn to plan, build the case for, test and run a mobile app service: user needs, a minimum product, data handling, store rules, analytics and support. You work through a small booking app.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner29 h of lessons plus practiceHELD

Learn to plan, build the case for, test and run a mobile app service: user needs, a minimum product, data handling, store rules, analytics and support. You work through a small booking app.

  • Understand Explain the stages of an app project and the roles in a small team.
  • Apply Write user stories and acceptance tests for a core flow.
  • Analyse Break monthly running cost and user retention into parts and find the weakest step.
  • Evaluate Judge a data and privacy plan against the relevant federal rules and store policies.
  • Create Build a release plan, a test log and a support process for version 1.0.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a product brief, 12 user stories with tests, a release checklist, a privacy note and a retention analysis for a small booking app.

Condition

Given a business idea, a spreadsheet, a free prototype tool and 6 hours of working time, with a mentor to review.

Standard

Stories are testable, release checklist has at least 20 items, retention arithmetic is correct, every personal data item has a purpose, and at least 8 of 10 check answers are right.

Who it is for: Founders, product assistants, junior developers, designers and small business owners. Requirements: Basic arithmetic, a spreadsheet and a device with a browser. No coding is needed to study. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Apps collect personal data, take payments and are published through stores with their own rules. Data protection, data localisation, payment, consumer and advertising rules are set by the relevant federal rules and the competent authority. This course gives no legal advice. Check with the competent Russian authority and a local adviser, especially for health, finance or child users. Do not promise users results the app cannot deliver.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Problem, user and product brief 3 hUnderstand

An app succeeds when it solves one problem for a named user better than their current way.

  • State the user, the problem and the current workaround in three sentences.
  • Interview five users and record exact words, not opinions about features.
  • Define the single main job the app must do.
  • Set a measurable goal such as 100 bookings in the first 60 days.
Worked example. A salon owner takes 40 bookings a week by phone and loses about 6 to missed calls. An app that captures even 4 of those gives 4 x 52 = 208 extra bookings a year; at 1,500 RUB each, 312,000 RUB a year of revenue.

Activity. Write the brief and interview notes for the booking app.

Standard to meet. Brief names user, problem, workaround and a number to reach.

Module 2 User stories and acceptance tests 4 hApply

A story states who, what and why. A test says how to know it is done.

  • Write stories as: As a customer I want to book a slot so that I do not phone.
  • Add acceptance tests with clear pass or fail.
  • Split stories larger than 3 days of work.
  • Rank stories: must, should, could for version 1.0.
Worked example. Story: book a slot. Test: choose service, date, time; see confirmation within 3 seconds; a message arrives within 1 minute; a slot already taken cannot be booked twice.

Activity. Write 12 stories with tests and rank them.

Standard to meet. Every must-story has a clear, testable pass condition.

Module 3 Design, prototype and usability 4 hCreate

A prototype tests the idea before expensive code. Five test users find most big problems.

  • Sketch screens for the main flow with no more than 4 taps to book.
  • Use a clear tap size of at least 44 pixels and readable text.
  • Test a clickable prototype with five users and record where they stop.
  • Fix the top three problems before building.
Worked example. Five tests: 4 of 5 users fail to find the date picker. One fix (a bigger button) is applied and the next test passes at 5 of 5.

Activity. Build a prototype and run five tests.

Standard to meet. Test log shows time to finish, errors and three fixes.

Module 4 Build choices, quality and testing 4 hEvaluate

Native, cross-platform or web app: each trades cost, speed and quality. Testing must cover devices, networks and edge cases.

  • Compare options by cost, time to market, performance and skills.
  • Test on at least three screen sizes and two OS versions.
  • Test with slow or lost connection and with interrupted calls.
  • Track defects by severity and fix all blockers before release.
Worked example. Cross-platform build 10 weeks at two developers; native on two systems 18 weeks. For a simple booking app the saving of 8 weeks is worth more than a small speed gain.

Activity. Choose a build approach and justify it with a table.

Standard to meet. Choice is argued with numbers and risks.

Module 5 Data, privacy and store rules 4 hEvaluate

Collect only what you need and tell users why. Store rules are checked before every release.

  • List each data item, its purpose, who sees it and how long it is kept.
  • Ask consent in plain words and let users delete their data.
  • Check store policies on payments, content and permissions.
  • Know that personal data rules, including where data is stored, come from the relevant federal rules and the competent authority.
Worked example. The app asks for name, phone and email. Location is not needed for booking, so it is not collected. Removing one unneeded item cuts risk and the number of consent screens by one.

Activity. Write a privacy note with a data table.

Standard to meet. Every item has a purpose and retention time; no unneeded permission.

Module 6 Launch, analytics and growth 4 hAnalyse

Launch is the start of learning. Measure activation and retention, not downloads.

  • Define activation, for example first booking within 7 days.
  • Compute retention at day 1, day 7 and day 30.
  • Track cost per install and cost per booking.
  • Run one change at a time and compare.
Worked example. Of 1,000 installs, 400 activate (40 percent). Day-30 retention is 120 users = 12 percent of installs. If spend is 50,000 RUB, cost per active user is 50,000 / 400 = 125 RUB.

Activity. Calculate the funnel and find the weakest step.

Standard to meet. Correct percentages and a named improvement test.

Module 7 Running cost and support 3 hApply

A service has ongoing costs and users who need help.

  • List costs: hosting, store fees, messages, payment fees, support time and updates.
  • Set a first-response time target, for example 4 working hours.
  • Keep a defect and request log and review weekly.
  • Plan updates for new OS versions.
Worked example. Hosting 6,000 RUB, messages 4,000 RUB, tools 3,000 RUB, support 8 hours at 600 RUB = 4,800 RUB. Total 17,800 RUB a month. Each booking at 60 RUB margin needs 297 bookings to cover cost.

Activity. Build a monthly cost table and a support playbook.

Standard to meet. Costs are complete; playbook has response targets.

Module 8 Release plan and review 3 hCreate

A release is a controlled event with checks, a rollback plan and a review.

  • Make a 20-item release checklist: tests, store listing, privacy, support, monitoring.
  • Release to a small group first (staged rollout).
  • Define rollback triggers, such as crash rate above 2 percent.
  • Hold a review two weeks after launch.
Worked example. Staged rollout to 10 percent of 2,000 users = 200. Crash rate 3 percent is above the 2 percent trigger, so release pauses and a fix is made.

Activity. Write the release checklist and the review agenda.

Standard to meet. Checklist has 20 or more items with owners and a rollback trigger.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence products: product brief, user stories with tests, prototype, release checklist, privacy note, retention analysis and support playbook. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why interview users before building?

Model answer: To confirm a real problem and avoid building unwanted features.

Q2. Give an acceptance test for booking.

Model answer: A booked slot shows confirmation, and a second booking of the same slot is blocked.

Q3. Why use a prototype?

Model answer: To test the idea cheaply with users before coding.

Q4. 1,000 installs, 400 active: activation rate?

Model answer: 40 percent.

Q5. Why collect minimal data?

Model answer: It lowers risk and meets purpose-limitation duties.

Q6. What is a staged rollout?

Model answer: Releasing to a small share first to catch problems.

Q7. Name a rollback trigger.

Model answer: A crash rate above a set limit such as 2 percent.

Q8. Who sets personal data rules?

Model answer: The relevant federal rules and the competent authority.

Q9. Why measure retention, not downloads?

Model answer: Retention shows whether users find lasting value.

Q10. What do you record in a support log?

Model answer: Request, time, severity, fix and user feedback.
