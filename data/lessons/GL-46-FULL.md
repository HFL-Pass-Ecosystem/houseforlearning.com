# GL-46 DNA Analysis (Awareness Level): full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.
This is an AWARENESS course. You learn how things work, the rules and the ethics. You do not practise treatment, diagnosis or clinical work, and you use only public, made-up or simulated material.
Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a curious person who wants to understand what a DNA test can and cannot tell them.
1. Market scan: Read the public guidance of a national health body and one ethics body about consumer genetic tests. Note what they say about accuracy, privacy and family impact.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Draw the lab flow in plain steps: sample, label, extraction, reading, quality check, report, storage or destruction.
2. Write down where mistakes or mix-ups could happen and one control for each.
3. Write a plain-language note on what a result can and cannot say.
4. List what is outside this course: interpreting a real person's results and medical decisions.
Evidence: Lab flow with controls and a plain-language note.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Genetics basics: genes are instructions inside cells. Most traits depend on many genes plus environment. A single marker rarely gives a clear yes or no.
- Consent: a person must agree freely and understand what will be done with the sample and the data, including whether it can be shared or used for research.
- Data rules: genetic data is sensitive and also reveals information about relatives. Strong protection, clear limits on sharing, and clear deletion are essential.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Use only public, made-up or simulated example reports. Practise reading the sections: what was tested, how sure the lab is, what it means, what it does not mean.
2. Write three questions a person should ask before taking a test.
3. Write what a person should do next: speak with a qualified genetic counsellor or clinician.
Evidence: Example-report notes and question list.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Discuss two made-up cases: a test result shared with a relative, and a company asked to share data.
2. Each learner states what rule applies and why.
3. Write the top three principles.
Evidence: Three principles.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: controls identified in the lab flow, clarity of the plain note, completeness of the question list, peer rating.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Lab flow: sample > unique code > extraction > reading > quality check > report > storage or deletion.
2. Risk: two tubes swapped. Control: two-person label check and barcode scan.
3. Plain note: 'A result shows a probability, not a certainty, and may matter for relatives.'
4. Question list: Who sees my data? Can I withdraw? What happens to the sample?

## Case exercise
A made-up person asks to share their result with a cousin. Questions: What should they consider? What does consent cover? What does a counsellor add? Write a one-page plain-language note.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What can a result not say? 2. Why does DNA data affect relatives? 3. What should consent cover? 4. Name a lab mix-up risk and a control. 5. Why talk to a counsellor? 6. What is outside this course? 7. What does deletion mean in practice? 8. Why is environment important? 9. What question would you ask first? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Genetic and health data rules, consent for samples and data, laboratory accreditation, cross-border transfer.
Personal data: in the EU the data-protection regulation applies. India's data-protection Rules are phased in: consent managers around Nov 2026 and the main duties around May 2027. UAE executive regulations are still uncertain: check the official portal. Collect little, ask consent clearly, protect it, delete it.
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
Awareness only. No interpretation of real results, no medical advice. No real samples or genetic data. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
