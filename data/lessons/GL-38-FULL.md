# GL-38 P2P Lending Platform Concepts: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a saver and a borrower who both need to understand the risk.
1. Market scan: Read the public rules and summary pages of 3 lending platforms or regulator guides. Note what is explained about risk, fees and what happens if a borrower does not pay.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Draw a flow of money from saver to borrower and back. Mark where fees, delays and losses occur.
2. Design a borrower screening checklist for a sample platform using made-up profiles only.
3. Write the risk disclosure a saver must read before taking part, in plain words.
4. Check which licences or registrations your country asks for, and write down the source.
Evidence: Flow diagram, screening checklist and risk disclosure.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Credit basics: a loan has interest, a term and a risk that it is not repaid. Higher promised returns usually mean higher risk. Spreading small amounts over many loans lowers, but does not remove, the risk.
- Licensing awareness: lending and taking savings are regulated. Never run a real scheme without the right permission. This course uses made-up data only.
- Disclosure: say what can go wrong, what fees apply, and that money can be lost.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Test the checklist on 10 made-up borrower profiles. Record which pass and why.
2. Show the disclosure to 5 people and ask them to say back what the risks are.
3. Revise until at least 4 of 5 can explain it.
Evidence: Test records and revised disclosure.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Present your screening checklist.
2. Peers try to find a profile that fools it.
3. Add one control.
Evidence: Updated checklist.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: share of testers who can explain the risk, number of checklist gaps found, clarity of fee table.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Flow: saver > platform > borrower; fees at start and each month; losses if a borrower stops paying.
2. Checklist for a made-up borrower: stable income shown, purpose clear, amount under a fixed share of income, no recent missed payments.
3. Test: of 10 made-up profiles, 6 pass; 2 fail on income; 2 fail on missing records.
4. Risk disclosure line: 'You can lose some or all of the money you lend.'

## Case exercise
A made-up borrower has high income but two recent late payments and asks for a large amount. Questions: Pass or fail under your checklist? What extra check would help? What does the saver read? Write a one-page risk note in plain words.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What are interest, term and default? 2. Why can a high return mean high risk? 3. What does spreading loans do and not do? 4. Why do you need a licence? 5. What goes in a disclosure? 6. Why use made-up profiles? 7. What fee information must be shown? 8. How do you know a disclosure works? 9. What gap did peers find? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Lending and deposit-taking licences, disclosure duties and investor protection in your country. This course is concepts only.
Personal data: in the EU the data-protection regulation applies. India's data-protection Rules are phased in: consent managers around Nov 2026 and the main duties around May 2027. UAE executive regulations are still uncertain: check the official portal. Collect little, ask consent clearly, protect it, delete it.
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
Concept course only. No real lending, no real savings, no real borrower data. Not financial advice. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
