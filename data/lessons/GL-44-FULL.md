# GL-44 Online Therapy Support (Awareness Level): full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.
This is an AWARENESS course. You learn how things work, the rules and the ethics. You do not practise treatment, diagnosis or clinical work, and you use only public, made-up or simulated material.
Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a person who needs safe, licensed help and a service that points them to it.
1. Market scan: Read the public guidance of two professional bodies or regulators about delivering counselling at a distance. Note the rules on licence, consent, privacy and emergencies.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Write a plain summary of what a licensed provider must do before the first online session.
2. Draw the flow from first contact to referral: screening of urgency, consent, session, follow-up, and when to refer or call emergency help.
3. Write a short privacy notice a client could understand.
4. List the things this course does not teach: diagnosis, treatment, and clinical practice.
Evidence: Summary, flow and privacy notice.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Licensing: only a licensed professional may give therapy. A service that connects people to professionals must check licences and say clearly who is who.
- Consent and privacy: say what is recorded, who sees it and how long it is kept. Use secure tools and never store sessions without clear permission.
- Referral: if someone is in danger, the plan is to direct them to local emergency services or a crisis line straight away. Know the numbers for your country.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Role-play only with scripts and no real stories. Practise the opening: who you are, what the service is and is not, confidentiality and its limits.
2. Practise a safe handover: how to tell someone gently that you are referring them, and where to find emergency help.
3. Record what you found hard and what you would add to the flow.
Evidence: Script and reflection.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Discuss two made-up ethics dilemmas.
2. Each learner writes what the service should do, and what it must not do.
3. Agree three firm rules.
Evidence: Three rules.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: completeness of the flow, clarity of the privacy notice, number of correct referral points, peer rating.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Flow: first contact > urgency check > consent > session > follow-up > referral if needed.
2. Opening script: 'I am a service coordinator, not a therapist. Here is how your information is used. If you are in danger, call the emergency number now.'
3. Privacy notice: what is kept, who sees it, how long, how to ask for deletion.
4. Reflection: what felt awkward, what you will add to the script.

## Case exercise
A made-up user writes that they feel unsafe. Questions: What should the service do right now? What must it not do? Who is told? Write the steps for your country's emergency services and crisis line (look up the real numbers).

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Who may deliver therapy? 2. What must a privacy notice say? 3. When do you direct someone to emergency help? 4. Why must you say what the service is not? 5. What is outside this course? 6. How do you check a licence? 7. What are the limits of confidentiality? 8. Why is secure software needed? 9. What would you add to the flow? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Professional licensing, telehealth rules, consent, confidentiality and emergency duties of your country.
Personal data: in the EU the data-protection regulation applies. India's data-protection Rules are phased in: consent managers around Nov 2026 and the main duties around May 2027. UAE executive regulations are still uncertain: check the official portal. Collect little, ask consent clearly, protect it, delete it. AI tools: if you use an AI tool in this course, the EU AI Act transparency duties (for example telling people they are dealing with AI, and marking AI-made content) have applied since 2 Aug 2026. Stand-alone high-risk AI systems are now due from 2 Dec 2027, and AI inside regulated products from 2 Aug 2028 (these dates were delayed; check the official source for any change).
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
Awareness only. No therapy, no counselling practice, no diagnosis. No real stories or patient data. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
