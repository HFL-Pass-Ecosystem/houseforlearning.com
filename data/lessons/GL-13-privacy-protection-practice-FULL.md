# GL-13 Privacy Protection Practice: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised privacy principles, including those in the European data-protection regulation, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the UAE personal-data law. This is not a licence or a certificate. This module teaches practice, not legal advice. Laws differ by country and change; confirm the rule that applies with a qualified adviser. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free spreadsheet, a free document editor, a free diagram tool.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What personal data is, and why people care
Goal: choose a real or sample service and list the personal data it touches.
1. Explain personal data in plain words: any information that can identify a person, directly or with other facts (name, phone, photo, location, ID number, online identifier).
2. Learn the core principles: lawful and fair, clear purpose, only what you need, accurate, kept no longer than needed, kept secure, and the person can see, fix and ask to delete their data.
3. Choose a service (a clinic booking page, a tutoring app, a shop with a loyalty card). Get written permission if it is real, or use a made-up sample.
4. List every place data enters, is stored, moves and leaves.
Evidence: the data list and the permission note.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a data map, a gap list, a fix plan, a notice and a breach-response sheet.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Map the data
1. Draw a data map: the form or counter where it is collected, each system that stores it, each person or company who gets it, where it is deleted.
2. For each item write: why we need it, lawful basis, how long we keep it, who can see it.
3. Mark sensitive items (health, religion, children, finances, biometrics, exact location).
4. Mark any transfers to another country and who is responsible.
Evidence: data map and data table.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Privacy principles, consent and breach response
Short modules:
- Principles: purpose, minimisation, accuracy, storage limit, security, accountability; rights of access, correction and deletion.
- Consent: when it is needed, how to ask clearly, how to record it, how to withdraw it; consent is not the only lawful basis; a pre-ticked box is not consent.
- Breach response: detect, contain, assess harm, tell the authority and the people within the time your law says, learn and fix.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Find gaps and plan fixes
1. For each data item ask: is it needed, is it kept too long, is it too widely visible, is it protected?
2. Write the gap list: gap, risk to people, fix, effort, owner, date.
3. Rewrite the privacy notice in plain language at a level a 14-year-old can follow: what, why, how long, who, your rights, how to contact.
4. Write a one-page breach-response sheet with roles and a clock.
Evidence: gap list, rewritten notice, breach sheet.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Peers act as a customer and read your notice. Note every unclear line.
2. Peers test a deletion request: how long would it take to find and erase one person's data?
3. A mentor with compliance or legal-awareness experience reviews the gap list and tells you which gap is most serious.
Evidence: notice feedback, deletion walk-through, reviewed gap list.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Fix the notice and the top three gaps (on paper, or in the sample service).
2. Count: data items before and after minimisation, retention times before and after.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: ready, ready after fixes, or needs an adviser.
Evidence: before/after counts and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the service, the data map, the gaps, the fixes, the notice and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Biometric Sensor and Security Solutions; Chatbots for Business; Cloud Migration as a Service.
3. Contribute back: share your data-map template, plain-language notice or gap-list sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Clinic booking form collects 12 fields. After minimisation, 6 are needed. Data items cut by 50%.
2. Retention: appointment records kept 7 years; the law-based need is 3 years for this record type (confirm locally). Reduce to 3 years: storage of old records falls by 4/7, about 57%.
3. Breach response clock: if the law says 72 hours, and the breach is found at 10:00 on Monday, the notice is due by 10:00 on Thursday.
4. Deletion test: finding one person's data in 4 systems took 90 minutes. Target: 30 minutes by adding one index sheet.

## Case exercise
A person asks to delete all their data, but an invoice with their name must be kept for tax. Questions: What do you delete, what keep, and why? What do you tell the person? Write the reply.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is personal data? 2. Name four privacy principles. 3. What rights can a person have over their data? 4. Why is a pre-ticked box not consent? 5. Name two sensitive data types. 6. What are the steps of breach response? 7. Why write a notice in plain language? 8. What does minimisation mean? 9. Why can transferring data abroad matter? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Law check
Law check (31 Oct 2026, from public sources; confirm with an adviser): India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 has its Rules notified in November 2025 and switches on in phases; the consent-manager part starts about November 2026 and the main duties of organisations from about May 2027. The UAE personal-data law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) has been in force since January 2022, but sources disagree on whether its executive regulations are issued; check the UAE legislation portal and the UAE Data Office. The EU data-protection regulation (GDPR) is unchanged in its core principles. For AI: the EU AI Act is now on a delayed schedule. Under the 2026 amending regulation, rules for stand-alone high-risk uses (for example credit decisions about people) apply from 2 December 2027, rules for AI inside regulated products from 2 August 2028, and the transparency duties (telling people they deal with AI or AI-made content) have applied since 2 August 2026, with a grace period to 2 December 2026 for some older content-generating systems.
