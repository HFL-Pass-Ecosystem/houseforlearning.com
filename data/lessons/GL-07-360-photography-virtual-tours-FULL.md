# GL-07 360-Degree Photography and Virtual Tours: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised accessibility and image-rights practice. This is not a licence or a certificate. Get written permission from the owner of the space and from anyone who appears; blur faces, number plates and private papers. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a phone or low-cost 360 camera, a free stitching and tour tool, a free image editor, a free spreadsheet.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Why a virtual tour, and for whom
Goal: choose one real space and the person who will use the tour.
1. Explain what a 360 image is: a full sphere of view, shown on a screen so the viewer can look around.
2. List 5 places where a tour helps a buyer decide (a classroom, a shop, a guest room, a workshop, a community hall).
3. Choose one space with an owner who agrees in writing. Ask the owner what a visitor should see first and what must not be shown.
4. Write the plan: spaces to capture (6 to 10 spots), what to hide, how the tour will be shared.
Evidence: written permission, the list of spots and the hide list.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a spot map, a set of captured images, a published tour and viewer feedback.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Capture the space
1. Walk the space with a floor plan. Mark each capture spot at about a step-and-a-half apart in rooms and further in halls.
2. Prepare: clean and light the space, close doors, remove personal items, switch on lights that help.
3. Capture each spot at eye level on a steady stand, with the camera at the same height. Keep your own body out of view by stepping away or using a timer.
4. Take two exposures in bright rooms with windows so you can balance light later.
Evidence: spot map and raw captures.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Capture technique, stitching and publishing
Short modules:
- Capture technique: height, level, overlap, light, avoiding moving people, avoiding mirrors showing the camera.
- Stitching: joining overlapping shots into one sphere, fixing seams and a tilted horizon, how to retouch the stand out of the floor.
- Publishing: link spots with arrows, add short captions, write alt text for each spot, keep file sizes small so it loads on a phone, set the privacy and sharing options.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Stitch, fix and build the tour
1. Stitch each spot. Check seams, horizon and colour.
2. Blur faces and number plates and remove anything on the hide list.
3. Build the tour: start spot, links between spots, one caption each, one alt text each, and a simple map.
4. Test on a phone with slow data and on a computer.
Evidence: stitched images and the draft tour link.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Viewing circle
1. Peers walk your tour and say where they got lost or bored.
2. Count: load time, number of spots, number of confusing links.
3. A mentor with photo or property experience reviews exposure, seams and rights.
Evidence: viewing notes, counts, reviewed rights checklist.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Fix the confusing links, seams and slow images.
2. Ask the space owner and three visitors to use it and give a 1-to-5 score for ease and truth to the real place.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: publish, improve, or keep private.
Evidence: visitor scores and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the space, the capture plan, the finished tour, the visitor scores and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Virtual Event Planning; Online Car Dealership Operations; Virtual Interior Design Consulting.
3. Contribute back: share your capture checklist, hide-list template or alt-text examples so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Tour of a 6-room hall. 8 spots, each 6 photos if the camera is a normal phone, or 1 if a 360 camera. Phone route = 48 images; 360 route = 8.
2. Stitching: 2 seams out of 8 spots show an error, so 25% need a fix.
3. File size: 8 images at 6 MB = 48 MB. Compressed to 1.5 MB each = 12 MB. On a 2 Mbit/s link, 12 MB (96 Mbit) takes about 48 seconds if loaded at once; lazy loading shows the first spot in under 5 seconds.
4. Visitor scores: 10 visitors rated ease 4.2 average (sum 42/10) and truth to place 4.5 (sum 45/10).

## Case exercise
A visitor's face and a car number plate are visible in spot 5, and the owner wants a private office showing a visible paper on a desk hidden. Questions: What must you do before publishing? Who decides? Write the hide list and the steps.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is a 360 image? 2. Why keep the camera at the same height? 3. What must you get before capturing a private space? 4. What is stitching? 5. Why add alt text to each spot? 6. Why compress images? 7. What should be blurred? 8. Why test on slow data? 9. How did you measure visitor satisfaction? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. Do not publish interiors of homes or offices without written permission. Blur people and plates.
