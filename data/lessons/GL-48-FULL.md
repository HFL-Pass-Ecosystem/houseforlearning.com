# GL-48 Nanotechnology in Medicine (Awareness Level): full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.
This is an AWARENESS course. You learn how things work, the rules and the ethics. You do not practise treatment, diagnosis or clinical work, and you use only public, made-up or simulated material.
Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a student or professional who wants a sober picture of promise and risk.
1. Market scan: Read two public reviews from research or regulatory bodies on nanomaterials in medicine. Note the stated benefits, unknowns and safety questions.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Pick three public case studies, for example a drug-delivery method, a diagnostic strip and a coating. Read only open sources.
2. For each, write what problem it addresses, how the nanoscale helps, what the evidence is, and what safety questions remain.
3. Draw a simple scale chart from a hair width down to a virus and a molecule.
4. Write a table of benefits and risks for each case.
Evidence: Three case summaries and a scale chart.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Materials: at the nanoscale, materials can behave differently, which gives new uses and new questions about how they move in the body and the environment.
- Safety: testing looks at where materials go, how long they stay, and any harm, in the short and long term. Where data is missing, caution applies.
- Regulation: authorities assess nanomedicines case by case and ask for evidence on quality, safety and effect. Rules change; check current guidance.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Rate the strength of the evidence for each case from 1 to 5 and say why.
2. Write a plain-language paragraph explaining one case to a teenager.
3. List the questions a regulator would ask.
Evidence: Evidence ratings and plain paragraph.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Hold a short journal-club: each learner explains one source in 3 minutes.
2. Peers ask what the evidence does not show.
3. Agree one open question per case.
Evidence: Open-questions list.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: evidence ratings justified, clarity of the plain paragraph, regulator questions listed, peer rating.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Case 1: targeted delivery. Problem: reaching a tissue. Evidence: strong in trials. Safety question: how long does it stay in the body?
2. Case 2: a diagnostic strip. Evidence: moderate; limits: false negatives.
3. Scale: hair 70,000 nm, virus 100 nm, molecule 1 nm.
4. Plain paragraph: 'Tiny particles can carry medicine to where it is needed, but we must be sure they leave the body safely.'

## Case exercise
A news item says a nanomaterial 'cures' a disease based on a small test. Questions: What evidence rating (1 to 5) do you give? What is missing? What would a regulator ask? Write a one-page response in plain words.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is a nanometre compared with a hair? 2. Why can nanoscale materials behave differently? 3. What safety questions are asked? 4. Why is evidence rating useful? 5. What does the regulator look at? 6. What is a benefit and a risk in one case? 7. Why keep reading current guidance? 8. What is outside this course? 9. What did the journal club teach you? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Review of nanomaterials and medical products by the national authority; claims in advertising.
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
Awareness only. Open sources only. No health claims, no product promotion. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
