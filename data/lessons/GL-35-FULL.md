# GL-35 Online Library Building: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a learner who wants a trustworthy set of free readings in one subject.
1. Market scan: Look at 5 open libraries or collections. Note how items are arranged, whether licences are stated, and how easy it is to find something.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Pick a subject and collect 30 items that are free to use: public-domain works or items with an open licence.
2. For each item record title, creator, date, source link and the licence, copied exactly as published.
3. Design a simple catalogue: subject, level, language, format.
4. Publish a draft catalogue as a shared spreadsheet or page for testing.
Evidence: Catalogue with licence column complete for all items.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Licensing: open licences allow reuse under conditions, such as giving credit. Public domain means no rights remain, but the rules differ by country. Never add an item whose licence you cannot confirm.
- Metadata: the labels that make an item findable. Be consistent: same spelling, same date format, same subject words.
- Accessibility: use clear headings, good contrast, captions for audio and video, and text that can be enlarged.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Check every item opens, and note accessibility: text size, contrast, captions, readable by screen readers.
2. Invite 5 test readers to find three items each; time how long it takes.
3. Fix the three most common problems.
Evidence: Test results and fixes list.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Show your catalogue to curators.
2. Peers try to find an item in under a minute.
3. Agree changes to labels and layout.
Evidence: Revised label list.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: items with a confirmed licence, average time to find an item, accessibility problems fixed, reader rating.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Subject: local history. Pick 30 items.
2. Record: title, creator, date, link, licence text copied exactly, e.g. 'Public domain' or 'Open licence, credit the creator'.
3. Test: 5 readers find 3 items each; average time 2.5 minutes; target under 1 minute.
4. Fix: add subject and level labels and a search box.

## Case exercise
You find a popular item whose licence is unclear. Questions: Do you add it? What do you do to find out? What do you tell readers meanwhile? Write the rule for the team in three lines, and fix two labels in the catalogue.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is metadata? 2. Why record the licence for each item? 3. What does public domain mean, and why check by country? 4. Name two accessibility basics. 5. What do you do if you cannot confirm a licence? 6. Why test with real readers? 7. How do you make labels consistent? 8. What is your catalogue's strength? 9. What would you add next? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Copyright and open-licence terms of each item; rules on public-domain status differ by country; accessibility duties for public services.
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
Only items with a confirmed free licence. Always credit creators as the licence asks. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
