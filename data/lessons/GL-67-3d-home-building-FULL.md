# GL-67 3D Home Building: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This is a review course of printed-construction methods. It does not qualify anyone to design or build a house. Building codes and engineers are required for any real structure. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What a printed building is
Goal: understand the method and the questions to ask.
1. In your own words: a large printer places layers of a cement-like mix (or other material) following a digital plan to form walls.
2. List what is printed (usually walls) and what is still done by hand or other methods (foundation, roof, doors, windows, wiring, plumbing, finishes).
3. Find three public project reports. Record location, year, area, stated cost and what it included.
4. Write one question to answer by the end.
Evidence: notes and the three project records.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: what counted in the cost, which parts were not printed, what code approval was needed.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a project comparison table, a cost per square metre table, a materials and codes note and a risk note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the project comparison
Goal: compare projects fairly.
1. Make a table: project, year, floor area in m2, wall material, time to print walls, total cost (with currency and year), what the cost includes.
2. Convert to cost per square metre and mark which costs are incomparable (land, finishes, labour rates).
3. Convert currencies with a stated date or keep the local currency and say so.
4. Mark each number 'reported' or 'estimate'.
Evidence: the comparison table with sources.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Materials, codes and safety
Short modules:
- Materials: cement-based mixes, clay and earth mixes and others; each has strength, drying time and weather limits. Reinforcement and joints still matter.
- Codes: buildings must meet local codes for strength, fire, energy, and accessibility, and must be approved by the authority; ask whether printed walls are accepted where you live.
- Safety: machine hazards on site, working at height, dust, curing in hot or cold weather.
- Social view: jobs for local builders, training, and whether the home is affordable for those who need it.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Compare with a standard method
Goal: see the real advantage, if any.
1. Find the cost per m2 of a conventional wall in your region (local price list).
2. Compare time, waste, labour and quality.
3. List conditions where printing helps (repeating designs, remote sites, speed) and where it does not (small one-off jobs, heavy finishing).
4. Write a one-page finding.
Evidence: the comparison and the finding.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Case review circle
Goal: challenge the evidence.
1. Present the table in 6 minutes.
2. Peers ask what costs were left out.
3. A construction mentor adds missing factors such as foundations and inspection.
4. Update your finding.
Evidence: peer questions and updated finding.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Write the briefing
Goal: produce a plain briefing for a housing planner.
1. Summarise what printed building offers today and the limits.
2. Give a cost range for your region with clear caveats.
3. List what to ask a supplier or builder before trusting a claim.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: briefing.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the cases, the comparison, the risks and the briefing.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a housing planner or a builder.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: 3D Printing, Plastic Alternatives, Greenhouse Gas Auditing.
3. Contribute back: share your project comparison template or supplier question list so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Project X: 60 m2 house, total cost 18,000 = 300 per m2 (currency and year stated).
2. Project Y: 120 m2, total 72,000 = 600 per m2.
3. Walls only: printed walls cost 4,200 for 60 m2 of floor = 70 per m2 of floor; conventional walls 5,100 = 85 per m2. Difference = 18%.
4. Time: printed walls 3 days vs conventional 15 days = 80% less time for walls.

## Case exercise
A planner is told printed homes cost half as much. Questions: What was included in the claim? Which parts were not printed? Which code approval is needed? Write a half-page briefing with the questions to ask a supplier.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is usually printed? 2. What is still done by hand? 3. Why is cost per m2 hard to compare? 4. Why do codes matter? 5. Name two site safety hazards. 6. Why mark numbers 'reported' or 'estimate'? 7. When does printing help? 8. What should you ask a supplier? 9. Why convert currencies with a date? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Awareness and review only: no structural design advice. Any real building needs a licensed engineer, approval and inspection.
