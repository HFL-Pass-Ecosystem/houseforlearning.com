# GL-64 Water Management and Conservation: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module audits water use on a home or small site. No plumbing alterations are required; use meters, buckets and observation. Drinking water treatment must be done only by qualified people. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Where water goes
Goal: learn the main uses and pick a site.
1. List uses at a home or small site: drinking, cooking, washing, toilets, laundry, garden, cleaning, leaks.
2. Learn typical units: litres per day, cubic metres (1 cubic metre = 1,000 litres).
3. Pick a site with permission. Collect the last 6 bills or meter readings.
4. Write the question: where can the site save water without harm to health?
Evidence: the use list and bill summary.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: how will you measure, what is the risk to health, how do you count water the meter cannot see.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a water balance table, a leak check record, a savings plan, a cost sheet and a quality note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Measure and build the water balance
Goal: measure real use and split it by activity.
1. Read the meter at the same time each day for 7 days and compute litres per day.
2. Measure flows: time how long a bucket of known volume takes to fill at each tap and shower. Flow = volume divided by time.
3. Estimate each activity: flow x minutes x uses. Add them and compare with the meter total.
4. Explain any gap (leaks, mistakes in estimates).
Evidence: the 7-day readings and the balance table.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Measurement, reuse and quality
Short modules:
- Measurement: meters, sub-meters, bucket tests, leak tests (no use for an hour, does the meter still move?).
- Reuse: rainwater harvesting for gardens, grey water for gardens only where local rules allow, never for drinking.
- Quality: clean storage, covers against mosquitoes, regular cleaning; drinking water treatment and testing only by qualified people.
- Rules: water rights, rain harvesting and grey water laws differ locally.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Find leaks and quick wins
Goal: cut waste in the cheapest way first.
1. Run the overnight meter test and a toilet dye test (a few drops of food colour in the tank; colour in the bowl without flushing means a leak).
2. List fixes by cost: washers, aerators, shorter showers, full loads, timers for gardens.
3. Carry out only safe fixes with the owner's consent and record before and after.
4. Check again after one week.
Evidence: the leak records and before and after readings.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Audit-sharing circle
Goal: compare audits and learn.
1. Present the balance and the first savings.
2. Peers check if the numbers add up.
3. A water professional mentor adds missing risks.
4. Update the plan.
Evidence: peer checks and mentor comments.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Savings plan and cost
Goal: show a plan the owner can follow.
1. Estimate litres saved per day for each action and the yearly cash saved = litres x 365 x price per cubic metre divided by 1,000. Currency shown.
2. Cost of each action and payback.
3. Add rain-harvesting size: roof area x rainfall x 0.8 efficiency (check this rule with a local adviser).
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: savings plan, payback and rain estimate.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the audit, the leaks, the savings and the plan.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from the site owner.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Greenhouse Gas Auditing, Plastic Alternatives, Vertical Farming.
3. Contribute back: share your water balance template or leak test sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Meter readings: 4,210 m3 to 4,214 m3 in 10 days = 4 m3 = 4000 litres, i.e. 400 litres a day.
2. Shower flow: 9-litre bucket fills in 45 s = 12 litres per minute. 8 minutes = 96 litres per shower.
3. Leak: toilet leaking 3 litres an hour = 72 litres a day = 2.16 m3 a month.
4. Saving: fixing that leak at a price of 1.50 per m3 = 2.16 x 1.50 = 3.24 a month (currency shown).

## Case exercise
A small workshop pays for 120 m3 a month but staff estimate only 80. Questions: How do you find the 40 m3 gap? Which checks come first? Which reuse is allowed locally? Write a one-page savings plan with payback.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. How many litres in a cubic metre? 2. How do you find a tap flow? 3. How do you test for a leak? 4. What is grey water and where may it be used? 5. Why never use stored non-drinking water for drinking? 6. How do you compute yearly savings? 7. Why check after one week? 8. Name two quick wins. 9. Why compare the meter and the estimate? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. No change to plumbing without a qualified person. No drinking-water treatment advice. Use anonymous addresses in shared files.
