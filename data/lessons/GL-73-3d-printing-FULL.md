# GL-73 3D Printing: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module designs, prints and tests three useful parts. Printers are hot and have moving parts, and melted plastic gives off fumes: use a ventilated room, never leave a printer running unattended, and keep hands clear. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What 3D printing can make and what it cannot
Goal: choose three parts to make.
1. Describe the basic method: a printer builds a part layer by layer from a digital model.
2. List good first parts: a hook, a clip, a box lid, a cable guide, a spare knob, a stand.
3. Check what each part must do: carry a load, stay in the sun, touch food (usually not suitable), or only hold something light.
4. Choose three parts and write what each must do, in measurable terms.
Evidence: notes and the three part specifications.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: what load does it carry, which material suits, how long will it print.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: three models, three printed parts, a test record, a cost per part sheet and a safety checklist.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Model and print part one
Goal: learn the whole path from model to part.
1. Use free modelling software to create the model with exact measurements in millimetres.
2. Export the model and use free slicing software to set layer height, walls, infill, supports and speed.
3. Print a small calibration part first to check size and fit.
4. Print part one, measure it with a ruler or caliper and compare with the model.
Evidence: the model file, the slicer settings and the measured part.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Modelling, materials and safe operation
Short modules:
- Modelling: design for printing: keep overhangs gentle, avoid thin walls, add small gaps (about 0.2 to 0.4 mm) for parts that must fit together.
- Materials: common filaments differ in strength, heat resistance and ease; check the material's safety sheet; food-contact use needs a suitable material and process.
- Safe operation: hot end and bed are hot, fumes need ventilation, no loose clothes, use a flat stable table, do not leave it unattended, have a fire plan.
- Settings: more walls and infill add strength; smaller layers add detail and time.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Print and refine parts two and three
Goal: improve by changing one thing at a time.
1. Print part two. Change one setting from part one, such as infill or orientation.
2. Print part three with a second material if you have one.
3. Record print time, weight of material, failures and fixes.
4. Fix at least one failed print by changing the model or the settings.
Evidence: the print log with failures.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Print-clinic circle
Goal: solve problems together.
1. Bring a failed print. The circle diagnoses it (warping, stringing, poor first layer, layer shift).
2. A mentor with printing experience demonstrates one fix.
3. Swap one good part to check fit.
4. Update your notes.
Evidence: clinic notes and the fix list.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Test the parts and cost them
Goal: check the parts do their job and price them.
1. Test each part against its specification: load test with weights, fit test, heat test within a safe limit.
2. Cost per part = material grams x price per gram + power used + printer wear share + your time. Currency shown.
3. Compare with buying a similar part.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: test record and cost per part sheet.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the three parts, the tests, the costs and the decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a person who uses one of your parts.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: 3D Home Building, Smart Apparel, Plastic Alternatives.
3. Contribute back: share your part specification template or print-fault guide so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Part: 25 g of filament; price 20 per kg = 0.02 per g, so material = 0.50. Print time 2 hours at 150 W = 0.3 kWh x 0.20 = 0.06 energy.
2. Printer wear and time share: 0.40; total cost = 0.96 (currency shown).
3. Fit gap: shaft 8.00 mm, hole 8.30 mm gives a gap of 0.30 mm, inside 0.2 to 0.4 mm.
4. Load test: part held 6 kg, needed 3 kg, safety margin = 100%.

## Case exercise
A print warps and a lid does not fit. Questions: What causes warping? Which setting do you change first? What does the part need to do? Compare print cost with buying. Write a one-page fault note.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. How does a printer build a part? 2. Why print a calibration part? 3. Why add small gaps for fitting parts? 4. What does infill change? 5. Why ventilate the room? 6. Why never leave a printer unattended? 7. What is warping? 8. How do you compute cost per part? 9. Why change one setting at a time? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Ventilate, never leave the printer unattended, keep hands clear. Do not print weapons or safety-critical parts. Respect others' designs and licences.
