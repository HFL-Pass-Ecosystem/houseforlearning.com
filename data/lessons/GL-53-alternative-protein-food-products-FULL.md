# GL-53 Alternative Protein Food Products: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module compares plant-based and fermented protein foods. It is not nutrition or medical advice. Check food and labelling rules locally. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Why people look for other proteins
Goal: understand the reasons and pick three products to compare.
1. List why people choose other proteins: health choices, cost, religion or culture, animal welfare, environment, allergies.
2. Walk a shop or market. Write 10 protein products (beans, tofu, tempeh, seitan, mycoprotein, pulses, eggs, fish, etc.), price per 100 g of protein (with currency) and who buys them.
3. Pick three alternative products to compare in the module.
4. Write a one-page question: who is this comparison for and what do they need to know?
Evidence: the shop table and the three chosen products.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is the buyer, what counts as better, which source will you trust.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a comparison table, a cost sheet, a label review, a tasting record and a short regulation note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the comparison table
Goal: compare cost, protein, nutrition and rules for three products.
1. From each label copy per 100 g: energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate, fibre, salt. Use the label, not claims on the front.
2. Cost per 20 g of protein = (pack price divided by pack protein grams) times 20.
3. Note ingredients, allergens, processing steps and shelf life.
4. Note the sources for each fact and the date you checked them.
Evidence: a comparison table with sources.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Food science, labelling and regulation
Short modules:
- Food science: proteins are chains of amino acids; plant proteins vary in which amino acids are low, and mixing foods (for example grains with pulses) can balance them. Heat, salt, acid and fermentation change texture and taste.
- Labelling: names must not mislead, allergens must be clear, claims such as 'high protein' follow local rules. Write the rule you must follow and who told you.
- Regulation: some new protein types need approval before sale in some countries. Check the approvals list of your country's food authority.
- Safety: fermented foods need clean method and control of time and temperature.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Tasting and texture trial
Goal: test how the three products behave in one simple recipe.
1. Choose one simple dish (a curry, a wrap, a stir-fry). Cook it with each protein using the same method.
2. Record cooking time, loss of weight, texture, taste and what needs more seasoning.
3. Cost one portion of each dish. Add the currency.
4. Ask 5 volunteers, with their agreement and any allergy checked, to score taste and texture 1 to 5.
Evidence: cooking record, portion cost and the scores.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Tasting-and-debate circle
Goal: argue fairly and learn from different views.
1. Present your table. Peers choose a side: best value, best taste, best nutrition.
2. Debate with rules: every claim needs a source.
3. A food technologist or dietitian mentor says which claims are too strong.
4. Write down two views you changed your mind about.
Evidence: debate notes and mentor comments.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Write the buyer's guide
Goal: turn the comparison into a one-page guide for a shopper or canteen buyer.
1. Rank the three products by cost, nutrition, taste and ease of cooking. Explain the ranking.
2. List the cautions: allergens, salt level, new-food rules.
3. Write the guide in plain words and the reader's language.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision about what you would test next.
Evidence: the buyer's guide and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the table, the dish trial, the guide and what you still do not know.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a shopper or canteen buyer.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Lab-Grown Meat (Awareness Level), Vertical Farming, Ghost Kitchens.
3. Contribute back: share your comparison table or tasting score sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Product A: 200 g pack price 3.00, protein 20 g per 100 g = 40 g per pack. Cost per 20 g protein = 3.00 / 40 x 20 = 1.50.
2. Product B: 400 g pack price 2.80, protein 8 g per 100 g = 32 g. Cost per 20 g = 2.80 / 32 x 20 = 1.75.
3. Product C: 500 g pack price 4.50, protein 12 g per 100 g = 60 g. Cost per 20 g = 4.50 / 60 x 20 = 1.50. Best value is C.
4. Portion cost: dish uses 150 g of C = 150 / 500 x 4.50 = 1.35 plus other ingredients 0.80 = 2.15 per portion (same currency).

## Case exercise
A canteen serves 200 portions a day and wants one meatless day a week. Using the three products, which would you choose and why? Check allergens, salt and taste scores. Compute the weekly cost of 200 portions. Write a one-page buyer's guide with cautions.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Why compare protein cost per 20 g? 2. Where should you read nutrition facts? 3. Name two ways to balance plant proteins. 4. What must a label show about allergens? 5. Why might a new protein need approval? 6. Why use the same method for each dish? 7. Why record sources and dates? 8. What is salt level and why watch it? 9. Name one risk in fermented foods. 10. What would you test next?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. No health claims or diet advice. Check allergies before any tasting and get consent. Keep claims tied to labels and public sources.
