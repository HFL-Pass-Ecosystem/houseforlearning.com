# GL-68 Space Tourism Awareness: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This is an awareness course built on public information. It does not train anyone to fly or to sell flights. Facts, prices and rules change quickly; check the date on every source. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What space tourism is today
Goal: separate the real offers from the hopes.
1. List the kinds of experience in public reports: short suborbital flights, orbital stays, high-altitude balloon rides and ground-based space experiences.
2. For each kind write: how long, how high, who may go, price range reported (with currency and date), and how many people have gone.
3. Mark each fact 'reported', 'estimate' or 'plan'.
4. Write a question: who is the customer and what are they really buying?
Evidence: the table of experiences with dates and marks.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: what is proven, what is promised, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a market note, a safety and regulation note, a sample product brief and a risk list.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Study safety and regulation
Goal: understand what protects travellers and what does not.
1. Read public statements of aviation and space regulators about passenger flights. Note whether participants fly at their own risk and what 'informed consent' means.
2. List the health screening and training in public reports.
3. List past incidents from public sources, stated plainly and respectfully, and what changed after them.
4. Write three questions a traveller should ask before buying.
Evidence: the safety notes and the three questions.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Safety, regulation and market
Short modules:
- Safety: launch and re-entry are the most dangerous parts; vehicles are new and flights are few, so safety records are short.
- Regulation: rules differ by country; some give a 'learning period' where traveller protection is lighter. Check the current position.
- Market: price, customer wealth, insurance, environmental effect and public opinion all matter.
- Ethics: environment, fairness, honest advertising.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the sample product brief
Goal: design on paper a responsible, honest offer from a ground-based or near-ground experience.
1. Choose a safe related product: an educational ground experience, a planetarium style show, a high-altitude balloon tour operated by a licensed operator, or a booking service that connects travellers to licensed providers.
2. Write the brief: customer, promise, what is included, what is not, safety statement, refund rules, price with currency.
3. Write the risk statement in plain words and the truthful marketing rules.
4. Test the brief on three readers and ask what they think they are buying.
Evidence: the product brief and three reader notes.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Debate circle
Goal: argue both sides with sources.
1. Motion: 'Space tourism should be allowed to grow with light rules.' Groups defend and oppose.
2. Each claim needs a source.
3. A mentor from aviation or policy judges the evidence.
4. Write down what changed your mind.
Evidence: debate notes.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Write the awareness briefing
Goal: explain it to a general reader.
1. Write two pages: what exists, how safe it is, who regulates it, who it is for, what it costs, and what it does to the environment.
2. Add three things to watch in the next year.
3. Make sure every number has a date and a source.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: the briefing.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the table, the safety findings, the brief and the briefing.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a general reader.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Boat and Helicopter Tourism, Nanosatellites and Space Data, Holographic and Immersive Theatre.
3. Contribute back: share your experience table or traveller question list so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. A report counts 600 seats flown. If 36 people flew twice, those 36 are counted twice, so unique people = 600 - 36 = 564. Always ask whether a figure counts seats or people.
2. Price range: 250,000 to 450,000 (reported, with date and currency). Range width = 200,000; midpoint = 350,000.
3. Risk statement: if 1 flight in 100 has a serious event, over 300 flights expect 3 events (illustration only, not a real figure).
4. Refund rule: weather delay of more than 72 hours gives full refund; with 40 bookings at 5,000 deposit, maximum refund = 200,000.

## Case exercise
A start-up advertises 'safe space flights for everyone'. Questions: What claims cannot be proven? What would informed consent say? What could be a responsible ground-based product? Write a half-page reply with the honest wording.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name three kinds of space experience. 2. Which parts of a flight are most risky? 3. What is informed consent? 4. Why are safety records short? 5. Why mark facts 'plan'? 6. Name one ethics question. 7. Why test the brief on readers? 8. What should a refund rule say? 9. Why do prices need dates? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Awareness only: no flight training or sales claims. Do not promise safety. Every figure needs a source and date.
