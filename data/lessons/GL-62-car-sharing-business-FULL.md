# GL-62 Car Sharing Business: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module models a small shared-car fleet. Insurance, licences and data rules vary by country and must be confirmed with an adviser before any real service. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Why people share cars
Goal: find who would use a shared car and for what trips.
1. List the reasons: occasional use, cost, no parking, young drivers, short stays.
2. Interview 5 non-owners and 5 owners about trips they make: purpose, distance, time of day, cost.
3. Estimate what a person pays yearly to own a car: depreciation, fuel, insurance, tax, parking, repair (use public figures, with currency).
4. Write the idea: a pilot of 3 to 5 cars in one area.
Evidence: interview notes and the ownership cost table.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is responsible for damage, how are drivers checked, what is the price.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a utilisation model, a pricing model, an insurance and rules checklist, a driver-check process and a decision note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the utilisation model
Goal: estimate how often each car is used.
1. Choose hours per day when a car could be booked.
2. Utilisation = booked hours divided by available hours. Set a low, middle and high case (for example 15, 25 and 35 percent).
3. Estimate trips per day and average trip length.
4. Show the effect of one idle day per week for service.
Evidence: utilisation sheet.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Insurance, telematics and fleet care
Short modules:
- Insurance: you need cover for each driver and each trip; check what is covered, excess amounts, and what drivers pay after damage.
- Telematics: location, fuel or charge level, door lock and trip data. Collect only what you need, tell users, keep it secure, and delete it on schedule.
- Fleet care: cleaning between bookings, regular service, tyre and brake checks, accident process.
- Driver checks: valid licence checked by a lawful method, age rules, behaviour rules.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Design the booking and check-in process
Goal: make the service simple and safe.
1. Write the booking steps: sign-up, licence check, book, unlock, drive, return, report.
2. Create a check-in sheet with photos of damage before and after.
3. Write the damage and fine process and how the user can appeal.
4. Test it with three classmates borrowing a private car with the owner's agreement, or by role-play.
Evidence: the process, check sheet and test notes.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Pricing circle
Goal: test the price against real people.
1. Present three price models: per hour, per kilometre, a mix.
2. Peers say what they would pay for sample trips.
3. A mentor says what costs you forgot.
4. Pick the best model.
Evidence: price votes and the chosen model.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Model profit and decide
Goal: see if the fleet can pay for itself.
1. Cost per car per month: depreciation or lease, insurance, service, cleaning, parking, data, support. Currency shown.
2. Revenue = bookable hours x utilisation x price per hour.
3. Break-even utilisation = monthly cost divided by (bookable hours x price). Compare with your cases.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: profit model and decision.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the demand, the model, the process and the decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a car owner or a possible user.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Micro-Mobility Services, Electric Car and EV Basics, Car Charging Stations.
3. Contribute back: share your utilisation sheet or check-in sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Bookable hours per day 16; booked 4 hours = 25% utilisation. 
2. Revenue per car per month at 4 h/day, 30 days, price 6 per hour: 4 x 30 x 6 = 720 (currency shown).
3. Cost per car per month: lease 300, insurance 120, service 40, cleaning 60, parking 80, data 10, support 40 = 650.
4. Break-even utilisation = 650 / (16 x 30 x 6) = 22.6%.

## Case exercise
A pilot of 3 cars reaches 20 percent utilisation in month 2. Questions: Profit or loss per car? Which price model do users prefer? What goes on the check-in sheet? What data do you not store? Write a one-page decision note.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Define utilisation. 2. What is break-even utilisation? 3. Why take photos before and after? 4. What data should you not collect? 5. Why check licences? 6. What does insurance excess mean? 7. Name two fleet care tasks. 8. Why test three price models? 9. What happens if utilisation is half? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Paper model and role-play only. Do not store licence numbers or ID in shared files. Confirm insurance and data law with an adviser.
