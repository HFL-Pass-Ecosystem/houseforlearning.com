# GL-51 Senior At-Home Care: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This is an awareness and routine-planning course, not nursing or medical training. Practise only with volunteers who agree, or with written role-play cases. Never give medical advice. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs care at home and what a good day looks like
Goal: understand the needs of an older person at home and describe a safe, dignified daily routine.
1. Read your country's public guidance on care at home. Write down in your own words what 'dignity', 'consent' and 'independence' mean in care.
2. List the daily activities that matter: waking, washing, dressing, meals, drinks, medicines (given by the person or a trained adult), movement, company, sleep.
3. Interview one older volunteer, with their agreement, about what a good day means to them. Write only what they allow you to share, and no names.
4. Write a one-page plan: who the case is about (a made-up person), the five most important parts of the day, and what could go wrong.
Evidence: your guidance notes and the one-page plan (no real names).

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: what is the person's choice here, what could cause a fall or an injury, who else must be told.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a daily routine, a carer checklist, a risk list, a short observation record from a role-play, and a handover note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the daily-care routine and carer checklist
Goal: turn the day into a clear routine with a checklist another carer can follow.
1. Write the routine in time blocks. For each block write: what happens, what help is needed, what the person decides for themselves.
2. Build the carer checklist with tick boxes: hands washed, door and walkways clear, drinks within reach, call button or phone within reach, medicines handled only as the family or the health professional has set out.
3. Add a 'stop and call' list: signs that need a trained professional or the emergency number (fall, chest pain, sudden confusion, not responding, choking). Write the emergency number for your country.
4. Walk through the routine in a role-play with a classmate acting as the person. Time each block and note where the checklist was unclear.
Evidence: the routine, the checklist, the stop-and-call list and the role-play notes.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Safeguarding, safe moving and dignity
Short modules:
- Safeguarding: what abuse and neglect look like (physical, emotional, money, neglect), why you report a worry to the right person at once, and why you never promise to keep a safety secret.
- Moving and handling: keep your back straight, let the person do what they can, use only the aids you are trained on, ask for training before lifting anyone. Never lift alone if it is unsafe.
- Dignity: knock before entering, explain before you act, ask before you touch, cover the person during washing, speak to the person, not about them.
- Privacy: keep notes without names where possible, keep what you learn private, share only with those who need it to care.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Run the routine as a supervised practice
Goal: practise the routine in a safe setting and record what you learn.
1. With a mentor watching, use the routine in role-play or in a training setting for at least five sessions.
2. After each session write: what went well, one thing that felt rushed, one thing that felt undignified (if any), what you change.
3. Practise a handover: in two minutes tell the next carer what happened, what changed and what to watch. Use the same order each time.
4. Test your stop-and-call list by reading five made-up situations and saying out loud what you would do.
Evidence: five session notes and a handover script.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Carers' circle
Goal: learn from other carers and sharpen your checklist.
1. Swap checklists with a peer. Mark one missing item and one unclear item on their sheet.
2. Share one hard moment from your practice (a refusal, a worry, a tired carer). The circle says how they would respond kindly.
3. Talk about carer wellbeing: sleep, breaks, asking for help, and why a tired carer makes more mistakes.
4. A mentor with care experience reviews your stop-and-call list.
Evidence: the peer marks, the mentor's comments and your revised checklist.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish, measure and write the family handbook
Goal: turn your work into a two-page handbook a family or new carer could use.
1. Fix the problems the circle found.
2. Write a two-page handbook: the routine, the checklist, the stop-and-call list and who to ring.
3. Measure: how many checklist items were missed in session 1 compared with session 5.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision: what would you improve before using it with a real family.
Evidence: the handbook and the before-and-after count.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the routine, the checklist, what you learned, and what is still unsafe or unknown.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a mentor or a volunteer role-play partner.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Online Therapy Support (Awareness Level), Virtual Medical Appointment Systems, Skincare Spa and Clinic Management.
3. Contribute back: share your checklist template or handover script so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Day blocks: waking and washing 60 min, breakfast 45, medicines handled as set out by the family or health professional 10, walk 30, lunch 45, rest 90, company 60, evening meal 45, evening care 45. Total = 430 min = 7.2 h of active care in the day.
2. Checklist miss rate: session 1 missed 6 of 20 items = 30%; session 5 missed 1 of 20 = 5%. Improvement = 25 percentage points.
3. Handover: 4 facts in 2 minutes (what happened, what changed, what to watch, who to call). 4 facts x 3 handovers a day = 12 facts a week-day to record.
4. Fall-risk walk: 8 hazards found (loose rug, cable, poor light, wet floor, clutter, no rail, high shelf, low chair); 6 fixed at once, 2 need the family. Fixed share = 75%.

## Case exercise
A made-up person, aged 82, lives alone with a family visit each evening. Mornings are rushed and drinks are often left out of reach. Questions: Which three blocks need the most help and why? Which hazards would you fix first? What goes on the stop-and-call list? How do you keep the person's choices at the centre? Write a one-page plan for the family.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name three signs that need an emergency call. 2. Why do you ask before you touch? 3. What should you do if you worry someone is being harmed? 4. Why keep notes free of names? 5. What is a handover and why is order useful? 6. Name two things that reduce falls at home. 7. Why is a tired carer a risk? 8. What decisions should stay with the person? 9. When must you not lift alone? 10. What would you improve before real use?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, nursing or medicine advice. Practise only with consenting volunteers or made-up cases. Local care rules and any required checks on carers must be confirmed with an adviser before any paid work.
