# GL-65 Greenhouse Gas Auditing: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module builds a simple carbon footprint for a small organisation. It is a learning exercise, not an assured report. Methods and factors change; always use the latest published factors for your country. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What a carbon footprint is
Goal: explain the idea and pick an organisation.
1. Learn the idea: add up greenhouse gases from activities, expressed in kg or tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e).
2. Learn the scopes: Scope 1 is direct burning of fuel on site or in own vehicles; Scope 2 is bought electricity, heat or cooling; Scope 3 is everything else in the chain (travel, purchases, waste, commuting).
3. Pick a small organisation (a school, shop, workshop or office) with permission.
4. Write the boundary: which sites and which year you will count.
Evidence: notes and the boundary statement.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: what is in and out of scope, which data is missing, which factors you used and from where.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a data list, a calculation sheet, a factor register, a footprint summary and an improvement list.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Collect activity data
Goal: gather the numbers.
1. List activities: electricity (kWh), fuel (litres), gas (units), vehicle kilometres, flights, purchases, waste (kg), water (m3).
2. Collect 12 months of bills and records. Note any gaps and how you estimated them.
3. Record the unit and the source for each number.
4. Mark each number 'measured', 'estimated' or 'guess'.
Evidence: the data list with quality marks.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Scopes, emission factors and reporting
Short modules:
- Calculation: emissions = activity x emission factor. For example electricity in kWh x grid factor in kg CO2e per kWh.
- Factors: use official or well-reviewed published factors for your country and year, and write the source and year. Factors for grid electricity differ greatly between countries.
- Two ways for electricity: location-based (grid average) and market-based (contracts). Show the one you use and say why.
- Reporting: show the boundary, scopes, total, biggest sources, methods, exclusions and data quality.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the calculation sheet
Goal: turn data into emissions.
1. In a free spreadsheet make columns: activity, quantity, unit, factor, factor source and year, emissions in kg CO2e.
2. Sum by scope and total. Convert to tonnes (divide by 1,000).
3. Make a chart of the top five sources.
4. Test one number by hand to check the sheet.
Evidence: the calculation sheet and the chart.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Peer review of footprints
Goal: check each other's work like an auditor.
1. Swap sheets. Check units, factors, double counting and gaps.
2. Ask: is the boundary clear, are the factors the latest, is the data quality honest.
3. A mentor with sustainability experience marks the biggest weakness.
4. Correct your sheet.
Evidence: review notes and the corrected sheet.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Write the footprint summary and the improvement list
Goal: turn the numbers into action.
1. Write a two-page summary: boundary, total, scopes, top sources, data quality, limits.
2. List five improvements with expected saving in kg CO2e per year and cost. Currency shown.
3. Rank by saving per money spent.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: summary and improvement list.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the boundary, the total, the biggest source and the improvements.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from the organisation's manager.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Water Management and Conservation, Solar Power Design and Installation Basics, Plastic Alternatives.
3. Contribute back: share your calculation sheet template or factor register so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Electricity: 12,000 kWh x 0.45 kg CO2e per kWh (example factor; use your own country's latest factor) = 5,400 kg = 5.4 t.
2. Fuel: 800 litres x 2.7 kg CO2e per litre (example) = 2,160 kg = 2.16 t.
3. Total of these two = 7.56 t CO2e. Electricity share = 71%.
4. Improvement: cut electricity by 20% = 12,000 x 0.2 x 0.45 = 1,080 kg saved a year.

## Case exercise
A school has data for power, fuel, waste and travel but not for purchases. Questions: What is the boundary? Which numbers are measured, estimated or guesses? Which three improvements give most saving per money spent? Write a one-page summary stating limits.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What does CO2e mean? 2. Define Scope 1, 2 and 3. 3. How do you calculate emissions from an activity? 4. Why record factor source and year? 5. Location-based vs market-based electricity? 6. Why mark data quality? 7. How do you avoid double counting? 8. Name three Scope 3 sources. 9. How do you rank improvements? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Learning exercise only: do not publish a footprint as assured or verified. Use the latest published factors and show their source.
