# GL-03 Artificial Intelligence in Practice: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised responsible-AI principles, the risk approach of the European AI Act (dates below) and the OECD AI principles. This is not a licence or a certificate. AI output can be wrong. A human checks everything before it is used, and no decision about a person is left to AI alone. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free AI assistant within its free limits (check the free-limits register first), a free spreadsheet, a free document editor.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What AI can and cannot do at work
Goal: choose one real work task where an AI tool might save time without lowering quality.
1. Write in your own words what a modern AI language tool does: it predicts likely text from patterns in a very large amount of training text; it does not know things the way a person does and can state wrong things confidently.
2. List 10 tasks you or a local business do each week (drafting messages, summarising notes, translating, sorting data). Mark each: low risk, medium, high.
3. Pick one low-risk task you can measure (time, error count, quality score from 1 to 5).
4. Measure the task done by hand three times first: minutes taken and quality score. This is your baseline.
Evidence: task list with risk marks and the baseline table.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a baseline, an AI-assisted trial of 10 items, an error log, a human-review checklist and a gains table.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Try the tool on the task
1. Write clear instructions for the tool: the goal, the audience, the format, what to avoid, and an example of good output. Save every instruction version.
2. Run the task with the tool on 10 real or sample items. Use no personal or confidential data; replace names with placeholders.
3. For each output record: minutes, whether it was right, what was wrong (wrong fact, missing point, wrong tone, invented source).
4. Make a human-review checklist of the checks you did (facts, numbers, names, tone, anything that could hurt someone).
Evidence: instruction versions, 10-item results table, review checklist.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): AI basics, bias and privacy, human review
Short modules:
- AI basics: training data, prompts, why answers change, why tools invent things, what a source check means and how to do it.
- Bias and privacy: tools reflect the data they learned from; check outputs for unfair assumptions about gender, region, age or religion; never paste personal, health, financial or secret data into a public tool; know where your country's data rules apply.
- Human review: a person is responsible for the final result; high-risk uses (hiring, lending, health, safety) need a qualified human and often a legal rule; keep a record of what AI did and what a person changed.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Improve and repeat
1. Read your error log. Group the errors: facts, missing points, tone, invented sources.
2. Rewrite the instructions to cut the biggest error group. Add an example and a rule such as 'say when you are not sure'.
3. Run 10 new items with the improved instructions. Record minutes, errors and quality score again.
4. Show the output to the person who would use it and note one thing they liked and one thing they would change.
Evidence: second results table and user note.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Case discussion circle
1. Share before and after numbers: time saved, errors found, quality score.
2. Discuss one case where the tool would be unsafe and why (for example a medical or legal answer).
3. Agree a short 'rules of use' sheet for the task. The mentor checks it for privacy and fairness.
Evidence: gains table and the rules-of-use sheet.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish a rules-of-use sheet in one page: when to use the tool, when not, what to check, who signs off.
2. Compute gains: minutes saved per item multiplied by items per month, minus the time spent checking. Keep it honest.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: adopt, adopt with limits, or do not adopt.
Evidence: rules-of-use sheet, gains calculation, decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the task, the baseline, the trials, the errors you found, the rules of use and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Chatbots for Business; Privacy Protection Practice; Robotics Fundamentals.
3. Contribute back: share your instruction templates, review checklist or rules-of-use sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Task: summarise customer emails. By hand: 12 minutes per email, quality 4/5. With the tool: 5 minutes plus 2 minutes checking = 7. Saved 5 minutes per email.
2. Items per month: 150. Saved = 150 x 5 = 750 minutes = 12.5 hours.
3. Error log over 10 items: 2 invented details, 1 missed point, 1 wrong tone. Errors per item = 4/10 = 0.4. After improved instructions on 10 new items: 1 error, so 0.1.
4. Value check: 12.5 hours at 40 an hour = 500 a month, minus 15 for the tool plan (if paid) = 485 (same currency).

## Case exercise
The tool saves 5 minutes per item but 1 in 20 outputs contains an invented figure. The items go to customers. Questions: Adopt, adopt with limits, or reject? What check would you add? Who signs off? Write a one-page rules of use.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. In one sentence, how does a language tool produce text? 2. Why can it state something wrong confidently? 3. Why measure the task by hand first? 4. Name three things to check in every output. 5. What data must you never paste into a public tool? 6. Give one example of a high-risk use of AI. 7. What does 'a human is responsible' mean in practice? 8. Calculate: 12 minutes by hand, 5 with the tool plus 2 to check. Minutes saved per item? 9. How can bias show up in an output? 10. What was your adopt or do-not-adopt decision and why?

## Law check
Law check (31 Oct 2026, from public sources; confirm with an adviser): India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 has its Rules notified in November 2025 and switches on in phases; the consent-manager part starts about November 2026 and the main duties of organisations from about May 2027. The UAE personal-data law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) has been in force since January 2022, but sources disagree on whether its executive regulations are issued; check the UAE legislation portal and the UAE Data Office. The EU data-protection regulation (GDPR) is unchanged in its core principles. For AI: the EU AI Act is now on a delayed schedule. Under the 2026 amending regulation, rules for stand-alone high-risk uses (for example credit decisions about people) apply from 2 December 2027, rules for AI inside regulated products from 2 August 2028, and the transparency duties (telling people they deal with AI or AI-made content) have applied since 2 August 2026, with a grace period to 2 December 2026 for some older content-generating systems.

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. Never present AI output as certain. Never use AI to impersonate someone. Say clearly when text was AI-assisted.
