# GL-66 Vertical Farming: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module plans a small indoor grow system on paper and, if you have a safe place, as a tiny trial. Mains electricity and water mix badly: use only low-voltage ready-made lights or licensed electricians. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What vertical farming is and what grows well
Goal: understand the idea and choose a crop.
1. Describe the idea: plants grown in stacked layers indoors with controlled light, water and nutrients, without or with little soil.
2. List crops that suit it (leafy greens, herbs, microgreens) and crops that do not (tall or heavy fruiting crops, usually).
3. Visit a market to find the selling price per kilo (currency shown) and who buys.
4. Choose one crop and one buyer (a cafe, a canteen, a household group).
Evidence: notes, price table and the chosen crop.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: how much light and water does it need, who buys it, what does the energy cost.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a system plan, an energy and yield sheet, a cost sheet, a trial record (if possible) and a decision note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Design the small system
Goal: size a system for one shelf or rack.
1. Choose the area (for example 1 square metre per layer and 3 layers = 3 square metres of growing area).
2. Light: plants need a daily amount of light; set hours per day, and the light power per square metre from a plant-light datasheet (a typical leafy-green range is a few tens of watts per square metre; check the datasheet).
3. Water and nutrients: choose a simple method (a recirculating tray or a drip). Plan a reservoir and a way to check water level and nutrient strength.
4. Draw the rack with air gaps for airflow, and mark where electricity and water are kept apart.
Evidence: the system drawing and the sizing notes.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Lighting, nutrients and economics
Short modules:
- Lighting: plants use particular colours of light; use plant lights made for the job and keep them at the right height; heat from lamps must be managed.
- Nutrients: plants need nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and trace elements; too much salt burns roots; check strength with a simple meter and check the acidity of the water.
- Hygiene: clean trays and tanks to stop slime and disease; wash hands; keep pets away.
- Economics: electricity is often the biggest running cost; measure it.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Energy and yield sheet
Goal: estimate what the system uses and produces.
1. Energy per day = light power in kW x hours per day. Per cycle = daily energy x days.
2. Yield estimate: use a published range for your crop, then take the low end because beginners get less. Source and date noted.
3. Energy per kilo = cycle energy divided by kilos produced.
4. Cost per kilo = (energy cost + seeds + nutrients + water + labour + depreciation) divided by kilos. Currency shown.
Evidence: the energy and yield sheet.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Grower circle
Goal: learn from growers.
1. Present the plan to a group with an experienced grower.
2. They ask about pests, power cuts and humidity.
3. Record the three most useful tips.
4. Update the plan.
Evidence: grower notes and updated plan.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Trial and decide
Goal: test one small tray if safe, and decide.
1. Grow one tray of microgreens under a ready-made low-voltage light, if you have a safe place. Record sowing date, light hours, watering, harvest date and weight.
2. Compare the real result to the estimate.
3. Rework the cost per kilo with real numbers.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: trial record and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the system, the numbers, the trial and the decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a buyer such as a cafe or canteen.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Water Management and Conservation, Alternative Protein Food Products, Greenhouse Gas Auditing.
3. Contribute back: share your energy sheet or tray log template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Area 3 m2; light 40 W per m2 = 120 W = 0.12 kW. 16 hours a day = 1.92 kWh a day; 14-day cycle = 26.88 kWh.
2. Yield: microgreens 0.4 kg per m2 per cycle (low-end figure) x 3 m2 = 1.2 kg.
3. Energy per kg = 26.88 / 1.2 = 22.40 kWh per kg.
4. Cost per kg: energy 26.88 kWh x 0.20 = 5.38; seeds 2.00, nutrients 0.50, water 0.10, labour 4.00; total 11.98 / 1.2 kg = 9.98 per kg (currency shown).

## Case exercise
A cafe wants 5 kg of greens a week. Questions: How much area and how many layers? What is the energy cost? Is the price per kilo fair? What if the power is cut for a day? Write a one-page decision note.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is vertical farming? 2. Which crops suit it? 3. Why keep water and electricity apart? 4. Why is energy a big cost? 5. How do you compute cost per kilo? 6. Why take the low end of yield figures? 7. Why clean trays? 8. What does too much nutrient do? 9. What can go wrong in a power cut? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. No mains-voltage work without a licensed electrician. Food safety: wash hands, clean trays, sell nothing without checking local food rules.
