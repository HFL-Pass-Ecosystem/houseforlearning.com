# GL-43 Online Medical Supply and Equipment: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.
This is an AWARENESS course. You learn how things work, the rules and the ethics. You do not practise treatment, diagnosis or clinical work, and you use only public, made-up or simulated material.
Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a clinic or a family buying a simple, safe supply item.
1. Market scan: Pick one low-risk category such as bandages or blood-pressure monitors. Look at 5 public sellers. Note what product information, safety notices and returns policies they show.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Pick the category. Find the public rules for selling it in your country (many places class medical devices by risk).
2. Write a plain summary of the rules: what must be on the label, who may sell, what claims are not allowed.
3. Map the supply path: maker, importer, store, courier, buyer. Mark where records are kept.
4. Compare 3 suppliers by documents they provide, lead time, minimum order and price with currency.
Evidence: Regulation summary and supply map.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Device rules awareness: devices are grouped by risk and each group has different evidence and registration needs. Check with the authority in your country, and with an adviser.
- Storage: keep within the stated temperature and humidity, off the floor, away from sunlight and pests. Record checks.
- Traceability: every item should link to a batch so you can find it if something goes wrong.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Build a sample product page with required information and honest limits.
2. Design a batch-recording sheet: batch, expiry, storage, who received it, where it went.
3. Run a mock recall: show you can find every buyer of one batch within one hour using made-up records.
Evidence: Product page and mock recall record.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Question a panel of supplier representatives.
2. Ask what documents they give and how they handle a recall.
3. Add their answers to your checklist.
Evidence: Updated supplier checklist.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: time to trace a batch, supplier documents complete, label items correct, storage checks done.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Category: simple bandages (low risk). Summary: label shows name, batch, expiry, maker contact; no claims of healing.
2. Supply map: maker > importer > store > courier > buyer; batch code recorded at each step.
3. Supplier comparison: three suppliers; one lacks batch records, one has 6 weeks lead time, one meets both needs.
4. Mock recall: found all 12 buyers of batch B7 in 35 minutes using made-up records.

## Case exercise
A batch is found to have the wrong expiry date printed. Questions: What do you do first? Whom do you tell? How do you find affected buyers? What do you record? Write a one-page recall plan using made-up records.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Why are devices grouped by risk? 2. What must a label show? 3. What is traceability? 4. How do you store supplies safely? 5. What is a mock recall for? 6. Why compare supplier documents? 7. What claims must never be made? 8. What would you do if a batch is faulty? 9. What did the panel add? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Medical-device classification, registration, labelling, storage, traceability and recall duties of your country's authority.
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
Awareness only. No selling of real devices in the course. No claims that a product treats or cures anything. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
