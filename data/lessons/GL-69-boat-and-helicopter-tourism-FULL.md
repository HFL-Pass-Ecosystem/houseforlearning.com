# GL-69 Boat and Helicopter Tourism: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This course plans compliant tourist experiences on paper. Marine and aviation operations are strictly licensed. Learners do not operate boats or aircraft; they plan with licensed operators. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What guests want and what is possible
Goal: choose one experience and a guest group.
1. List experiences: harbour tour, sunset cruise, fishing trip, island transfer, scenic flight, heli transfer.
2. Interview 5 guests or tourists about what they would pay for, how long, what worries them.
3. List local operators and note, from public information, what licences they must hold.
4. Choose one experience and write a one-page idea.
Evidence: the experience list and guest notes.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is licensed, what is the weather limit, how many people are safe.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a licence and rules table, a safety briefing script, a pricing sheet and an itinerary plan.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Map the rules and licences
Goal: find who needs which permission.
1. Find the marine and aviation authorities for your area.
2. List what the vessel or aircraft, the crew and the operator must hold, as published by the authority. Note the date you checked.
3. List weather, passenger-number, lifejacket, insurance and no-fly or no-sail rules.
4. List who a tour planner contracts and who bears the risk.
Evidence: the rules table.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Safety, licensing and guest care
Short modules:
- Safety: written briefing before departure, lifejackets for every person, headcount, weather check, an emergency plan and a way to call for help.
- Licensing: operator and crew licences, vessel or aircraft certificates, insurance; a tour planner checks and keeps copies.
- Guest care: accessibility, seasickness, children, language, clear prices, a fair cancellation rule (weather).
- Environment: wildlife distance, noise, waste and fuel use.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Write the itinerary and the safety briefing
Goal: make a plan a licensed operator could run.
1. Write the itinerary with timings, meeting point, route and a bad-weather plan.
2. Write a 3-minute safety briefing script in plain language.
3. Write the headcount and emergency roles.
4. Test it with classmates as guests and fix unclear points.
Evidence: the itinerary, briefing script and test notes.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Operator panel
Goal: check the plan with practitioners.
1. Present to a panel including an operator or a safety officer.
2. They ask about weather decisions, cancellations and incidents.
3. Record each challenge.
4. Update the plan.
Evidence: panel notes and the updated plan.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Price the experience
Goal: decide a fair price.
1. Cost per guest = (operator fee + fuel + crew + insurance share + permits + marketing + planner time) divided by guests. Currency shown.
2. Price per guest = cost + margin. Test at 60, 80 and 100 percent of seats filled.
3. Write a clear price page: what is included, what is not, cancellation.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: price sheet and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the idea, the rules, the briefing, the price and the risks.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a guest or an operator.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Space Tourism Awareness, Virtual Event Planning, Micro-Mobility Services.
3. Contribute back: share your briefing script or rules table template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Boat with 12 seats; cost per trip: operator fee 300, fuel 80, crew 60, insurance share 40, permits 20, marketing 40 = 540.
2. Cost per guest at 12 guests = 540 / 12 = 45.00; at 8 guests = 67.50; at 6 guests = 90.00 (currency shown).
3. Price 55 per guest: income at 9 guests (75% full) = 495; profit = -45.
4. Headcount: 11 boarded; 11 lifejackets issued; 11 returned. Check passed.

## Case exercise
A scenic trip is booked for 10 guests. The forecast shows strong wind. Questions: Who decides to cancel and by which rule? What is the refund? What does the briefing say? Which licences do you check? Write a one-page itinerary with a bad-weather plan.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Who must be licensed? 2. Why do a headcount? 3. What goes in a safety briefing? 4. Why have a weather plan? 5. What is a fair cancellation rule? 6. Why keep copies of licences? 7. Name two environment rules. 8. How do you compute cost per guest? 9. Why test at 60, 80 and 100 percent? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Planning only. Learners do not operate boats or aircraft. Use only licensed operators. No guest personal data in shared files.
