# GL-15 Tele and Video Conferencing Operations: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised accessibility guidelines (such as captions and readable text) and recording-consent practice. This is not a licence or a certificate. Ask everyone before recording and say where the recording will be kept. Check the free limits of your tool first. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free video-meeting tool within its free limits (check the free-limits register first), a headset, a free document editor.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What makes an online session work
Goal: choose the kind of session and its audience.
1. List three online sessions you attended. Note what worked and what annoyed you (sound, late start, no captions, too many slides).
2. Choose the session type for your three sessions: a workshop, a training class, a community talk, a team meeting.
3. Know the audience: how many, what devices, what bandwidth, what languages, any accessibility needs.
4. Write the aim and the agenda for each session with times.
Evidence: the session list, audience notes and agendas.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: three hosted sessions, a technical checklist, an accessibility checklist, participant feedback.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Prepare the room and the tech
1. Test sound first: a headset beats a laptop microphone. Check light on your face, background and camera height.
2. Test the tool: sharing screen, chat, hand-raising, breakout rooms, captions.
3. Write the invitation: time with time zone, link, how to join by phone, what to prepare, who to contact.
4. Make a run sheet: minute by minute, who speaks, which slide, which activity.
Evidence: tech test notes, invitation, run sheet.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Audio and video set-up, accessibility and recording consent
Short modules:
- Audio and video: sound matters more than picture, mute by default for big groups, lower the video quality on weak links, a backup plan if the link drops (phone number, second device).
- Accessibility: turn captions on, share slides in advance, use readable fonts and contrast, describe visuals aloud, give breaks, allow chat questions.
- Recording consent: say clearly before you record, give people a way to opt out of camera or recording, keep the file private, delete after a stated time.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Host three sessions
1. Host session one with a small friendly group. Use a co-host to watch the chat and the clock.
2. After each session send a 3-question survey (clear sound? clear content? one thing to improve) and note the results.
3. Fix what you learned, then host sessions two and three with a larger or less familiar group.
4. Log each: start delay, drop-outs, technical problems, questions asked, survey scores.
Evidence: three session logs and survey results.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Host review circle
1. Peers watch a recording you are allowed to share (or notes) and give feedback on pace, clarity and inclusion.
2. Compare the three sessions: start delay, drop-outs, scores.
3. A mentor who runs training or events reviews your run sheet and your accessibility checklist.
Evidence: peer feedback and comparison table.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Rewrite your run sheet and checklists.
2. Host a fourth short session, or rehearse the changes, and measure again.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: offer hosting as a service, practise more, or stay as a participant skill.
Evidence: updated checklists, final scores and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the sessions, the checklists, the scores, the changes made and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Virtual Event Planning; Online Tutoring Platform; IT Service and Support.
3. Contribute back: share your run-sheet template, accessibility checklist or invitation wording so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Three sessions: 40, 55 and 30 invited. Joined 31, 38 and 24 = 77.5%, 69%, 80%. Overall joined 93 of 125 = 74.4%.
2. Start delay: 6, 4 and 1 minutes, average 3.7 minutes (11/3).
3. Bandwidth: if each participant needs about 1.5 Mbit/s to send video, a host sending one camera needs about 1.5 Mbit/s upload; test your own link before the session and keep a phone-in number ready.
4. Survey scores out of 5: sound 4.4, content 4.6, pace 4.0.

## Case exercise
A participant uses a screen reader and another is on a slow mobile link; one person refuses recording. Questions: What changes before the session? During it? What do you do with the recording? Write the checklist.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Why does sound matter more than picture? 2. Why use a co-host? 3. Name three accessibility actions. 4. What must you do before recording? 5. Why share slides in advance? 6. What is a backup plan if the link drops? 7. Why give the time zone in an invitation? 8. What is a 3-question survey for? 9. Why mute large groups by default? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. Never record without telling people. Never share recordings publicly without written permission.
