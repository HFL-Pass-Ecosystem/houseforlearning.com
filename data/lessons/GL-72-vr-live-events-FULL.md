# GL-72 VR Live Events: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module runs a small virtual-reality event. VR can cause nausea, dizziness and discomfort; take breaks, offer non-VR options and supervise children according to the platform's age rules. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What a VR live event is
Goal: choose an event and a guest group.
1. Describe a VR live event: guests join a shared virtual space through headsets or a screen and see a live session such as a talk, class, tour or music set.
2. List access routes: headset, phone, computer screen. Note that not everyone has a headset.
3. Choose an event: a 30-minute talk with questions, a guided tour, or a small class.
4. Write a one-page idea: who comes, what happens, how long, how do they join.
Evidence: notes and the event idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: how do people without a headset join, what if someone feels unwell, who moderates.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a run sheet, an accessibility plan, a moderation plan, a feedback summary and a risk note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Choose the platform and plan the run sheet
Goal: pick a free platform and write the run sheet.
1. Compare two or three free platforms or free tiers for virtual spaces. Compare number of guests, access on screen, headset, accessibility tools and privacy terms. Do not name makers in shared files.
2. Write the run sheet: time, what happens, who speaks, who moderates, backup plan.
3. Write the entry steps for guests in five simple steps with screenshots.
4. Test the space with two helpers.
Evidence: the platform comparison and the run sheet.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Platforms, comfort and moderation
Short modules:
- Platforms: check privacy rules, recording, age limits, data stored and whether you can invite by link or by code.
- Comfort and safety: take a break at least every 20 to 30 minutes, keep a seat, allow people to remove the headset at any time, avoid fast motion, offer a screen option.
- Moderation: set code of conduct, mute and remove tools, personal space bubbles, reporting steps, and a moderator for every group of guests.
- Accessibility: captions, audio description where possible, large text, and alternatives to hand controls.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Write the accessibility and moderation plan
Goal: make the event welcoming and safe.
1. Write the code of conduct in six lines.
2. Assign roles: host, moderator, technical helper, welcome guide.
3. Plan captions, language support and screen-only access.
4. Write a plan for problems: lost sound, a guest feels unwell, bad behaviour, platform failure.
Evidence: the plan with roles and the problem table.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Attendee-feedback circle
Goal: run a rehearsal and learn.
1. Run a 20-minute rehearsal with 6 to 10 guests.
2. Ask each guest: how comfortable were you, could you hear, could you join easily, what would you change?
3. A mentor with events or VR experience gives advice.
4. List fixes.
Evidence: feedback and fixes.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Run the event and measure
Goal: deliver a small live event.
1. Run the event with a moderator and a technical helper.
2. Measure: guests who joined, joined on time, stayed to the end, comfort scores 1 to 5, questions asked.
3. Gather written feedback.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: measure table, feedback and decision.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the event, the numbers, the guest feedback and what you will change.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a guest from the event.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Holographic and Immersive Theatre, Virtual Event Planning, eSports Management.
3. Contribute back: share your run sheet or code of conduct template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Event 60 min with a break every 20 minutes = 2 breaks needed (after minutes 20 and 40).
2. Joined on time: 18 of 24 booked = 75%; stayed to the end: 20 = 83%.
3. Comfort score average of 8 guests: 4, 5, 3, 4, 5, 4, 2, 5 = 4.00.
4. Moderators: 1 per 15 guests; for 24 guests = 2 moderators.

## Case exercise
A guest reports dizziness and another behaves badly in the chat. Questions: What are the first steps? Which rule applies? What would you change in the plan? Write a half-page incident note.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name three ways to join. 2. How often should guests take breaks? 3. Why offer a screen-only option? 4. What belongs in a code of conduct? 5. Why have a moderator? 6. Name two accessibility features. 7. What if someone feels unwell? 8. What should you check in privacy rules? 9. What do you measure after the event? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Follow the platform's age rules. Offer breaks and a non-VR option. Do not record guests without consent. No guest personal data in shared files.
