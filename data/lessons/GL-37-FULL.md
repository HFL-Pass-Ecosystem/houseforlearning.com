# GL-37 Crowdfunding Platform Practice: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a supporter who wants to know exactly what they get, and the risks.
1. Market scan: Read 5 campaign pages. Note the goal in currency, the rewards, the timeline, and how clearly risks are explained.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Pick a sample project and a funding goal with currency. Say what happens if the goal is not reached.
2. Design rewards: each with a cost to you, a price, and a delivery date. Do not promise what you cannot make.
3. Write the risk section in plain words: delays, cost rises, not reaching the goal.
4. Check the rules of the platform type you would use and of your country, and write them down.
Evidence: Campaign plan with rewards table and risk text.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Platform rules: some platforms are all-or-nothing and some keep what is raised. Fees and payment times differ. Read them fully.
- Rewards: price must cover making and delivering, including postage. Add a buffer for cost rises.
- Investor-protection awareness: raising money in return for a share or profit is regulated in many countries. This course covers rewards and donations only and does not give legal advice.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Run a practice campaign: share the page with 20 people as a test only; no money is taken.
2. Track visits, questions, and how many say they would back it.
3. Update the goal and rewards based on what you learn.
Evidence: Test results and revised plan.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Present your plan and the biggest risk.
2. Peers act as cautious supporters and ask what could go wrong.
3. Rewrite the risk section.
Evidence: New risk section.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: interest shown per 20 people, reward margin, goal realism, clarity score from readers.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Project: a set of 100 reusable cloth bags. Goal 3,000.
2. Reward: bag set at 25, cost 14 incl. postage 4: margin 11. To cover 3,000 you need about 120 backers; add a buffer: cost rise 10% so cost 15.4.
3. Risk text: 'Delivery may be 2 months late. If we raise less than the goal, we will not make the product and no money is taken.'
4. Practice test: of 20 people, 6 said they would back it at 25.

## Case exercise
The test shows 4 of 20 would back at 25 and 9 at 20. Questions: Which price? What is the margin at 20 with a cost of 15.4? What is the new goal? What risk would you add? Write a one-page revised plan.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is an all-or-nothing campaign? 2. Why add a buffer to reward costs? 3. What belongs in the risk section? 4. Why is this course not about investing? 5. Reward margin: price 50, cost 35 (same currency). What is it? 6. What happens if the goal is missed? 7. Why read platform fees? 8. What do cautious supporters ask? 9. What would you change in your plan? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Rules for reward and donation campaigns, advertising and consumer protection. Raising money for a share or profit is regulated separately and is not taught here.
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
No real money is collected in the course. No promises you cannot keep. Not legal or investment advice. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
