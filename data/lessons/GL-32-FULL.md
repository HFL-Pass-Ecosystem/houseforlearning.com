# GL-32 Influencer Marketing: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a small business that wants a real person to introduce its product to their audience.
1. Market scan: Find 10 creators in one niche. Note audience size, how engaged the comments are, how clearly they disclose paid posts, and what they might charge (with currency).
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Choose a small sample business and a goal (for example 30 visits to a page).
2. Shortlist 5 creators by fit, not size. Check comments for real conversation.
3. Write a one-page brief: goal, key message, must-say, must-not-say, disclosure, dates, payment.
4. Agree a small test with one creator in writing, with disclosure clearly stated.
Evidence: Creator shortlist and brief.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Disclosure: paid or gifted content must be marked clearly in the first lines. Check your country's advertising rules and write them down.
- Brand safety: look through a creator's past posts for anything that clashes with your values. Agree what happens if something goes wrong.
- Measurement: reach is not results. Track clicks, sign-ups or sales, then work out cost per result.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Run the test. Track visits with a unique link.
2. Record cost, reach, clicks, saves and comments in a sheet.
3. Ask the creator what the audience asked about.
Evidence: Campaign results sheet.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Show the campaign and one number that surprised you.
2. Peers critique the brief and the creator choice.
3. Write a better brief.
Evidence: Revised brief.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: cost per click, cost per result, engagement rate, share of posts correctly disclosed.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Goal: 30 visits to a page. Budget 100.
2. Shortlist: five creators; one has 50,000 followers with few real comments, another has 3,000 with long conversations. Choose by fit.
3. Result: cost 100, 450 reach, 60 clicks, 4 sign-ups. Cost per click 1.67; cost per sign-up 25 (same currency).
4. Brief line: 'Post must carry the word Ad or Sponsored in the first line.'

## Case exercise
A creator posts without the disclosure and the audience complains. Questions: What do you do right now? What does the contract say? How do you measure whether the campaign still worked? Write a one-page response and a better brief.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Why does fit beat size? 2. What goes in a brief? 3. How must paid posts be marked? 4. What is brand safety? 5. Cost per result: cost 200, results 20 (same currency). What is it? 6. Why use a unique link? 7. What should be agreed in writing? 8. How do you check a creator's audience is real? 9. What would you do if a post is not disclosed? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Advertising disclosure for paid or gifted posts; contracts with creators; use of AI-made images or voices must be marked where the rules say.
AI tools: if you use an AI tool in this course, the EU AI Act transparency duties (for example telling people they are dealing with AI, and marking AI-made content) have applied since 2 Aug 2026. Stand-alone high-risk AI systems are now due from 2 Dec 2027, and AI inside regulated products from 2 Aug 2028 (these dates were delayed; check the official source for any change).
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
No fake followers, no fake reviews. Everything agreed in writing and disclosed. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
