# GL-21 E-Commerce Growth Marketing: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised consumer-protection and fair-advertising principles. This is not a licence or a certificate. Follow your country's consumer and advertising rules; no false claims; honour returns and refunds. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free spreadsheet, free shop or catalogue page tools, free analytics within their limits (check the free-limits register first).

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Where do orders come from?
Goal: choose one small shop and two channels to compare.
1. Choose a small online shop (your own, a friend's, or a sample) with 5 to 20 products.
2. List possible channels: search, email to past buyers, a community group, a marketplace listing, a partner shop, printed cards in parcels.
3. Pick two channels you can run for 3 weeks without paid advertising, or with a small budget you set (with currency).
4. Define the goal: orders from each channel and the highest cost per order you accept.
Evidence: shop description, two channels, goal.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a conversion funnel table, channel logs, a cost-per-order comparison and a decision.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Prepare the shop for visitors
1. Check the basics: clear product names, true photos you took, price with tax shown as the law requires, delivery time and cost, return policy, contact.
2. Make the path short: product page to cart to payment in as few steps as possible; test on a phone.
3. Add a tracking method for each channel: a different link per channel, or a 'how did you hear about us' choice.
4. Write the offer and the message for each channel.
Evidence: shop checklist, tracking set-up, channel messages.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Channels, conversion and consumer rules
Short modules:
- Channels: what each is good at, speed versus lasting value, cost per visitor.
- Conversion: visitors, product views, carts, orders; where people drop out and common fixes (clear price, trust signals that are true, simple checkout).
- Consumer rules: truthful claims, clear total price, delivery promises kept, returns and refunds as the law requires, rules on reviews (no fake ones) and on emailing people.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Run the two channels for three weeks
1. Run both channels in parallel with the same offer strength.
2. Each day log by channel: visits, carts, orders, revenue, cost (money and hours).
3. Each week change one thing in the weaker step of the funnel and note it.
4. Record every customer question and complaint.
Evidence: three-week channel log.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Growth review circle
1. Calculate cost per order = (money spent + value of your hours) / orders, per channel.
2. Peers look at the funnel and say where they would drop out.
3. A mentor with online retail experience reviews your claims, prices and returns text.
Evidence: cost-per-order table and reviewed shop text.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Put more effort on the better channel and fix the biggest funnel gap.
2. Compare before and after in orders and cost per order.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale one channel, test a third, or pause.
Evidence: before/after table and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the shop, the channels, the numbers and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Marketplace Fulfilment Selling; E-Commerce Inventory Management; Digital Marketing.
3. Contribute back: share your funnel table, channel-message examples or returns-policy wording so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Channel 1 (email to past buyers): spent 0 money, 3 hours (value 150), orders 12, cost per order 12.5.
2. Channel 2 (community group posts): spent 100 on a small offer, 5 hours (250) = 350, orders 14, cost per order 25.
3. Funnel on channel 2: 700 visits, 70 carts (10%), 14 orders (20% of carts, 2% of visits).
4. Goal was 20 per order maximum: channel 1 meets it, channel 2 does not.

## Case exercise
A product page shows 'only 2 left!' always, and a competitor's review text was copied. Questions: What is wrong under fair-advertising rules? How do you fix it? How do you earn trust honestly? Write a half-page plan.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name three channels and one strength of each. 2. What is a conversion funnel? 3. How do you work out cost per order? 4. Why track channels separately? 5. What must a product page show by law in your country? 6. Why are fake reviews a problem? 7. What small change might raise conversion? 8. Why log customer questions? 9. Why compare channels over the same period? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No fake reviews, fake scarcity or invented discounts.
