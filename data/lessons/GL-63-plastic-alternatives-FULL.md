# GL-63 Plastic Alternatives: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module compares materials. Some tests use simple tools; use gloves and eye protection when cutting or heating, and never burn plastics. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Why people look for alternatives and what they replace
Goal: choose one single-use item and three alternatives.
1. List single-use items you see in a day: bag, bottle, straw, cutlery, food tray, wrap, sachet.
2. Pick one item and note how many are used per day in a place you can observe (counts only).
3. Find three alternatives (paper, plant fibre, reusable metal or glass, compostable material, thicker reusable plastic).
4. Write one page: what must the alternative do (hold, seal, last, cost)?
Evidence: the item list, counts and the alternatives.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: what does it cost, where does it go after use, does it work in real conditions.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a test plan, a results table, a lifecycle sketch, a cost table and a recommendation.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Test performance
Goal: test strength, water resistance and hot-food behaviour.
1. Buy or collect samples of the same size. Number them, not brand names.
2. Strength test: load with weights until it fails; record the weight.
3. Water test: fill with water for 30 minutes and for 4 hours; record leaks and softening.
4. Heat test: hot (not boiling) water or food for 15 minutes; record smell, softening, leaks. Do not heat plastics beyond normal food use.
Evidence: the results table.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Lifecycle thinking, standards and sourcing
Short modules:
- Lifecycle thinking: raw material, making, transport, use, end of life. A reusable item must be used many times to beat single-use; ask how many.
- Terms: biodegradable, compostable and recyclable are different; compostable often needs an industrial plant, not a garden heap. Check the standard named on the pack.
- Standards: look for the test standard your country accepts for compostable and food-contact claims.
- Sourcing: where the material comes from, whether it competes with food, and local waste systems.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Map the end of life
Goal: find what really happens after use.
1. Find out what your local waste system does with each material (collection, sorting, composting, landfill, burning).
2. Visit or interview a collector or recycler with permission. Ask what they accept and what spoils a batch.
3. Draw a lifecycle sketch for each alternative with your best estimate of where it ends up.
4. Estimate the number of uses a reusable item needs to break even on materials.
Evidence: lifecycle sketches and a break-even note.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Materials circle
Goal: compare and challenge.
1. Present the test table. Peers check fairness (same size, same load, same time).
2. A materials mentor points out missing factors.
3. Discuss whether the best test result is also the best choice for the planet and wallet.
4. Update your ranking.
Evidence: peer notes and updated ranking.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Cost it and recommend
Goal: give a clear recommendation.
1. Cost per use = price divided by number of uses. Include washing for reusables (water, energy, labour). Currency shown.
2. Weight the factors: performance, cost, end of life, availability. Show the weights.
3. Write a one-page recommendation for a business or school.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: cost table, scoring and recommendation.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the tests, the lifecycle, the cost and the recommendation.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a shop, school canteen or café that would use it.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Water Management and Conservation, Greenhouse Gas Auditing, Biofuel Production Overview.
3. Contribute back: share your test plan or scoring sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Single-use cup 0.05 each. Reusable cup 2.00. Break-even uses = 2.00 / 0.05 = 40 uses (ignoring washing).
2. With washing cost 0.02 per use: saving per use = 0.05 - 0.02 = 0.03; break-even = 2.00 / 0.03 = 67 uses.
3. Strength test: sample A held 4.0 kg, B 2.5 kg, C 6.0 kg. C is 50% stronger than A.
4. Score (weights: performance 40%, cost 30%, end of life 30%): A 6,7,5 = 6.0; B 8,5,8 = 7.1; C 5,9,6 = 6.5.

## Case exercise
A canteen uses 500 single-use trays a day. Questions: What are the three alternatives and their cost per use? What does the local waste system accept? What if returns are only 70 percent? Write a one-page recommendation.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name the five stages of a lifecycle. 2. Difference between compostable and recyclable? 3. Why must samples be the same size? 4. What is cost per use? 5. Why ask how many reuses are needed? 6. Name one end-of-life route. 7. Why not heat plastics hard? 8. Why weigh factors? 9. What spoils a recycling batch? 10. What is your recommendation?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Gloves and eye protection when cutting or heating. Never burn plastics. Claims need the named standard and a source.
