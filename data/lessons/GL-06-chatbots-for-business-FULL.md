# GL-06 Chatbots for Business: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised conversation-design, accessibility and privacy principles. This is not a licence or a certificate. Tell users they are talking to a bot, and never let a bot give medical, legal or investment advice. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free chatbot builder or a free code editor, a free spreadsheet, a free document editor.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Which customer questions should a bot answer?
Goal: collect the real questions one business gets and choose the 15 most common.
1. Ask one small business for their last 50 customer questions (messages, calls, notes) with names and numbers removed.
2. Group them: opening hours, prices, booking, delivery, complaints, other. Count each group.
3. Pick the 15 most common questions that have a clear, fixed answer. Mark the ones a bot must hand to a person (anger, money disputes, safety, anything sensitive).
4. Write the goal in numbers: for example, answer 60% of common questions correctly without a person.
Evidence: grouped question table and the goal.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a question-and-answer set, a conversation flow, a test of 30 questions and a hand-over rule.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Design the conversation
1. Write the greeting: say it is a bot, what it can help with, and how to reach a person.
2. Write each answer in two or three short sentences in friendly plain language. Add one follow-up button or question per answer.
3. Draw the flow on paper: start, topic choice, answer, 'did that help?', hand-over to a person.
4. Write the fallback message for questions it does not understand, with a way to reach a person.
Evidence: answer sheet, flow drawing, greeting and fallback text.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Conversation design, privacy and escalation
Short modules:
- Conversation design: short turns, one question at a time, clear choices, never trap the user, always offer a human.
- Privacy: collect the minimum, say why you ask, do not ask for passwords or payment numbers in chat, state how long chats are kept, follow the local data-protection law.
- Escalation: rules for when to hand over (anger, repeated failure, safety words), how the human sees the chat so the customer does not repeat themselves.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build and test
1. Build the bot in the free tool or a small program. Load the 15 answers and the fallback.
2. Test with 30 real questions that you did not use to write the answers. Record for each: understood or not, answer correct or not, would the customer be happy.
3. Fix the weakest answers and add missing phrases people really use, including spelling variants and other languages the business serves.
4. Test with two people who have never seen it, including one using a screen reader or a slow phone.
Evidence: 30-question test table, fixes, two user test notes.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Bot-review circle
1. Each peer asks your bot five questions, including one trick question. Note failures.
2. Work out the answer rate and the correct-answer rate. Compare with your goal.
3. A mentor reads the privacy text and the hand-over rule and checks the bot never gives advice it should not give.
Evidence: circle failure list, rates, reviewed privacy text.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Fix the failures and rerun the 30-question test.
2. Compare before and after: understood, correct, hand-over rate.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision for the business: launch small, improve first, or keep human-only.
Evidence: before/after table and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the question data, the conversation flow, the test numbers, the privacy text and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Artificial Intelligence in Practice; Lead Generation for Small Businesses; IT Service and Support.
3. Contribute back: share your question-grouping sheet, flow template or hand-over rule so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. 15 answers cover 60% of 50 real questions = 30 questions covered.
2. Test of 30 new questions: understood 24 (80%), correct 21 (70%), handed to a person 6 (20%).
3. Fix: add spelling variants for 3 answers; retest: understood 27 (90%), correct 25 (83%).
4. Cost view: if a person takes 4 minutes per question and the bot handles 25 of 30 correctly, it saves 100 minutes per 30 questions, but 5 wrong answers cost 15 minutes of repair.

## Case exercise
A customer is angry about a double charge and the bot keeps offering opening hours. Questions: Which rule failed? Write the hand-over trigger words and message. What does the human see before replying? Write a half-page fix.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Why tell the user it is a bot? 2. Why start with real customer questions? 3. Name two situations that must go to a person. 4. Why should the fallback offer a human? 5. What personal data should a bot never ask for? 6. Why test with questions you did not use to build the bot? 7. How do you measure the correct-answer rate? 8. Why test with a screen reader or slow phone? 9. Why must the human see the earlier chat? 10. What was your launch decision and why?

## Law check
Law check (31 Oct 2026, from public sources; confirm with an adviser): India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 has its Rules notified in November 2025 and switches on in phases; the consent-manager part starts about November 2026 and the main duties of organisations from about May 2027. The UAE personal-data law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) has been in force since January 2022, but sources disagree on whether its executive regulations are issued; check the UAE legislation portal and the UAE Data Office. The EU data-protection regulation (GDPR) is unchanged in its core principles. For AI: the EU AI Act is now on a delayed schedule. Under the 2026 amending regulation, rules for stand-alone high-risk uses (for example credit decisions about people) apply from 2 December 2027, rules for AI inside regulated products from 2 August 2028, and the transparency duties (telling people they deal with AI or AI-made content) have applied since 2 August 2026, with a grace period to 2 December 2026 for some older content-generating systems.

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No pretending to be a human. No advice in medicine, law or investment. Keep chat records no longer than needed.
