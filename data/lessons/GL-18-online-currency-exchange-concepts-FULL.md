# GL-18 Online Currency Exchange Concepts: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised consumer-protection and anti-money-laundering awareness. This is not a licence or a certificate. This is a concepts and comparison module. Moving money across borders is regulated; operating an exchange needs a licence. It gives no investment advice and you move only a small, real amount of your own money if you choose to test a transfer. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: free public rate sources, a free spreadsheet, a free document editor.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Why the same money costs different amounts
Goal: pick one real transfer need and a size to compare.
1. Explain in plain words: the mid-market rate is the middle between buy and sell prices; providers add a spread and sometimes a fee.
2. Choose one transfer: for example a family sending 500 in one currency to another country, or a student paying fees.
3. List the channels to compare: a bank, a money-transfer service, a card, a cash exchange, a digital wallet. Choose 4 without naming your preference.
4. Write the question: for this transfer, what arrives, how fast, and how safe?
Evidence: the transfer need and the list of channels.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a rate and fee comparison table, a timing record, a safety checklist and a recommendation.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Collect the numbers
1. Record the mid-market rate from a public rate source with the date and time.
2. For each channel, record: rate offered, fixed fee, percentage fee, amount received, delivery time, limits, how to complain.
3. Calculate the true cost = (mid-market amount - amount received) as a percentage, including the fee.
4. Repeat on three different days to see how rates move.
Evidence: comparison table for three days.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Spreads, fees and licensing awareness
Short modules:
- Spreads and fees: buy versus sell price, hidden margin in the rate, fees shown separately, why 'no fee' can still be expensive.
- Licensing awareness: exchanges and transfer firms need a licence in most countries; check the regulator's public list; be wary of promises of guaranteed profit.
- Safety: official apps only, confirm recipient details, use strong sign-in, never share one-time codes, know that scams often use urgency and pressure.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Check the details and the risks
1. Check each channel's licence on the regulator's public list and note the result.
2. Read the complaint and refund terms. Write the cost of a mistake (wrong account).
3. Note speed risk: what if it takes 3 days instead of 3 minutes?
4. Make the safety checklist for a first-time sender.
Evidence: licence checks, terms notes, safety checklist.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Comparison circle
1. Peers recalculate your true-cost percentage from your own raw numbers.
2. Discuss a scam story from public news (without naming people) and which check would have stopped it.
3. A mentor with banking or payments experience reviews your method.
Evidence: recalculation check and reviewed method.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Correct your numbers and rank the channels for three kinds of sender: small and urgent, small and cheap, large and planned.
2. Write a one-page guide a first-time sender can follow.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision (no investment advice).
Evidence: ranking, guide and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the transfer, the numbers, the true-cost method, the safety checklist and your recommendation.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Wallet Payment Solutions; P2P Lending Platform Concepts; Crowdfunding Platform Practice.
3. Contribute back: share your comparison sheet template or first-time-sender guide so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Transfer 500 to another currency. Mid-market amount received would be 41,000. Channel A gives 40,100 and fee 0 = true cost (41,000 - 40,100) / 41,000 = 2.2%. Channel B gives 40,700 with fee 6 (in the sending currency, about 490 in the other) = 40,210 equivalent, true cost 1.9%.
2. The cheapest changes: 2.2% versus 1.9% on 41,000 = 123 less at B.
3. Over three days the mid rate moved 0.8%, bigger than the gap between providers.
4. Speed: A arrives in minutes, B in 2 days. A small urgent sender may pay more.

## Case exercise
A provider promises 'zero fees' and 'guaranteed rate'. It is not on the regulator list. Questions: What do you check? What are the warning signs? What do you advise? Write a guide paragraph.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is the mid-market rate? 2. What is a spread? 3. How do you calculate true cost as a percentage? 4. Why can 'no fee' still be expensive? 5. Where do you check a provider's licence? 6. Name two scam warning signs. 7. Why compare on three different days? 8. What does a complaint policy tell you? 9. Why never share one-time codes? 10. What was your ranking and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No investment, trading or speculation advice. No promises of profit. Use only public rate sources.
