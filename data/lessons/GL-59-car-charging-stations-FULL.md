# GL-59 Car Charging Stations: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module plans a charging site on paper. Installation, connection to the grid and electrical work must be done by licensed people under local law. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs charging and where
Goal: find a site and the drivers it would serve.
1. Walk or map three candidate sites (a car park, a shop, a depot, a housing block). Note parking spaces, how long cars stay, and who owns the land.
2. Count passing or parked cars at two times of day for one week (only counts, no plate numbers).
3. Talk to 5 drivers or fleet managers: how far they drive, where they charge now, what they would pay.
4. Choose one site and write a one-page need statement.
Evidence: the site notes, the counts and five driver notes.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: how long do cars stay, what power is available, who pays for the connection.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a power plan, a site layout, a price model, a permit list and a risk note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Work out the power
Goal: find how much power the site needs and how much it has.
1. Ask the site owner for the supply size in kVA or kW and the building's peak use (from bills).
2. Chargers: power per charger in kW x number of chargers = connected load.
3. Not all run at full power together. Apply a diversity factor (for example 0.6 to 0.8 for a car park, lower for a depot with scheduled charging). Say why you chose it.
4. Compare required load with spare supply. If short, note options: fewer or slower chargers, load management, or a bigger connection.
Evidence: the power calculation sheet.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Connectors, load management and permits
Short modules:
- Connectors: slow alternating-current points use one connector type, fast direct-current points use another; the types differ by country, so check yours.
- Load management: share the available power between cars and slow down some chargers when demand is high.
- Permits: planning permission, electrical approval, fire rules, accessibility rules, and the agreement with the network operator. Write which apply and who confirmed them.
- Safety: weatherproofing, cable protection, emergency stop, signs, lighting, trip hazards.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Draw the site layout
Goal: show bays, cables, and how people move safely.
1. Sketch the bays so that cables reach without crossing walkways.
2. Mark the charger positions, the supply cabinet, protection posts, lighting, signs and an accessible bay.
3. Mark a one-way traffic flow and queuing space.
4. Walk the site with a classmate and test whether it works for a driver in a wheelchair and for a driver with a trailer.
Evidence: the layout sketch and the walk-through notes.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Site-plan critique
Goal: get others to find the problems.
1. Present the site plan in 8 minutes.
2. Peers and a mentor ask about power, safety, accessibility, and what happens in a fault.
3. Record each challenge.
4. Update the plan.
Evidence: critique notes and the updated plan.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Price it and write the business sheet
Goal: decide a price and see if the site pays.
1. Cost to deliver one kWh = energy price from supplier + losses (about 5 to 10 percent) + network fees. Add the currency.
2. Price per kWh = cost + running cost share + margin. Compare with prices in your market.
3. Payback = site cost divided by yearly margin. Test with half and double the expected use.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: price model, payback and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the site, the power plan, the layout, the price and the risks.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from the site owner or a fleet manager.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Electric Car and EV Basics, Solar Power Design and Installation Basics, Portable and Wireless Charging Solutions.
3. Contribute back: share your power calculation sheet or layout checklist so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Chargers: 6 x 11 kW = 66 kW connected load. Diversity 0.7 gives 66 x 0.7 = 46.2 kW peak.
2. Spare supply: 80 kW supply minus 45 kW building peak = 35 kW. Required 46.2 kW is above 35, so load management or fewer chargers is needed.
3. Option: 4 chargers x 11 = 44 kW x 0.7 = 30.8 kW, which is within the 35 kW spare. If the diversity factor proves too optimistic (0.9 gives 39.6 kW, above 35), cap each charger to 8 kW: 4 x 8 x 0.9 = 28.8 kW, within 35.
4. Price: energy 0.18 per kWh + losses 8% + fees 0.03 = 0.18 x 1.08 + 0.03 = 0.2244 cost; sell at 0.40 gives margin 0.1756 per kWh (currency shown).

## Case exercise
A shop car park has 30 bays, a 50 kW spare supply and cars stay 90 minutes on average. Questions: How many chargers and at what power? What diversity factor and why? Where do cables and walkways cross? Which permits? Write a one-page site plan with a price and payback.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is connected load? 2. Why use a diversity factor? 3. What does load management do? 4. Name two permits that may apply. 5. Why must walkways stay clear of cables? 6. How do you compute cost per kWh delivered? 7. Why test half and double the expected use? 8. Who does the electrical connection? 9. What makes a bay accessible? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Paper plan only. No electrical work. Counts of cars must not record number plates or faces. Confirm permits with an adviser.
