# GL-74 eSports Management: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module runs a small, safe tournament. Many players are young. Follow the age rules of the game, protect players' welfare and privacy, and never allow gambling. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What esports management is
Goal: choose a game, a format and a player group.
1. Describe the parts of a tournament: players, rules, platform, schedule, referees, broadcast, prizes.
2. List formats: single elimination, double elimination, group stage and final, league.
3. Choose a game that fits your players' ages and your platform; read the publisher's terms for events and note what is allowed (do not name makers in shared files).
4. Write a one-page idea: players, game mode, rounds, time, prize (non-cash if players are under 18).
Evidence: notes and the tournament idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: how do we keep it fair, who protects young players, what happens when someone cheats or leaves.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a rule book, a schedule, a welfare and safeguarding plan, a budget and a results report.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Write the rule book and the schedule
Goal: make clear, fair rules.
1. Rule book sections: eligibility, registration, game settings, match format, tie-breaks, late arrival, disconnection, cheating, behaviour, protests, prizes.
2. Build a bracket for your number of teams and the schedule with breaks.
3. Write how referees decide a protest and how a player can appeal.
4. Test the rules by reading them to a classmate and asking them to find gaps.
Evidence: the rule book, bracket and schedule.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Event rules, player welfare and sponsorship
Short modules:
- Event rules: fair play, anti-cheating checks, identity of players, equipment, age limits, parent or guardian consent for minors.
- Player welfare: breaks, posture, eye care, sleep, healthy food, time limits, protection from abuse and hate in chat, and a person to report to.
- Sponsorship: be honest with sponsors, avoid gambling, alcohol and harmful products for young audiences, and follow local advertising rules.
- Privacy: keep player data minimal and safe.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Plan welfare, safeguarding and the budget
Goal: make the event safe and affordable.
1. Write the welfare plan: break times, a quiet room, water, a first-aid contact.
2. Write the safeguarding plan: two adults present with young players, chat rules, reporting steps, who handles complaints.
3. Make the budget: venue or platform, equipment, referees, prizes, marketing, insurance. Currency shown.
4. Plan income: entry fees if allowed, sponsors, merchandise.
Evidence: welfare plan, safeguarding plan and budget.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Organisers' circle
Goal: test the plan with experienced organisers.
1. Present the plan to organisers from clubs or colleges.
2. They ask about disputes, delays and technology failures.
3. Record answers and add backup plans.
4. Update the rule book.
Evidence: circle notes and updated rules.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Run the tournament and report
Goal: deliver a small event and measure it.
1. Run the tournament with referees and a welfare contact.
2. Measure: number of teams, matches on time, disputes, player comfort scores, spectators, cost per player.
3. Collect feedback from players and referees.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: results report and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the format, the rules, the welfare plan, the numbers and the feedback.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a player or a referee.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: VR Live Events, Virtual Event Planning, Social Media Network Building.
3. Contribute back: share your rule book template or welfare checklist so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Single elimination with 16 teams: 15 matches; rounds = 4.
2. Double elimination with 8 teams: 14 or 15 matches (2n-2, plus one if the final is replayed; check your format).
3. Budget: venue 400, equipment 300, referees 150, prizes 200, marketing 100 = 1150. Entry fee 10 x 16 teams x 4 players = 640; sponsors 200; total income 840 (currency shown).
4. Welfare: 8-hour day with 10-minute breaks each hour = 80 minutes of breaks.

## Case exercise
A team of 15-year-olds is accused of cheating and one parent complains about chat abuse. Questions: Who decides and by which rule? How are the young players protected? Which sponsor is not suitable? Write a one-page incident plan.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name three tournament formats. 2. Why have tie-break rules? 3. What do you do about cheating? 4. Why have two adults with young players? 5. Name two welfare measures. 6. Why avoid gambling sponsors? 7. What data should you keep to a minimum? 8. What belongs in a budget? 9. How do you handle a protest? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. No gambling, no cash prizes for minors. Follow the game's terms and the age rules. Keep player data minimal. Safeguarding comes first.
