# GL-08 Drones: Operations and Applications: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised civil-aviation safety principles; the exact rules depend on your country. This is not a licence or a certificate. Flying is allowed only where your aviation authority permits it. If you do not have the permits, do the simulator route. Never fly over crowds, airports or restricted places. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free flight simulator or a supervised flight with a licensed operator, a free mapping tool, a free spreadsheet.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What drones are used for, and what the law says
Goal: pick one safe, legal mission and read the rule that governs it.
1. List 8 drone uses: crop checks, roof inspection, mapping, surveying, delivery, events, search support, power-line inspection.
2. Find your country's aviation authority page on drones. Write down in your own words: registration, maximum height, distance from people, no-fly zones, night flying, permits.
3. Pick one mission you could do legally and safely (for example mapping a small field for a farmer). Write who benefits and what data they need.
4. Write a one-page mission plan: purpose, place, date, who, what data.
Evidence: the rule summary with the source link and the mission plan.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a mission plan, risk assessment, flight log (real or simulated), a data set and a short report.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Plan the mission
1. Check the place on the airspace map and for restricted zones. Record what you found.
2. Check the weather: wind speed, rain, visibility, temperature. Write the limits for your aircraft and your skill.
3. Do a risk assessment: people nearby, animals, power lines, trees, signal loss, battery failure; for each, how you reduce it.
4. Write the checklist: before power on, before take-off, in flight, after landing, and the lost-signal action.
Evidence: airspace check, weather record, risk assessment, checklists.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Local aviation rules, safety and mission planning
Short modules:
- Local aviation rules: registration, operator and pilot requirements, permission for special areas, privacy rules for pictures of people.
- Safety: battery care, propeller checks, safe distance, spotter, emergency landing, never fly after alcohol or when tired.
- Mission planning: overlap for mapping, height versus detail, flight time versus battery, backup battery, how to store and name data.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Fly and capture (real with supervision, or simulated)
1. Run the full checklist out loud before each flight.
2. Fly the planned pattern. Log: start and end time, battery used, wind, problems, photos taken.
3. Do at least three flights or simulator runs. Compare planned versus actual path.
4. Process the images into a map or a set of labelled photos, with a note on accuracy.
Evidence: flight log with at least 3 flights or runs, processed output.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Safety-review circle
1. Present the log. Peers look for any skipped checks and any risk missing from your list.
2. Work out coverage achieved versus planned, minutes of flight per battery, and number of problems per flight.
3. A mentor with aviation or survey experience reviews your legal summary and the lost-signal action.
Evidence: peer risk notes, calculation sheet, reviewed legal summary.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Fix gaps in the checklist and replan a better mission.
2. Give the intended user the output (map or photos) and ask what decision they could make with it.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: offer this as a service, train more first, or stop.
Evidence: user feedback and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the mission, the legal summary, the log, the output and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Nanosatellites and Space Data; Internet of Things (IoT); 360-Degree Photography and Virtual Tours.
3. Contribute back: share your checklists, risk-assessment template or rule summary for your country so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Mapping a 4-hectare field. Aircraft battery gives 20 minutes. Planned 3 flights of 14 minutes = 42 minutes. Flight time with 30% reserve: 20 x 0.7 = 14 minutes usable, so 3 batteries needed.
2. Coverage: planned 4 ha, achieved 3.8 ha = 95%.
3. Wind limit: aircraft limit 8 m/s; forecast 5 m/s gusting 9. Decision: do not fly in gusts above the limit; fly early morning.
4. Image overlap: plan front and side overlap as your mapping tool recommends (for example 75% front and 65% side, check the tool's guide) and record what you used.

## Case exercise
Battery shows 30% with 2 minutes of path left and the signal weakens. Questions: What does your lost-signal and low-battery action say? Who decides? What do you log after landing? Write the action list.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name two things your aviation authority requires. 2. Why check weather before flying? 3. What is a lost-signal action? 4. Why use a spotter? 5. Why fly a pattern with overlap for mapping? 6. What is the difference between height and detail? 7. Why avoid flying after alcohol? 8. How do you protect other people's privacy? 9. How do you compare planned and actual path? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No flights over crowds, airports, prisons, power stations or private property without permission. Simulator route if permits are missing.
