# GL-01 Lead Generation for Small Businesses: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised consent and marketing-privacy principles. This is not a licence or a certificate. Marketing and data-protection law differs by country; check the local rule with an adviser before you contact anyone. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free spreadsheet, free form and landing-page tools, a free email tool within its free limits (check the free-limits register first).

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What a lead is, and who needs more of them
Goal: choose one real small business and define what a good lead means for it.
1. Visit or call one small business (a tutor, a repair shop, a clinic, a caterer). Ask: how do customers find you now, how many enquiries a week, how many become paying?
2. Define a lead in one sentence: a person who has asked to hear from the business and is able to buy. Define a qualified lead: one who also fits the price, place and time.
3. Write the ideal customer in five lines: who, where, what problem, what they pay now, where they look for help.
4. Set a 14-day goal: number of leads and the most you are willing to spend per lead (with currency).
Evidence: the owner's answers, your lead definition and the 14-day goal.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a funnel map, a landing page, a 14-day lead log and a cost-per-lead sheet.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the funnel
1. Draw the funnel on one page: where a stranger first sees the offer, the page they land on, the form they fill, the first reply they get.
2. Write the offer in plain words: what the person gets, by when, at what price or free. No false claims, no fake countdowns.
3. Build one simple landing page and one short form. Ask only for what you need (name and one way to reply). Add a clear consent line saying how you will use the details and how to opt out.
4. Test the form yourself and with two friends on a phone. Fix anything slow or confusing.
Evidence: funnel map, live landing page link, form test notes.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Consent, landing pages and email basics
Short modules:
- Consent: ask clearly, keep a record of when and how each person agreed, honour opt-out the same day. Rules such as the European data-protection regulation, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the UAE personal-data law all expect lawful, clear consent; read the one that applies to you.
- Landing pages: one goal per page, one headline, three benefits, one button, fast loading on a phone, readable text size, plain language.
- Email basics: a clear subject, a short message, one next step, a real sender name, an unsubscribe line. Never buy lists of strangers.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Run the 14-day lead drive
1. Share the offer through two free channels you control (for example a community group where posting is allowed, a printed notice at a partner shop, a personal message to people who asked to hear from you).
2. Each day log: date, channel, visits, form starts, leads, qualified leads, time spent, money spent (currency).
3. Reply to every lead within one working day. Note which replies turned into a conversation or a sale.
4. If a channel brings nothing in three days, change one thing (headline, image, place, time) and note it.
Evidence: the 14-day lead log.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Funnel-review circle
1. Show your log and funnel to the circle. Mark the step where most people dropped out.
2. Work out cost per lead = money spent plus the value of your hours at a fair rate, divided by leads. Work out cost per qualified lead the same way.
3. Peers try your page and say what they did not understand. The mentor, from a local trade or marketing background, reviews your consent line and your claims.
Evidence: reviewed funnel and cost-per-lead sheet, peer test notes.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Fix the biggest drop-out step and run three more days.
2. Compare before and after: leads per day, cost per lead, share of qualified leads.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision for the business owner: continue, change or stop, with the reason.
Evidence: before/after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the business, the funnel, the consent line, the numbers and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Digital Marketing; E-Commerce Growth Marketing; Chatbots for Business.
3. Contribute back: share your funnel map, consent-line wording or cost-per-lead sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Offer: a free 20-minute home-tuition trial. 14 days, spend 600 on printed notices and 4 hours of your time valued at 50 an hour = 600 + 200 = 800. Leads 40, qualified 16.
2. Cost per lead = 800 / 40 = 20. Cost per qualified lead = 800 / 16 = 50.
3. Drop-out check: 400 visits, 80 form starts (20%), 40 forms sent (50% of starts, 10% of visits). The form is the weak step: cutting it from 6 fields to 2 lifted completion to 70% in the second week, so 80 starts gave 56 leads.
4. Revised cost per lead = 800 / 56, about 14.3 (rounded).

## Case exercise
A tutor pays 50 per paying student they get. Her cost per qualified lead is 50 and 1 in 4 qualified leads books paid lessons worth 400 in the first month. Questions: What is her cost per paying student? Is the channel worth it? Which step would you fix first? Write a half-page decision.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is the difference between a lead and a qualified lead? 2. Why must a form say how the details will be used? 3. Name two things a good landing page needs. 4. How do you work out cost per lead, counting your own time? 5. Why is a bought list of strangers a poor idea? 6. What should you do the same day someone opts out? 7. A channel brings 0 leads in 3 days: what do you do? 8. Calculate: spent 600, got 30 leads, 10 qualified (same currency). Cost per qualified lead? 9. Why reply within one working day? 10. What was your decision for the business and why?

## Law check
Law check (31 Oct 2026, from public sources; confirm with an adviser): India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 has its Rules notified in November 2025 and switches on in phases; the consent-manager part starts about November 2026 and the main duties of organisations from about May 2027. The UAE personal-data law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) has been in force since January 2022, but sources disagree on whether its executive regulations are issued; check the UAE legislation portal and the UAE Data Office. The EU data-protection regulation (GDPR) is unchanged in its core principles.

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No fake scarcity, no invented reviews, no pretending to be a person you are not. Only contact people who agreed.
