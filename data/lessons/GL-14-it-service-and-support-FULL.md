# GL-14 IT Service and Support: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised service-management and customer-communication practice. This is not a licence or a certificate. Use a sample environment; do not touch a real customer's device without written permission and a backup. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free ticketing tool or a spreadsheet, a free remote-help practice setup, a free document editor.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What a good help desk feels like
Goal: define the service, the customers and the promise.
1. Describe a good help-desk experience you had and a bad one. What made the difference?
2. Define the service: who can ask, how (phone, chat, email), what you will fix, what you will not, and when you are open.
3. Define priorities: urgent (cannot work), high, normal, low, with target first-reply and fix times.
4. Write your promise on one page in plain words.
Evidence: service description, priority table, one-page promise.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a ticket log of at least 20 simulated issues, a knowledge-base set and a satisfaction survey.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Set up the help desk
1. Build the ticket form: name placeholder, contact, device, what happened, what they tried, urgency.
2. Write 10 common issues (password reset, no internet, printer, slow computer, email not syncing, lost file, suspicious message, screen sharing, software install, new starter set-up).
3. For each, write a step-by-step fix in plain words, with a picture only if it is your own.
4. Write reply templates for first reply, need more information, fixed, escalated.
Evidence: ticket form, 10 fix guides, 4 reply templates.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Ticketing, communication and escalation
Short modules:
- Ticketing: one ticket per issue, status, owner, notes, time stamps, closing only when the customer confirms.
- Communication: calm tone, no jargon, repeat back the problem, say what happens next and when, never blame the user.
- Escalation: when to ask a senior (security, data loss, repeated failure, anything you are not sure is safe), how to hand over notes so the customer does not repeat themselves.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Run the simulation
1. A peer or mentor plays customers and sends you at least 20 issues over several sessions, including angry and confused ones.
2. For each ticket record: time to first reply, time to fix, steps taken, outcome, customer mood.
3. Practise three escalations with proper hand-over notes.
4. Update your fix guides when you find a better way.
Evidence: ticket log with 20 or more tickets and three escalation notes.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Service review circle
1. Present: average first-reply time, average fix time, share fixed first time, customer scores.
2. Listen to two recorded or written replies; peers mark tone and clarity.
3. A mentor from an IT service team picks one ticket and shows how they would have done it differently.
Evidence: metrics table, tone feedback, mentor example.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Rewrite the three weakest guides and templates.
2. Run another 10 tickets and compare the metrics.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: ready to serve real customers, more practice, or specialise in one area.
Evidence: before/after metrics and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the service, the metrics, the best and worst tickets and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Tele and Video Conferencing Operations; Internet Infrastructure Basics; Chatbots for Business.
3. Contribute back: share your fix guides, reply templates or priority table so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Simulation of 20 tickets: average first reply 12 minutes, average fix 38 minutes, fixed first time 14 of 20 = 70%.
2. After guide fixes, 10 tickets: first reply 8 minutes, fix 26 minutes, fixed first time 8 of 10 = 80%.
3. Improvement in fix time = (38 - 26) / 38, about 31.6%.
4. Priority: 'cannot work' reply target 15 minutes, fix 4 hours; 'normal' reply 4 hours, fix 2 working days.

## Case exercise
A user says their email stopped working and asks you to remote in and also gives you their password in the chat. Questions: What do you do about the password? What permission do you need? When do you escalate? Write the reply.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Why one ticket per issue? 2. What are the four priority levels? 3. Why repeat the problem back to the customer? 4. When should you escalate? 5. What should a hand-over note contain? 6. What does 'fixed first time' mean? 7. Why close only when the customer confirms? 8. Why avoid jargon? 9. Name two things you must not do on a real device. 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. Never ask for a password in a reply. Never take over a device without permission. Keep ticket notes free of personal data.
