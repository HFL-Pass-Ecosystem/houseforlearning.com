# EU-01 Food Industry and Food Technology: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Learning Course, about 150 hours (DO 105 h, SHARE 30 h, STUDY 15 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised food-safety principles; this is not a licence or a certificate. Check local food rules with an adviser before you sell anything. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 5 h): Who eats what, and who will pay
Goal: pick one small food product and one real first buyer.
1. Walk a local market or shop for one hour. Write down 10 products, their price per kilo or per pack (with currency) and who buys them.
2. Find a gap: a taste, a size, a diet, a price point, or a freshness that is missing.
3. Talk to 5 possible buyers (a cafe, a shop, a canteen, neighbours). Ask: what do you buy now, how often, what do you pay, what annoys you?
4. Write a one-page product idea: product, buyer, price you hope for, pack size.
Evidence: the market walk table and the 5 buyer notes.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 7 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who buys it, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course: a pilot batch record, a cost sheet, a label, a buyer's written feedback.
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 35 h): Make the pilot batch
1. Write the recipe for one small batch. Weigh every ingredient. Note time and temperature.
2. Agree with a local anchor partner (a shared kitchen, a small producer or a training kitchen) where you will make it.
3. Make 3 trial batches. After each, change one thing only. Record taste, texture, colour and yield (weight out divided by weight in).
4. Pick the final recipe and scale it to a 30-day pilot size.
Evidence: recipe card, 3 trial records, final recipe.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 5 h): Food safety, shelf life, packs and labels
Short modules:
- Food safety basics: the hazard-and-critical-control-point way of thinking. List each step, ask what could go wrong (germs, chemicals, foreign bodies, allergens), and decide how you stop it. Hand washing, clean surfaces, separating raw and ready food, safe temperatures.
- Shelf life: how heat, water in the food, acid, salt and sugar change how long food lasts. Test it: keep samples and check on days 1, 3, 7, 14.
- Packs and labels: name, ingredients in order of weight, allergens clearly shown, net weight, batch code, date, maker contact. Each country has its own rules; write down the rule you must follow and who told you.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 35 h): Run the 30-day pilot
1. Produce small batches to your plan and sell or give for paid trial to real buyers.
2. Keep a daily log: date, batch code, ingredients cost, units made, units sold, waste, complaints.
3. Keep a sample of each batch until it expires.
4. If a buyer complains, write what happened and what you changed.
Evidence: 30-day log.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 15 h): Costing circle
1. Build the cost sheet: ingredients, packaging, labour hours at a fair rate, energy, kitchen rent, delivery, waste. Put the currency on every line.
2. Cost per unit = total cost divided by units sold. Margin = (price - cost) divided by price.
3. Present to the circle. A trade mentor reviews the sheet and tells you which cost you forgot.
4. Taste test: peers score taste, look and price from 1 to 5 and say why.
Evidence: cost sheet reviewed by a mentor, taste scores.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 35 h): Finish and measure
1. Fix the biggest problem found in the circle (cost, taste or shelf life).
2. Run one more week and compare: cost per unit, waste percent, repeat buyers.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: before/after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 8 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, recipe, safety plan, cost sheet, buyer feedback, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one buyer.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, buyer note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 5 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your next-step plan for 90 days: what to sell, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course (for example Trading and Distribution or Online Sales).
3. Contribute back: share your recipe card template, label checklist or cost sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name three kinds of food hazard. 2. Why separate raw and ready food? 3. What is yield and how do you calculate it? 4. What does the label need to show? 5. Name two things that make shelf life longer. 6. Calculate margin: price 120, cost 84 (same currency). 7. Why keep a sample of each batch? 8. What should you do after a complaint? 9. Which costs do beginners forget? 10. What is your next-step decision and why?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, pack or slide. No personal data of buyers in shared files. No health claims on labels (for example "cures" or "boosts immunity").
