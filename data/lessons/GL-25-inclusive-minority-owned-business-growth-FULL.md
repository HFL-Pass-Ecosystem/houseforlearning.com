# GL-25 Inclusive and Minority-Owned Business Growth: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with the inclusion, equality and decent-work ideas in the UN Sustainable Development Goals. This is not a licence or a certificate. Programme names, schemes and eligibility rules change and differ by country; always confirm on the official page. Nothing here is financial or legal advice. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free spreadsheet, a free document editor, public programme pages of governments and development bodies.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who is held back, and what helps
Goal: describe one real business and the barrier that slows its growth.
1. Explain in plain words: some owners face extra barriers (little collateral, fewer contacts, language, disability, gender or social bias, rural location). Programmes and networks exist to open doors.
2. Choose one real small business with the owner's agreement, or a sample one.
3. Ask the owner: what is the single biggest thing stopping growth (money, customers, skills, paperwork, contacts)?
4. Write the 12-month growth goal in numbers (sales, jobs, new customer type).
Evidence: business description, the main barrier and the goal.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a growth plan, a funding map, a network list and a readiness checklist.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Map the funding and support options
1. List 6 to 8 public options in your country or region: small-business loans, guarantee schemes, grants, public tenders set aside for small or underrepresented owners, microfinance, savings groups.
2. For each, record: who qualifies, amount range, what proof is needed, how long, cost, official link.
3. Check each against the business: eligible, maybe, not eligible, and why.
4. Note where a recognised certification or registration is needed and what it involves (awareness only).
Evidence: funding and support map with eligibility checks.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Funding options, certification awareness and networks
Short modules:
- Funding options: debt versus grant versus savings versus investors; cost of each; what lenders look at (cash flow, history, security); why bad terms hurt growth.
- Certification awareness: some schemes and tenders look for registration or recognised status; know what proof is needed, who issues it, and that it is free or low-cost in many places; avoid anyone who charges large fees for a promise.
- Networks: trade associations, peer groups, mentors, supplier-development programmes, incubators, women's and community business groups.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Write the growth plan
1. Draft the one-page plan: goal, three actions, who helps, money needed and when, risks.
2. Prepare the document pack the options ask for (registration, simple accounts, bank statements, plan, quotes). Mark what is missing.
3. List 10 people and groups to contact and write a short honest message for each.
4. Contact at least three (with the owner's agreement) and record the replies.
Evidence: growth plan, document-pack checklist, contact log.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Plan review circle
1. Peers read the plan as a lender would and note questions they would ask.
2. Check the numbers: does the loan fit the cash flow? Show a simple repayment table.
3. A mentor with small-business finance or enterprise-support experience reviews the plan.
Evidence: lender questions, repayment table, reviewed plan.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Fill the gaps in the document pack.
2. Rehearse a 3-minute pitch to a lender or a customer and record the feedback.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: apply now, build readiness first, or choose a different route.
Evidence: pitch feedback and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the business, the barrier, the options, the plan and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Business Coaching Practice; Crowdfunding Platform Practice; P2P Lending Platform Concepts.
3. Contribute back: share your funding-map template, document-pack checklist or plain-language pitch outline so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Business wants a loan of 12,000 over 24 months at 12% a year. Simple monthly payment about 565 (12,000 x 0.0471 from a standard payment formula). Total repaid 13,560.
2. Cash flow: monthly surplus 900. Payment 565 is 63% of surplus: acceptable with a buffer of 335.
3. Funding map: 6 options reviewed, 3 eligible, 1 maybe, 2 not.
4. Pitch practice: 3 minutes; feedback scores 4 of 5 on clarity, 3 on numbers.

## Case exercise
A person offers to 'guarantee' a grant for a 10% fee paid upfront. Questions: What are the warning signs? Where do you check? What is a safer path? Write the reply to the owner.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name three barriers some owners face. 2. Name three public funding options. 3. What do lenders look at? 4. Why can bad loan terms hurt growth? 5. What is a repayment table for? 6. Why avoid anyone charging large fees for a promise? 7. What goes in a document pack? 8. Why contact people with the owner's agreement? 9. How do networks help? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No stereotypes. No promises of funding. Confirm every scheme on its official page.
