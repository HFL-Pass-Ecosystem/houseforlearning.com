# GL-24 Selling Personal Protective Equipment: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised product-safety and conformity-assessment awareness for protective equipment. This is not a licence or a certificate. Protective equipment protects lives. You must not claim a protection level that a product does not have; real sales need proof of the right standard and local rules. This module is mapping and awareness, not certification. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free spreadsheet, public standards summaries and regulator pages, a free document editor.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Why this product is different
Goal: choose one category of protective equipment and define who needs it.
1. Explain in plain words: protective equipment is worn or used to reduce a hazard (head, eye, ear, hand, foot, breathing, high visibility, fall protection).
2. Learn that protective equipment is regulated: it must meet a standard for its hazard before it can be sold as protective, and labels must be accurate.
3. Choose one category (for example work gloves, safety footwear, eye protection) and a buyer type (a workshop, a farm, a school lab, a construction crew).
4. Interview one buyer: what hazard, what do they buy now, what complaints, who decides.
Evidence: category, buyer interview notes.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a standards map, a buyer map, a supply check and a labelling checklist.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Map standards and buyers
1. Find the relevant standard family for your category and your country or region's rule on conformity. Write what the standard covers and the marking a compliant product shows.
2. Note the risk class if the rule uses classes (simple, intermediate, complex) and what proof that class needs.
3. List 10 possible buyers and rank by need, size and how they buy (tender, shop, online).
4. Write what each buyer asks for before purchase (test report, certificate, sample, price).
Evidence: standards map, buyer ranking, buyer asks list.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Standards awareness, labelling and supply checks
Short modules:
- Standards awareness: what a standard is, what 'tested to' means versus 'looks like', what documents a supplier should hold, why you ask for them and check the issuing body is real.
- Labelling: the markings that must be on the product and pack, size and use instructions, language rules, batch and date, maker identity.
- Supply checks: ask for the document pack, verify with the issuing body, order samples, test fit and feel, inspect a batch, keep records of lot and supplier for traceability and recall.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Run a supply and claims check
1. Request documents from two suppliers for the same category. Compare completeness and consistency.
2. Verify at least one document with its issuing body or public register and record the answer.
3. Write the product claim sheet: what you may say (only what the document proves) and what you must never say.
4. Write the buyer one-page: use, limits, instructions, care, when to replace.
Evidence: document comparison, verification record, claim sheet, buyer page.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Compliance review circle
1. Peers try to find a claim that goes beyond the proof and mark it.
2. Discuss a public recall or counterfeit case (no company names) and which check would have caught it.
3. A mentor with safety or quality experience reviews your claim sheet.
Evidence: claims marked, reviewed claim sheet.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Rewrite claims to match proof exactly.
2. Create a recall-readiness page: how you would trace and withdraw a lot.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: ready to sell this category, need more proof, or choose a simpler category.
Evidence: recall page and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the category, the standards map, the supply check, the claims and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Industrial safety courses in the catalogue; Marketplace Fulfilment Selling; E-Commerce Inventory Management.
3. Contribute back: share your standards-map template, document checklist or claim-sheet template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Gloves: 500 pairs at 2.40 = 1,200. Packaging 100, shipping 150: 1,450 total, 2.90 per pair. Price 4.50. Margin per pair = 1.60 (35.6%).
2. Documents: supplier A gives a test report and a marking matching the standard family; supplier B gives a certificate with a different model name. B fails the consistency check.
3. Claim sheet: allowed 'tested to the standard shown in the report for mechanical risks'; not allowed 'suitable for all chemicals'.
4. Recall readiness: lot code on every carton; 500 pairs in 5 lots of 100; a lot can be pulled and buyers contacted in a day.

## Case exercise
A buyer asks you to confirm your gloves protect against a chemical not listed in the test report. Questions: What do you reply? What proof would be needed? What are the risks of saying yes? Write the reply.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Why is protective equipment regulated? 2. What does 'tested to a standard' mean? 3. Name three things a label should show. 4. Why verify a certificate with the issuing body? 5. What is traceability? 6. Why must claims match proof exactly? 7. What is a risk class? 8. What should you do if you doubt a document? 9. What is a recall-readiness page? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. Never claim protection a product is not proven to give. No counterfeit goods. No brand names or logos.
