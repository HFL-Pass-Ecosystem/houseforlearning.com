# GL-71 Holographic and Immersive Theatre: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module prototypes a short immersive show with free tools. Use ordinary light and sound safely. 'Hologram' effects here mean illusions such as projection on gauze or reflection tricks, not true holograms. Keep exits clear and avoid flashing lights that can harm people. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What immersive theatre is
Goal: understand the forms and pick a story.
1. Describe: the audience is inside the story space, not in front of a stage. List forms: walk-through, promenade, projection rooms, sound-led, small-group.
2. List effects: projection on gauze or walls, reflections, light and sound cues, simple props.
3. Choose a short story (5 to 10 minutes) from your own writing or from public-domain material.
4. Write a one-page idea: audience size, space, story, what they do.
Evidence: notes and the idea page.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: where does the audience stand, how do they exit safely, what is the one memorable moment.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a script and cue sheet, a space plan, a safety plan, a rights list and a test showing.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Write the script and cue sheet
Goal: turn the idea into timed cues.
1. Break the story into five to eight scenes.
2. For each scene write: what the audience sees, hears, and does; how long; what the cast or recording does.
3. Create the cue sheet: time, light, sound, projection, actor. Number each cue.
4. Mark every cue that needs a safety check (strobe, fog, loud sound, dark).
Evidence: the script and cue sheet.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Staging, content rights and safety
Short modules:
- Staging: sightlines, where to stand, one-way flow, quiet spaces for those who need a break.
- Rights: your own story and sounds are yours; public-domain works are free to use but check the date and country; music and images need a licence or permission.
- Safety: clear exits, exit lights, no strobes beyond safe limits, volume limits, no open flames, fire rules, accessible routes, a headcount.
- Care: warn the audience about dark, noise and touch; let anyone step out.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the prototype
Goal: make the effects with free tools.
1. Make the projection content with free design and video tools. Test on gauze, a sheet or a wall in a darkened room.
2. Test a simple reflection illusion (a clear angled sheet reflecting an image on to a dark background) with safe, stable materials.
3. Record or build the sound with free tools; test the volume with a meter app.
4. Rehearse with three people and time each scene.
Evidence: the content files, test notes and rehearsal times.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Show critique
Goal: learn from audience reaction.
1. Run the 5 to 10 minute show for 8 to 12 guests.
2. After the show, guests write one thing they felt, one thing they did not understand, and one thing they would change.
3. A theatre mentor comments on pace and safety.
4. Sort comments into fix now, fix later, ignore.
Evidence: guest comments and sorted list.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Revise and decide
Goal: improve the prototype.
1. Fix the top three problems.
2. Run the show again for a new group.
3. Compare scores or comments before and after.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision: expand, change or stop.
Evidence: before and after comments and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the idea, the script, a short video clip, the feedback and the revision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a guest from the audience.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: VR Live Events, Podcasting, 3D Printing.
3. Contribute back: share your cue sheet template or safety plan so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Scenes: 6 scenes of 1, 2, 2, 3, 1.5, 0.5 minutes = 10.0 minutes.
2. Audience 10, space 30 m2 = 3 m2 each; exits 2 clear.
3. Sound: test at 70, 80 and 90 dB; choose 80 dB maximum for the main show and 70 for quiet scenes (check local limits).
4. Feedback: 12 guests; 9 liked scene 3 (75%); 5 did not understand scene 5 (41%).

## Case exercise
A show uses strobing light in the final scene. Questions: Is it safe to keep? How do you warn guests? What changes with an audience of 20 in the same room? Write a half-page safety revision.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What makes theatre immersive? 2. What is a cue sheet? 3. Why keep exits clear? 4. What is wrong with strobes? 5. When is a story public domain? 6. Why warn guests about dark and noise? 7. What is an audience sightline? 8. Why run the show twice? 9. How do you sort feedback? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Keep exits clear, no strobes or open flames, safe volume. Use only your own, public-domain or licensed content. No filming of guests without consent.
