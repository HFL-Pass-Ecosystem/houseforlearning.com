# GL-60 Portable and Wireless Charging Solutions: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module tests charging devices for efficiency and safety. Use only low-voltage devices you own, never open a charger or battery, and never charge on soft furniture or unattended overnight. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): How charging works and why it gets hot
Goal: understand efficiency, heat and the risks.
1. Learn the basics: charger converts mains to low voltage; battery stores energy; some energy always becomes heat.
2. Efficiency = energy stored in the battery divided by energy taken from the wall, as a percentage.
3. List the charger types: wired, power banks, wireless pads, car chargers. Note where each is used.
4. Write one page: what would a fair test look like?
Evidence: notes and a test idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: how do we keep each test fair, how do we measure heat safely, when do we stop.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a test plan, a results table, a safety checklist and a buying guide.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Write the test plan
Goal: design a fair test for 3 to 5 chargers.
1. Choose chargers you already own or borrow. Mark each by number, not brand.
2. Choose one device to charge, always starting from the same battery level, in the same room temperature.
3. Use a plug-in energy meter (any type) for wall energy, and note the battery capacity from the device's own battery information.
4. Write the steps, what you measure (wall energy, time, case temperature by touch-safe thermometer or by hand for a short time) and the stop rule: stop if hot to touch, swollen, smelly or damaged.
Evidence: the test plan with a stop rule.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Standards, heat safety and efficiency
Short modules:
- Standards: look for the safety marks required in your country on chargers and cables; cheap unmarked chargers may skip safety tests.
- Heat safety: batteries can fail if they overheat, are crushed or are overcharged; never charge damaged devices or cover chargers during use.
- Efficiency: wireless charging is usually less efficient than wired because of alignment and heat.
- Standby loss: some chargers use power when nothing is connected.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Run the tests
Goal: collect the results.
1. Run each test twice and average. Keep a table: charger number, start level, end level, time, wall energy in Wh, notes on heat.
2. Test one wired and one wireless charger with the same device.
3. Measure standby draw of each charger for 30 minutes with nothing connected.
4. Stop at once if anything goes wrong and write what happened.
Evidence: results table and incident log (if any).

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Test-results circle
Goal: compare results and spot errors.
1. Present the table in 6 minutes.
2. Peers look for unfair steps (different start level, different room, short tests).
3. A mentor with electronics experience comments on safety.
4. Redo one test if the circle finds a fault.
Evidence: peer notes and the redo (if any).

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Calculate and write the buying guide
Goal: turn data into advice.
1. Efficiency for each charger and the cost of wasted energy over a year = wasted Wh x charges per year x price per kWh divided by 1,000. Add the currency.
2. Rank chargers by efficiency, heat and standby draw.
3. Write a one-page buying guide: what to look for and what to avoid, without brand names.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: calculation sheet and buying guide.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the test, the table, the ranking and the guide.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a friend who uses the buying guide.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Car Charging Stations, Electric Car and EV Basics, Smart Apparel.
3. Contribute back: share your test plan or results table template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Charger 1: battery 15 Wh stored; wall energy measured 19 Wh. Efficiency = 15 / 19 = 79%.
2. Wireless pad: wall energy 25 Wh for the same 15 Wh. Efficiency = 60%. Wasted = 10 Wh.
3. Standby: 0.4 W for 24 h = 9.6 Wh a day = 3.50 kWh a year.
4. Yearly waste for the pad: 10 Wh x 365 charges = 3.65 kWh x 0.20 = 0.73 (currency shown).

## Case exercise
Three chargers are tested: one wired, one pad, one unknown mark. One became very warm and smelled. Questions: What do you do now? Which result is unfair and why? Rank the three. What does your buying guide say? Write a half-page guide without brand names.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Define charging efficiency. 2. Why start each test at the same battery level? 3. Why is wireless usually less efficient? 4. What should you do if a battery swells? 5. What is standby draw? 6. Why average two runs? 7. Why avoid unmarked chargers? 8. Why not cover a charger? 9. How do you compute yearly waste cost? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Never open chargers or batteries. Never leave tests unattended. Stop at the first sign of heat, swelling or smell. Use no brand names in shared files.
