# GL-47 Stem Cell Therapy (Awareness Level): full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.
This is an AWARENESS course. You learn how things work, the rules and the ethics. You do not practise treatment, diagnosis or clinical work, and you use only public, made-up or simulated material.
Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: a person who has seen bold claims and wants to separate evidence from hype.
1. Market scan: Collect 5 public statements or adverts about stem cell treatments. Note the claims made, the evidence offered, and whether a regulator has commented.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Read a public overview from a national health body or a respected research society on what stem cells are and which uses are proven.
2. Write a one-page summary: proven uses, experimental uses, uses with no evidence.
3. Make a red-flag list for adverts: miracle language, no trial data, high fees, testimonials in place of evidence.
4. Write who regulates this field in your country and how to report a doubtful clinic.
Evidence: Summary, red-flag list and regulator note.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Regulation: treatments need testing in controlled trials and approval before routine sale. Unapproved treatments can be harmful and expensive.
- Evidence quality: a stronger study has a comparison group, enough people, results shared in a journal and repeated by others. A single story is not evidence.
- Ethics: informed consent, honest information, fair access, and respect for the source of any cells.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Review 3 adverts against your red-flag list and score each.
2. Write a short message you could send to a relative who is tempted by an advert: what to ask and who to see first.
3. Note which of the questions can be checked from public sources.
Evidence: Advert scores and relative message.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Present your red-flag scores.
2. Discuss one study summary: what it shows and what it does not.
3. Agree five questions to ask before any treatment.
Evidence: Five questions.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: red flags found, correct classification of uses, clarity of the relative message, peer rating.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Summary: some blood-related uses are established; many other uses are experimental; some adverts have no evidence.
2. Red-flag list: 'miracle cure', no trial data, very high fee, only testimonials, 'works for everything'.
3. Score: three adverts, 4, 5 and 2 red flags out of 5.
4. Questions before any treatment: Is it approved here? Where is the trial data? Who regulates this?

## Case exercise
A relative wants to pay a large sum for an advertised treatment. Questions: What do you ask? Which public sources do you check? Whom do you suggest they see first? Write the message and one report route for a doubtful clinic.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is a controlled trial? 2. Name three red flags in an advert. 3. Why is one story not evidence? 4. Who regulates this field in your country? 5. What is informed consent? 6. Why can unapproved treatment be harmful? 7. What would you ask a clinic first? 8. What is outside this course? 9. What did peers add? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Approval of cell-based treatments, advertising rules for treatments, and clinic licensing in your country.
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
Awareness only. No treatment advice, no promotion of any clinic. Not medical advice. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
