# GL-02 Robotics Fundamentals: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised machinery-safety and risk-assessment thinking. This is not a licence or a certificate. Work only with low-voltage kits or a simulator; never connect mains power or work with batteries you do not understand without a qualified adviser. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free robot simulator or a low-cost classroom kit, a free code editor, a free spreadsheet.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What a robot is, and what job it could do
Goal: pick one simple task that a small robot or a simulated robot can do.
1. List the three parts of every robot: sense (sensors), think (a controller and a program), act (motors or other movers). Draw one robot you know, such as a floor cleaner, and label each part.
2. Walk through a home, shop or workshop. Write down 5 repetitive tasks (moving, sorting, following a line, measuring).
3. Pick one task that is simple, safe and repeatable. Write the success rule in numbers (for example: follow a 2-metre line without leaving it, 8 times out of 10).
4. Write a one-page plan: task, parts or simulator, success rule, what could go wrong.
Evidence: the task list and the one-page plan.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a robot or simulator build log, a working program, 10 test runs and a safety note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build and first program
1. Set up the simulator or assemble the kit with a mentor. Check each wire, each moving part and each battery before power is on.
2. Write the smallest program: make the robot move forward for 2 seconds and stop. Run it 5 times and record what happens.
3. Add one sensor reading (for example distance or light). Print the reading to the screen and check it against a ruler or a known object.
4. Write the control rule in plain words first (if the sensor says X, then do Y), then in code.
Evidence: build log, first program, sensor readings table.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Sensors, motors and safe operation
Short modules:
- Sensors: what they measure (distance, light, touch, speed), how noisy they are, why one reading is never enough and how to average several.
- Motors: speed, torque, why wheels slip, why batteries sag, how to stop quickly and safely, why moving parts get a guard.
- Safe operation: a clear test area, an emergency stop you can reach, hands away from moving parts, a second person watching, a written risk list (what, how bad, how likely, what you do).
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Make it do the task
1. Write the full program for your task. Build it in small steps and test after each step.
2. Run 10 official test runs. For each record: date, version of the program, result (pass or fail), time taken, and what you saw.
3. When it fails, change one thing only (a speed, a threshold, a sensor position) and run again. Keep every version.
4. Fill in the risk list and review it with the mentor before the final runs.
Evidence: 10 test runs, program versions, risk list.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Show-and-test circle
1. Demonstrate to the circle. Peers watch for unsafe moments and unclear behaviour.
2. Work out the pass rate = passed runs divided by total runs. Compare with your success rule.
3. Trade ideas: each peer suggests one change to the sensor, the rule or the layout. The mentor reviews the risk list.
Evidence: demo notes, pass-rate table, reviewed risk list.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Apply the best change from the circle and run 10 more tests.
2. Compare before and after: pass rate, time per run, number of near misses.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: improve, add a second task, or stop.
Evidence: before/after table and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the task, the build, the program, the test results, the risk list and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Internet of Things (IoT); Artificial Intelligence in Practice; 3D Printing.
3. Contribute back: share your build guide, wiring diagram in your own drawing, or test-run sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Task: line-following robot, 2-metre line. 10 runs: 7 pass, 3 leave the line. Pass rate = 7/10 = 70%. Success rule was 80%.
2. Change one thing: lower speed from 60% to 45% power. 10 runs: 9 pass. Pass rate 90%.
3. Time cost: at 60% power a run took 8 s, at 45% it took 11 s. Time per run rose by 3 s, or 37.5% (3/8).
4. Sensor averaging: three readings 12, 15 and 12 cm; average = 39/3 = 13 cm. One reading of 15 would have been 2 cm off.

## Case exercise
Your robot passes 9 of 10 runs but twice nearly hit a bench edge. Questions: What is the pass rate? Is it safe enough to demonstrate? Which two controls would you add? Write a risk list with what, how bad, how likely, and what you do.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Name the three parts every robot has. 2. Why is one sensor reading not enough? 3. What should you do before switching power on? 4. Why change only one thing between test runs? 5. What is a pass rate and how do you calculate it? 6. Name two safe-operation habits. 7. What does an emergency stop do and where must it be? 8. Why do batteries make robot behaviour change over time? 9. What goes into a risk list? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No weapons, no robots near people or animals without supervision, no mains power. Keep the test area clear.
