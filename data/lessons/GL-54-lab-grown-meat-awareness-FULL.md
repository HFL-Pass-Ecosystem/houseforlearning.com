# GL-54 Lab-Grown Meat (Awareness Level): full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This is an awareness course built on public information. It is not a production course. Approval status changes by country and date; always check the current official source. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What cultivated meat is and why it is discussed
Goal: explain the idea in plain words and list the claims people make.
1. In your own words describe the idea: animal cells grow in a controlled vessel with nutrients to make meat tissue without raising the whole animal.
2. List claims for it (animal welfare, land use, emissions, food security) and claims against (cost, energy use, taste, safety questions, jobs).
3. For each claim write the kind of evidence needed to prove it.
4. Write one question you want answered by the end.
Evidence: your explanation and the claim list.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: which claim is a fact, which is an opinion, which source is neutral.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a science summary, a cost-curve note with sources, an approvals map, and an ethics note.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Review the science and cost curve
Goal: summarise how it is made and what drives cost.
1. Read at least three public review articles or official reports. Record title, date and type of source.
2. Draw a block flow in words: cell source, growth medium, vessel, harvest, shaping, cooking.
3. List what makes it costly: growth medium, energy, equipment size, sterile control. Note which reports say costs have fallen and by how much, with date and source.
4. Mark each number as 'reported', 'estimate' or 'confirmed' and never mix them.
Evidence: the source log and the block flow.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Cell culture basics, regulation and ethics
Short modules:
- Cell culture basics: cells need nutrients, warmth, oxygen control and sterile conditions; scaling from a small dish to a large vessel is the hard step.
- Regulation: regulators in some places have approved limited sales and many have not. Check the official list for your country and write the date.
- Safety review: regulators look at cell source, growth medium, contamination control and allergens.
- Ethics: animal welfare, farmers' livelihoods, cultural and religious views on food, honest labelling.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the approvals and positions map
Goal: map where it stands in at least five countries and who says what.
1. Choose five countries including yours. For each write approval status and date from an official source.
2. Record the main position of farmers' groups, consumer groups and food authorities, quoting their public statement in short, in your own words.
3. Note labelling rules proposed or in force.
4. Mark anything you could not confirm.
Evidence: the approvals map and source log.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Journal-club circle
Goal: read like a scientist and test each other's reading.
1. Each learner presents one source in 4 minutes: what it claims, how it knows, what it leaves out.
2. The circle asks who paid for the work and what might be missing.
3. A mentor from food science or policy comments.
4. Update your claim list.
Evidence: presentation notes and the updated list.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Write the neutral briefing
Goal: write a balanced two-page briefing for a decision maker.
1. State what is known, what is estimated and what is not known.
2. Give both sides of the main arguments fairly.
3. List what would change the picture (cost milestones, approvals, taste tests).
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision: what you would watch in the next year.
Evidence: the briefing.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the briefing, the map, and your three watch items.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a reader who knows nothing about the topic.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Alternative Protein Food Products, Vertical Farming, Biofuel Production Overview.
3. Contribute back: share your source log or briefing template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Source log: 12 sources read; 7 reports, 3 journal reviews, 2 official notes. Share of official/regulatory sources = 2/12 = 16%.
2. Cost claim: one report says cost per kg fell from 100 to 20 (same currency, same basis). Fall = (100-20)/100 = 80%. Label: 'reported'. A fossil-free price comparison needs the same basis, so mark it 'not comparable' until checked.
3. Scale: a vessel grows 1 unit of cells to 10 units each cycle; after 3 cycles = 10^3 = 1,000 units (idealised; real growth is lower).
4. Approvals map: 5 countries checked; 2 allow limited sale, 1 is reviewing, 2 not yet. Approved or limited share = 40%.

## Case exercise
A newspaper says cultivated meat will be cheaper than farmed meat next year. Questions: Which numbers would you ask for? Which are 'reported', 'estimate' or 'plan'? Who gains if the claim is believed? Write a half-page reply that is fair to both sides.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Explain the idea in two sentences. 2. Name two claims for and two against. 3. Why is scale-up hard? 4. Why mark numbers 'reported' or 'estimate'? 5. Where do you check approval status? 6. What do regulators check? 7. Why does the funder of a study matter? 8. Name one ethics question. 9. Why must labels be honest? 10. What will you watch next?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Awareness only: no production, no health claims. Quote no figure without a source and date. Present both sides fairly.
