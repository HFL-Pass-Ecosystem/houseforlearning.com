# GL-56 Solar Power Design and Installation Basics: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This module designs a rooftop system on paper. Installation must be done by qualified, licensed people. Never work on a roof or on electrical equipment without training. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): How a rooftop system works and what a site needs
Goal: learn the parts and pick a site to size.
1. Learn the parts: panels, mounting, DC cables, inverter, protection, meter, earthing; a battery is optional.
2. Learn the rule: yearly energy = system size in kW x peak sun hours per day x 365 x performance ratio (about 0.75 to 0.85).
3. Choose a site (a home or small shop). From bills, write its monthly use in kWh and the price per kWh (with currency).
4. Note the roof direction, tilt, shade and free area on a sketch.
Evidence: notes, bill summary and roof sketch.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: how much shade, which direction the roof faces, what happens in a power cut.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a sizing sheet, a bill of materials with prices, a single-line sketch, a safety list and a payback sheet.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Size the system
Goal: choose a system size that fits roof, use and budget.
1. Find peak sun hours for your location from a free public solar map (use the yearly average and a low month).
2. Roof limit: usable area divided by area per panel gives the maximum number of panels. Use the panel size in the datasheet you choose (any maker; do not name it in shared files).
3. Target size: yearly use divided by (peak sun hours x 365 x 0.8). Pick the smaller of roof limit and target.
4. Compute yearly energy for a low, middle and high sun case.
Evidence: the sizing sheet with three cases.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Sizing, electrical safety and standards awareness
Short modules:
- Sizing: match inverter size to panels (the DC to AC ratio is often 1.1 to 1.3). Check string voltage stays within the inverter's limits in cold weather.
- Electrical safety: DC stays live in daylight; use isolators, correct cable ratings, earthing and protective devices; only licensed installers connect to the grid.
- Work at height: fall protection, safe access and weather rules.
- Standards awareness: your country has rules for installation and grid connection; find the name of the rule and who approves systems.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Draw the layout and list materials
Goal: show how the system fits and what it costs.
1. Sketch the panel layout on the roof, leaving access paths and keeping clear of edges and shade.
2. Draw a single-line sketch: panels, strings, isolator, inverter, protection, meter. Label each part by function, not by brand.
3. List materials and quantities. Get at least three local quotes (any suppliers, not named in shared files) and enter prices with currency.
4. Note the warranty periods asked for.
Evidence: layout, single-line sketch and priced materials list.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Installer Q&A circle
Goal: have a trained installer test your design on paper.
1. Present the design to an installer or electrician mentor.
2. They ask about cable size, protection, shade and inspection.
3. Record each correction.
4. Revise the sketch.
Evidence: Q&A notes and the revised sketch.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Payback and the customer summary
Goal: explain the numbers honestly to a customer.
1. Yearly saving = energy used on site x price per kWh + exported energy x export price (if any).
2. Simple payback = net cost divided by yearly saving. Show a case with 0.5 percent a year panel ageing.
3. Write a one-page summary a home owner can read: size, cost, saving, risks, what a licensed installer must do next.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: payback sheet and the customer summary.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the site, the design, the cost, the payback and the risks.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from a home owner or an installer.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Wind Energy Fundamentals, Car Charging Stations, Greenhouse Gas Auditing.
3. Contribute back: share your sizing sheet or customer summary template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Yearly energy = 4.0 kW x 5.0 h x 365 x 0.80 = 5,840 kWh.
2. Panel count: 10 panels of 0.4 kW = 4.0 kW. Roof area needed at 2.0 m2 per panel = 20 m2 (leave 20% for access = 25 m2).
3. Saving: use on site 70% of 5,840 = 4,088 kWh x 0.15 = 613; exports 30% x 0.05 = 88. Total yearly saving = 701 (currency shown).
4. Payback: net cost 4,800 / 701 = 6.8 years.

## Case exercise
A home uses 5,000 kWh a year. The roof faces east and has 18 m2 of unshaded area. Questions: How many panels fit and what size? How does an east roof change output? What must a licensed installer do? Write a one-page customer summary with a low, middle and high sun case.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. Write the yearly energy rule. 2. What is performance ratio? 3. Why is DC dangerous in daylight? 4. What is a DC to AC ratio? 5. Why leave access paths on a roof? 6. Why get three quotes? 7. What is simple payback? 8. Who may connect to the grid? 9. Why show low, middle and high sun? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Design on paper only. Never install, climb or wire. All real work is done by licensed people under local rules.
