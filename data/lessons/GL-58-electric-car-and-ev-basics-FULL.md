# GL-58 Electric Car and EV Basics: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised standards; this is not a licence or a certificate. This is a knowledge and maintenance-checklist module. High-voltage systems can kill. Never open, test or repair high-voltage parts unless you are trained and authorised. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number. Units are always shown too.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): How an electric car works
Goal: name the parts and what each does.
1. Learn the main parts: battery pack, battery management system, inverter, motor, onboard charger, charge port, thermal system, low-voltage battery, brakes with energy recovery.
2. Learn the units: kWh is energy in the pack, kW is power. Range = pack kWh divided by consumption in kWh per 100 km, times 100.
3. Look at an electric vehicle with the owner's permission. Photograph or sketch the visible parts, never touching orange high-voltage cables.
4. Write one page: how does energy travel from the plug to the wheels?
Evidence: the parts table and the energy path page.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: what is high voltage, who may work on it, what do you do after a crash.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a component map, a maintenance checklist, a charging cost sheet and a safety card.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the component map
Goal: make a labelled map of the car's main systems and what can fail.
1. Draw a block diagram: plug, charger, pack, inverter, motor, wheels, with the low-voltage side separate.
2. For each block write its job, how it can fail, and a sign you can notice (warning light, noise, range drop).
3. Mark high-voltage parts in a clear colour on your diagram and write 'trained staff only'.
4. Compare with a public owner's guide of any model and fix errors.
Evidence: the labelled component map.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Batteries, charging and high-voltage safety
Short modules:
- Batteries: cells make modules, modules make a pack. Heat, deep discharge and fast charging all age a pack. Capacity falls slowly over years.
- Charging: slow home charging, faster public charging and the fastest direct-current charging. Connector types differ by country.
- High-voltage safety: packs run at hundreds of volts; orange cables mark high voltage; only trained people with insulated tools and a safe-off procedure may work on them.
- After a crash or flood: keep away, switch off if safe, call the emergency services and tell them it is an electric vehicle.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Write the maintenance checklist
Goal: separate what an owner can check from what needs a trained technician.
1. Owner checks: tyre pressure and wear, wiper blades, lights, brake fluid level, coolant level where visible, warning lights, charge cable condition.
2. Technician checks: pack health report, insulation tests, cooling system, brake service, software updates.
3. Add a schedule from the owner's guide: daily, monthly, yearly.
4. Test the checklist with a volunteer owner and fix unclear items.
Evidence: the checklist and the volunteer notes.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Workshop circle
Goal: have a technician test your knowledge.
1. A trained technician mentor quizzes the group on the component map.
2. Peers swap checklists and mark anything unsafe.
3. Discuss three real-world cases from public reports (without naming makers).
4. Revise your safety card.
Evidence: quiz notes, peer marks and the revised safety card.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Cost it and finish the owner's guide
Goal: compare running costs honestly.
1. Cost per 100 km = consumption in kWh per 100 km x price per kWh. Compare with a petrol or diesel car using your local fuel price (currency shown).
2. Include public charging cost, home tariff and yearly maintenance.
3. Write a two-page owner's guide: parts, checks, charging tips, safety card.
4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: cost comparison and the owner's guide.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.
1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the map, the checklist, the cost comparison and the safety card.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from an owner or a trained technician.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Car Charging Stations, Micro-Mobility Services, Car Sharing Business.
3. Contribute back: share your component map or checklist template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Range = pack 60 kWh / 16 kWh per 100 km x 100 = 375 km (ideal; in cold or at speed it is less).
2. Cost per 100 km at home tariff 0.15 per kWh: 16 x 0.15 = 2.40. A car using 7 litres per 100 km at 1.50 per litre = 10.50 per 100 km.
3. Yearly saving over 15,000 km: (10.50 - 2.40) x 150 = 1,215 (currency shown).
4. Charging time: 7 kW point, 30 kWh needed = 4.3 h (ignoring losses; add about 10%: 4.7 h).

## Case exercise
An owner drives 40 km a day and has a 3 kW home point. Questions: How long does it take to recover 40 km of range? What owner checks should be on the list? What must only a trained technician do? What do you do after a flood? Write a one-page owner's guide.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is the difference between kWh and kW? 2. How do you work out range? 3. What colour marks high-voltage cables? 4. Who may work on high voltage? 5. Name two things that age a battery. 6. What is the first thing to do after a crash? 7. Name two owner checks. 8. Why do connector types matter? 9. How do you compute cost per 100 km? 10. What is your decision?

## Rules for this course
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. No work on high-voltage parts. Do not touch orange cables. Follow the maker's published safety guide and local law. No vehicle photographs with number plates or people.
