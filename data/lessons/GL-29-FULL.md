# GL-29 Business Process Outsourcing Services: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised good practice; this is not a licence, a certificate or professional advice. Check local rules with an adviser before you act for real. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every number.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Who needs this, and who is your first user
Goal: name one real first user: one small company that wants a repeated task done for it.
1. Market scan: List 10 tasks small firms outsource (data entry, support replies, invoice checks, scheduling). Note the usual price per task or per hour (with currency) and the accuracy buyers expect.
2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.
Evidence: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).
Evidence: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Start the practice task
Task steps:
1. Pick one process, for example sorting and answering customer questions from a sample mailbox. Use sample data only.
2. Write the process as numbered steps with who does what, how long each takes, and where mistakes happen.
3. Time 20 sample tasks. Work out the average minutes per task and the error rate.
4. Draft a price per task and a service level: speed, accuracy, hours of work, what you will not do.
Evidence: Process document and timing sheet.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Short modules
- Service levels: a written promise of speed and quality, such as 95% of tasks done within 24 hours with at most 2 errors per 100. Say what happens if you miss it.
- Quality control: sample checks, a checklist, a place to record errors, and a fix that stops the same error coming back.
- Data security: work only with what you need, use strong sign-in with a second step, never keep customer data on personal devices, and delete it when the job ends as agreed.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Continue and log
Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.
1. Run a 2-week trial on sample or consented data. Log tasks done, minutes, errors, rework.
2. Add a check step: a second person reviews 1 in 5 tasks.
3. Update the price and the service level using real numbers.
Evidence: Trial log with error rate.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Review circle
1. Walk the circle through your process in 5 minutes.
2. Peers look for the step most likely to go wrong.
3. Add one control to that step.
Evidence: Annotated process map.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
2. Measure the result against your goal: minutes per task, error rate, on-time share, margin per task.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.
Evidence: a before and after table and the decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Process: reply to customer questions. Steps: receive, sort, find answer, reply, log. Times: 1, 1, 3, 2, 1 = 8 minutes.
2. Error rate: 20 tasks, 2 with a wrong answer = 10%. Target: at most 2 in 100.
3. Price: 8 minutes at a cost of 30 per hour = 4 labour; overhead 25% = 1; margin 20% on 5 = price about 6.3 per task (same currency).
4. Control: second person checks 1 in 5 replies; errors recorded in a table with cause and fix.

## Case exercise
Your trial shows 8 minutes per task and 6% errors. The buyer wants 2% and 6 minutes at a price of 5.5. Questions: Can you meet the service level? What changes cut time and errors? What price keeps 15% margin? What would you say no to? Write a one-page offer.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is a service level? 2. Give one example of a measurable service level. 3. What is an error rate? 4. Why sample-check work? 5. Name two data security habits. 6. Price per task: 6 minutes at an hourly cost of 30 (same currency). What is the labour cost? 7. What happens when you miss the promise? 8. Why write the process down? 9. What would you refuse to do? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?

## Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)
Points to check for this course: Contracts and data-processing agreements with the customer, confidentiality, cross-border data transfer.
Personal data: in the EU the data-protection regulation applies. India's data-protection Rules are phased in: consent managers around Nov 2026 and the main duties around May 2027. UAE executive regulations are still uncertain: check the official portal. Collect little, ask consent clearly, protect it, delete it. AI tools: if you use an AI tool in this course, the EU AI Act transparency duties (for example telling people they are dealing with AI, and marking AI-made content) have applied since 2 Aug 2026. Stand-alone high-risk AI systems are now due from 2 Dec 2027, and AI inside regulated products from 2 Aug 2028 (these dates were delayed; check the official source for any change).
Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

## Rules for this course
Only sample or consented data. Never take on work involving personal records without a written data-protection agreement. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
