# GL-11 Biometric Sensor and Security Solutions: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised data-protection principles: lawful basis, consent, minimisation, security. This is not a licence or a certificate. Biometric data is among the most sensitive personal data. In this module you evaluate options on paper and with sample or consenting volunteer data only; you do not collect biometric data from real people without a lawful basis and written consent. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free spreadsheet, a free document editor, a free diagram tool.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): What biometrics are, and when they are the wrong answer
Goal: choose one use case and decide whether biometrics fit it at all.
1. Explain biometrics in plain words: identifying or checking a person by body or behaviour traits such as fingerprint, face, iris, voice or typing rhythm.
2. Learn two jobs: verifying (are you who you say you are, one-to-one) and identifying (who is this, one-to-many). Identifying is riskier.
3. Choose a use case (office door, school attendance, bank app, warehouse). Ask: would a card, a code or a person do the job as well?
4. Write the need in one line and the harm if it goes wrong (wrong person let in, right person locked out, data stolen).
Evidence: use-case description and the 'is it needed' note.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: an options table, an accuracy worksheet, a privacy impact note and a recommendation.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Compare the options
1. List 3 to 4 options: for example fingerprint, face, card and PIN, card plus PIN. For each, note cost, speed, ease of use, and what happens when it fails.
2. Make an accuracy worksheet: false accepts (wrong person let in) versus false rejects (right person refused). Explain why lowering one raises the other.
3. Note who might be treated unfairly: people with worn fingerprints, different skin tones in poor light, disabilities, children, the elderly. Write what the fall-back path is.
4. List where the data would be stored, who could see it and for how long.
Evidence: options table, accuracy worksheet, fairness and fall-back notes.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Accuracy, consent and data protection
Short modules:
- Accuracy: false accept and false reject rates, thresholds, why published accuracy may differ from your place, why testing in real conditions matters.
- Consent: free, informed, specific, withdrawable; a real alternative must exist; in many places biometric data needs extra protection and a clear lawful basis.
- Data protection: store a template not a picture where possible, encrypt, limit access, delete on leaving, log access, plan for a breach: you cannot change your fingerprint like a password.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Write the privacy impact note
1. Answer in plain words: what data, why, how long, who sees it, what could go wrong, what you do about each.
2. Draft the notice a person would read before enrolling: purpose, alternative, retention, who to ask to delete.
3. Draft the breach plan on one page: who is told, in what time, what is done.
4. Score each option from 1 to 5 on need, accuracy, fairness, privacy risk and cost.
Evidence: impact note, notice, breach plan, scoring table.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Challenge circle
1. Peers play the regulator and the affected person: why this much data, why not a card, what if it leaks.
2. Compare your scores and rework any that look favourable to your preferred option.
3. A mentor with security or legal-awareness experience reviews the notice and the breach plan.
Evidence: challenge notes, revised scores, reviewed notice.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Update the note with the challenges.
2. Write the final recommendation in one page, including 'do not use biometrics' if that is the honest result.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision.
Evidence: final recommendation and decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the use case, the options, the privacy impact, the recommendation and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Privacy Protection Practice; DDoS and Cyber-Attack Prevention; Internet Infrastructure Basics.
3. Contribute back: share your options table template, notice wording or breach-plan template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Door system: 1,000 attempts a day. False reject rate 2% = 20 valid staff refused a day. False accept rate 0.1% on 50 outsider attempts = 0.05 wrong entries a day, about 1 in 20 days.
2. Lower the threshold to cut rejects to 0.5% (5 a day) and false accepts rise to 0.8% = 0.4 a day, about 2 in 5 days. The trade-off is clear.
3. Card plus PIN: cost 5 per card, 300 staff = 1,500, no sensitive data stored, lost-card risk handled by revoking.
4. Score: need 2/5, accuracy 4/5, fairness 3/5, privacy risk 2/5 (high risk), cost 3/5; card plus PIN wins on privacy.

## Case exercise
A school wants face recognition for attendance of 800 children. Questions: Is it needed? What is the less intrusive option? What consent would be needed from whom? Write a one-page recommendation.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What is the difference between verifying and identifying? 2. Define false accept and false reject. 3. Why does lowering one raise the other? 4. Why is biometric data special? 5. What does free consent mean? 6. Why must an alternative exist? 7. Why store a template rather than a picture? 8. Name one group who might be treated unfairly. 9. What goes in a breach plan? 10. What was your recommendation and why?

## Law check
Law check (31 Oct 2026, from public sources; confirm with an adviser): India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 has its Rules notified in November 2025 and switches on in phases; the consent-manager part starts about November 2026 and the main duties of organisations from about May 2027. The UAE personal-data law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) has been in force since January 2022, but sources disagree on whether its executive regulations are issued; check the UAE legislation portal and the UAE Data Office. The EU data-protection regulation (GDPR) is unchanged in its core principles.

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No collecting real biometric data without a lawful basis and written consent. No surveillance of people.
