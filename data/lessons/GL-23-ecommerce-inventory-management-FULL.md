# GL-23 E-Commerce Inventory Management: full lesson text (DRAFT v0.1, 31 Oct 2026)

Long Module, about 60 hours (DO 42 h, SHARE 12 h, STUDY 6 h). Brand-neutral. Aligned with recognised stock-control and demand-planning practice. This is not a licence or a certificate. Use sample or your own shop data; never share real customers' details in stock files. Currency is always shown: use your own currency and write it next to every money number.

Tools: a free spreadsheet, a free form tool for counts, a free document editor.

Journey: Idea > Action > Value > Empowerment > Regeneration.

## Lesson 1 - Idea (STUDY, 2 h): Why running out or overbuying both cost money
Goal: choose a sample shop and write the stock problem in numbers.
1. Explain in plain words: too little stock loses sales and customers; too much stock ties up money, takes space and may expire or go out of fashion.
2. Choose a sample shop with 20 products (or a real small shop with permission) and 6 months of sales numbers.
3. Group products by sales speed: fast, medium, slow. Mark the 5 products that matter most.
4. Write the aims: never out of stock on the top 5, lower stock value, fewer slow items.
Evidence: shop and data description, product groups and aims.

## Lesson 2 - Idea (SHARE, 3 h): Kick-off circle
1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a stock sheet with reorder points, a forecast, a count record and a review.
Evidence: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

## Lesson 3 - Action (DO, 14 h): Build the stock sheet
1. Create the sheet: product code, name, supplier, cost, price, lead time (days), minimum order, units on hand, units on order.
2. Add monthly sales for the last 6 months and compute the average per day.
3. Compute for each product: days of stock = on hand / average daily sales.
4. Mark products with fewer days of stock than the lead time.
Evidence: stock sheet with days of stock.

## Lesson 4 - Action (STUDY, 2 h): Demand forecasting and reorder logic
Short modules:
- Demand forecasting: averages, seasonality (festivals, weather), trends, promotions; why a forecast is a range not one number.
- Reorder logic: reorder point = average daily sales x lead time + safety stock; safety stock covers uncertainty; order quantity balances order cost and storage cost.
- Counting: regular counts, cycle counts for fast items, recording damage and returns, finding differences between the sheet and the shelf.
Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

## Lesson 5 - Action (DO, 14 h): Forecast and set reorder points
1. Forecast the next 3 months for the top 10 products with a low, middle and high figure.
2. Set safety stock for each (for example 20% of lead-time demand to start) and justify the choice.
3. Compute reorder point and order quantity for each of the top 10.
4. Add a simple warning column that turns red at the reorder point.
Evidence: forecast table, reorder points, warning column.

## Lesson 6 - Value (SHARE, 6 h): Stock review circle
1. Run the sheet forward with three invented weeks of sales: when would each order trigger?
2. Peers test it with an unusual week (a sudden promotion, a late delivery).
3. A mentor with retail or warehouse experience reviews your safety stock and your counting plan.
Evidence: forward simulation and reviewed settings.

## Lesson 7 - Value (DO, 14 h): Finish and measure
1. Adjust reorder points and safety stock.
2. Do a mock stock count of 10 products with an invented count and explain every difference.
3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: use as is, refine with more data, or switch method.
Evidence: adjusted sheet, mock count, decision note.

## Lesson 8 - Empowerment (SHARE, 3 h): Review room
1. Present to a review room: the shop, the stock sheet, the forecasts, the tests and your decision.
2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.
Evidence: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

## Lesson 9 - Regeneration (STUDY, 2 h): Check and next step
1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Marketplace Fulfilment Selling; E-Commerce Growth Marketing; Box Subscription Services.
3. Contribute back: share your stock-sheet template, reorder-point calculator or count form so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).
Evidence: check result and the 90-day plan.

## Worked examples
1. Product A: sells 5 a day, lead time 8 days, safety 10. Reorder point = 5 x 8 + 10 = 50.
2. On hand 62, on order 0, days of stock = 62 / 5 = 12.4. Orders trigger in 2.4 days (12 units / 5).
3. Product B: sells 0.5 a day, 120 on hand, days of stock = 240. Overstock; stop ordering until below 40 days.
4. Mock count: sheet says 30, shelf shows 27; 2 damaged and 1 unrecorded sale explain the 3.

## Case exercise
A festival in 3 weeks will double demand for product A, and the supplier lead time grows to 14 days. Questions: New reorder point? When must you order? What is the risk of ordering too much? Write the plan.

## Check questions (draft set, 10)
1. What does too much stock cost? 2. What does too little stock cost? 3. How do you compute days of stock? 4. State the reorder point formula. 5. What is safety stock for? 6. Why is a forecast a range? 7. Why do regular counts matter? 8. How can seasonality change a forecast? 9. What should a warning column do? 10. What was your decision and why?

## Rules for this module
No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. No real customer data in stock files. Show currency on every money cell.
