# Business, Engineering and Building: course detail (DRAFT)

Method and recording only. Figures invented. Engineering and safety rules vary by country; always cite your own national standard and a qualified person must review. Expert review before publish. No fees.

## BUS-001 Lead a team through change
**Worked example:** Change: new ordering system in 6 weeks. Affected: order team, finance, customers. Messages: week 0 "why and when" (manager); week 3 "training dates" (team lead); week 6 "go-live help desk" (project lead). Measures: orders entered per day, errors per 100, staff confidence score 1 to 5.
**Case card:** a firm moves from paper forms to an online form; learners map groups, plan 3 messages, pick 3 measures.
**Checks:** 1 Why explain the reason first? *People accept change better when they know why.* 2 Name 3 groups affected by a change. *Staff, customers, suppliers.* 3 One good simple measure? *Errors per 100 items.* 4 What if resistance stays high? *Listen, find the cause, adjust support.*

## BUS-002 Negotiate a supplier contract
**Worked example prep:** Target price 100; walk-away limit 115; trades: longer contract for lower price, faster payment for discount, volume for free delivery. Result inside limit: 108 with 12-month term. One-page summary lists price, term, delivery, payment, review date.
**Case card:** role-play pair; supplier wants 120.
**Checks:** 1 What is a walk-away limit? *The worst deal you will accept.* 2 Name a trade. *Longer term for a better price.* 3 What goes in the summary? *Price, term, delivery, payment, review date, who signs.* 4 Who signs? *The person with authority under your policy.*

## BUS-003 Write a one-page business case
**Worked example layout:** Problem (2 lines); options A, B, do nothing; costs and benefits with sources; choice and why; main risk and the plan for it; next step with date.
**Case card:** a firm considers a second delivery van.
**Checks:** 1 Why include "do nothing"? *It is the baseline option.* 2 Cite what? *Where each number came from.* 3 How long? *One page.* 4 Biggest risk to state? *The one most likely or most harmful, with a plan.*

## ENG-001 Select a cable to the standard
**Worked example (three-phase):** Load 22 kW, 400 V, power factor 0.85. Current I = P / (sqrt3 x V x pf) = 22,000 / (1.732 x 400 x 0.85) = about 37.4 A. Select a cable whose tabulated rating, after correction for installation method and temperature, is above 37.4 A; then check voltage drop is within the limit in your national standard. Cite the clause and table number.
**Case card:** three loads with lengths and installation methods.
**Checks:** 1 Formula for three-phase current? *P / (sqrt3 x V x pf).* 2 Why correct the rating? *Heat, grouping and method change what the cable can carry.* 3 What else after current? *Voltage drop and short-circuit withstand.* 4 Where is the rule? *Your national standard; cite clause and table.*

## ENG-002 Check a protection setting
**Worked example:** One-line: supply, main breaker, feeder breaker, motor. Rule of thumb for order: devices nearer the load must trip faster than devices nearer the supply for the same fault (selectivity). Compare given settings with the coordination curve; if the feeder curve crosses above the main's, report the mismatch and ask the responsible engineer.
**Case card:** two curves and a one-line diagram.
**Checks:** 1 What is selectivity? *Only the device nearest the fault trips.* 2 Who changes a setting? *Only the authorised person.* 3 What do you report? *The device, the setting, the mismatch, the evidence.* 4 Is a pass on paper enough? *No; tests on site confirm.*

## ENG-003 Prepare a site test record
**Worked example fields:** circuit, device, test type, reading, limit, pass/fail, tester, date, instrument serial and calibration date.
**Case card:** readings for 6 circuits; learners fill the sheet and judge pass/fail against given limits.
**Checks:** 1 Why record calibration? *Readings are only valid with a calibrated instrument.* 2 Blank fields allowed? *No; write "N/A" with a reason.* 3 What if a reading fails? *Mark fail, tell the responsible person, retest after fixing.* 4 Who signs? *The tester and, where needed, the reviewer.*

## BLD-001 Inspect a site for safety
**Worked example (hazard, risk, action):** Unprotected edge on first floor: risk High; action edge protection today; owner site manager; due same day. Trailing cable across walkway: Medium; reroute; electrician; tomorrow.
**Case card:** a set of 10 invented site photos described in text.
**Checks:** 1 Order of control (highest first)? *Eliminate, substitute, engineer controls, admin, PPE.* 2 What does a good action list have? *Owner and date.* 3 Who stops work for imminent danger? *Anyone; follow stop-work rule.* 4 Record where? *The inspection log.*

## BLD-002 Estimate quantities
**Worked example:** Wall 12 m x 2.4 m = 28.8 m2; minus a door 2.0 m2 and window 1.5 m2 = 25.3 m2; add 10 percent waste = 27.8 m2. Enter in the bill of quantities with unit, description, quantity.
**Case card:** a simple room drawing.
**Checks:** 1 Why add waste? *Cutting and breakage losses.* 2 Deduct openings? *Yes, per the measurement rule you use.* 3 State units. *Always, e.g. m2.* 4 Source of rules? *Your measurement standard.*

## BLD-003 Prepare a handover pack
**Worked example index:** as-built drawings; test certificates; manuals; warranties; maintenance schedule; contact list; permits.
**Case card:** a pack with 3 planted gaps.
**Checks:** 1 Why an index? *So the client finds items fast.* 2 Name 3 documents. *Warranties, test certificates, manuals.* 3 Who accepts? *The client or their representative, signed.* 4 When is it complete? *When every item on the contract list is present.*
