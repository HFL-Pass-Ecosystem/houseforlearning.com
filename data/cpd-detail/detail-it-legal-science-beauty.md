## ITC-001 Configure a secure network
**Worked example:** Two networks: staff and guest. Staff: strong passphrase, current encryption, admin page not reachable from guest. Guest: internet only. Default passwords changed; firmware updated; settings written in a one-page record with date and who.
**Case card:** a small office with 8 staff and public Wi-Fi.
**Checks:** 1 Why separate guest and staff? *A guest cannot reach internal devices.* 2 First change on a new router? *Replace default passwords.* 3 Why document? *Someone else can repeat or audit it.* 4 How often update firmware? *Regularly, per policy.*

## ITC-002 Respond to a security alert
**Worked example triage:** High: ransomware note on a server; Medium: failed logins from one address; Low: one blocked spam. Playbook order: identify, contain (isolate the device), preserve evidence, escalate, recover, report.
**Case card:** five alerts.
**Checks:** 1 Contain before what? *Before recovery, to stop spread.* 2 Who do you tell? *Your incident lead per the playbook.* 3 Should you wipe a device at once? *No; preserve evidence first.* 4 What goes in the report? *What, when, impact, actions, lessons.*

## ITC-003 Document a change
**Worked example:** Change: update mail server. Reason: security fix. Risk: 30-minute outage. Rollback: restore snapshot. Test: send/receive test. Approver and time recorded.
**Case card:** a firewall rule change.
**Checks:** 1 Why a rollback plan? *To recover if it fails.* 2 Who approves? *The change owner per policy.* 3 What is tested? *That the service works and nothing else broke.* 4 Record the result? *Yes, with time.*

## LEG-001 Draft a clear client letter
**Worked example:** "Dear Ms K, You asked what happens next. In short: we will file the form by 10 Nov and you will need to sign by 8 Nov. If anything changes we will tell you the same day. Please call if you have questions." Under one page, next step and date included.
**Case card:** explain a filing step.
**Checks:** 1 Replace "heretofore". *"until now" or remove.* 2 What must a letter include? *Next step and date.* 3 Check readability how? *A simple readability test and a colleague read.* 4 Advice limits? *Say what you cannot advise on.*

## LEG-002 Review a contract clause by clause
**Worked example table:** Clause number, one-line summary, risk (H/M/L), reason, suggested wording. Risk clauses to look for: payment terms, termination, liability, indemnity, change of terms.
**Case card:** a sample 8-clause contract.
**Checks:** 1 Why summarize each clause? *To confirm understanding.* 2 Name 3 risk clause types. *Liability, termination, indemnity.* 3 Safer wording example? *Replace "unlimited liability" with a stated cap.* 4 Who decides? *The client with advice from a qualified lawyer.*

## LEG-003 Manage a file to the audit standard
**Worked example file order:** index; instructions; correspondence; notes with dates; documents; billing; closing checklist.
**Case card:** a messy sample file.
**Checks:** 1 Why date every note? *Shows the order and timing of decisions.* 2 What is on the closing checklist? *Outcome, documents returned, retention date.* 3 Who may see it? *Only authorised people.* 4 Retention? *Per your regulator and policy.*

## SCI-001 Design a repeatable test
**Worked example:** Question: does fertiliser A raise plant height? Control: no fertiliser. Same soil, light, water. Measure height at day 14 in cm. Repeats: 10 plants each group. Write the method so another lab can repeat it.
**Case card:** a question about drying time of paint.
**Checks:** 1 Why a control? *To compare against no treatment.* 2 Why repeats? *To see the spread.* 3 What must the method state? *Materials, steps, measures, units.* 4 Change one thing at a time? *Yes.*

## SCI-002 Analyse results with error bars
**Worked example:** group A heights 10, 12, 11; mean 11; spread (standard deviation, sample) = 1. Group B: 14, 15, 13; mean 14; spread 1. Plot means with error bars of one SD; difference of 3 is large compared with the spread, so likely real; a formal test confirms.
**Case card:** two small datasets.
**Checks:** 1 Mean of 4, 6, 8? *6.* 2 What do error bars show? *Spread or uncertainty.* 3 Label axes how? *Name and unit.* 4 Does a gap prove a cause? *No; design and test matter.*

## SCI-003 Report findings for peer review
**Worked example sections:** Title; abstract; introduction; methods; results; discussion; limits; references.
**Case card:** turn a result table into a short report.
**Checks:** 1 What goes in limits? *What was not tested and possible errors.* 2 Cite how? *One agreed style, every source.* 3 Where do results go? *Results, not discussion.* 4 Why share raw data? *So others can check.*

## BEA-001 Take a client history
**Worked example fields:** name; contact; reason for visit; allergies; skin conditions; medicines; pregnancy; previous reactions; consent to record. Referral rule: if a condition is outside scope, refer on and record it.
**Case card:** a client reports a skin rash and a new medicine.
**Checks:** 1 Why ask about allergies? *To avoid harm.* 2 When refer on? *When it is beyond your scope or unsafe to proceed.* 3 Privacy? *Keep the record secure; share only with consent.* 4 Date and sign? *Yes, every record.*

## BEA-002 Apply infection control
**Worked example order:** wash hands; clean tools with approved cleaner; disinfect or sterilise per tool type; dry; store covered; log date and who. Single-use items are used once and discarded.
**Case card:** six items: towel, tweezers, nail file, couch cover, brush, gloves.
**Checks:** 1 When wash hands? *Before and after each client and after glove removal.* 2 Single-use means? *Use once, then discard.* 3 Why a log? *Proof of routine for audits.* 4 Follow whose rule? *Your local regulator and product instructions.*

## BEA-003 Record consent
**Worked example:** Explain treatment, benefits, risks, aftercare in plain words; client reads and signs and dates; you countersign; review at each visit if anything changes.
**Case card:** a client hesitates about a patch test.
**Checks:** 1 Can consent be withdrawn? *Yes, at any time.* 2 Plain words for "contraindication"? *A reason not to do the treatment.* 3 Where is the form kept? *Securely, per your retention rule.* 4 Minors? *Follow local rules on guardian consent.*
