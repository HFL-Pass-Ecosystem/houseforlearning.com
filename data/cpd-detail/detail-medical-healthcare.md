# Medical and healthcare: course detail (DRAFT)

Teaches communication and recording only. No clinical advice. All cases and figures are invented worked-example figures; no real person or place. A clinical subject expert must review every line, and local rules override the examples. No fees.

---
## CPD-MED-001 Plain English for ward handovers

**Handover order used in this course (a common pattern; use your workplace's order if it differs):** Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation/request. We call it SBAR.

**Worked example (spoken, about 40 seconds):**
"Situation: Patient A, bed 4, is due his 6 pm dressing change and says the pain is worse than this morning. Background: he had knee surgery three days ago; the notes say he is allergic to one named medicine, shown on his wristband. Assessment: he looks tired and the dressing is dry. Request: please check his pain score at 6 and tell the doctor if it is higher than this morning's. Can you repeat the plan back to me?"

**Case card for practice:** Bed 7, Patient B, new this shift, speaks quietly, asks for the same question twice, family visiting at 5 pm, night nurse needs to know about a fluids chart that is open.

**Role-play checklist (scored):** states the patient and bed; gives the one main concern; gives only needed background; names the open task; uses no slang; asks for a repeat-back; confirms who holds each open task.

**Check questions with answers**
1. What does the "R" in SBAR ask for? *A clear request or recommendation.*
2. Why ask the listener to repeat the plan back? *To confirm they understood, catching errors early.*
3. Replace the slang "he's gone off": *"He is not eating or drinking as usual" or the specific fact.*
4. A handover is going well but you did not say who holds the open fluids chart. What do you do? *State it before you leave: name the person and the task.*
5. True or false: abbreviations are always safe if you know them. *False. Use only those the workplace approves; plain words are safer.*

## CPD-MED-002 Recording care notes to the standard

**Worked example (a good note):** "14 Mar, 10:20. Patient A reported pain in the right knee, 7 out of 10. Dressing dry and intact. Offered a rest and a drink; he accepted both. Told nurse in charge at 10:25. Signed: initials, role."

**A poor note (do not copy):** "Patient grumpy, pain bad, gave him something." *Opinion word, no number, no time, unclear action, unsigned.*

**Correcting an error:** draw one line through the wrong entry so it can still be read, write "error", initial and date it, then add the correct entry. Never erase or cover it. (Check your workplace's rule.)

**Case card for practice:** a short scenario about a fall with no injury; learners write the note, then score it on the checklist.

**Checklist:** date and time; facts only; what was done; who was told and when; signature and role; no personal details beyond need.

**Check questions with answers**
1. Which is fact: "He seemed upset" or "He was crying"? *"He was crying."*
2. How do you correct a mistake in a paper note? *Single line through, "error", initials and date, then the right entry.*
3. Why include the time of each entry? *So the order of events is clear to anyone who reads it.*
4. Name two details you should leave out of a note shared with a wider team. *Anything not needed for care, such as home address or family details.*
5. Who may read your note? *Only people with a care need to know.*

## CPD-MED-003 Talking with patients and families in a second language

**Four-step pattern used in this course:** Listen, Acknowledge, Explain, Check.

**Worked example (a worried relative):** "I can see this is worrying for you. (acknowledge) Let me explain what is happening now: the doctor has been asked to look at your mother, and I will tell you when they arrive. (explain) Would you like me to write that down? (check) What questions do you have?"

**Introduction script:** "Good morning, [name]. My name is ___ and I am your nurse today. I will check your blood pressure now. Is that all right?"

**Escalation message (who, what, when):** "To: nurse in charge. Patient D, bed 2, started feeling dizzy at 3:10 pm. He is sitting. Please come now."

**Case card for practice:** a daughter asks why her father is not eating; learners practise the four steps in two minutes.

**Check questions with answers**
1. Put in order: Explain, Listen, Check, Acknowledge. *Listen, Acknowledge, Explain, Check.*
2. How do you check a patient understood? *Ask them to say it back in their own words.*
3. Rewrite in plain words: "You are nil by mouth." *"Please do not eat or drink anything until the doctor says."*
4. When should you escalate? *When a patient looks worse, is in distress, or you are unsure; follow the local route.*
5. Why use the patient's name? *It is respectful and helps them know you are speaking to them.*
