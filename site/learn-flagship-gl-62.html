Car Sharing Business

Plan a small car-sharing or ride-sharing service: legal checks, insurance, booking, safety, pricing and fairness to drivers and riders.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner32:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain models: peer-to-peer, fleet and carpool.
  • Apply Set up booking, ID and insurance steps.
  • Analyse Compute cost per hour and per km.
  • Evaluate Judge safety, fairness and rules.
  • Create Write a launch plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design a small car-sharing service with legal checks, driver and rider vetting, booking flow, insurance, pricing and safety plan.

Condition

A computer, a spreadsheet, a practice case, two peers and a mentor. Real vehicles or riders are not used.

Standard

Legal and insurance checks are listed with sources; cost per km is correct; vetting and safety steps are written; fairness rules for drivers are included; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Entrepreneurs, community groups, fleet staff, employers with commute schemes. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Models of car and ride sharing 3 hUnderstand

Sharing a car can mean lending your own, booking a fleet by the hour or sharing journeys. Each model has different legal, trust and money needs.

  • Peer-to-peer: owners rent their cars to others through a platform. Needs strong identity checks and clear damage rules.
  • Fleet sharing: one operator owns cars, members book by the hour. Needs capital, parking and maintenance.
  • Carpooling: people with spare seats share trips and fuel costs. Needs matching and safety rules.
  • Choose by density, trust and parking in your community.
Worked example. A residential building with 120 flats and few parking spaces runs two shared cars booked by the hour. A village with long commutes starts with carpooling because it needs no vehicles.

Activity. Compare the three models for one community in a table and choose one with reasons.

Standard to meet. Table has pros, cons, capital needed and risks; choice is justified.

Module 2 Legal checks and insurance 5 hApply

Sharing can void ordinary insurance. Check before the first trip.

  • Ask the insurer in writing what is covered when the car is shared, who is a named driver, and what is excluded.
  • Check licence, registration, tax and commercial rules for charging fees.
  • Write terms: damage, fines, fuel or charge, cleaning, late return, accidents.
  • Keep copies and update yearly.
Worked example. An insurer confirms cover only for registered, declared vehicles and named drivers. An undeclared sharing claim would be refused, so the service declares every car and driver.

Activity. Build a legal checklist with a source for each item.

Standard to meet. Each item has an authority or insurer, answer and date.

Module 3 Vetting drivers and identity 4 hApply

You are responsible for who drives your cars.

  • Check licence, age, driving record and ID documents.
  • Set minimum experience and a trial drive for new members.
  • Re-check licences monthly and block expired ones automatically.
  • Protect personal data and explain how it is used.
Worked example. A monthly licence expiry report blocks three members until they upload new documents.

Activity. Write the vetting steps and a monthly routine.

Standard to meet. Steps cover document check, trial drive, expiry and data protection.

Module 4 Booking, handover and return 4 hApply

A clear handover prevents most disputes.

  • App or form: choose car, time, deposit.
  • Photograph the car, fuel or charge level and mileage at pickup and return.
  • Late and cancellation rules; support line.
  • Release the deposit within 48 hours of a clean return.
Worked example. A 2,000 INR deposit is held and released after a clean return. Photos show a new scratch from the previous driver, who is charged.

Activity. Design the booking and handover flow in eight steps.

Standard to meet. Flow has times, photos and deposit rules.

Module 5 Costs, pricing and break-even 5 hAnalyse

Know the cost per hour and per kilometre before setting prices.

  • Fixed costs: insurance, depreciation, parking, tax. Variable costs: fuel or power, tyres, servicing.
  • Cost per hour = fixed cost per day / hours available; cost per km = variable cost / km.
  • Utilisation = hours booked / hours available; break-even depends on it.
  • Show members an example bill before they book.
Worked example. Fixed cost 520 INR per day and variable 7 INR per km. A 4-hour, 40 km trip costs about 208 + 280 = 488 INR. At 40 per cent utilisation of 10 available hours (4 booked hours), the 520 INR fixed cost needs at least 130 INR per booked hour before any variable cost.

Activity. Build a cost sheet and price three sample trips.

Standard to meet. Maths is correct and currency is shown.

Module 6 Safety, incidents and support 4 hEvaluate

Plan for breakdowns, accidents and misuse.

  • Emergency numbers in the car and the app; first-aid kit and warning triangle.
  • Steps: make everyone safe, call help, photograph, report, record.
  • Roadside help and a replacement car plan.
  • Incident log and monthly review.
Worked example. A driver reports a flat tyre; roadside help arrives in 40 minutes and the booking is extended at no charge. The log shows three flat tyres in a month, so tyre checks are added.

Activity. Write an incident plan and run a mock breakdown.

Standard to meet. Plan has steps, contacts and a recorded mock run.

Module 7 Fairness, access and community 3 hEvaluate

A shared service should include people with fewer options.

  • No discrimination in access; clear and equal vetting rules.
  • Offer pay-as-you-go, lower deposits or a payment plan for low-income members.
  • Provide accessible options such as hand controls where possible.
  • Publish fees and an appeals route.
Worked example. A rule offers hand controls on request, with notice, and caps late fees so they cannot exceed the hourly rate.

Activity. List fairness rules and a fee cap.

Standard to meet. Rules are clear and apply to all.

Module 8 Pilot and launch plan 4 hCreate

Test small and measure.

  • Start with one car and 20 members for 30 days.
  • Measure utilisation, damage per 100 trips, member score and cost per trip.
  • Fix the top three problems.
  • Decide: grow, change model or stop.
Worked example. The pilot reaches 60 per cent utilisation, one minor damage and a member score of 4.4 out of 5. The team adds a second car near a station.

Activity. Write a pilot plan with targets and a decision rule.

Standard to meet. Targets are numbers and a stop rule is stated.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: legal checklist, booking flow, cost sheet, safety plan, launch plan. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

ten locations and global

Your place

Licensing and insurance rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
