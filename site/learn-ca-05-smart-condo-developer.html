Smart condo developer

Run a real smart condo developer pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small smart condo developer offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: property, construction and smart buildings. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read the market for smart homes 6 hUnderstand

Smart condos add sensors, controls and data to ordinary flats.

  • Buyers care about energy bills, security, comfort and resale value.
  • List features by cost and value: smart thermostat, leak sensors, access control, EV charging, shared-space booking.
  • Interview ten buyers or tenants: which features they would pay for.
  • Check local condo prices and absorption (units sold per month).
  • Walk two finished smart buildings and list what residents actually use and what sits idle.
  • Ask about winter: heating, ventilation and parking with a plug-in outlet matter more in cold regions. Write which feature solves a real winter problem.
  • Separate must-have features that every unit gets from optional upgrades that a buyer can add for a price.
Worked example. Leak sensors cost 80 per unit and cut insurance claims; buyers rank them above voice assistants. For a 40-unit building, 40 x 80 = 3,200 buys sensors for every unit, a small share of a 17.2M build cost.

Activity. Interview ten people and rank features by willingness to pay.

Standard to meet. Ranking backed by interviews.

Module 2 Know development rules 6 hApply

Developers follow zoning, building and condo law.

  • Zoning and official plans decide what you may build.
  • Building code and permits come before work; inspections follow.
  • Condo law (provincial) governs sale, deposits, disclosure and cooling-off.
  • Smart systems that collect resident data fall under privacy law.
  • Check what happens to each feature if the internet fails for a day.
  • Energy and accessibility rules in the building code affect your design; check the provincial code edition in force.
  • Check the provincial rule on how long deposits stay in trust and what must be disclosed to buyers before they sign.
Worked example. Row: 'Take buyer deposits | provincial condo law | hold in trust and give disclosure | before any sale | lawyer's trust account record'. If each of 40 units takes a deposit, there are 40 separate deposit records to reconcile with the trust account.

Activity. Build a rules table for a 40-unit building.

Standard to meet. Rules cited with source and date.

Module 3 Plan the building and the smart layer 10 hApply

Design the shared network and standards first.

  • Choose open standards so devices from different makers work together.
  • Plan cabling, power and network closets at design time.
  • Decide who owns data from sensors and who can see it.
  • Plan updates and support for ten years.
  • Ask the building operator who will pay for software updates in year 5.
  • Draw a floor plan marking every device, cable run, power point and network closet, and give each device a number.
  • Make every smart function work by hand if the network fails: lights, door locks and heating must still operate.
Worked example. Open standard chosen so that a failed hub vendor can be replaced without rewiring. If each of 8 units on a floor has 12 devices, the floor plan lists 8 x 12 = 96 devices, each with a power point, a network path and an owner.

Activity. Draw a one-page system plan for a floor.

Standard to meet. Every device has a power, network and owner.

Module 4 Cost the development 8 hEvaluate

Land, construction, soft costs and financing.

  • Hard costs: construction. Soft costs: design, permits, fees, marketing.
  • Financing cost grows each month of delay.
  • Contingency of 10 to 15%.
  • Compute profit = sales - all costs.
  • Cost features per unit and per year, not only at purchase.
  • Run a stress case: costs up 10% and sales down 5%. Write the new profit and margin.
  • Check the provincial and municipal charges and taxes that apply to new buildings and add each to the soft costs.
Worked example. 40 units x 520,000 = 20.8M; cost per unit 430,000 = 17.2M; profit 3.6M (17%). With costs up 10%, cost is 18.92M and profit falls to 1.88M (9%). A 10 to 15% contingency on 17.2M is 1.72M to 2.58M, so a 10% contingency (1.72M) leaves profit of 1.88M. Teaching figures in CAD.

Activity. Build a development pro forma with a 10% cost rise case.

Standard to meet. Margin shown under stress.

Module 5 Manage construction and quality 8 hApply

Quality is checked at each stage.

  • Stage gates: foundation, frame, services, finishes.
  • Walk the site weekly with a checklist.
  • Test smart systems before handover.
  • Keep a defects list and close it with dates.
  • Photograph every wall before it is closed, so later repairs know where cables run.
  • Write the pass criteria for each gate before work starts, such as inspection signed and cable test records filed.
  • Do not let finishes start until services pass their test; fixing a hidden cable later costs more.
Worked example. Defect list: 62 items at handover walk; 55 closed in 14 days (89%); 7 still open and carried to a new dated list. Repeat the walk when the 7 are done and sign off only when the count is zero.

Activity. Write a stage-gate checklist.

Standard to meet. Each gate has pass criteria.

Module 6 Sell, hand over and support 6 hCreate

Buyers need clear information.

  • Give plain disclosure and a demonstration unit.
  • Train residents to use systems.
  • Set a help desk and response times.
  • Collect satisfaction after 90 days.
  • Test each smart system with the owner's phone, not only the installer's.
  • Give each buyer a written handover pack: warranty terms, system guide, who to call and what the building owner can see.
  • Make sure features remain usable if a resident does not own a smartphone.
Worked example. Demo-unit visits: 120; offers 30; sales 22. Rates: offers 30/120 = 25%, sales 22/30 = 73%. 22 of 40 units sold is 55%, so 18 units remain to sell. If the sales team tracks this each week, it can see early whether the pace is on plan.

Activity. Run a mock sales funnel.

Standard to meet. Funnel numbers recorded.

Module 7 Manage data, safety and sustainability 6 hEvaluate

Building smart means building trusted.

  • Collect only needed data; tell residents; allow deletion.
  • Secure networks and update devices.
  • Plan fire, flood and power-cut responses.
  • Aim for energy performance targets.
  • Give residents a one-page guide in the languages residents use (French in Quebec).
  • Change every default password before handover and keep an access list of who can open the system.
  • Review sensor and camera data rules once a year and tell residents what changed.
Worked example. Policy line: 'Camera data kept 30 days, viewed only after an incident.' A camera that records 30 days and is viewed twice a year, each time after an incident, leaves a short log of 2 entries that residents can ask to see.

Activity. Write a resident data note and risk register.

Standard to meet. Notes in plain words.

Module 8 Pilot a floor and plan scale 8 hCreate

Prove on one floor.

  • Pilot with 8 units for 90 days.
  • Measure energy savings, faults and satisfaction.
  • Stop rule: more than 3 faults per unit per month means redesign.
  • Plan the next phase.
  • Collect resident feedback at 30 and 90 days and file it.
  • Compare each pilot unit's energy use with a similar unit without the smart layer, using meter readings.
  • Log each fault with date, device, cause and fix time to see which device fails most.
Worked example. Energy savings 14%; faults 1.5 per unit per month, so 8 units give 12 faults a month, well under the stop line of 3 per unit (24 a month); proceed. If a unit used 900 kWh a month before, 14% is a saving of 126 kWh.

Activity. Run the pilot review.

Standard to meet. Review has measures and decision.

assessment

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why plan cabling at design time?

Model answer: Retrofits cost far more.

Q2. What is contingency?

Model answer: Extra budget for cost rises.

Q3. Why use open standards?

Model answer: Devices from different makers work together and can be replaced.

Q4. What does condo law govern?

Model answer: Sale, deposits and disclosure.

Q5. Why keep deposits in trust?

Model answer: The law requires it to protect buyers.

Q6. What is a stage gate?

Model answer: A check before the next stage starts.

Q7. What should residents be told about data?

Model answer: What is collected, why and how to delete it.

Q8. Why train residents?

Model answer: So systems are used correctly.

Q9. What triggers a redesign in the pilot?

Model answer: More than 3 faults per unit per month.

Q10. What is profit in a pro forma?

Model answer: Sales minus all costs.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
