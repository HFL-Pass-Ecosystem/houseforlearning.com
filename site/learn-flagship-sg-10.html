Biotechnology Laboratory Skills

Learn safe laboratory practice, record keeping and simple measurement, using approved teaching activities. You learn safety, notebooks, weighing and pipetting, solutions, clean technique, data and error, ethics and reporting.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 150 h guidedPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Learn safe laboratory practice, record keeping and simple measurement, using approved teaching activities. You learn safety, notebooks, weighing and pipetting, solutions, clean technique, data and error, ethics and reporting.

  • Understand Explain why records and safety matter in a lab.
  • Apply Measure, pipette and prepare solutions.
  • Analyse Analyse data and errors.
  • Create Write a clear data report.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Complete six supervised teaching-lab exercises with accurate records, safe handling and a final data report that includes a table, a chart and the limits of your results.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3 to 6 months. A supervised teaching lab or simulation, a free spreadsheet, a supervisor, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Safety sign-off; six records; accurate pipetting and weighing; data report; at least 70% on the final check.

Learning only. Work only in an approved, supervised teaching setting. Do not culture or handle unknown biological material outside it, and check with your local authority for the rules that apply to you.

Requirements: none. Who it is for: learners 16 and over, workers changing field, small-business starters, teams. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (150 h guided)

Module 1 Lab safety 14 hApply

Safe habits come first in a laboratory. You wear protection, know the exits and handle waste properly. A signed induction shows you are ready to start.

  • Wear a lab coat, gloves and eye protection at all times in the work area, and tie back long hair.
  • Learn the location of exits, the first-aid kit, the eye wash and the fire equipment before you start work.
  • Never taste, sniff or touch chemicals or samples, and never eat or drink in the lab.
  • Label every container with the contents, the concentration, the date and your initials.
  • Put waste in the correct bin as your supervisor shows you, and check with your local authority for waste rules.
Worked example. A 250 mL bottle with no label is found on the bench. It cannot be used, and goes to the supervisor. A correct label carries four items: the name, the concentration, the date and the initials.

Activity. Complete the induction, pass a short safety quiz and sign the safety agreement, with your supervisor countersigning.

Standard to meet. You score at least 90% on the quiz, find all safety points on a walk-round and hold a signed induction record.

Module 2 Records 12 hUnderstand

A lab record is the proof of what you did. If it is not written, it did not happen. Clear, honest records let others repeat your work.

  • Write entries in ink with the date, and record what you did at the time and not later from memory.
  • Correct a mistake by drawing a single line through it and adding your initials and the date, so the original is still readable.
  • Put units on every number, such as 5.02 g or 10.0 mL.
  • Include the method, the materials, the batch or lot numbers and any change from the plan.
  • A common mistake is to use scrap paper and copy the notes later.
Worked example. You weigh a sample and write 5.02 g with the date and your initials. Later you notice a slip, so you cross it out with one line, write the correct value of 5.20 g and initial it.

Activity. Keep a lab notebook for all six exercises and have your supervisor check at least three entries.

Standard to meet. Every entry is dated, has units, shows corrections by a single line with initials, and your supervisor can repeat the work from it.

Module 3 Measure accurately 22 hApply

Accurate weighing and pipetting need care and practice. You check your skill by repeating measurements. Small errors early can grow into large errors later.

  • Level the balance, tare it with the container and handle it gently with the doors closed.
  • Hold the pipette upright, press to the first stop, draw up slowly by easing the plunger back, and dispense by pressing to the first stop and then on to the second stop.
  • Use a new tip for each sample and check the volume is in range for the pipette.
  • Repeat each measurement three times to see how close the results are.
  • A common mistake is to pipette too fast, which traps air and gives a wrong volume.
Worked example. You pipette 1.00 mL of water three times and weigh each. The readings are 0.99 g, 1.01 g and 1.00 g, so the mean is 1.00 g and the range is 0.02 g, which is within 2% of the target.

Activity. Complete a pipetting and weighing exercise with at least nine repeat measurements and calculate the mean and range for each volume.

Standard to meet. Your mean is within 2% of the target for each volume and your range is recorded correctly.

Module 4 Solutions 22 hApply

A solution is a substance dissolved in a liquid. Concentration tells you how much is in a given volume. You calculate first, then prepare and label.

  • Concentration is the amount of substance divided by the volume, for example grams per litre.
  • Convert millilitres to litres by dividing by 1,000 before you calculate in grams per litre.
  • To dilute, use the rule that the starting concentration times the starting volume equals the final concentration times the final volume.
  • Add the solid or stock to a little liquid, mix, then top up to the final volume and label.
  • A common mistake is to add the full volume of water on top of the stock, which gives a final volume that is too large.
Worked example. 5 g dissolved to make 100 mL is 5 divided by 0.1 L, which is 50 g/L. To make 100 mL at 10 g/L from this stock, you need 10 times 100 divided by 50, which is 20 mL of stock topped up with 80 mL of water.

Activity. Prepare three solutions of different concentrations, with the calculation shown, and label each one correctly.

Standard to meet. Your calculations are correct, your prepared solutions match the target within the tolerance and each is fully labelled.

Module 5 Sterile technique 20 hApply

Sterile technique keeps unwanted organisms out of your work. Small habits such as clean surfaces and quick, careful handling make a big difference. You also dispose of waste correctly.

  • Wipe the bench with the approved disinfectant before and after work, and wash your hands.
  • Work close to the flame or in the hood only as your supervisor has taught, and keep lids off for as short a time as possible.
  • Do not touch the inside of lids or the parts of tools that touch samples.
  • Put used items in the right waste container, and treat waste as your supervisor instructs, for example by autoclaving at the setting they give.
  • A common mistake is to talk or lean over open containers.
Worked example. You prepare 10 plates and 1 shows unexpected growth. That is 1 divided by 10, or 10%, so you review each step to find where contamination entered and record the likely cause.

Activity. Practise sterile technique on approved exercises while your supervisor observes and writes a note on your habits.

Standard to meet. Your supervisor notes correct habits on all steps and your contamination rate in practice is no more than 10%.

Module 6 Data and error 24 hAnalyse

Data has a centre and a spread. The mean describes the centre and the range describes the spread. Looking for outliers and sources of error helps you trust your results.

  • The mean is the sum of the values divided by how many there are.
  • The range is the largest value minus the smallest value.
  • An outlier is a value far from the rest, and you should look for a cause before you decide how to treat it.
  • List the sources of error, such as reading a scale, a worn pipette tip or a temperature change.
  • A common mistake is to report only the mean and not the spread.
Worked example. Three readings are 9.6, 9.8 and 10.0. The sum is 29.4, so the mean is 29.4 divided by 3, which is 9.8. The range is 10.0 minus 9.6, which is 0.4.

Activity. Analyse the results from at least three exercises by calculating the mean and range and writing the likely sources of error.

Standard to meet. Your means and ranges are correct and you list at least two realistic sources of error for each exercise.

Module 7 Ethics and quality 16 hEvaluate

Honest work matters more than neat results. You never change data and you follow the rules on consent and the care of living things. Methods must be clear enough for someone else to repeat.

  • Never change, invent or delete a result to make it look better, and report all values including odd ones.
  • Follow the rules on consent, privacy and the treatment of any living things, and check with your local authority and your supervisor.
  • Write the method clearly enough that another person could repeat it and get similar results.
  • Report anything that went wrong, such as a spill or a mistake, so others can learn.
  • A common mistake is to drop an outlier without telling anyone.
Worked example. Five readings are 9.8, 9.9, 9.7, 9.8 and 11.5. All five have a mean of 50.7 divided by 5, which is 10.14. Without the outlier the mean is 39.2 divided by 4, which is 9.8. You report both and explain the choice.

Activity. Write a one-page note on how you kept honest records, how you handled one outlier and how you would make a method repeatable.

Standard to meet. Your note reports both values with and without any outlier and explains your reasoning, and your supervisor agrees it is honest and clear.

Module 8 Report 20 hCreate

A short, clear report shares your findings with others. It states the aim, method, results and limits in plain words. A table and a chart make the numbers easy to see.

  • Open with a title and a one-sentence aim that says what you set out to find.
  • Describe the method briefly with the key materials and steps, so a reader can repeat it.
  • Present the results in a table with units and one clear chart with labelled axes.
  • State one conclusion supported by the numbers and list the limits, such as the small number of repeats.
  • A common mistake is to draw a conclusion that your data does not support.
Worked example. Your report has a title, an aim, a method of five lines, a table of six exercises, one chart of means with ranges and a conclusion that the pipetting stayed within 2% of target, with a limit that only three repeats were used.

Activity. Write a two-page report on your six exercises and give it to a peer to review, then revise it based on their notes.

Standard to meet. Your report has all required parts, a peer confirms it is clear and your conclusion matches the data in your table.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why label everything?

Model answer: Unlabelled items cause accidents and wasted work. (Covered in module 1: Lab safety. To pass that module: You score at least 90% on the quiz, find all safety points on a walk-round and hold a signed induction record.)

Q2. How do you correct an error in a lab notebook?

Model answer: Draw a single line through it, then add the correct value, your initials and the date. (Covered in module 2: Records. To pass that module: Every entry is dated, has units, shows corrections by a single line with initials, and your supervisor can repeat the work from it.)

Q3. Why repeat measurements?

Model answer: To check precision, meaning how close the results are to each other. (Covered in module 3: Measure accurately. To pass that module: Your mean is within 2% of the target for each volume and your range is recorded correctly.)

Q4. What is the concentration of 5 g in 100 mL?

Model answer: 5 g divided by 0.1 L gives 50 g/L. (Covered in module 4: Solutions. To pass that module: Your calculations are correct, your prepared solutions match the target within the tolerance and each is fully labelled.)

Q5. Why use sterile technique?

Model answer: It prevents contamination, which would spoil results and could be unsafe. (Covered in module 5: Sterile technique. To pass that module: Your supervisor notes correct habits on all steps and your contamination rate in practice is no more than 10%.)

Q6. What is the mean of 9.6, 9.8 and 10.0?

Model answer: The sum is 29.4, and divided by 3 the mean is 9.8. (Covered in module 6: Data and error. To pass that module: Your means and ranges are correct and you list at least two realistic sources of error for each exercise.)

Q7. Why must you never alter data?

Model answer: It breaks trust and can lead to harm if others rely on it. (Covered in module 7: Ethics and quality. To pass that module: Your note reports both values with and without any outlier and explains your reasoning, and your supervisor agrees it is honest and clear.)

Q8. What goes into a lab report?

Model answer: The aim, the method, the results and the limits. (Covered in module 8: Report. To pass that module: Your report has all required parts, a peer confirms it is clear and your conclusion matches the data in your table.)

Q9. What is the task of this course, in one sentence?

Model answer: Complete six supervised teaching-lab exercises with accurate records, safe handling and a final data report that includes a table, a chart and the limits of your results. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. What standard must your final evidence meet?

Model answer: Safety sign-off; six records; accurate pipetting and weighing; data report; at least 70% on the final check. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic, First words, tools and safety. Speaker note: Learner can say what Biotechnology Laboratory Skills is about and name the safety rule. Use module 1, Lab safety, as the first short unit (about 2 to 6 hours). Say this rule aloud: Learning only. Work only in an approved, supervised teaching setting. Do not culture or handle unknown biological material outside it, and check with your local authority for the rules that apply to you. No record is needed at this level.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness, What the field is and who it serves. Speaker note: Learner explains the idea in plain words: Explain why records and safety matter in a lab. Shared with schools and community groups. Note for the guide: ask one learner to explain it to a neighbour and check the words are right.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation, Guided tasks with a checklist. Speaker note: Learner does guided tasks from the first three modules (Lab safety; Records; Measure accurately) with a checklist. Evidence is a small set of dated records. Note for the guide: sit beside the learner for the first record, then step back.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner, Run the full task alone (this course). Speaker note: Task: Complete six supervised teaching-lab exercises with accurate records, safe handling and a final data report that includes a table, a chart and the limits of your results. Standard: Safety sign-off; six records; accurate pipetting and weighing; data report; at least 70% on the final check. Note for the guide: a mentor reviews the records; two peers mark with the rubric above.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist, One narrow area to depth. Speaker note: Learner picks one area, for example Data and error (the longest module at 24 h), and goes deeper than this course asks. Learner then teaches a peer circle of three or four. Note for the guide: check the teaching, not only the work.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced, Lead a team and review evidence. Speaker note: Learner leads a small team or designs a service in this field and marks other people's evidence against the rubric. Roles this points to: Laboratory assistant; Quality control assistant; Sample handler. Note for the guide: sample three records from the team and compare marks.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert, Set and audit the standard. Speaker note: Learner writes the method for Biotechnology Laboratory Skills as a short guide, audits other learners' evidence, and publishes worked examples back to learners. Note for the guide: worked examples keep currency and units on every number and are checked by a second expert.

Notes: credits stack and you can step in and out. This course is L4. Aligned with, not accredited by, national credit frameworks.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local custom changes rules, language, currency and who you practise with. Check each rule with your local authority before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
