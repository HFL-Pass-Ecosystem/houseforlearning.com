flagship course

Advanced Waste Management for Industry

Run industrial and hazardous waste systems: audits, treatment, recovery, extended producer duties, legal records and cost-saving circular projects.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5 Specialist8 modules · 40 h lessons plus practice

Run industrial and hazardous waste systems: audits, treatment, recovery, extended producer duties, legal records and cost-saving circular projects.

  • Define industrial waste classes and duties.
  • Apply audits and storage rules.
  • Analyse waste streams and costs.
  • Evaluate treatment and recovery options.
  • Create a compliance and reduction plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Audit a site, classify its streams and write a compliance and waste-reduction plan with costs and dates.

Condition

About 12 weeks part-time. A factory or training site, weighing scales, a mentor with site experience.

Standard

Streams are classified and weighed, records match the law, and the plan shows savings and owners.

Who it is for: Environment, health and safety staff, plant supervisors, recycling entrepreneurs. Requirements: Waste Management and Circular Resources or equivalent. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Waste classes and duties 4 hDefine
  • Hazardous, non-hazardous, e-waste, battery, plastic, biomedical and construction waste.
  • Duties of the maker of waste from source to disposal.
  • Producer duties for packaging and electronics.
  • Licences and authorised handlers.
Worked example. Spent solvent is hazardous and may leave site only with an authorised handler and a manifest.

Activity. Classify ten streams from a site.

Standard to meet. Assign each stream a class, rule and handler.

Module 2 Waste audit 6 hApply
  • Walk the process and list every stream.
  • Weigh by stream for two weeks.
  • Find sources: spoilage, offcuts, packaging, spills.
  • Draw a flow of material in and waste out.
Worked example. A plant finds 40% of its waste is packaging from one supplier.

Activity. Run an audit and draw the flow.

Standard to meet. Record weights, sources and flow.

Module 3 Storage, labelling and handling 5 hApply
  • Separate incompatible wastes.
  • Labels with name, hazard, date.
  • Secondary containment for liquids.
  • Training and spill kits.
Worked example. Acids and bases stored apart in bunded bays with labels and spill kits.

Activity. Inspect a store with a checklist.

Standard to meet. Use the checklist to find and close gaps.

Module 4 Treatment and recovery options 6 hEvaluate
  • Reduce, reuse, recycle, recover energy, treat, dispose.
  • Solvent recovery, metal recovery, co-processing and secure landfill.
  • Cost per tonne and carbon of each route.
  • Check that the partner is authorised.
Worked example. Solvent recovery cuts disposal cost by more than half and lowers purchase of fresh solvent.

Activity. Compare three routes for one stream.

Standard to meet. Include cost, risk and legal check in the table.

Module 5 Records, reporting and audits 5 hApply
  • Manifests, registers and annual returns.
  • Keep copies for the stated years.
  • Prepare for inspection.
  • Fix findings within dates.
Worked example. An annual return lists type, quantity and handler for each stream.

Activity. Build a register and a return.

Standard to meet. Complete entries and check that they agree with weights.

Module 6 Data, sensors and improvement 5 hAnalyse
  • Track waste per unit of product.
  • Simple sensors and weighbridge data.
  • Charts by week and by shift.
  • Set targets and review.
Worked example. Waste per tonne of product falls from 42 kg to 31 kg after a change.

Activity. Chart four weeks of data.

Standard to meet. Chart shows trend and one action.

Module 7 Industrial symbiosis and producer duties 4 hEvaluate
  • Waste of one firm as input to another.
  • Take-back and packaging plans.
  • Contracts with clear quality limits.
  • Share savings fairly.
Worked example. Fly ash from a plant goes to a brick maker under a contract.

Activity. Find two exchange options.

Standard to meet. Give options buyers, quality limits and savings.

Module 8 Capstone: compliance and reduction plan 5 hCreate
  • Audit.
  • Classify.
  • Plan with costs.
  • Present to a mentor.
Worked example. A plan that cuts disposal cost by 20% in a year.

Activity. Present.

Standard to meet. Plan is lawful, costed and dated.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Who may carry hazardous waste?

Model answer: An authorised transporter with the manifest. (Covered in module 1: Waste classes and duties. To pass that module: Assign each stream a class, rule and handler.)

Q2. What does a waste audit find?

Model answer: What waste is made, where and how much. (Covered in module 2: Waste audit. To pass that module: Record weights, sources and flow.)

Q3. Why keep incompatible wastes apart?

Model answer: They can react and cause harm. (Covered in module 3: Storage, labelling and handling. To pass that module: Use the checklist to find and close gaps.)

Q4. Which comes first, recycle or reduce?

Model answer: Reduce. (Covered in module 4: Treatment and recovery options. To pass that module: Include cost, risk and legal check in the table.)

Q5. Why keep manifests?

Model answer: Proof of lawful transfer. (Covered in module 5: Records, reporting and audits. To pass that module: Complete entries and check that they agree with weights.)

Q6. What is waste per unit of product?

Model answer: Waste divided by output. (Covered in module 6: Data, sensors and improvement. To pass that module: Chart shows trend and one action.)

Q7. What is industrial symbiosis?

Model answer: One firm's waste becomes another firm's input. (Covered in module 7: Industrial symbiosis and producer duties. To pass that module: Give options buyers, quality limits and savings.)

Q8. What proves the plan works?

Model answer: Lower waste per unit and clean records. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: compliance and reduction plan. To pass that module: Plan is lawful, costed and dated.)

Q9. Why weigh waste?

Model answer: Measure change. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why check partners are authorised?

Model answer: The maker of waste stays responsible. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L5 Specialist. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know waste classes.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain duties.
  3. L3 Foundation. Help with audits.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Run site compliance alone.
  5. L5 Specialist. Design reduction and recovery projects.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead environment, health and safety.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set industrial waste policy.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Waste classes

Classify 20 industrial wastes into the correct classes (hazardous, recyclable, general and others) with no more than 2 errors.

Speaker notes. Teacher lays out 20 waste labels and counts the errors. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Duties explained

Explain 5 duties of a waste producer, such as storage, labels, records, authorised carriers and reporting. Check local rules with an expert.

Speaker notes. Teacher asks who is responsible for a drum of used oil and checks the duty is named. Covered in module 1, Waste classes and duties.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Audit help

Help with a waste audit by weighing 5 streams over a week and entering the weights in a table with a total that matches the weighbridge.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the total against the weighbridge slip. Covered in module 2, Waste audit.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Site compliance alone

Run site compliance alone with correct storage and labelling for all containers, a manifest for every load and a monthly record check.

Speaker notes. Teacher walks the store and checks each container label. Covered in module 3, Storage, labelling and handling.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Reduction and recovery

Design a reduction project that cuts waste to landfill by 15 percent, with weights before and after and a cost and payback table.

Speaker notes. Teacher recomputes the payback period. Covered in modules 4 and 6.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: EHS leadership

Lead environment, health and safety for a site with 4 audits a year, a corrective action list closed within 30 days and an incident record.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the close dates on the action list.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Industrial waste policy

Draft an industrial waste policy with 4 measured aims, partner checks that carriers are authorised and a yearly review. Check local rules with an expert.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks every partner has a valid authorisation on file.

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Risk lesson (estimates, not quotes). In India a rough sheet figure is Rs 4,500 per tonne processed against a unit cost of Rs 3,200 and a fixed cost of Rs 300,000 a month: a margin of about 29% and a break-even of about 231 units a month. If the price falls 25% to about Rs 3,375, break-even rises from 231 to 1,715 units a month. Plan a price floor or a lower cost before you launch, and test the plan at the lower price. These sheet figures are separate from the sample numbers in the modules. Check them with a local price expert.
