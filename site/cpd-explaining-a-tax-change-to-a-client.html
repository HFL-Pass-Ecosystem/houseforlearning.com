Explaining a tax change to a client

Clear client communication about a rule change. Process only: no tax advice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 3 hours (180 minutes) of CPDSelf-study plus role-playAccountancy

Clear client communication about a rule change. Process only: no tax advice.

  • Summarize a rule change, given an official note, in under 150 words with the date it applies.
  • Identify who is affected, given 5 client profiles, with at least 4 correct.
  • Draft a client message, given a template, clear, polite and with one action.
  • Verify the source, given the message, by naming the official source of each fact.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Find and check an official rule change, summarise it in about 100 words, show its effect on a sample client and file a dated, sourced client note, referring on when unsure.

Condition

Given a short invented official notice, a sample client profile and a client note template, working in a three-hour supervised session; process only, no advice.

Standard

Produce a note under 150 words that states what changes, from when and the action, cites the official source and date, includes a worked example and refers any doubt to a senior.

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Find and check the rule: source and date 20 min
  • Go to the official source, not a summary
  • Note the date of the notice
  • Say when you are unsure
Worked example. Notice N-12, dated 3 March, says a filing date moves from 1 April to 1 May. Check: the date on the notice matches its title and the page is on the official site. If a transitional case is unclear, write "unsure, referred to senior".

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "find and check the rule: source and date": summarize a rule change, given an official note, in under 150 words with the date it applies.

Standard to meet. Find the official source of a rule change, check its date and confirm what it says, and state plainly when you are unsure and must refer on.

Module 2 The 100-word summary 25 min
  • What changes, from when, what to do
  • About 100 words
  • Plain words only
Worked example. Sample line: "From the next period the filing date moves from 1 April to 1 May. Please use the new date and keep a copy of the notice. Source: Notice N-12, dated 3 March." A full summary expands this to about 100 words.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "the 100-word summary": identify who is affected, given 5 client profiles, with at least 4 correct.

Standard to meet. Write a summary of a rule change in about one hundred words that states what changes, from when, and what the client should do.

Module 3 A worked example with a sample client 25 min
  • Use last year's real figures
  • Show before and after
  • Keep it in the client's terms
Worked example. Invented rule: the limit on item X rises from 1,000 to 1,200. Client spent 1,500 last year. Counted amount was 1,000; it would be 1,200, so 200 more (20 percent). Example is a pattern, not advice.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "a worked example with a sample client": draft a client message, given a template, clear, polite and with one action.

Standard to meet. Calculate the effect of a rule change on a sample client using last year's figures, and show it as a short worked example in the client's terms.

Module 4 Citing and dating the note 15 min
  • Cite the official source
  • State both dates
  • File with the source in the client file
Worked example. Footer: "Source: Official notice N-12, dated 3 March. Note written 10 March. Filed in client file C-204." Three facts: source, date of notice, where filed.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "citing and dating the note": verify the source, given the message, by naming the official source of each fact.

Standard to meet. Cite the official source and its date in every client note, and file the note with the source in the client file.

Module 5 Practice and feedback 10 min
  • Write from a short invented rule
  • Read the feedback list
  • Correct and redraft
Worked example. Draft 1 had source, example and referral but no date: 3 of 4 points. Feedback: add the date the rule starts. Draft 2 has all 4 points: source, date, example, referral.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "explaining a tax change to a client" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (summarize a rule change; identify who is affected; draft a client message; verify the source).

Standard to meet. Practise writing a client note from a short invented rule change, then use the feedback to correct gaps in source, date and example.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Tick each point honestly
  • Fix gaps before sending
  • Refer, do not advise
Worked example. Check list: 1 source cited, 2 date stated, 3 worked example included, 4 doubt referred. Note passes only with 4 of 4 ticked, then goes to a senior.

Practise: Take the end check for "explaining a tax change to a client": show that you can verify the source, given the message, by naming the official source of each fact, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Check your finished note against the list: source cited, date stated, worked example included, and any doubt referred to a senior.

Module 7 Second case: a date change 45 min
  • Work case card 2
  • Say what you verified first
  • Write the note and cite the source
Worked example. Case card 2: a rule moves a filing date by 30 days, from 1 April to 1 May (April has 30 days). Verified first: official notice and date, who is affected, whether any exceptions apply. Client note: "Your date moves from 1 April to 1 May. Source: Notice N-12, dated 3 March."

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second case: a date change": verify the source, given the message, by naming the official source of each fact. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Write a client note for a rule that moves a filing date by 30 days, stating what you verified first and citing the official source and its date.

Module 8 Teach-back and peer check 35 min
  • Explain your note aloud in one minute
  • Peer checks the four points
  • Fix any gap using feedback
Worked example. Teach-back: "The date moved from 1 April to 1 May. I checked Notice N-12, dated 3 March. For you it means one extra month. I am not giving advice; ask your adviser about special cases." Peer ticks source, date, example, referral: 4 of 4.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "teach-back and peer check": verify the source, given the message, by naming the official source of each fact. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Explain your finished client note aloud in one minute so that a peer can confirm it has the source, the date, a worked example and a referral.

Worked example and case cards

Worked example (about 100 words)

"From the next period the rule on X changes so that Y. For you, using last year's figures, this would mean about Z more/less. The date it starts is D. Please keep records of Q. Source: [official notice, date]."

Case card

a short invented rule change and client profile.

Case card 2

checks

10 check questions with model answers

What must the note cite?

Model answer: The official source and its date. (Covered in module 4: Citing and dating the note. To pass that module: Cite the official source and its date in every client note, and file the note with the source in the client file.)

Why give a worked example?

Model answer: It shows the effect in the client's terms. (Covered in module 3: A worked example with a sample client. To pass that module: Calculate the effect of a rule change on a sample client using last year's figures, and show it as a short worked example in the client's terms.)

What if you are unsure?

Model answer: Say so, check the source, escalate. (Covered in module 1: Find and check the rule: source and date. To pass that module: Find the official source of a rule change, check its date and confirm what it says, and state plainly when you are unsure and must refer on.)

Do you give advice beyond your role?

Model answer: No; refer to a qualified adviser. (Covered in module 6: Check. To pass that module: Check your finished note against the list: source cited, date stated, worked example included, and any doubt referred to a senior.)

Why state the date of the rule?

Model answer: Rules change; the date shows what applied. (Covered in module 4: Citing and dating the note. To pass that module: Cite the official source and its date in every client note, and file the note with the source in the client file.)

Can you advise without checking the source?

Model answer: No. (Covered in module 1: Find and check the rule: source and date. To pass that module: Find the official source of a rule change, check its date and confirm what it says, and state plainly when you are unsure and must refer on.)

Why use one worked example?

Model answer: Clients understand numbers better than clauses. (Covered in module 3: A worked example with a sample client. To pass that module: Calculate the effect of a rule change on a sample client using last year's figures, and show it as a short worked example in the client's terms.)

What if the rule is unclear?

Model answer: Say so, and refer to the senior. (Covered in module 1: Find and check the rule: source and date. To pass that module: Find the official source of a rule change, check its date and confirm what it says, and state plainly when you are unsure and must refer on.)

Where is the note stored?

Model answer: In the client file with the source. (Covered in module 4: Citing and dating the note. To pass that module: Cite the official source and its date in every client note, and file the note with the source in the client file.)

A filing date of 1 April moves by 30 days. What is the new date, and how do you confirm it?

Model answer: 1 May, because April has 30 days. Confirm it against the official notice, not by counting alone. (Covered in module 7: Second case: a date change. To pass that module: Write a client note for a rule that moves a filing date by 30 days, stating what you verified first and citing the official source and its date.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What is a rule change. Speaker note: Rules sometimes change and clients need to know. Learners at this level can say what a rule is and why the date it starts matters. They do not yet read official notices.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Where rules come from. Speaker note: Show the difference between an official notice and someone's summary of it. Learners can name where official notes are published and why a date on the notice shows which version applied.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Summarise with help. Speaker note: Learners read a short invented notice and fill a template: what changes, from when, what to do. They work with a mentor and keep to about 100 words, with the source written at the bottom.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: A client note you can file. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner finds and checks the source, writes a summary, adds a worked example from last year's figures, cites and dates the note, files it and refers any doubt to a senior.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Many clients, one change. Speaker note: The specialist sorts clients into who is affected, adapts the worked example per profile and prepares a standard message that others can reuse, while keeping every fact tied to its source.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Updates across a firm. Speaker note: The advanced worker runs a process for tracking changes: who watches sources, how notes are reviewed, how old versions are kept, and how staff are told. Stress version control and dates.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Designing the change service. Speaker note: The super expert designs the whole client communication service: source rules, review levels, templates, training, and measures such as notes sent before the start date. They set clear limits on advice.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own body's forms, units and currency. Local professional bodies set the rules for CPD hours; check them first.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Credit and levels: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Credit and levels: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Credit and levels: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Credit and levels: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Credit and levels: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Credit and levels: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Credit and levels: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Credit and levels: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Personal data: the Data Protection Act. Credit and levels: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Credit and levels: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
