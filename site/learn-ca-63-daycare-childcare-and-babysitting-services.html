Daycare, Childcare and Babysitting Services

Run a real daycare, Childcare and Babysitting Services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small daycare, Childcare and Babysitting Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: health, care, wellness and education. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the types of child care and what children need 6 hUnderstand

Child care takes many forms. First learn the words and what children need at each age.

  • Name the types: licensed centre, licensed home care, unlicensed home care, nanny and babysitter.
  • Explain the basic needs of children: safety, food, rest, play, comfort and caring adults.
  • Describe broad age stages: baby, toddler, preschool and school age.
  • Explain that children learn through play, routine and kind talk.
  • Say what a carer does not do: give medical care beyond first aid or replace parents.
  • Compare the day of a toddler with the day of a school-age child and list how their needs differ.
  • List questions a parent may ask a carer on a first visit.
Worked example. A toddler cries when a parent leaves. A carer greets the child by name, offers a favourite toy, keeps a calm voice and tells the parent the child settled after ten minutes. The daily note adds that the child napped 60 minutes and ate half of the snack.

Activity. Write a one-page glossary of ten terms and describe the needs of one age group.

Standard to meet. Task: define terms and needs. Condition: one page, own words. Standard: each term is correct and needs match the age group.

Module 2 Know the rules and licensing 6 hApply

Child care rules are set by each province. Know which type of care needs a licence.

  • Explain that licensing, number of children per carer and training are set by provinces and territories; check the current rule with the provincial child care authority.
  • Explain that unlicensed care is often limited to a small number of children; check the exact limit locally.
  • Explain that background checks, including a vulnerable-sector check, are commonly required; check what applies.
  • Explain that you must report suspected abuse or neglect to the child protection authority; learn the local duty and the number to call.
  • Explain that child care fee support and space programmes differ by province; check the current rules.
  • Keep copies of your licence, training certificates and check results ready to show.
  • Write the phone number of the provincial child care authority in your file.
Worked example. A person wants to care for six children at home. Before starting she asks the provincial authority whether that needs a licence, and waits for the answer in writing. She does not start until she has it.

Activity. Make a rules table with rule, who enforces it, what you must do and a dated official source.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a dated source; no rule is guessed.

Module 3 Keep children safe and healthy 10 hApply

Safety comes first. Prevent harm, and know what to do if something goes wrong.

  • Never leave a child alone; keep the required ratio of adults to children.
  • Check rooms and toys for hazards such as small parts, cords, hot items and unlocked doors.
  • Follow safe sleep rules for babies; get the current guidance from the public health unit.
  • Wash hands before meals and after toilet, and clean toys and surfaces on a schedule.
  • Take first aid and CPR training that fits the ages you care for; keep it current.
  • Plan and practise fire and emergency drills with the children.
  • Use car seats and strollers as the maker's instructions say.
Worked example. Each morning a carer does a ten-point walk round: gates, plugs, cleaning products, floor, toys. One day she finds a loose button in the play area and removes it. If the walk takes 5 minutes a day, that is 5 x 5 = 25 minutes in a five-day week.

Activity. Create a safety checklist for a room and use it for a week.

Standard to meet. Task: use a safety checklist. Condition: one week. Standard: every item is checked and dated and each hazard found is fixed and recorded.

Module 4 Plan a day: routines, meals and play 8 hEvaluate

A calm routine helps children feel safe. Plan a day in small blocks.

  • Plan blocks: arrival, play, snack, outdoor time, rest, lunch, quiet play, pick-up.
  • Offer play that fits the age: sensory play, building, stories, music, movement.
  • Ask parents about allergies and diets and post them where staff can see.
  • Follow food safety rules for storing and serving food; check local guidance.
  • Prepare for weather: spare clothes, sun cover in summer and warm gear in winter.
  • Warn children before a change, for example 'five minutes until tidy-up'.
  • Keep a rainy-day plan with indoor movement games.
Worked example. A day plan for 3-year-olds has 45 minutes of outdoor time in the morning, a story of 15 minutes, then lunch. Outdoor time and story together fill 45 + 15 = 60 minutes. A child with a nut allergy has a picture card at the lunch table.

Activity. Write a one-day plan with times, activities, meals and safety notes for one age group.

Standard to meet. Task: plan a day. Condition: one age group. Standard: blocks fit age, allergies are posted and safety notes are included.

Module 5 Guide behaviour and support learning 8 hApply

Children learn from adults who are calm, clear and kind.

  • Set a few clear rules in simple words and show what to do instead.
  • Notice good behaviour and name it.
  • Stay calm when a child is upset; get down to their level and name the feeling.
  • Never use hitting or shaming; follow the rules on discipline in your province.
  • Support early learning through talk, songs, counting and picture books.
  • Offer two choices so the child feels some control.
  • Give a calm result that fits the action, such as helping to tidy a mess, never a punishment.
Worked example. A child grabs a toy. The carer says 'Ask with words', models 'Can I have a turn?' and helps both children take turns of two minutes each. She notes it for the parent.

Activity. Practise three behaviour situations with a partner and write what you said.

Standard to meet. Task: guide behaviour. Condition: three role-plays. Standard: language is calm and clear, no shaming is used and the child is shown what to do.

Module 6 Talk with parents and keep records 6 hCreate

Parents trust you with what matters most. Talk often and write things down.

  • Give a short daily note: food, sleep, play, mood and any incident.
  • Write an incident report the same day for any injury, with time, what happened and what you did.
  • Keep consent forms, emergency contacts, allergies and pick-up lists.
  • Protect children's privacy; never share photos or details without consent.
  • Meet families in English, French or another language where you can; use plain words.
  • Hold a short chat at drop-off to hear about changes at home.
  • Find out how long records must be kept; check the provincial rule.
Worked example. A child bumps his head. The carer applies first aid, writes a report with the time and treatment, tells the parent at pick-up and asks her to sign. The report is filed the same day with the signed copy.

Activity. Create a daily note, an incident form and a consent form.

Standard to meet. Task: build family forms. Condition: plain words. Standard: forms cover who, what, when and signature, and keep private data safe.

Module 7 Set fees and plan a small child care business 6 hEvaluate

A child care business must cover costs and follow the law. This is not financial advice; ask a licensed accountant.

  • List costs: food, supplies, rent share, insurance, training, equipment, your time.
  • Fee per child per day = (monthly costs + target income) / (child-days you can fill).
  • Check what insurance is needed and whether the provincial authority sets fee rules; check locally.
  • Write a contract: hours, fees, late pick-up, sickness rules, holidays and notice.
  • Plan for empty spaces: you will not fill every day.
  • Write a sick-child policy that says when a child must stay home.
  • Track child-days filled each month and compare them with your plan.
Worked example. Monthly costs are 960 CAD and target income is 2,240 CAD, a total of 3,200. You can fill 160 child-days. Fee = 3,200 / 160 = 20 CAD per child per day. If only 140 days fill, income is 140 x 20 = 2,800, which is 400 short of the plan (teaching numbers only).

Activity. Build a cost sheet and a fee, and write a one-page family agreement.

Standard to meet. Task: set a fee. Condition: a spreadsheet. Standard: costs are listed, the fee sum is correct and the agreement is clear.

Module 8 Run a child care service for real 8 hCreate

Run it steadily: routines, safety, records and improving.

  • Review safety checks, incident reports and attendance every week.
  • Ask parents for feedback each term and act on it.
  • Take part in training each year and keep certificates.
  • Look after yourself: breaks, backup carer and a plan for sick days.
  • Write a 12-month plan with milestones, a budget and a risk list.
  • Check first aid kits and emergency contact sheets each month.
  • Keep a list of backup carers and tell families how to reach them.
Worked example. After a month of logs, a carer sees that 6 of 8 scrapes happen at pick-up time. She changes the entry area and the scrapes fall to 2 the next month.

Activity. Run a 60-day pilot under supervision and write the review.

Standard to meet. Task: run a pilot. Condition: 60 days under a qualified supervisor. Standard: logs are complete, risks are fixed and the review reasons from evidence.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
