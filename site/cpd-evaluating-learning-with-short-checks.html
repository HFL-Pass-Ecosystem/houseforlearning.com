CPD · Teaching

Evaluating learning with short checks

Quick, fair checks during a lesson that tell you what to do next.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 3 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playTeaching

Quick, fair checks during a lesson that tell you what to do next.

  • Select a check type, given a lesson goal, matching the verb in the goal.
  • Construct five questions, given a topic, with one correct answer each and no trick wording.
  • Interpret results, given a class result table, naming two learners or topics needing support.
  • Plan the next step, given the results, with a clear action for the next lesson.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Choose and build short checks during a lesson, read class results to find the weakest topics and plan a re-teach and re-check

Condition

Given a lesson goal, a topic and class result tables, in a 3-hour practical session

Standard

Select a check type that matches the lesson verb, write five clear questions, rank the weakest topics from a table and plan a re-teach with a re-check within a week

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Why short checks 15 min
  • Short checks show what to do next.
  • Use them often, such as once per part of the lesson.
  • They are for teaching, not for ranking learners.
Worked example. A 3-question exit slip takes 3 minutes. One slip per lesson, 5 lessons a week, gives 15 data points a week for 1 class.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "why short checks": select a check type, given a lesson goal, matching the verb in the goal.

Standard to meet. Explain why short checks during a lesson tell you what to do next, and describe how often to use them.

Module 2 Choosing a check type 25 min
  • Match the check to the verb in the goal.
  • Exit slips, mini whiteboards and hands-up polls differ in speed.
  • Pick the quickest check that shows the verb.
Worked example. Goal verb: "calculate" so use mini whiteboards, 5 items, 4 minutes. Goal verb: "list" so use a hands-up poll, 1 minute.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "choosing a check type": construct five questions, given a topic, with one correct answer each and no trick wording.

Standard to meet. Select a check type, such as an exit slip, mini whiteboards or a hands-up poll, that matches the verb in the lesson goal.

Module 3 Reading results quickly 20 min
  • Find the lowest percentage first.
  • Name the two weakest topics.
  • Share class patterns, not individual marks.
Worked example. Exit slip: Q1 90 percent, Q2 85, Q3 40. Weakest: Q3 at 40 percent, then Q2 at 85 percent. Class pattern shared; no names.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "reading results quickly": interpret results, given a class result table, naming two learners or topics needing support.

Standard to meet. Interpret a class result table quickly, naming the two weakest topics, and share class patterns rather than individual marks.

Module 4 Planning a re-teach 20 min
  • Re-teach the weakest topic with one new example.
  • Keep it to about 10 minutes.
  • Re-check within a week with a few items.
Worked example. Q3 at 40 percent. Plan: 10-minute re-teach with one new example, re-check next lesson with 2 items. Target: 70 percent or more.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "planning a re-teach": plan the next step, given the results, with a clear action for the next lesson.

Standard to meet. Plan a short re-teach for the weakest topic with one new example, and set a re-check of a few items within a week.

Module 5 Practice 5 min
  • Write five clear questions.
  • One correct answer each.
  • No trick wording.
Worked example. Topic: fractions. Q1 "What is the common denominator of 1/2 and 1/3?" Answer: 6. Five questions, 5 single answers, 0 trick words.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "evaluating learning with short checks" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (select a check type; construct five questions; interpret results; plan the next step).

Standard to meet. Construct five clear questions for a topic, each with one correct answer and no trick wording.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Read a new table.
  • Rank the weakest aims and plan the next step.
  • Record the pattern on a class tracker.
Worked example. Table of 6 aims. Weakest two: aim 4 at 35 percent, aim 6 at 48 percent. Next step: re-teach aim 4 first. Tracker updated.

Practise: Take the end check for "evaluating learning with short checks": show that you can plan the next step, given the results, with a clear action for the next lesson, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Interpret a new result table, rank the weakest aims, plan the next step, and record the pattern on a simple class tracker.

Module 7 Second worked case: the results table 45 min
  • Use the second table: Q1 95, Q2 60, Q3 55, Q4 90.
  • Rank the weakest two.
  • Plan the re-teach.
Worked example. Q1 95, Q2 60, Q3 55, Q4 90. Average = (95+60+55+90) / 4 = 75 percent. Weakest two: Q3 at 55 and Q2 at 60. Re-teach Q3 first.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second worked case: the results table": plan the next step, given the results, with a clear action for the next lesson. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Rank the weakest two questions from the second table and plan a 10-minute re-teach with a 2-item re-check.

Module 8 Peer check and class tracker 45 min
  • Swap tables with a partner.
  • Compare rankings.
  • Keep a simple class tracker.
Worked example. Tracker with 4 aims and 3 weekly checks: aim 3 moves 55 to 70 to 85 percent. Peer agrees on the 2 weakest aims in 5 of 6 tables.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "peer check and class tracker": plan the next step, given the results, with a clear action for the next lesson. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Compare your ranking with a peer's, resolve any difference and record the pattern on a class tracker over three checks.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Name two quick checks.

Model answer: Exit slip, mini whiteboards, hands-up poll. (Covered in module 2: Choosing a check type. To pass that module: Select a check type, such as an exit slip, mini whiteboards or a hands-up poll, that matches the verb in the lesson goal.)

What does a low Q3 tell you?

Model answer: The topic was not understood by many; re-teach. (Covered in module 3: Reading results quickly. To pass that module: Interpret a class result table quickly, naming the two weakest topics, and share class patterns rather than individual marks.)

Why re-check after?

Model answer: To see if the re-teach worked. (Covered in module 4: Planning a re-teach. To pass that module: Plan a short re-teach for the weakest topic with one new example, and set a re-check of a few items within a week.)

Should marks be public?

Model answer: Not individual marks; share class patterns. (Covered in module 3: Reading results quickly. To pass that module: Interpret a class result table quickly, naming the two weakest topics, and share class patterns rather than individual marks.)

One quick check with no equipment.

Model answer: Hands-up or finger votes. (Covered in module 2: Choosing a check type. To pass that module: Select a check type, such as an exit slip, mini whiteboards or a hands-up poll, that matches the verb in the lesson goal.)

What if 100 percent get it right?

Model answer: Move on or extend. (Covered in module 4: Planning a re-teach. To pass that module: Plan a short re-teach for the weakest topic with one new example, and set a re-check of a few items within a week.)

Why avoid reading out marks?

Model answer: Protects dignity. (Covered in module 3: Reading results quickly. To pass that module: Interpret a class result table quickly, naming the two weakest topics, and share class patterns rather than individual marks.)

How soon re-check?

Model answer: Within a week. (Covered in module 4: Planning a re-teach. To pass that module: Plan a short re-teach for the weakest topic with one new example, and set a re-check of a few items within a week.)

Where to record patterns?

Model answer: A simple class tracker. (Covered in module 3: Reading results quickly. To pass that module: Interpret a class result table quickly, naming the two weakest topics, and share class patterns rather than individual marks.)

What do you do if every learner gets 100 percent on a short check?

Model answer: Move on to the next goal or add an extension task, because the class has shown the outcome is met. (Covered in module 3: Reading results quickly. where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What a short check is. Speaker note: A short check is a quick task during a lesson, such as an exit slip. The learner can name two quick checks and say why teachers use them.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Checks tell you what to do next. Speaker note: The learner understands that results guide teaching, not just marks. A low result on one question tells the teacher what to re-teach. They see that a result of 40 percent on one question is a signal about the lesson, not about the learners.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Make a simple check. Speaker note: The learner can write a small set of clear questions and run a hands-up poll or mini whiteboard check with support from a template. They practise using a five-question template and a simple tally sheet to record the results.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Check, read, re-teach. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner selects a check type, writes five clear questions, ranks the weakest topics from a table and plans a re-teach with a re-check within a week.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Deeper analysis. Speaker note: The specialist looks at results by question and by group, separates a weak question from a weak topic, and chooses the most efficient re-teach. They also test whether a weak question was badly worded before deciding to re-teach the whole topic.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Coach and compare. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner helps colleagues build shared check banks, compares patterns across classes and coaches on dignity and fair sharing of results. They keep results private by showing only class patterns and agree one tracker format with the team.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Lead assessment practice. Speaker note: The super expert designs a department-wide approach to formative checks, trackers and re-check timing, and measures whether re-teaching raises results. They also compare results before and after re-teaching to show which methods work best for the school.

Your place

Use your own body's forms, units and currency. Local professional bodies set the rules for CPD hours; check them first.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Credit and levels: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Brazil (Sao Paulo)
