Warehousing and Storage Services

Eight modules take you from the first customer to a running, measured service in warehousing and storage services.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, start and run warehousing and storage services in São Paulo: serve a seller or maker, deliver safe stock keeping, keep records, and show a working month of numbers.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. Own phone or computer, real customers or a real practice site, a peer and a mentor for the final review. Local rules and licences checked against official sources.

Standard

Every module standard is met; all claims are true and backed by records; peers and a mentor mark the evidence 'Meets' or better.

Who it is for: students, graduates, career changers, owners, workers and returners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, tracker, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know your customer and market 4 hAnalyse

Every sound business in warehousing and storage services begins with one clear customer and one clear problem. In São Paulo and the wider state, demand is real but crowded, so a small, well-described customer group wins before a broad one.

  • In warehousing and storage services: Know what clients store: dry, chilled, bulky.
  • Write the customer in one sentence: a seller or maker. Add the place, the moment of need and what they use today.
  • List five rivals or substitutes within reach. For each, record price, strength, weakness and how people reach it.
  • Interview at least eight real customers with open questions. Record exact words, not your summary.
  • Count the market in units: how many pallets could one area buy in a month? Use two public sources and your own count, then take the lower figure.
  • Choose one first segment and say why the others wait.
Worked example. Weak: 'Everyone needs safe stock keeping.' Strong: 'Our first customer is a seller or maker within 8 km of the centre, who today pays R$ 61 per pallet and complains about delay. We target 900 pallets a month by month three.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: ten customers are interviewed for warehousing and storage services. Seven say price, two say speed, one says trust. Question: which first message do you write, and which interview record backs it? Model answer: lead with the pallet price and speed promise, keep trust proof as the second line, and cite the seven price answers by date.

Activity. Write the one-sentence customer, a five-row rival table and eight interview records. Share them with a peer for a challenge round.

Standard to meet. Customer is specific; rival table has prices; every claim traces to an interview or a source.

Module 2 Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil 4 hUnderstand

Brazil asks every trader to be visible and accountable. Know which register, tax regime and sector rules apply before the first sale, and check each against the current official text.

  • In warehousing and storage services: Warehouses need fire approval, tax documents and licences for special goods.
  • Choose a legal form: micro-entrepreneur (MEI) only if your activity appears on the official MEI list, or a company registered with a CNPJ. Regulated activities need their sector authorisation first. Check the current limits and lists on the official portal.
  • Learn the tax regime that fits your size and write down which taxes you will collect and pay, and when.
  • Find the sector rules for warehousing and storage services: licences, inspections, professional registers. List each with the office that issues it and the renewal date.
  • Personal data of customers falls under Brazil's data protection law (LGPD). Collect only what you need, say why, keep it safe, and name who answers questions.
  • Consumer law gives buyers clear rights on information, returns and complaints. Put your terms in plain Portuguese.
Worked example. Checklist for a new warehousing and storage services trader: 1) legal form (CNPJ, or MEI only if your activity is on the official list); 2) city operating licence; 3) sector licence or register; 4) tax set-up; 5) privacy notice; 6) written terms. Each has an owner and a date.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a neighbour says registration can wait until the first R$ 275 of sales. Question: what do you do? Model answer: do not delay; write the compliance table first, confirm the register, tax and sector licence with the official office, and start only where the table shows no open blocker.

Activity. Build your own six-line compliance table for your first location. Mark what is done, what is pending and who confirms it.

Standard to meet. Every line cites an official source; nothing is assumed; pending items have dates. Local law always wins over this course.

Module 3 Design the offer 4 hCreate

An offer states what the customer gets, how well, by when and at what price. For safe stock keeping, the offer must make the quality visible and the next step easy.

  • In warehousing and storage services: Offer per pallet, per day pricing with receiving and picking.
  • Describe the core offer in three lines: what is delivered (safe stock keeping), the standard it meets, and the time it takes.
  • Add one proof for each promise: a sample, a test result, a guarantee or a customer record.
  • Price from cost upward and from customer value downward, then pick a price inside both.
  • Write a simple one-page terms sheet: what is included, what is extra, how to cancel.
  • Create two options at most, so choice stays easy.
Worked example. Offer: 'Safe stock keeping, delivered within agreed time, checked against a written standard, price R$ 55 per pallet. Proof: a dated record the customer can check and sign. Changes or cancellation by written agreement before work begins.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a customer asks for a discount from R$ 55 to R$ 49 per pallet. Question: decide with numbers. Model answer: contribution falls from R$ 33 to R$ 27 per pallet; offer an extra of value (for example faster service) before cutting price, and accept only if the lower contribution still clears break-even.

Longer notes. Decide which cargo you store: pallets, shelves, cold rooms or small boxes. Walk the building with a tape measure and mark only usable positions, leaving out aisles, docks and fire exits. Add rent, energy, labor, insurance and forklift costs for one month, then divide by positions to see the cost of one place. Price receiving, counting, picking and packing as separate services. Compare two nearby warehouses and state your edge, such as opening hours or road access. Write clear terms for damage, delay and unpaid storage. Show your fire-safety plan and insurance policy to a broker before accepting the first customer's goods.

Extra worked example. A warehouse charges R$ 55 per pallet a month. Handling labor, shrink wrap and energy for each pallet cost R$ 22, leaving R$ 33, or 60 percent of the price. Fixed costs of R$ 28.000 a month for the lease, night watch, insurance and software are covered after 849 pallets, since R$ 28.000 divided by R$ 33 is 848,5. With 1.500 pallets, sales are R$ 82.500, contribution is R$ 49.500 and profit is R$ 21.500. Counting taped pallet positions on the floor plan gives the 1.500.

Activity. Write the offer card, the proof list and the terms sheet. Test them with three customers and note every point where they hesitated.

Standard to meet. Promise, proof and price are consistent; terms fit one page; three customer reactions are recorded.

Module 4 Run the work step by step 5 hApply

Good operations make results repeatable. For warehousing and storage services, write each step so a new person could follow it on their first day, and measure each step.

  • In warehousing and storage services: Receive, check, put away, pick, pack, dispatch; cycle counts weekly.
  • Map the process from first contact to paid and closed. Typical stages: enquiry, quote, preparation, delivery of the pallet, check, payment, follow-up.
  • Name the key resource at each stage: a mapped, counted store. Note what happens if it is missing.
  • Write a one-page standard procedure per stage: who, what, tool, time, record.
  • Choose three measures: speed, quality (errors per 100) and customer rating. Review them every week.
  • Fix the cause of a defect, not the symptom. Ask 'why' five times and record the answer.
Worked example. Stage record: 'Prepare. Owner: lead. Time: 30 min. Tool: checklist. Record: signed sheet. Defect found on 1 of 375 pallets last week; cause: missing check on a mapped, counted store; fix: add check at start, review next Friday.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: of 1.500 pallets last month, 90 had a defect traced to a mapped, counted store. Question: what is the defect rate and what is your first action? Model answer: about 6 defects per 100; stop the cause (add a check at the start), correct affected customers, log the cause, and re-measure next week.

Activity. Draw the process map and write procedures for the two riskiest stages. Run each once and time it.

Standard to meet. Procedures are followable by a stranger; measures have weekly numbers; one defect is traced to a root cause.

Module 5 Safety, quality and risk 4 hEvaluate

The most serious risk in this field is lost stock or a fire. A calm, written risk routine protects people first and the business second.

  • In warehousing and storage services: Fire load, racking collapse and forklift accidents.
  • List ten things that could go wrong. Score each for how likely (1 to 5) and how serious (1 to 5). Multiply and sort.
  • For the top five, choose a control: remove the hazard, replace it, guard it, train people, or use protective gear, in that order of preference.
  • Write who does what in an emergency, and rehearse it once a quarter.
  • Keep an incident and near-miss log. Near-misses are free lessons.
  • Check that insurance and legal duties match the risk you actually carry.
Worked example. Risk row: 'Hazard: lost stock or a fire. Likelihood 3, severity 5, score 15. Control: written check before each pallet, trained staff, log sheet. Owner: site lead. Review monthly.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a near-miss occurs: lost stock or a fire nearly happened but no one was hurt. Question: what do you do in the next 24 hours? Model answer: make safe, record the event, ask the five whys, add or tighten one control, brief the team, and set a review date; near-misses count as lessons, not blame.

Activity. Build a ten-row risk register and a one-page emergency plan. Run a ten-minute drill with a peer and note two improvements.

Standard to meet. Top risks have controls ranked by preference; drill done; log started. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.

Module 6 People, skills and service 4 hApply

People deliver the promise. Define roles, train to a standard and give feedback in the open.

  • In warehousing and storage services: Train forklift drivers and keep certificates.
  • Write a role card for each person: purpose, five tasks, the standard for each, and who to ask for help.
  • Train by showing, doing with support, then doing alone with a check. Record sign-off per task.
  • Set service rules for answering customers: reply times, tone, how to apologise and fix.
  • Hold a ten-minute team check each day: yesterday's problem, today's plan, one risk.
  • Build a growth path: what a new person can do in week 1, month 3 and month 12.
Worked example. Training record: 'Task: handle a complaint. Shown 2 May; done with support 4 May; done alone 8 May, checked: apology, fix, follow-up call. Signed by lead.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a new hire completes training but a customer complaint shows a skipped step. Question: whose gap is it? Model answer: first the system: check the role card, the training sign-off and the checklist; then coach the person and fix the document so the step cannot be skipped.

Longer notes. Write one routine for each post: receiving, put-away, picking and dispatch. At receiving, require a count against the delivery note and a photo of any damaged box before the driver leaves. Teach staff to lift with the legs, drive forklifts only with a license and stack no higher than the marked line. Plan shifts to match truck arrival times. When a customer asks where an item is, the system must answer in two minutes. Run a stock count of ten random locations each week. Forklift licenses, safe stacking heights and overtime pay are worth one conversation with a safety technician and a labor adviser.

Extra worked example. A warehouse handles 1.500 pallets a month and 3 percent, or 45 (a worked-example rate, not market data), are misplaced or damaged, each costing R$ 22 of rehandling and claims. Losses are R$ 990. After labeled bays and a double scan at dispatch, errors fall to 1 percent, or 15 pallets. Losses become R$ 330, so the saving is R$ 660 a month, or R$ 7.920 in a year. Bay labels painted on the floor and a second scan at the dock door are the two changes behind the drop.

Activity. Write two role cards and a training plan for one new hire. Role-play a hard customer conversation twice and review.

Standard to meet. Role cards have measurable standards; training shows sign-off steps; service rules are written in plain words.

Module 7 Money: cost, price and break-even 5 hAnalyse

A business lives or dies on a few numbers. Know your contribution per unit, your fixed costs and the volume you need.

  • In warehousing and storage services: Utilisation percent drives profit; avoid empty racks.
  • Contribution per pallet = price minus variable cost. Fixed costs are paid whether you sell or not.
  • Break-even units = fixed cost divided by contribution. Plan to pass it by a safe margin.
  • Make a 12-month cash plan. Cash timing matters as much as profit: customers may pay late, suppliers may want money first.
  • Keep money for business and money for the owner apart.
  • Test the plan: what if sales fall by a quarter, or cost rises by a tenth?
Worked example. Worked example, all figures in reais: price R$ 55, variable cost R$ 22, contribution R$ 33 per pallet. Fixed cost R$ 28.000 a month. Break-even = R$ 28.000 / R$ 33 = 848,48, so 849 pallets a month. At 1.500 pallets a month, profit = R$ 33 x 1.500 - R$ 28.000 = R$ 21.500. These are teaching figures that belong to this example only; later examples and the case study use their own figures. Use your own.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: sales fall to 1.125 pallets a month. Question: what is the monthly result? Model answer: R$ 33 x 1.125 - R$ 28.000 = R$ 9.125; compare with break-even of 849 pallets and decide whether to cut fixed cost or raise volume first. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build your own table: price, variable cost, contribution, fixed cost, break-even and a downside case at 75% of sales. Show it to a peer or mentor.

Standard to meet. Arithmetic is correct; every figure has a source or is marked as an assumption; a downside case is shown.

Module 8 Grow, measure and renew 4 hEvaluate

Growth should follow proof. Measure, learn, then add; and keep returning to the customer.

  • In warehousing and storage services: Grow with value-added services.
  • Pick one growth lever at a time: new customers, a higher repeat rate, a better price, or a new offer.
  • Set one measurable target for 90 days, such as 'raise repeat customers from 20% to 30%'.
  • Ask each customer how they found you, and record it. Spend more where the record shows results.
  • Review monthly: numbers, defects, complaints, cash. Write three decisions each time.
  • Plan the renewal of licences, equipment and skills a year ahead.
Worked example. 90-day plan: 'Lever: repeat customers. Action: follow-up call after every pallet. Target: repeat rate from 20% to 30%. Check: weekly count. Review date: day 45 and day 90.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: you can add 300 pallets a month by one of two levers: a follow-up call to past customers or a price rise of R$ 6 to R$ 61. Question: how do you choose? Model answer: test the cheaper reversible lever first (the call), measure for 30 days, and only then try the price change, so you can tell which one worked.

Longer notes. Compute occupancy every Monday as used positions divided by total positions, and note the average number of days a pallet stays. Call customers whose volume has fallen before they move out. Offer a discount for six-month contracts and a premium for peak season. Measure revenue per square meter and compare it with last quarter. Expand only after occupancy stays high for several months. Send each customer a short monthly report on delays, errors and damage. Visit three local shops and ask about their storage problems. Storage contracts, especially six-month discounts, deserve a lawyer's read, and the invoices an accountant's.

Extra worked example. A warehouse averages 1.500 pallets at R$ 55, with R$ 22 of variable cost and R$ 28.000 of fixed cost, so profit is R$ 21.500. Visits to local shops fill an assumed 200 more positions (a worked-example figure, not market data), reaching 1.700. Sales rise to R$ 93.500 and profit to R$ 28.100, a gain of R$ 6.600 a month. Fixed costs stay unchanged. Over twelve months it is R$ 79.200. A monthly delay-and-damage report mailed to shops is how he would win them.

Activity. Write a 90-day plan with one target, three actions and a review date. Present it in five minutes and take questions.

Standard to meet. Target is measurable and dated; actions are small enough to start this week; review date is in the calendar.

case study

A case to work through

Situation. The owner runs a rented shed in the north zone of Sao Paulo and stores pallets for small online sellers. He charges R$ 55 per pallet a month. Handling labor, wrap and energy cost about R$ 22 per pallet. His fixed costs are R$ 28.000 a month for the lease, a night watch contract, insurance and software. Right now he holds about 1.500 pallets. He thought the business was fine because money comes in every month, but his account is always low. One client also claims that three pallets disappeared, and the owner cannot prove whether they ever arrived because he has no entry and exit record. Another seller asks for a discount in return for bringing more goods next year. The owner wants to know his break-even point and how to protect himself from stock disputes.

Task. Say what you would do first, next and last, and what record you would keep.

Model answer. First, find the margin: R$ 55 minus R$ 22 leaves R$ 33 per pallet. Next, break-even: R$ 28.000 divided by R$ 33 is 848,5, so he needs 849 pallets, which means 1.500 pallets sit only 651 above it. At 1.500, sales are R$ 82.500, contribution is R$ 49.500 and profit is R$ 21.500. Then test the discount (R$ 50 is a worked-example test price, not market data): at R$ 50 the margin is R$ 28, break-even rises to 1.000 pallets, so a discount needs a signed volume that fills that space. Last, build the control routine. Number each client area. Write a receipt for every pallet that enters, with date, quantity and condition, signed by both sides. Do the same for every exit. Count stock monthly and compare it with the log. Ask a lawyer and an insurance broker to read the contract, then place a numbered receipt book at each client area; the three missing pallets are a dispute he can settle only with such paper.

levels L1 to L7

Where this sits on the ladder

Seven levels, one slide each. Read the slide, then read the speaker notes under it. Credits stack and you can step in and out; this course is L4.

Slide 1 of 7: L1 Basic: warehousing and storage

  • Name common storage terms such as pallet, rack, bin and batch
  • List basic safety rules for lifting and walking routes
  • Recognise labels and simple scanning steps
Notes for slide 1

Learners walk a warehouse floor on paper: pallet, rack, bin, batch, forklift aisle. They learn safe lifting, marked walkways and what a barcode label tells a scanner. Six to ten hours of short lessons with a term-matching quiz; the pass is correct vocabulary and the main safety rules.

Slide 2 of 7: L2 Awareness: warehousing and storage

  • Describe how goods flow from receiving to dispatch
  • Explain stock rotation and why records must match shelves
  • Identify storage types and customers in Sao Paulo
Notes for slide 2

Ten to fifteen hours trace a delivery from the gate to the dock, put-away, picking and dispatch, and explain first-in first-out and why the system count must equal the shelf. The written flow chart names storage types, from dry racking to cold rooms, and who uses them in Sao Paulo.

Slide 3 of 7: L3 Foundation: warehousing and storage

  • Apply put-away and picking steps to real goods
  • Demonstrate a stock count and a damage report
  • Calculate space use and simple storage charges
Notes for slide 3

On sample goods, under supervision for 20 to 30 hours, the learner puts away and picks orders, runs a cycle count on one aisle, writes a damage report with photos and works out pallet positions used against those free. The portfolio shows counts that match records. Accuracy beats speed.

Slide 4 of 7: L4 Practitioner: warehousing and storage

  • Plan a storage or warehousing service with layout, systems and customers
  • Start it by choosing a site, insurance and checking the current official text for fire, safety and business rules
  • Run receiving, counts, dispatch and billing each week
Notes for slide 4

The core launch plan fixes layout, shelving, software or paper records, insurance and a site, after checking the current official text on fire and operating permits. Sixty to eighty hours across 8 modules produce a customer offer, a start-up checklist and four weeks of receiving, count and billing sheets.

Slide 5 of 7: L5 Specialist: warehousing and storage

  • Analyse picking errors and slow zones to speed work
  • Improve layout, labelling and stock accuracy
  • Negotiate with carriers and customers on service terms
Notes for slide 5

Pick-error logs and travel paths expose slow zones. The specialist relabels bays, moves fast sellers near the dock, raises count accuracy and negotiates carrier pick-up windows and customer service terms. Forty to sixty hours; the case study must show error rate or pick time before and after.

Slide 6 of 7: L6 Advanced: warehousing and storage

  • Evaluate systems, automation and expansion options
  • Design safety audits, staffing and cost control
  • Manage risk from fire, theft, damage and peaks
Notes for slide 6

Managers weigh scanners, conveyors or more racking against volume forecasts, write safety audit routines and shift plans, and set budgets per cost line. Fire, theft, water damage and peak-season overload each get a risk note and a response. Sixty to eighty hours with results reviewed afterwards.

Slide 7 of 7: L7 Super expert: warehousing and storage

  • Create operating standards for accuracy and safety
  • Mentor supervisors and managers
  • Lead change projects and share methods with the sector
Notes for slide 7

Top-tier work sets site-wide standards for stock accuracy and safe handling and coaches supervisors and managers. Over 100 hours of mentoring, writing and change projects shared with the sector, judged by peers. Fire and labour rules evolve, so each point is rechecked against current official text. Aisles stay clear and counts stay true.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, taxes, data duties and customer rights. This course is written for São Paulo. Check the current official text for your own place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registration: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registration: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registration: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registration: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registration: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registration: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registration: registration with ACRA. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registration: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)
