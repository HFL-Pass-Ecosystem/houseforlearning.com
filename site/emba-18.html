executive MBA subject 18

Consulting Methodology and Action Project

Year 2, term E: action project. 3.5 ECTS, about 88 hours, 4 parts. Part 1 is free.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task, condition and standard

Task

Deliver an action project: frame a client problem, research it and present a recommendation.

Condition

Given a client sponsor, eight to twelve weeks and access to client data.

Standard

The problem is framed with the client, the recommendation is evidence-based and the client agrees next steps.

Who it is for: Final-year learners who will solve a real problem for a real organisation.

how this subject works

Free entry, voice and your learning style

Part 1 is free to try. Read it, listen to it and answer it before you decide. Join the programme, or this subject on its own, to open the other parts.

  • Listen. Every part has a Listen button that reads it aloud.
  • Speak or type. Answer each part by talking (press Speak) or by typing. Your answers stay on this device only.
  • Learn your way. Every part has a See, Hear, Do and Read-and-write route. Not sure which suits you? Take the eight-question learning-style check.

4 parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 Problem framing 22 hAnalyse: frame

A well-framed problem is half solved. Agree with the client what decision they must make and how success will be judged.

  • Write the key question as a decision
  • Agree scope: what is in and out
  • Agree success measures

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Vague: "improve sales". Framed: "Which two of five regions should we add 10 sales staff to by June to raise sales by 2m?"

Activity: Write the key question for your project and agree it with the sponsor.

Standard: One decision question, scope and measures agreed in writing.

Learn your way

  • See. Sketch "problem framing" as a picture: boxes, arrows and the key number.
  • Hear. Press Listen, or explain the worked example aloud to someone.
  • Do. Do the activity with your own work or a practice case.
  • Read and write. Write the idea in three lines in your own words below.

Your answer. Write the key question for your project and agree it with the sponsor.

Part 2 Hypotheses and research design 22 hApply: plan research

Start with possible answers (hypotheses) and plan what evidence would prove or disprove each.

  • Break the question into parts that do not overlap
  • Write a hypothesis for each part
  • Plan data, interviews and analysis for each

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Hypothesis: "Region C has the most unmet demand." Evidence: market size, current share, lost bids. If C's share is 8% against 20% elsewhere, it supports the hypothesis.

Activity: Write an issue tree and a research plan.

Standard: Parts do not overlap and each has a test.

Learn your way

  • Hear. Press Listen, or explain the worked example aloud to someone.
  • Do. Do the activity with your own work or a practice case.
  • Read and write. Write the idea in three lines in your own words below.

Your answer. Write an issue tree and a research plan.

Part 3 Analysis and storyline 22 hEvaluate: synthesise

Turn findings into a short storyline: the answer, three supporting points, and the evidence for each.

  • Answer first
  • Three supporting points
  • Each slide has one message in its title

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Answer: "Add staff in C and E." Points: biggest gap, fastest payback (8 months), lowest hiring cost.

Activity: Write the storyline on one page before making slides.

Standard: Answer, three points and evidence aligned.

Learn your way

  • See. Sketch "analysis and storyline" as a picture: boxes, arrows and the key number.
  • Hear. Press Listen, or explain the worked example aloud to someone.
  • Do. Do the activity with your own work or a practice case.
  • Read and write. Write the idea in three lines in your own words below.

Your answer. Write the storyline on one page before making slides.

Part 4 Action Project proposal and delivery 22 hCreate: deliver

Present to the client, handle questions and agree next steps. A good project leaves the client able to act.

  • Proposal at the start, delivery at the end
  • Practise with tough questions
  • Agree owners and dates for next steps

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Final meeting: client agrees to hire 6 staff in C and 4 in E by June; a review in September checks the 2m target.

Activity: Deliver the final presentation and a short report.

Standard: Client agrees next steps with owners and dates.

ten checks

Check yourself

Say or write your answer first, then read the model answer.

  1. Q1. Why frame the problem as a decision?
    Model answer: It makes clear what the client must choose. (Covered in part 1: Problem framing.)
  2. Q2. Name three things to agree at the start.
    Model answer: Key question, scope, success measures. (Covered in part 1: Problem framing.)
  3. Q3. What is a hypothesis?
    Model answer: A possible answer to test. (Covered in part 2: Hypotheses and research design.)
  4. Q4. Why must issue tree parts not overlap?
    Model answer: To avoid double work and gaps. (Covered in part 2: Hypotheses and research design.)
  5. Q5. Region share 8% against 20% elsewhere supports what?
    Model answer: Unmet demand in that region. (Covered in part 2: Hypotheses and research design.)
  6. Q6. What comes first in a storyline?
    Model answer: The answer. (Covered in part 3: Analysis and storyline.)
  7. Q7. How many messages per slide?
    Model answer: One, in the title. (Covered in part 3: Analysis and storyline.)
  8. Q8. Why write the storyline before slides?
    Model answer: To keep the logic clear and short. (Covered in part 3: Analysis and storyline.)
  9. Q9. What should a project leave the client with?
    Model answer: The ability to act, with agreed next steps. (Covered in part 4: Action Project proposal and delivery.)
  10. Q10. What do next steps need?
    Model answer: Owners and dates. (Covered in part 4: Action Project proposal and delivery.)
