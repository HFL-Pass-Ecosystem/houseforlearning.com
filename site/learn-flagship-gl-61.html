flagship course

Micro-Mobility Services

Plan and run a small shared bike or scooter service: fleet, safety, parking, pricing, maintenance and city rules.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner31:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain micro-mobility types and where they fit.
  • Apply Plan fleet size, zones and parking.
  • Analyse Estimate use and revenue.
  • Evaluate Judge safety and city impact.
  • Create Write an operations plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan a 30-day pilot of a shared micro-mobility service for one campus or district, with fleet, safety rules, maintenance, pricing and a community plan.

Condition

A computer, a map, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. Real vehicles are not required.

Standard

Fleet and cost maths correct; safety rules and rider age limits stated; parking and permits checked; maintenance plan has daily checks; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Campus managers, tourism staff, city assistants, entrepreneurs. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Types, fit and local conditions 3 hUnderstand

Different vehicles suit different places. Match the vehicle to the streets, the weather and the riders.

  • Bikes, e-bikes, scooters, cargo bikes and adaptive trikes have different range, speed, cost and injury risk.
  • Short trips under 5 km are the main use; e-assist helps on hills and with longer trips.
  • Check street width, slopes, surface, rain and heat before choosing.
  • Scooters have higher injury risk at speed; plan speed limits.
Worked example. A flat tourist promenade suits light bikes. A hilly university suits e-bikes with 60 km range. A district with wide pavements may allow scooters with a 15 km/h limit.

Activity. Compare three vehicle types for one site in a table.

Standard to meet. Include range, cost, risk and fit in the table.

Module 2 Zones, parking and rules 4 hApply

Order on the street keeps the service welcome.

  • Place parking where people already walk: gates, stations and shops.
  • Use geofencing (a map boundary set in the app) to slow vehicles in busy zones or end trips only in marked bays.
  • Mark no-go zones near hospitals and schools.
  • Work with the owner or authority.
Worked example. A campus limits scooters to 10 km/h in the library courtyard through the app and marks bays at four gates.

Activity. Draw a zone map for a campus or district.

Standard to meet. Mark the zones and justify them.

Module 3 Fleet sizing and rebalancing 4 hAnalyse

Not too many and not too few vehicles.

  • Fleet = peak trips / trips per vehicle per day.
  • Keep 15 to 20 per cent of vehicles under repair or charging.
  • Use trip data to move vehicles before peak times.
  • Start small and measure.
Worked example. 60 peak trips at 6 trips per vehicle need 10 vehicles plus 2 spares. With 20 per cent unavailable, only about 10 are on the street at once.

Activity. Size a fleet for your case with spare allowance.

Standard to meet. Check that the maths is correct.

Module 4 Rider safety 5 hEvaluate

Prevent injuries.

  • Helmets, lights, speed limits and age limits.
  • A short in-app safety quiz before the first ride.
  • Incident log, insurance and clear parking rules.
  • Share lessons weekly.
Worked example. After a five-question quiz is added, near misses fall by a third in a month. A slow zone near pedestrians reduces collisions.

Activity. Write safety rules and a first-ride quiz of five questions.

Standard to meet. Write measurable rules and a clear quiz.

Module 5 Maintenance and charging 4 hApply

Safe vehicles every day.

  • Daily check: brakes, tyres, lights, bell and lock.
  • Replace worn tyres and pads on a schedule.
  • Train staff to handle batteries; never charge damaged packs.
  • Keep spare parts and a repair log.
Worked example. A weekly brake and tyre check finds three worn pads before any incident. A tag shows the last check date.

Activity. Create a check card and weekly calendar.

Standard to meet. Include ten items on the card and complete the calendar.

Module 6 Pricing, access and money 4 hEvaluate

Cover costs and stay fair.

  • Unlock fee plus per-minute price; day and weekly passes.
  • Costs: vehicles, staff, charging, repair, insurance and permits.
  • Student and low-income discounts.
  • Break-even trips per vehicle per day.
Worked example. Revenue of 90 INR per vehicle-day (an estimate; check your market) against a cost of 70 INR gives a margin of 20 INR. Break-even is 3.5 trips per vehicle per day at an average fare of 20 INR.

Activity. Build a pricing sheet and test two prices.

Standard to meet. Show the currency and calculate totals correctly.

Module 7 Community and environment 3 hEvaluate

Be a good neighbour.

  • Noise, clutter and accessibility.
  • Complaint line with a 24-hour response.
  • Recycling of batteries and parts.
  • Adaptations for riders with disabilities where possible.
Worked example. A trike option and a pickup service help riders who cannot use a scooter. A fine for blocking paths is explained and enforced fairly.

Activity. List community actions and owners.

Standard to meet. Assign each an owner and a date.

Module 8 Pilot plan and stop rules 4 hCreate

Test before scaling.

  • Targets for trips, safety and complaints.
  • Stop rules: pause if serious injuries occur.
  • Review weekly and report monthly.
  • Decide: stop, change or grow.
Worked example. The pilot is paused after two incidents to review rules, then resumed with stricter zones and a rider quiz.

Activity. Present the plan with stop rules.

Standard to meet. Set targets as numbers and state stop rules.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: zone map, fleet sheet, safety rules, price sheet, maintenance plan. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Fleet and zonesUnclearClearClear and tested
SafetyGapsRules setRules enforced and logged
FinanceNo costsCostsBreak-even shown
Pilot dataTargets with no baselineBaseline trips, damage and complaints recorded before launchBaseline compared with week four, and each rule change linked to the data
Road, helmet and parking rulesRules not checkedRoad, speed, helmet and parking rules for the place are written downRules checked with the local authority and a permit step recorded

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Typical trip length?

Model answer: Under about 5 km. (Covered in module 1: Types, fit and local conditions. To pass that module: Include range, cost, risk and fit in the table.)

Q2. Fleet formula?

Model answer: Peak trips / trips per vehicle per day. (Covered in module 3: Fleet sizing and rebalancing. To pass that module: Check that the maths is correct.)

Q3. Name a safety rule.

Model answer: Helmets or speed limits. (Covered in module 4: Rider safety. To pass that module: Write measurable rules and a clear quiz.)

Q4. Daily check items?

Model answer: Brakes, tyres, lights, bell, lock. (Covered in module 5: Maintenance and charging. To pass that module: Include ten items on the card and complete the calendar.)

Q5. Break-even idea?

Model answer: Trips per day needed to cover costs. (Covered in module 6: Pricing, access and money. To pass that module: Show the currency and calculate totals correctly.)

Q6. Why parking zones?

Model answer: To avoid clutter. (Covered in module 2: Zones, parking and rules. To pass that module: Mark the zones and justify them.)

Q7. Why insurance?

Model answer: Covers incidents. (Covered in module 4: Rider safety. To pass that module: Write measurable rules and a clear quiz.)

Q8. What is rebalancing?

Model answer: Moving vehicles to demand. (Covered in module 3: Fleet sizing and rebalancing. To pass that module: Check that the maths is correct.)

Q9. Name a community issue.

Model answer: Noise or blocked paths. (Covered in module 7: Community and environment. To pass that module: Assign each an owner and a date.)

Q10. What is a pilot?

Model answer: A short test before scaling. (Covered in module 8: Pilot plan and stop rules. To pass that module: Set targets as numbers and state stop rules.)

ten locations and global

Your place

City permits and helmet laws differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.
