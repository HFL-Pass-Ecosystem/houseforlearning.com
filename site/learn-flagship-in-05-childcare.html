flagship course

Childcare Products and Services

Choose, test and offer safe products and services for babies and young children, and run a small childcare service with care and records.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 39 h lessons plus practice

Choose, test and offer safe products and services for babies and young children, and run a small childcare service with care and records.

  • Define the stages of child development from birth to six.
  • Apply safety checks to toys, furniture and spaces.
  • Analyse a product for age fit and risk.
  • Evaluate a service against safeguarding rules.
  • Create a daily plan and a safety file.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Review a childcare product and run a small day plan for young children, with safety checks and records.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. A play space or creche visit, product samples, a supervisor.

Standard

Every check is written and dated, hazards are removed or reported within one day, staff to child ratios are met, and the daily plan fits the age group.

Who it is for: Parents, carers, early-years assistants, small business owners. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Child development from birth to six 4 hDefine
  • List key milestones: smiles at 2 months, sits at 6 months, walks at about 12 months, joins words at about 2 years.
  • Group needs into body, language, thinking, feelings and social skills.
  • Remember that children vary and a gap of a few months is common; note when to refer a concern.
  • Match play to stage: touch and sound for babies, building and pretend play for toddlers, rules and stories for older children.
Worked example. A 9-month-old explores by mouthing, so any toy must be too big to swallow; a 4-year-old can follow a three-step game.

Activity. Make a one-page chart of stages with a play idea for each.

Standard to meet. Chart covers birth to six years in five areas with one safe play idea per stage.

Module 2 Safe toys and products 5 hAnalyse
  • Check the age label and look for small parts: nothing that fits through a tube of 3.2 cm across for children under three.
  • Look for sharp edges, loose parts, long cords over 22 cm, button batteries and magnets.
  • Check materials for safe paint and no strong smell; read the care label.
  • Test by pulling, twisting and dropping, then record pass or fail.
Worked example. A toy car loses a wheel after 10 pulls; the wheel fits the 3.2 cm tube, so it is failed for under threes.

Activity. Test five toys with a written checklist.

Standard to meet. Each toy has a signed result and failed items are removed from use.

Module 3 Safe sleep, feeding and hygiene 5 hApply
  • Place babies to sleep on their back on a firm flat surface with no pillows, loose blankets or soft toys.
  • Wash hands for 20 seconds before feeding and after nappy changes or toilet use.
  • Sterilise bottles, store prepared milk as advised and discard what is left after a feed.
  • Record feeds and sleeps and note anything unusual.
Worked example. A feed log for one baby shows 6 feeds in 24 hours at 90 ml each; a sudden drop to 40 ml is reported to the parent the same day.

Activity. Write a routine card for sleep, feeds and hand washing and follow it for a week.

Standard to meet. Routine is followed daily and logged without gaps.

Module 4 Safe spaces and furniture 5 hEvaluate
  • Walk the space at child height and list hazards: sockets, sharp corners, hot items, stairs, water.
  • Check cots, high chairs and gates for stable build and slats no wider than 6 cm apart.
  • Keep cleaning liquids and medicines locked away and out of sight.
  • Plan exits and a fire route, and practise it.
Worked example. A room walk finds 11 hazards; 9 are fixed that day with covers and corner guards, and 2 are reported for repair by the owner.

Activity. Audit a room and write a hazard list with actions and dates.

Standard to meet. Every hazard has an owner and a due date, and fixed items are checked again.

Module 5 First aid and emergencies 5 hApply
  • Learn choking response by age, for a baby back blows and chest thrusts, and call for help early.
  • Know how to cool a burn under running water for 20 minutes.
  • Keep a stocked first aid kit and check it monthly against a list.
  • Keep emergency numbers and parent contacts on the wall and write every incident down.
Worked example. A kit checklist of 15 items shows 2 plasters missing and one expired item, which are replaced the same week.

Activity. Run a practice drill and complete an incident form.

Standard to meet. Drill is completed in the time set and the form has date, time, action and parent informed.

Module 6 Safeguarding and respectful care 5 hEvaluate
  • Never leave a child alone with anyone who is not cleared; use two adults for bathroom help where possible.
  • Know signs of harm: sudden fear, unexplained marks, big changes in behaviour.
  • Report concerns to the supervisor at once and write facts without opinions.
  • Respect privacy: share information only with parents and those who need it.
Worked example. A child has the same bruise pattern on two days; the carer writes date, place and the exact words of the child and tells the supervisor the same day.

Activity. Write a safeguarding policy page and role-play one report.

Standard to meet. Policy names who to tell, report is factual and the child is kept safe.

Module 7 Daily plan and activities 5 hCreate
  • Build a day with a steady rhythm of play, meals, rest and outdoor time.
  • Keep ratios, for example one adult to three children under two, and one adult to eight children aged three to six.
  • Plan one activity for language, one for movement and one for creativity, each ten to twenty minutes.
  • Prepare a rainy day option and a quiet option.
Worked example. A morning for six toddlers with two adults: arrival 15 minutes, songs 15, free play 40, snack 20, outdoor 30, story 15, rest 45.

Activity. Write a day plan for a group and run one morning.

Standard to meet. Plan fits the age, ratio is met and times add up to the session length.

Module 8 Capstone: safety file and day plan 5 hCreate
  • Review one product and one space and record the findings in a safety file.
  • Write a day plan and run it with a supervisor.
  • Record attendance, incidents and feedback for one week.
  • Present risks found, fixes made and what you would change.
Worked example. A file with 5 tested products, a room audit with 11 hazards closed, a one-week log and a day plan with ratio 1 to 3 for toddlers.

Activity. Present the file and answer questions.

Standard to meet. File is complete, dated and signed; no hazard is left without action.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why can two healthy children reach a milestone months apart?

Model answer: Children develop at different speeds, so a range is normal. (Covered in module 1: Child development from birth to six. To pass that module: Chart covers birth to six years in five areas with one safe play idea per stage.)

Q2. What is the small parts test?

Model answer: Anything that fits through a 3.2 cm tube can choke a child under three. (Covered in module 2: Safe toys and products. To pass that module: Each toy has a signed result and failed items are removed from use.)

Q3. How long should hands be washed?

Model answer: About 20 seconds with soap and water. (Covered in module 3: Safe sleep, feeding and hygiene. To pass that module: Routine is followed daily and logged without gaps.)

Q4. Why inspect a room from child height?

Model answer: Children reach and see different things than adults do. (Covered in module 4: Safe spaces and furniture. To pass that module: Every hazard has an owner and a due date, and fixed items are checked again.)

Q5. How long should a burn be cooled?

Model answer: About 20 minutes under running water. (Covered in module 5: First aid and emergencies. To pass that module: Drill is completed in the time set and the form has date, time, action and parent informed.)

Q6. What should a report of concern contain?

Model answer: Facts only: date, what was seen or said and who was told. (Covered in module 6: Safeguarding and respectful care. To pass that module: Policy names who to tell, report is factual and the child is kept safe.)

Q7. Why keep a steady daily rhythm?

Model answer: Children feel secure when they know what comes next. (Covered in module 7: Daily plan and activities. To pass that module: Plan fits the age, ratio is met and times add up to the session length.)

Q8. What shows a service is safe?

Model answer: Written checks, fixed hazards, correct ratios and clear records. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: safety file and day plan. To pass that module: File is complete, dated and signed; no hazard is left without action.)

Q9. Why keep written records of checks?

Model answer: They prove checks happened and help spot repeat problems. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why tell parents about small incidents?

Model answer: Parents need full information and trust grows from honesty. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know basic child safety at home.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain the stages of development and safe sleep.
  3. L3 Foundation. Help in a childcare setting under supervision.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Run a small group safely and keep records.
  5. L5 Specialist. Lead a childcare service and train helpers.
  6. L6 Advanced. Manage several sites and audit safeguarding.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set childcare safety standards and train providers across a region.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Child safety at home

List 10 home hazards for young children and the fix for each, with no error.

Speaker notes. Teacher shows a room photo and asks learners to spot hazards. Covered in module 4.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Development and safe sleep

Explain 4 stages of development from birth to six and 5 safe sleep rules.

Speaker notes. Learner explains a stage with a typical skill. Covered in module 1, Child development from birth to six.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Supervised care help

Help in a childcare setting for 3 sessions under supervision, completing a hygiene and feeding checklist each time.

Speaker notes. Teacher signs each checklist after watching a handwash and meal routine. Covered in module 3.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Small group care

Run a group of 6 children safely for a half day and keep a complete attendance and incident record.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the record and a mock fall response. Covered in module 2, Safe toys and products.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Lead a childcare service

Lead a service with 3 helpers, train them on 5 safety routines and check each with a signed sheet.

Speaker notes. Teacher watches one routine taught and reviews the sheets. Check local rules with an expert for licensing.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Audit across sites

Audit safeguarding at 2 sites using a 15-point checklist and write a fix plan with dates.

Speaker notes. Teacher reads the audit and tests that each gap has an owner. Check local rules with an expert.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Set regional standards

Set a safety standard of 10 rules and train 12 providers, then check 80 percent follow it after one month.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the standard and the follow-up visit records.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Risk lesson (estimates, not quotes). In India a rough sheet figure is Rs 900 per product sold against a unit cost of Rs 600 and a fixed cost of Rs 30,000 a month: a margin of about 33% and a break-even of about 100 units a month. If the price falls 25% to about Rs 675, break-even rises from 100 to 400 units a month. Plan a price floor or a lower cost before you launch, and test the plan at the lower price. These sheet figures are separate from the sample numbers in the modules. Check them with a local price expert.
