Trading and Distribution

Build and cost a mock trade file for one real product, from quote to delivery plan, with margins, risks and checks.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Build and cost a mock trade file for one real product, from quote to delivery plan, with margins, risks and checks.

Condition

Real or model setting, partner or mentor, free tools, currency written on every number. This course teaches process and safety awareness only; local authorities set rules.

Standard

Evidence reviewed by a mentor; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better; two peer reviews given.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Find a product with a supplier and a buyer 6 hUnderstand

Trading means moving a product from where it is cheap or plentiful to where it is needed.

  • Choose one product you can understand and test (for example packaged food items or hand tools).
  • Get three supplier quotes and two buyer needs: price, quantity, time.
  • Check quality, shelf life, and any rules for the product.
  • Never trade restricted goods; check licence and sanctions first.
Worked example. Product: stainless steel water bottles. Supplier quotes: 3.2, 3.5 and 3.9 per unit for 1,000 units. Buyer needs: 500 units, delivered in 30 days, price ceiling 6.

Activity. Write the one-page idea and a quotes table in your currency.

Standard to meet. Three supplier quotes and two buyer needs on one page.

Module 2 Trade documents 6 hAnalyse

Each document proves one thing. If they disagree, goods get stuck.

  • Quote: what you offer and at what price. Order: what the buyer accepts.
  • Commercial invoice: what is sold, quantity, price, currency, terms.
  • Packing list: what is in each package, weights, sizes.
  • Transport document: proof the goods were handed to the carrier. Certificate of origin: where goods come from.
Worked example. Invoice says 500 units; packing list shows 480. Fix before shipping: a mismatch can delay customs.

Activity. Prepare a draft invoice and packing list for your mock deal and cross-check every figure.

Standard to meet. No mismatches between documents.

Module 3 Choose terms and cost the deal 35 hApply

Trade terms decide who pays freight and who carries risk at each point.

  • Standard international terms split cost and risk between seller and buyer. Pick one and write why.
  • Landed cost = product + packing + freight + insurance + duties and taxes + bank charges + your costs.
  • Show currency and the exchange rate you used, with the date.
  • Margin = (selling price − landed cost) ÷ selling price.
Worked example. Product 3.5 ×500 = 1,750; packing 100; freight 250; insurance 25; duties 8% of 2,125 = 170; bank 40; total 2,335. Selling at 5.6 ×500 = 2,800. Margin = 465 ÷ 2,800 = 16.6%.

Activity. Build the cost model in a spreadsheet; make three scenarios (freight +20%, currency −5%, delay).

Standard to meet. All lines have currency; scenarios show margin.

Module 4 Customs, payment and compliance 10 hApply

Rules protect people, the state and the trade. You must follow them.

  • Classification codes decide duty; use official tariff lists for your country.
  • Payment terms: advance, open account, bank guarantee; each changes risk.
  • Exchange-rate risk: a 5% swing can remove your margin.
  • Licences and sanctions: check before any deal; some destinations are not allowed.
Worked example. Payment at sight against documents keeps the goods under control until paid.

Activity. Write a compliance checklist for your product and route with sources and dates.

Standard to meet. Every check has a source; no restricted goods.

Module 5 Run the mock trade file 35 hAnalyse

Assemble the whole file as if the deal were real.

  • Enquiry → quote → order → invoice → packing list → shipping plan.
  • Create a timeline in days from order to delivery.
  • Identify three risks and what you will do about each.
  • Keep the file in order with a contents list.
Worked example. Risk table: late supplier (buffer 5 days), damage (better packing), currency (hedge or quote in buyer's currency).

Activity. Assemble the file; have a peer try to find errors.

Standard to meet. Peer finds at most two minor errors; timeline is realistic.

Module 6 Margins, pricing and negotiation 14 hEvaluate

A fair price keeps both sides in business.

  • Know your walk-away price before you talk.
  • Trade volume for price: offer a discount only for a larger order.
  • Never promise delivery you cannot control.
  • Keep notes of every negotiation point.
Worked example. Buyer asks for 5.0. Your cost is 4.67 per unit. Walk-away 5.2. Counter 5.4 for 500 or 5.25 for 800.

Activity. Role-play a negotiation; record the result and margin.

Standard to meet. Agreement is inside your limit; notes written.

Module 7 Improve and test with a real buyer 28 hCreate

Ask a real small buyer to comment on your quote for training purposes only.

  • Do not take payment or sign a real contract in this course.
  • Ask what they would change: price, quantity, speed.
  • Update your file and cost model.
  • List what you would need to go live: licences, bank, insurance.
Worked example. Buyer says: delivery in 21 days, not 30. Air freight adds 180; margin drops to 10%: decide to offer 30 days only.

Activity. Update the file and record the feedback.

Standard to meet. File version dated; changes explained.

Module 8 Review room and next step 8 hCreate

Present, check and plan.

  • Show your file, margin and risk table.
  • Give peers feedback.
  • Take the check.
  • Choose a next course: Online Sales or IT Business.
Worked example. Score sheet: documents 5, costing 4, compliance 4.

Activity. Present for 8 minutes.

Standard to meet. All rubric lines at Meets or better.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name three documents in a trade file.

Model answer: Invoice, packing list and transport document (also order and certificate of origin). (Covered in module 2: Trade documents. To pass that module: No mismatches between documents.)

Q2. What is landed cost?

Model answer: Product plus packing, freight, insurance, duties, bank charges and your costs. (Covered in module 3: Choose terms and cost the deal. To pass that module: All lines have currency; scenarios show margin.)

Q3. Margin on price 2,800, cost 2,335?

Model answer: 16.6%. (Covered in module 3: Choose terms and cost the deal. To pass that module: All lines have currency; scenarios show margin.)

Q4. Why check sanctions and licences?

Model answer: Some goods and places are not allowed; breaking rules has severe penalties. (Covered in module 4: Customs, payment and compliance. To pass that module: Every check has a source; no restricted goods.)

Q5. How can exchange rates hurt margin?

Model answer: If the buyer's currency falls before payment you receive less in your currency. (Covered in module 4: Customs, payment and compliance. To pass that module: Every check has a source; no restricted goods.)

Q6. What do trade terms decide?

Model answer: Who pays freight and who carries risk at each point. (Covered in module 3: Choose terms and cost the deal. To pass that module: All lines have currency; scenarios show margin.)

Q7. Why cross-check documents?

Model answer: Mismatches delay customs and payment. (Covered in module 2: Trade documents. To pass that module: No mismatches between documents.)

Q8. One way to reduce payment risk.

Model answer: Bank guarantee or payment against documents. (Covered in module 4: Customs, payment and compliance. To pass that module: Every check has a source; no restricted goods.)

Q9. What is a walk-away price?

Model answer: The lowest price you will accept; set before negotiating. (Covered in module 6: Margins, pricing and negotiation. To pass that module: Agreement is inside your limit; notes written.)

Q10. Can this course sign real deals?

Model answer: No: it is training only; real deals need licences and legal advice. (Covered in module 7: Improve and test with a real buyer. To pass that module: File version dated; changes explained.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Use local units, rules and authorities.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Business set-up: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Product and food rules: FSSAI.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Business set-up: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Product and food rules: NVWA.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Business set-up: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Product and food rules: the municipality food-control team (for example Dubai Municipality).

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Business set-up: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Product and food rules: ANVISA and MAPA.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Business set-up: federal or Ontario business registration. Personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Product and food rules: the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA).

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Business set-up: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Product and food rules: the FDA and the state health department.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Business set-up: registration with ACRA. Personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Product and food rules: the Singapore Food Agency (SFA).

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Business set-up: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Product and food rules: Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) and the state health authority.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Business set-up: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Product and food rules: the Centre for Food Safety.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Business set-up: local business registration in the country where you work. Personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Product and food rules: the national food-safety authority.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Business set-up: a trade and business licence or company registration. Personal data: the Data Protection Act. Product and food rules: the public health authority.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Business set-up: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Product and food rules: the federal health-risk authority.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
