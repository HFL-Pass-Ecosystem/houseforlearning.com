Grade 12 Commerce: Mathematics

4 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5Post-secondary4 units36 teaching weeks36-week school yearBloom levels: Apply
  • Apply Operate on matrices and functions.
  • Apply Find derivatives and simple integrals.
  • Apply Calculate probability and optimise.
  • Apply Compute index numbers and trends.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 12 Commerce Mathematics, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 4 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

unit by unit

4 units with worked examples

Unit 1 Relations, functions and matrices Weeks 1 to 9Apply

In this unit you work on 'Relations, functions and matrices', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: operate on matrices and functions. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Relations, functions and matrices' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: operate on matrices and functions. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: [1 2; 3 4] + [0 1; 1 0] = [1 3; 4 4]. The determinant of [1 2; 3 4] = 1 x 4 - 2 x 3 = -2. Method: Model run for 'Relations, functions and matrices'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Add matrices: A = [[1, 2], [3, 4]] and B = [[0, 1], [2, 3]]. A + B adds matching entries: [[1, 3], [5, 7]]. And 2A = [[2, 4], [6, 8]]. Same order is needed.
  2. Step 2, Multiply: AB uses rows of A with columns of B. Entries: 1x0 + 2x2 = 4; 1x1 + 2x3 = 7; 3x0 + 4x2 = 8; 3x1 + 4x3 = 15. So AB = [[4, 7], [8, 15]].
  3. Step 3, Order matters: BA = [[3, 4], [11, 16]], which differs from AB. For BA, entry (1,1) = 0x1 + 1x3 = 3. So matrix multiplication is not commutative.
  4. Step 4, Transpose: the transpose of A swaps rows and columns, [[1, 3], [2, 4]]. Business use: a matrix of unit sales per shop times a column of prices gives total revenue per shop.
  5. Step 5, Functions: f(x) = 2x + 1 is one-one since different x give different y. But f(x) = x^2 on all real numbers is not one-one because f(2) = f(-2) = 4.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: multiply [[2, 0], [1, 3]] by [[1, 4], [2, 0]]. Answer: row 1 gives [2, 8], row 2 gives [1 + 6, 4 + 0] = [7, 4], so the product is [[2, 8], [7, 4]].

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Operate on matrices and functions: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

Unit 2 Calculus: differentiation and integration Weeks 10 to 18Apply

In this unit you work on 'Calculus: differentiation and integration', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: find derivatives and simple integrals. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Calculus: differentiation and integration' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: find derivatives and simple integrals. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: d/dx of (3x squared + 5x) = 6x + 5; at x = 2 the gradient is 17. Limit as x goes to 2 of (x squared - 4)/(x - 2) = limit of (x + 2) = 4. Method: Model run for 'Calculus: differentiation and integration'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Differentiate: d/dx of 3x^4 - 5x^2 + 2 = 12x^3 - 10x. A constant differentiates to zero and each power drops by one.
  2. Step 2, Marginal cost: C(x) = 0.5x^2 + 20x + 100 has C'(x) = x + 20. At x = 10 units the marginal cost is 30, the extra cost of making the 11th unit, roughly.
  3. Step 3, Maximise revenue: R(x) = 50x - x^2. R'(x) = 50 - 2x = 0 gives x = 25. R(25) = 1,250 - 625 = 625. Second derivative -2 is negative, so it is a maximum.
  4. Step 4, Integrate: the integral of 3x^2 + 4x dx = x^3 + 2x^2 + C. Check by differentiating: 3x^2 + 4x, which returns the original expression.
  5. Step 5, Definite integral: from 1 to 3 of 2x dx = [x^2] from 1 to 3 = 9 - 1 = 8. This is the area under y = 2x from x = 1 to x = 3, a trapezoid with parallel sides 2 and 6 and width 2: (2 + 6)/2 x 2 = 8.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: differentiate 4x^3 - 2x + 7 and integrate 6x^2 - 2. Answer: 12x^2 - 2; and 2x^3 - 2x + C.

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Find derivatives and simple integrals: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

Unit 3 Probability and linear programming Weeks 19 to 27Apply

In this unit you work on 'Probability and linear programming', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: calculate probability and optimise. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Probability and linear programming' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: calculate probability and optimise. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: A fair die has 6 faces and 3 are even, so P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2. Method: Model run for 'Probability and linear programming'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Conditional probability: P(A and B) = 0.2 and P(B) = 0.5, so P(A given B) = 0.2/0.5 = 0.4. The conditional probability uses only the outcomes in B.
  2. Step 2, Linear programming set-up: maximise profit Z = 3x + 2y with x + y <= 4, x <= 3, x >= 0 and y >= 0. Draw the feasible region and list its corner points.
  3. Step 3, Corners: (0, 0), (3, 0), (3, 1) where x = 3 meets x + y = 4, and (0, 4). Check (3, 1): 3 + 1 = 4 and x = 3, both lines hold. The feasible region is the quadrilateral joining them.
  4. Step 4, Evaluate Z: at (0, 0) Z = 0; at (3, 0) Z = 9; at (3, 1) Z = 9 + 2 = 11; at (0, 4) Z = 8. The maximum is 11 at (3, 1). The best point is always a corner.
  5. Step 5, Check feasibility: (3, 1) satisfies x + y = 4 <= 4 and x = 3 <= 3. A nearby interior point like (2, 1) gives Z = 8, which is less, confirming the corner is best.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: maximise Z = 5x + 4y with x + y <= 6, x <= 4, x, y >= 0. Answer: corners (0,0), (4,0), (4,2), (0,6) give 0, 20, 28, 24, so the maximum is 28 at (4, 2).

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Calculate probability and optimise: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

Unit 4 Index numbers and time series Weeks 28 to 36Apply

In this unit you work on 'Index numbers and time series', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: compute index numbers and trends. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Index numbers and time series' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: compute index numbers and trends. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: Data 4, 7, 7, 9, 13. Mean = 40 / 5 = 8. Median (middle value) = 7. Mode (most common) = 7. Method: Model run for 'Index numbers and time series'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Price relative: an item costs 40 in the base year and 50 now. Index = 50/40 x 100 = 125. So prices are 25 percent higher than the base year.
  2. Step 2, Laspeyres index: base prices 10 and 20, base quantities 5 and 3, current prices 12 and 25. Sum of p1q0 = 12 x 5 + 25 x 3 = 60 + 75 = 135. Sum of p0q0 = 50 + 60 = 110.
  3. Step 3, Index = 135/110 x 100 = 122.73. The basket that cost 110 would now cost 135, a rise of about 22.7 percent. It uses base-year quantities as weights.
  4. Step 4, Moving average: yearly sales 20, 24, 28, 26, 30. Three-year averages are (20 + 24 + 28)/3 = 24, (24 + 28 + 26)/3 = 26 and (28 + 26 + 30)/3 = 28. They rise steadily, showing the trend.
  5. Step 5, Check: the averages 24, 26, 28 rise by 2 each time, while the raw sales 20, 24, 28, 26, 30 dip at 26. So the moving average has smoothed out the short-term dip and shows the upward trend.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: base prices 8 and 5, base quantities 2 and 4, current prices 10 and 6. Find the Laspeyres index. Answer: (20 + 24)/(16 + 20) x 100 = 44/36 x 100 = 122.22.

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Compute index numbers and trends: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Unit 1 (Relations, functions and matrices): Multiply [[2, 0], [1, 3]] by [[1, 4], [2, 0]].

Model answer: row 1 gives [2, 8], row 2 gives [1 + 6, 4 + 0] = [7, 4], so the product is [[2, 8], [7, 4]]. (Covered in unit 1: Relations, functions and matrices.)

Q2. Unit 2 (Calculus: differentiation and integration): Differentiate 4x^3 - 2x + 7 and integrate 6x^2 - 2.

Model answer: 12x^2 - 2; and 2x^3 - 2x + C. (Covered in unit 2: Calculus: differentiation and integration.)

Q3. Unit 3 (Probability and linear programming): Maximise Z = 5x + 4y with x + y <= 6, x <= 4, x, y >= 0.

Model answer: corners (0,0), (4,0), (4,2), (0,6) give 0, 20, 28, 24, so the maximum is 28 at (4, 2). (Covered in unit 3: Probability and linear programming.)

Q4. Unit 4 (Index numbers and time series): Base prices 8 and 5, base quantities 2 and 4, current prices 10 and 6. Find the Laspeyres index.

Model answer: (20 + 24)/(16 + 20) x 100 = 44/36 x 100 = 122.22. (Covered in unit 4: Index numbers and time series.)

Q5. Unit 1 (Relations, functions and matrices): Multiply. Show how you do it and say what the result is.

Model answer: AB uses rows of A with columns of B. Entries: 1x0 + 2x2 = 4; 1x1 + 2x3 = 7; 3x0 + 4x2 = 8; 3x1 + 4x3 = 15. So AB = [[4, 7], [8, 15]]. (Covered in unit 1: Relations, functions and matrices.)

Q6. Unit 2 (Calculus: differentiation and integration): Marginal cost. Show how you do it and say what the result is.

Model answer: C(x) = 0.5x^2 + 20x + 100 has C'(x) = x + 20. At x = 10 units the marginal cost is 30, the extra cost of making the 11th unit, roughly. (Covered in unit 2: Calculus: differentiation and integration.)

Q7. Unit 3 (Probability and linear programming): Linear programming set-up. Show how you do it and say what the result is.

Model answer: maximise profit Z = 3x + 2y with x + y <= 4, x <= 3, x >= 0 and y >= 0. Draw the feasible region and list its corner points. (Covered in unit 3: Probability and linear programming.)

Q8. Unit 4 (Index numbers and time series): Laspeyres index. Show how you do it and say what the result is.

Model answer: base prices 10 and 20, base quantities 5 and 3, current prices 12 and 25. Sum of p1q0 = 12 x 5 + 25 x 3 = 60 + 75 = 135. Sum of p0q0 = 50 + 60 = 110. (Covered in unit 4: Index numbers and time series.)

Q9. Unit 1 (Relations, functions and matrices): Order matters. Show how you do it and say what the result is.

Model answer: BA = [[3, 4], [11, 16]], which differs from AB. For BA, entry (1,1) = 0x1 + 1x3 = 3. So matrix multiplication is not commutative. (Covered in unit 1: Relations, functions and matrices.)

Q10. Unit 2 (Calculus: differentiation and integration): Maximise revenue. Show how you do it and say what the result is.

Model answer: R(x) = 50x - x^2. R'(x) = 50 - 2x = 0 gives x = 25. R(25) = 1,250 - 625 = 625. Second derivative -2 is negative, so it is a maximum. (Covered in unit 2: Calculus: differentiation and integration.)

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Count and name shapes

On the slide: L1 Basic · Count and name shapes

Notes: Count objects up to 20 and name a circle, square and triangle. Notes: give ten buttons and two trays; learners count aloud, move three across and say how many are left (7), then hunt the room for one shape of each kind.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Add, subtract and measure

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Add, subtract and measure

Notes: Add and subtract within 100 and measure a length with a ruler. Notes: work 46 + 27 = 73 on a number line, jumping 20 then 7; learners measure a pencil in centimetres and explain how they checked their answer a second way.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Fractions and decimals

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Fractions and decimals

Notes: Compare fractions and decimals and use the four operations with whole numbers. Notes: shade 3/4 and 6/8 on equal strips to show they match, then add 0.5 + 0.25 = 0.75 and write it as 75 per cent of one whole.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Equations and percentages

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Equations and percentages

Notes: Solve one-step and two-step equations and percentage problems, showing each step. Notes: practice case is 3x + 5 = 20, so x = 5, checked by substituting back; then a 15 per cent discount on 400 leaves 340, because 400 x 0.85 = 340.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Geometry, data and graphs (this course)

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Geometry, data and graphs

Notes: Use the right-angled triangle rule, the mean and a straight-line graph to solve a problem. Notes: legs of 6 and 8 give a longest side of 10; the data 4, 6, 6, 8, 11 have mean 7; plot y = 2x + 1 and read y = 7 at x = 3.

This course: Grade 12 Commerce Mathematics is at this level. Its units include Relations, functions and matrices; Calculus: differentiation and integration; Probability and linear programming; Index numbers and time series.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Proof, calculus and algebra

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Proof, calculus and algebra

Notes: Prove results and use calculus and matrices to model change. Notes: differentiate f(x) = x^3 - 3x to get 3x^2 - 3, so turning points sit at x = 1 and x = -1; the second derivative 6x shows a minimum then a maximum. Learners prove the square root of 2 is irrational.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Research-level modelling

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Research-level modelling

Notes: Formulate and defend a proof or statistical model, stating assumptions and limits. Notes: for 25 values with mean 50 and standard deviation 10, the standard error is 2 and a 95 per cent interval is about 45.9 to 54.1 (t = 2.064). Learners check residuals and write a referee-style critique.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

India (Bengaluru)

Use INR in money problems; deliver in English, Hindi, Kannada; personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Use AED in money problems; deliver in Arabic, English; personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Use BRL in money problems; deliver in Portuguese (Spanish and English support); personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Canada (Toronto)

Use CAD in money problems; deliver in English, French; personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

United States

Use USD in money problems; deliver in English, Spanish; personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Singapore

Use SGD in money problems; deliver in English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Australia

Use AUD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Use HKD in money problems; deliver in Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Use GEL or ILS in money problems; deliver in Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Use KYD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Data Protection Act; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Mexico

Use MXN in money problems; deliver in Spanish, English; personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; guardian contact for every learner under 18.
