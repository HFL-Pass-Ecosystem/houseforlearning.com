Transportation and storage

Run a pilot of transportation and storage in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small transportation and storage offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: local, logistics and field services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Understand freight and storage markets 6 hUnderstand

Goods move by road, rail, air and sea; storage buffers timing.

  • Map one route: origin, mode, handover, destination, with times.
  • Know cost drivers: distance, weight, speed, handling, empty returns.
  • Study cold chain and dangerous goods as special cases.
  • Choose a niche: local last-mile, cold storage, small-parcel consolidation.
  • Learn each mode's strength: road for flexibility, rail for heavy bulk over distance, air for speed, sea for volume; write one example for each.
  • Note seasonal swings such as harvest, holiday peaks and spring road limits; check the provincial weight rule.
  • Interview three shippers about volume, timing and damage history.
Worked example. A shipper sends 12 pallets weekly to three towns. If a loaded trip costs 100 per pallet and empty returns add 30%, the cost is 130 per pallet. Over 12 pallets that is 1,560 instead of 1,200, a difference of 360 a week.

Activity. Map one route with costs.

Standard to meet. Show costs per mode.

Module 2 Know transport and safety rules 6 hApply

Transport is regulated.

  • Commercial driver licensing, hours-of-service and vehicle inspections apply.
  • Dangerous goods need training and documents.
  • Food and medicine storage need temperature records.
  • Cross-border rules need customs paperwork.
  • Learn that axle weights and spring road limits are set by the province; check the provincial rule before you plan a load.
  • Write a daily pre-trip inspection checklist and keep signed copies.
  • Check insurance requirements for carriers and warehouse operators; check the provincial and federal rule.
Worked example. Row: 'Drive commercial truck | provincial and federal transport rules | licence, logs, inspection | each day | log book'. A second row: 'Store chilled food | food safety rule | temperature log against the limit in the rule | at the interval the rule sets; check it | log sheet'. Date each source.

Activity. Build a rules table.

Standard to meet. Cite a source and a date for every row.

Module 3 Plan routes and warehouse flow 10 hApply

Good plans save kilometres and handling.

  • Group stops by area and time window.
  • Load in reverse stop order.
  • Warehouse flow: receive, check, put-away, pick, pack, ship.
  • Use simple routing software or a spreadsheet to compare route options.
  • Order stops by time window first, then by distance.
  • Design the dock with separate receiving and shipping areas where possible and keep aisles clear.
  • Count touches: every extra handling adds cost and damage risk, so cut one.
Worked example. Route A: 22 stops in 188 km versus 241 km unplanned. The saving is 53 km, which is 53 / 241 = 22%. At 1.20 per km for fuel and wear that is 63.60 saved per route.

Activity. Plan a 15-stop route two ways.

Standard to meet. Compare distances.

Module 4 Cost per load and per pallet position 8 hEvaluate

Know your cost.

  • List the cost items: vehicle, driver, fuel, insurance and warehouse rent.
  • Warehouse cost per pallet per month.
  • Utilisation: loaded share of capacity.
  • Work out the margin per load and the number of loads needed to cover fixed costs.
  • Work out vehicle cost per km: fuel, tires, repairs, depreciation and insurance divided by km driven.
  • For the warehouse, divide rent, utilities and labour by pallet positions to get cost per position per month.
  • Test a utilisation case: if loads run 70% full, revenue per trip falls.
Worked example. Load price 2,400; cost 1,800; margin 600 (25%); fixed cost 24,000 a month gives 24,000 / 600 = 40 loads. At 70% utilisation revenue is 2,400 x 0.7 = 1,680 against the same 1,800 cost, a loss of 120 per load. Teaching figures in CAD.

Activity. Build a cost sheet with 70% utilisation case.

Standard to meet. Show the cases.

Module 5 Run on time and damage-free 8 hApply

Reliability sells.

  • Track on-time and damage rates.
  • Inspect each load at pickup for damage and secure it before departure.
  • Do preventive maintenance on a schedule.
  • Handle damage claims fast and keep the customer informed.
  • Record arrival time at each stop against the promised window.
  • Photograph the load at pickup and at delivery and get a signed receipt.
  • Schedule maintenance by kilometres and log it; a missed service goes in the risk register.
Worked example. On-time 94%: 47 of 50 deliveries arrived in the window. Damage 0.8%: 4 pallets of 500 were damaged. Track both every week and set a target for next month.

Activity. Keep a 4-week delivery log.

Standard to meet. Compute rates.

Module 6 Win shippers and partners 6 hCreate

Prove reliability.

  • Prove reliability with trial loads before asking for a long contract.
  • Quote in writing with terms.
  • Partner with brokers and warehouses.
  • Track the funnel from shippers contacted to quotes to customers.
  • Write a one-page rate card with minimum charge, waiting time and cancellation terms.
  • Offer a trial of five loads at the normal price, judged against an agreed on-time target.
  • Ask partner brokers for one thing to improve and record the answer.
Worked example. Funnel: 25 shippers, 8 quotes, 3 customers. Quote rate is 8 / 25 = 32%, win rate is 3 / 8 = 37.5%, and overall 3 / 25 = 12%. To win six customers you would contact about 50 shippers.

Activity. Pitch three shippers.

Standard to meet. Pitch has numbers.

Module 7 Manage safety, weather and cargo risk 6 hEvaluate

Plan for hazards.

  • Set fatigue and speed controls and follow the hours-of-service rules.
  • Plan for winter driving with route alternatives and buffer days.
  • Carry cargo insurance that matches the value you move.
  • Prevent theft with locked yards, sealed loads and checked drivers.
  • Set a rule for when a driver must stop for rest or bad weather, and state that no one is penalised for stopping.
  • Prepare a winter kit and check the provincial rule on tires and chains.
  • Compare cargo insurance limits with the value of a pallet.
Worked example. Risk: 'Winter delay | 4 x 3 | buffer days and alerts | dispatcher | each season'. The score is 4 x 3 = 12. If a lost pallet is worth 6,000 and cover is 5,000, 1,000 is uninsured.

Activity. Write a risk register.

Standard to meet. Name an owner for each item.

Module 8 Pilot a lane 8 hCreate

Prove one route.

  • Run the pilot for 60 days.
  • Measure on-time rate and cost per pallet.
  • Stop rule: margin under 10% means re-price.
  • Plan growth to more lanes only if the margin holds.
  • Choose a lane with a willing shipper and repeat loads, such as two a week.
  • Record cost per pallet and on-time rate every week.
  • Decide in writing on day 60 whether to continue, re-price or stop.
Worked example. Margin 22%: at a price of 2,400 the cost is 2,400 x 0.78 = 1,872 and the margin is 528. That is above the 10% stop line (240), so proceed and plan growth.

Activity. Run pilot review.

Standard to meet. Review has decision.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is utilisation?

Model answer: Share of vehicle or space used. (Covered in module 4: Cost per load and per pallet position. To pass that module: Show the cases.)

Q2. Why group stops?

Model answer: To cut distance. (Covered in module 3: Plan routes and warehouse flow. To pass that module: Compare distances.)

Q3. What logs do drivers keep?

Model answer: Hours-of-service logs. (Covered in module 2: Know transport and safety rules. To pass that module: Cite a source and a date for every row.)

Q4. Why photograph loads?

Model answer: To handle damage claims. (Covered in module 5: Run on time and damage-free. To pass that module: Compute rates.)

Q5. What raises cost per pallet?

Model answer: Empty return trips. (Covered in module 1: Understand freight and storage markets. To pass that module: Show costs per mode.)

Q6. What is on-time rate?

Model answer: Deliveries on time divided by all. (Covered in module 5: Run on time and damage-free. To pass that module: Compute rates.)

Q7. Who trains for dangerous goods?

Model answer: Dangerous goods work needs training and documents. (Covered in module 2: Know transport and safety rules. To pass that module: Cite a source and a date for every row.)

Q8. Why buffer days?

Model answer: Winter delays. (Covered in module 7: Manage safety, weather and cargo risk. To pass that module: Name an owner for each item.)

Q9. What triggers re-pricing?

Model answer: Margin under 10%. (Covered in module 8: Pilot a lane. To pass that module: Review has decision.)

Q10. Why insure cargo?

Model answer: Loss and damage claims. (Covered in module 7: Manage safety, weather and cargo risk. To pass that module: Name an owner for each item.)

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
