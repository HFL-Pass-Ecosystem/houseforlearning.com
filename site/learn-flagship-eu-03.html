Online Sales and E-Commerce Operations

Set up and run a small online shop: product pages, stock, orders, delivery, payments, customer service and simple numbers.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 150 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%USD 39 per learner per month, or USD 79 with mentor review

Set up and run a small online shop: product pages, stock, orders, delivery, payments, customer service and simple numbers.

  • Understand Explain the order journey from visit to delivery.
  • Apply Build product pages and run 20 real orders.
  • Analyse Read conversion, order value and return numbers.
  • Evaluate Judge delivery, price and page changes by results.
  • Create Write clear policies and customer messages.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a small online shop through at least 20 real or trial orders and keep a full record.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free or low-cost shop tool, a product with stock, bank or UPI-style payment page, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

20 orders processed; every order tracked; policies written; numbers reported weekly; data-protection and consumer rules checked; 70% on the check.

Collect only the personal data you need, keep it safe and delete it when no longer needed; follow your place's consumer and data law.

Requirements: None. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (150 h guided)

Module 1 The order journey 8 hUnderstand

Every sale is a chain: visit, view, add to basket, checkout, pay, pick, pack, ship, deliver, support. A break anywhere loses money.

  • Draw the chain for your shop and mark where customers drop out.
  • Learn the four key numbers: visits, conversion rate, average order value, returns.
  • Know who does what: you, courier, payment partner, customer.
  • Set a promise: price, delivery time and returns.
Worked example. 100 visits, 3 orders: conversion 3%. Average order EUR 28. Revenue EUR 84 per 100 visits. Doubling conversion to 6% doubles revenue without more visitors.

Activity. Draw your order journey and compute the four numbers for a sample week.

Standard to meet. Journey complete; four numbers calculated correctly.

Module 2 Product pages that sell 14 hCreate

Buyers decide in seconds. A page answers: what is it, is it for me, can I trust it, what does it cost, when does it arrive?

  • Title: plain words a buyer would search.
  • Photos: clear, several angles, scale.
  • Description: size, material, use, care; the top 3 benefits first.
  • Price, delivery and returns visible without scrolling.
Worked example. Weak title 'Item 17'. Strong title 'Glass water bottle 750 ml with cork lid'. Add: 'Delivered in 3 working days. Returns within 14 days.'

Activity. Build five product pages and have two peers try to buy.

Standard to meet. Peers find price, delivery and returns within 10 seconds.

Module 3 Stock and suppliers 16 hApply

Stock is cash sitting on a shelf. Too little loses sales; too much traps money.

  • Count stock weekly and compare with the shop's records.
  • Reorder point = daily sales x supplier lead time + safety stock.
  • Track cost per item including delivery to you.
  • Mark slow sellers and discount before they become dead stock.
Worked example. Sales 4 per day, lead time 5 days, safety stock 8: reorder when 28 remain.

Activity. Set reorder points for your five products.

Standard to meet. Counts match records within 2%; reorder rules written.

Module 4 Payments, delivery and returns 16 hApply

Choose simple, trusted ways to be paid and to deliver.

  • Use bank or UPI-style payment pages and trusted partners; never store card numbers.
  • Compare couriers on price, speed, tracking and damage record.
  • Set a returns policy you can afford and describe it clearly.
  • Reconcile payments with orders every week.
Worked example. Courier A EUR 4.20 in 2 days; courier B EUR 3.50 in 4 days. For orders over EUR 40 offer free delivery on A.

Activity. Write delivery and returns rules and test the process with one order.

Standard to meet. Rules published; test order delivered; reconciliation done.

Module 5 Customer service 14 hCreate

Service wins repeat buyers. Answer fast, politely and with a clear next step.

  • Answer within one working day; say when you will update.
  • Use short templates for the ten commonest questions.
  • When something is wrong, apologise once, fix it, and say what changed.
  • Keep a log of problems and review it monthly.
Worked example. Template: 'Thank you for your message. Your order 1042 left us on Monday and the tracking link is below. If it has not arrived by Friday, write to us and we will trace it.'

Activity. Write ten templates and role-play two hard messages.

Standard to meet. Each template has a clear next step; two role-plays reviewed.

Module 6 Get visitors and measure 20 hAnalyse

Traffic costs time or money. Choose a few channels and measure each.

  • Free channels: search-friendly titles, social posts, word of mouth, marketplace listings.
  • Paid: only with a budget cap and a target cost per order.
  • Track source of each order if you can.
  • Cost per order = spend divided by orders from that spend.
Worked example. EUR 30 on a social ad brings 6 orders: EUR 5 per order. Average margin per order EUR 9, so the ad earns EUR 4 per order.

Activity. Run two small channels for two weeks and compare.

Standard to meet. Spend, orders and cost per order tabulated.

Module 7 Run 20 orders 48 hApply

Practice is the course. Process orders with care and learn from every error.

  • Order log: date, order number, items, value, delivery, issue, fix.
  • Pack carefully; photograph packed orders for proof.
  • Review mistakes weekly and add a check to stop repeats.
  • Keep personal data only as long as needed.
Worked example. Week 3 log shows two wrong-size orders. New check added: read size aloud when picking. No repeats in week 4.

Activity. Complete 20 orders with a full log.

Standard to meet. 20 orders logged; every issue has a fix.

Module 8 Report, decide and plan 14 hEvaluate

Report to yourself like a manager: weekly numbers, decisions, next steps.

  • Weekly report: visits, conversion, average order, returns, margin.
  • Change one thing at a time and compare.
  • Three lessons learned.
  • Decide: grow, fix or stop.
Worked example. Page change raised conversion from 2.5% to 3.4% in two weeks; plan: replicate on all products.

Activity. Present at a review room; give two peers feedback.

Standard to meet. Decision supported by numbers.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: your record, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Conversion rate for 5 orders from 200 visits?

Model answer: 2.5%. (Covered in module 1: The order journey. To pass that module: Journey complete; four numbers calculated correctly.)

Q2. Reorder point formula?

Model answer: Daily sales x lead time + safety stock. (Covered in module 3: Stock and suppliers. To pass that module: Counts match records within 2%; reorder rules written.)

Q3. What must a product page show quickly?

Model answer: Price, delivery and returns. (Covered in module 2: Product pages that sell. To pass that module: Peers find price, delivery and returns within 10 seconds.)

Q4. Why never store card numbers?

Model answer: It creates security and legal risk; use trusted payment partners. (Covered in module 4: Payments, delivery and returns. To pass that module: Rules published; test order delivered; reconciliation done.)

Q5. What belongs in an order log?

Model answer: Date, order number, items, value, delivery, issue, fix. (Covered in module 7: Run 20 orders. To pass that module: 20 orders logged; every issue has a fix.)

Q6. Cost per order for EUR 40 spend and 8 orders?

Model answer: EUR 5. (Covered in module 6: Get visitors and measure. To pass that module: Spend, orders and cost per order tabulated.)

Q7. Why reconcile payments weekly?

Model answer: To catch missing or wrong payments early. (Covered in module 4: Payments, delivery and returns. To pass that module: Rules published; test order delivered; reconciliation done.)

Q8. How soon should you answer a customer?

Model answer: Within one working day, with a next step. (Covered in module 5: Customer service. To pass that module: Each template has a clear next step; two role-plays reviewed.)

Q9. Why change one thing at a time?

Model answer: So you know what caused the result. (Covered in module 8: Report, decide and plan. To pass that module: Decision supported by numbers.)

Q10. What does average order value mean?

Model answer: Total revenue divided by number of orders. (Covered in module 1: The order journey. To pass that module: Journey complete; four numbers calculated correctly.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
