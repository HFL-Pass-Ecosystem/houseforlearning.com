flagship course

Textiles and Fashion Sustainability

Measure and cut the water, energy, chemical and waste footprint of fabric and garments, and build circular, fair-work fashion products.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5 Specialist8 modules · 40 h lessons plus practice

Measure and cut the water, energy, chemical and waste footprint of fabric and garments, and build circular, fair-work fashion products.

  • Define impact categories along the fashion chain.
  • Apply measurement and reduction steps.
  • Analyse a product life cycle.
  • Evaluate claims, labels and suppliers.
  • Create a circular product plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Measure the footprint of one product, set reduction targets and design a circular version with a costed plan.

Condition

About 12 weeks part-time. A product sample, supplier data or a unit visit, a mentor.

Standard

Measurements have units and sources, targets are dated, and every green claim is supported by evidence.

Who it is for: Designers, merchandisers, unit supervisors, artisan co-operative leaders. Requirements: Textiles and Sustainable Manufacturing or equivalent. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Where the impact sits 4 hDefine
  • Fibre growing, spinning, dyeing, making, transport, use, disposal.
  • Water, energy, chemicals, waste and working conditions.
  • The biggest impact is often in dyeing and in use.
  • Draw a map of one product chain.
Worked example. A cotton shirt: most water in growing, most chemicals in dyeing, most energy in washing and drying over its life.

Activity. Map one product chain with the main impacts.

Standard to meet. Map shows every step and one impact each.

Module 2 Measure what you use 6 hApply
  • Litres of water, units of power, kilograms of chemical and waste per kilogram of fabric.
  • Meter and weigh; do not guess.
  • Daily log and monthly summary.
  • Compare with a simple target.
Worked example. A dye house uses 120 litres per kilogram; the target is 90.

Activity. Measure one process for a week.

Standard to meet. Log has units, dates and a summary.

Module 3 Cut water, energy and chemicals 6 hApply
  • Reuse rinse water; fix leaks.
  • Heat recovery and efficient motors.
  • Choose safer dyes and fixing agents.
  • Treat effluent before release.
Worked example. Counter-current rinsing cuts water use by about a third in one trial.

Activity. Write a reduction plan with three actions.

Standard to meet. Each action has a saving, cost and owner.

Module 4 Waste, offcuts and circular design 6 hCreate
  • Cut patterns to reduce offcuts.
  • Reuse offcuts in small products.
  • Design for repair and for recycling: single fibres, simple trims.
  • Take-back and resale.
Worked example. Offcuts from kurtas are turned into bags and patchwork covers.

Activity. Design one circular product from waste.

Standard to meet. Design shows material source, steps and end-of-life plan.

Module 5 Fair work and artisan livelihoods 4 hEvaluate
  • Fair pay, hours and safe rooms.
  • Home workers and women in the chain.
  • Supplier code and visits.
  • Record grievances and fix them.
Worked example. A code of conduct is signed by suppliers and checked on a visit.

Activity. Write a supplier code and a visit checklist.

Standard to meet. Code covers pay, hours, safety and complaints.

Module 6 Claims, labels and standards 4 hEvaluate
  • Avoid vague words such as eco-friendly without proof.
  • Use recognised labels only if certified.
  • Keep test reports and records.
  • Say what is true: percent recycled, litres saved.
Worked example. Say 40% recycled cotton, with the test report on file.

Activity. Audit five claims.

Standard to meet. Each claim is proved or rewritten.

Module 7 Costs, prices and markets 5 hAnalyse
  • Cost of materials, labour and process changes.
  • Price for fair pay and margin.
  • Markets for sustainable products: online, craft fairs, institutions.
  • Tell the story with facts.
Worked example. A 12% price premium is accepted by buyers who see the saving data.

Activity. Cost and price one product.

Standard to meet. Numbers add up; premium is justified with facts.

Module 8 Capstone: circular product plan 5 hCreate
  • Choose one product.
  • Measure, target, redesign.
  • Cost and claim.
  • Present.
Worked example. A plan to cut water by 25% and reuse 60% of offcuts within a year.

Activity. Present.

Standard to meet. Plan has measures, targets, costs and proved claims.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
MeasurementMissing or unclearMeets the stated checkUnits and sources
ClaimsGuessingEvidence keptAll proved
RecordsGapsCompleteComplete and reviewed by a peer
Safety and ethicsOverlookedFollowedFollowed and taught to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Where is the water impact largest for cotton?

Model answer: Growing the fibre. (Covered in module 1: Where the impact sits. To pass that module: Map shows every step and one impact each.)

Q2. Why measure per kilogram?

Model answer: It lets you compare days and batches fairly. (Covered in module 2: Measure what you use. To pass that module: Log has units, dates and a summary.)

Q3. Name two ways to cut water use.

Model answer: Reuse rinse water and fix leaks. (Covered in module 3: Cut water, energy and chemicals. To pass that module: Each action has a saving, cost and owner.)

Q4. What does design for recycling mean?

Model answer: Making items that can be taken apart and reprocessed. (Covered in module 4: Waste, offcuts and circular design. To pass that module: Design shows material source, steps and end-of-life plan.)

Q5. Why visit suppliers?

Model answer: To check the code is followed. (Covered in module 5: Fair work and artisan livelihoods. To pass that module: Code covers pay, hours, safety and complaints.)

Q6. Why avoid vague green words?

Model answer: They can mislead and break rules. (Covered in module 6: Claims, labels and standards. To pass that module: Each claim is proved or rewritten.)

Q7. How justify a price premium?

Model answer: With true, verified benefits. (Covered in module 7: Costs, prices and markets. To pass that module: Numbers add up; premium is justified with facts.)

Q8. What proves a green claim?

Model answer: Test data and records. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: circular product plan. To pass that module: Plan has measures, targets, costs and proved claims.)

Q9. Why record test reports?

Model answer: To prove claims. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why design for repair?

Model answer: It keeps products in use longer. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L5 Specialist. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know chain steps.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain impacts.
  3. L3 Foundation. Help measure.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Run a reduction plan alone.
  5. L5 Specialist. Lead circular product lines.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead sustainability for a brand.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set sector standards.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Chain steps

Name the 7 steps of the textile chain from fibre to garment end of life in order, with one impact for each step.

Speaker notes. Teacher asks the learner to trace a cotton shirt and checks the order. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Impact explained

Explain 4 main impacts (water, energy, chemicals and waste) of making a T-shirt, using one number for each from a given source.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the numbers are tied to a source. Covered in module 1, Where the impact sits.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Help measure

Measure water, energy and offcut waste for one batch with a meter or scale and record the values in a table with units.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the meter readings and units. Covered in module 2, Measure what you use.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Reduction plan alone

Run a reduction plan alone that cuts one measured input by 10 percent over 4 weeks, with weekly readings and actions logged.

Speaker notes. Teacher compares the first and last readings. Covered in module 3, Cut water, energy and chemicals.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Circular product lines

Lead a circular product line that turns offcuts into at least 3 new items, with a design for repair note and cost per item.

Speaker notes. Teacher inspects one product and its repair note.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Brand sustainability lead

Lead a brand plan with 3 targets and check every green claim against a test report before use. Check local rules with an expert on labels.

Speaker notes. Teacher hides one claim without a report and checks it is rejected.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Sector standards

Set sector standards for measurement, fair work and claims, and review 3 firms against them with written scores.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks scores are backed by records.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
