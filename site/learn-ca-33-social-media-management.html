Social Media Management

Run a pilot of social Media Management in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small social Media Management offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: content, creative and marketing work. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the words of social media management 6 hUnderstand

A social media manager plans, posts and watches content for a business.

  • Learn the terms: platform, post, story, reach, engagement, follower, hashtag, call to action, content calendar.
  • Reach is how many people saw a post; engagement is how many acted.
  • Each platform has its own style and audience.
  • Follow five local business accounts and note what they post and how people respond.
  • Calculate an engagement rate as actions divided by reach.
  • Compare two platforms by who uses them and what they like.
  • Describe the tone of each of five accounts in three words.
Worked example. A café posts a photo of a new pastry with the line 'Fresh at 8 a.m. tomorrow.' If 500 people saw it (reach) and 40 liked or commented, engagement is 40 / 500 = 8%. Likes and comments show engagement.

Activity. Define ten terms and review five accounts.

Standard to meet. Task: review five accounts. Condition: from public pages. Standard: each has audience, post types and one idea to improve.

Module 2 Explain the workflow from plan to report 6 hApply

Social media work follows steps. Name them to stay on schedule.

  • The steps: goals, audience, content plan, create, schedule, post, reply, measure, report.
  • Set one goal at a time: more enquiries, more visits or more followers.
  • A content calendar lists date, platform, topic, text, image and status.
  • Agree who approves posts and how fast.
  • Write each goal as a number, a measure and a date.
  • Write down the name of each approver and the reply time you expect from them.
  • Plan to create a week of posts in one sitting.
Worked example. Goal: 20 enquiries in a month. Plan: three posts a week with one clear action each, so 3 x 4 = 12 posts. If each post brought 2 enquiries that would be 24, but check the real numbers at the end of the month.

Activity. Build a four-week content calendar for a pretend shop.

Standard to meet. Task: build a calendar. Condition: from a brief. Standard: every row has date, platform, topic, text, image and approver.

Module 3 Create and schedule posts to a standard 10 hApply

Now you make. Write clear posts that serve the goal.

  • Put the main point first. Use short words.
  • One post, one message, one action.
  • Add descriptive text for images so people using screen readers understand them.
  • Check spelling, links and facts before scheduling.
  • Write the first line so it stands alone.
  • Check image sizes for each platform.
  • Test two posting times and compare the results.
Worked example. Before: 'Big news!!!' After: 'New winter hours: open 8 to 4, Tuesday to Saturday. Visit us.' The new post gives the hours, days and action. A week of 3 posts checked for 10 minutes each takes 30 minutes.

Activity. Create and schedule a week of posts and ask a friend to find mistakes.

Standard to meet. Task: create one week of posts. Condition: from a brief in three hours. Standard: each post has one message and action, image text is provided and no errors remain.

Module 4 Follow Canadian rules for social media marketing 8 hEvaluate

Posts that sell are advertising. Rules apply.

  • Advertising must be truthful. Do not post fake reviews or false claims.
  • Paid or gifted endorsements must be clearly disclosed.
  • Commercial messages by email or text need consent and an unsubscribe; check the federal anti-spam rules.
  • Contests have rules; in some provinces they need extra steps. Check before you run one. Respect copyright and ask before using a person's photo.
  • Keep a record of consent for every email address.
  • Draft disclosure wording such as 'Sponsored' or 'Gifted'.
  • Check the platform's own contest rules as well as the provincial rules.
Worked example. A client wants to repost a customer photo. You ask the customer for permission first and credit them. Save the reply with the date.

Activity. Build a table of rule, who sets it, what you must do and where you checked, for ten post types.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: using official public pages. Standard: each row has a dated source; unclear items say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.

Module 5 Price your service and check it pays 8 hApply

You are paid for steady results and time.

  • Time includes planning, creating, posting, replying and reporting.
  • Compare hourly, monthly package and per-post pricing.
  • A monthly package needs a clear list: posts, platforms, replies and report.
  • Track hours per client to see if the price is fair.
  • Time each task: plan, create, reply and report.
  • Work out what one extra post or one extra round of replies costs you in time.
  • Review the price after the first month using your time log.
Worked example. A package of 12 posts and a report takes 14 hours at $45: 14 x 45 = $630 a month, or $630 / 12 = $52.50 per post. If replies add 3 hours, the true cost is 17 x 45 = $765, so raise the price or limit replies.

Activity. Time a week of work and set a monthly package.

Standard to meet. Task: price a monthly package. Condition: from your time log. Standard: hours, rate and package list are shown and a mentor can follow the numbers.

Module 6 Measure results and report 6 hCreate

Numbers show what is working.

  • Choose a few measures tied to the goal: reach, clicks, enquiries.
  • Compare this month with last month.
  • Report in plain words: what happened, why, what we do next.
  • Do not claim the number of followers equals sales.
  • Write the one number tied to the goal.
  • Make a table with this month, last month and the change.
  • Describe two possible causes and test one next month.
Worked example. Posts with a photo of the product got 3 times more clicks than text-only posts. If text-only posts got 20 clicks and photo posts got 60, then 60 / 20 = 3. Next month: more product photos.

Activity. Collect a month of data and write a one-page report.

Standard to meet. Task: write a monthly report. Condition: from real or practice data. Standard: three measures, a cause for each change and two next steps.

Module 7 Protect accounts, people and reputation 6 hEvaluate

A manager holds the keys to a brand voice. Be careful.

  • Use strong passwords and two-step sign-in; give access by role and remove it when people leave.
  • Reply to comments politely. For complaints, move to a private channel and fix the problem.
  • Do not share customer information. Privacy law applies.
  • Write a short agreement: scope, fees, approvals, who owns the accounts and content, and how to end it.
  • List who has access to each account and what role they hold.
  • Prepare reply templates for five common comments.
  • Write a rule to pause scheduled posts when bad news breaks.
Worked example. A customer complains in a comment. You reply: 'Sorry about that. Please message us your order number so we can fix it.' You agree a reply target with the client, such as one business day.

Activity. Write an agreement and a response guide for ten comments.

Standard to meet. Task: produce an agreement and a guide. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: scope, fees, approvals and ownership are in plain words.

Module 8 Run a real account for 60 days and decide 8 hCreate

Now you run it, measure and decide.

  • Set a goal for one real or practice account.
  • Record posts, hours, results and feedback each week.
  • Ask the client what changed.
  • Choose: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Calculate cost per post.
  • Compare the first 30 days with the last 30 days.
  • Ask the client how many enquiries came from social media.
Worked example. After 60 days: 24 posts, 30 hours, enquiries up from 6 to 14 a month. At $45 an hour, 30 hours is $1,350, or 1,350 / 24 = $56.25 per post. Enquiries rose by 14 - 6 = 8.

Activity. Run the pilot and present a 12-month plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a 60-day account pilot. Condition: real client and agreement. Standard: calendar kept, report written and the decision uses numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
