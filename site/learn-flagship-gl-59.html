flagship course

Car Charging Stations

Plan, site and run a small public or shared EV charging point: demand, power, safety, pricing, uptime and users.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner34:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain charger types, connectors and grid limits.
  • Apply Choose a site and size the supply.
  • Analyse Estimate demand, usage and revenue.
  • Evaluate Judge safety, cost, uptime and fairness.
  • Create Write a site plan and operations sheet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan one charging point or small hub with site survey, power sizing, safety plan, tariff, maintenance plan and 12-month cash view.

Condition

A computer, a map, a spreadsheet, two peers and a licensed electrician mentor. No live installation work is done by learners.

Standard

Power and demand maths is correct; protective devices and earthing are listed and approved by a licensed person; accessibility is planned; uptime target and tariff shown; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Site hosts, shop and hotel owners, utilities assistants, installers. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Charger types, speeds and connectors 3 hUnderstand

Chargers differ in power, speed and cost. Choose by how long drivers will stay.

  • AC points (3 to 22 kW) suit long stays such as homes, workplaces and hotels; the car converts AC to DC.
  • DC fast chargers (50 kW and above) suit quick stops on routes.
  • Connector types and standards depend on region; check which cars you must support.
  • Smart chargers can share power and report faults.
Worked example. A shopping mall installs 7 kW points because shoppers stay about two hours and add 14 kWh each. A highway site installs 60 kW units because drivers want 30 kWh in about 30 minutes.

Activity. Compare three charger types in a table with power, time to add 30 kWh, cost range and best site.

Standard to meet. Check that table values are correct and sourced.

Module 2 Choosing a site 4 hApply

The right place and the right grid connection decide success.

  • Visible, safe, well lit, accessible, near amenities such as food and toilets.
  • Distance to the electricity supply and spare transformer capacity.
  • Permission from the land owner, the utility and the local authority.
  • Space for growth, queuing and turning.
Worked example. A site 20 m from the main panel saves cable cost; another 300 m away needs trenching and a new transformer, doubling cost.

Activity. Complete a mock site survey for two sites and score five criteria.

Standard to meet. Justify scores in writing.

Module 3 Power sizing and load management 5 hApply

Do not overload the supply.

  • Total kW = number of chargers x kW each x diversity factor (the share likely to be in use at the same time).
  • Compare with supply capacity; use load management when limited.
  • Plan growth with spare conduit.
  • Include power for lights, screens and equipment.
Worked example. Four 7 kW points on a 20 kW supply use load sharing so total draw never exceeds 20 kW; with all four in use each car gets about 5 kW.

Activity. Size a site and show the calculation.

Standard to meet. Check that the maths is correct and respect the supply limit.

Module 4 Demand and revenue 5 hAnalyse

Estimate real use before you invest.

  • Count nearby EVs and traffic; talk to likely users.
  • Utilisation = hours in use / hours open.
  • Revenue = kWh sold x price minus electricity cost and fees.
  • Test low, medium and high demand cases.
Worked example. At 15 per cent utilisation a 7 kW point sells about 25 kWh a day; at 5 per cent it sells about 8 kWh a day and may not break even.

Activity. Build a 12-month model with three demand cases.

Standard to meet. Show the currency and list assumptions.

Module 5 Safety and standards 5 hEvaluate

Protect users and property.

  • Residual current device (RCD), earthing, surge protection, emergency stop and a fire plan.
  • Weather protection, cable management and trip-free layout.
  • Regular inspection and testing by qualified staff.
  • Clear signs and instructions.
Worked example. A charger fails an earth test and is locked out until a licensed electrician repairs it; the fault is logged and reported.

Activity. List the safety measures for your site.

Standard to meet. Obtain approval of the list from a licensed person.

Module 6 Pricing and payment 4 hEvaluate

Make prices clear and fair.

  • Per kWh, per minute, session fee and idle fee.
  • Display the price before charging begins.
  • Use payment methods approved in your place.
  • Offer a way for users without a smartphone.
Worked example. An idle fee after 30 minutes frees the bay for other drivers, and users are told by a sign and a message. A price of 12 INR per kWh covers a 7 INR per kWh electricity cost and fees (prices are estimates; check your market).

Activity. Design a tariff and test it with three examples.

Standard to meet. Solve the examples correctly.

Module 7 Operations and uptime 4 hApply

Chargers must work when drivers arrive.

  • Remote monitoring and a fault response target.
  • Maintenance schedule and spare parts.
  • A helpline number printed on the unit.
  • Record faults and fixes.
Worked example. A target to fix faults within 24 hours is met 95 per cent of the time by keeping spare connectors on site.

Activity. Write an operations sheet with targets.

Standard to meet. Set measurable targets.

Module 8 Inclusion and the site plan 4 hCreate

Everyone should be able to charge.

  • Step-free access, bay width, lighting and a call button.
  • Multi-language instructions and simple screens.
  • Share the plan with the community.
  • Review after six months.
Worked example. Wider bays and a call button let wheelchair users charge independently. A review at six months adds two more bays after queues exceed 15 minutes.

Activity. Finish the plan and a review date.

Standard to meet. Write the plan so that peers can follow it and see accessibility steps.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: site survey, sizing sheet, safety plan, tariff, maintenance plan, cash view. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Power sizingErrorsCorrectCorrect with growth plan
SafetyMissingListedListed and approved
Business viewNo numbers12-month modelModel with sensitivity
Site dataPower assumedSupply capacity from the utility letter or meter and load recordedLoad profile logged across a week and the hub sized to it
Grid, electrical and planning rulesPermits not checkedGrid connection, electrical code and planning consent for the place are written downSteps checked with the utility and the answers filed

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is load sharing?

Model answer: Dividing supply among chargers. (Covered in module 3: Power sizing and load management. To pass that module: Check that the maths is correct and respect the supply limit.)

Q2. Which suits long stays?

Model answer: AC chargers. (Covered in module 1: Charger types, speeds and connectors. To pass that module: Check that table values are correct and sourced.)

Q3. Utilisation formula?

Model answer: Hours in use / hours open. (Covered in module 4: Demand and revenue. To pass that module: Show the currency and list assumptions.)

Q4. Name a safety measure.

Model answer: RCD, earthing or emergency stop. (Covered in module 5: Safety and standards. To pass that module: Obtain approval of the list from a licensed person.)

Q5. Why display price?

Model answer: Fairness and trust. (Covered in module 6: Pricing and payment. To pass that module: Solve the examples correctly.)

Q6. What is idle fee?

Model answer: Charge for occupying a bay after charging. (Covered in module 6: Pricing and payment. To pass that module: Solve the examples correctly.)

Q7. Who approves electrics?

Model answer: Licensed electrician. (Covered in module 5: Safety and standards. To pass that module: Obtain approval of the list from a licensed person.)

Q8. Why monitor remotely?

Model answer: Quick faults response. (Covered in module 7: Operations and uptime. To pass that module: Set measurable targets.)

Q9. Name an access feature.

Model answer: Step-free route or wide bays. (Covered in module 8: Inclusion and the site plan. To pass that module: Write the plan so that peers can follow it and see accessibility steps.)

Q10. Revenue formula?

Model answer: kWh x price minus cost. (Covered in module 4: Demand and revenue. To pass that module: Show the currency and list assumptions.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Utility and safety rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global
