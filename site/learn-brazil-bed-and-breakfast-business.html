Bed and Breakfast Business

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, start and run bed and breakfast business in São Paulo: serve a leisure or business guest, deliver a guest room and a morning meal, keep records, and show a working month of numbers.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. Own phone or computer, real customers or a real practice site, a peer and a mentor for the final review. Local rules and licences checked against official sources.

Standard

Every module standard is met; all claims are true and backed by records; peers and a mentor mark the evidence 'Meets' or better.

Who it is for: students, graduates, career changers, owners, workers and returners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, tracker, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know your customer and market 4 hAnalyse

Every sound business in bed and breakfast business begins with one clear customer and one clear problem. In São Paulo and the wider state, demand is real but crowded, so a small, well-described customer group wins before a broad one.

  • In bed and breakfast business: Guests: leisure, business and family; ask what they want from a stay.
  • Write the customer in one sentence: a leisure or business guest. Add the place, the moment of need and what they use today.
  • List five rivals or substitutes within reach. For each, record price, strength, weakness and how people reach it.
  • Interview at least eight real customers with open questions. Record exact words, not your summary.
  • Count the market in units: how many room-nights could one area buy in a month? Use two public sources and your own count, then take the lower figure.
  • Choose one first segment and say why the others wait.
Worked example. Weak: 'Everyone needs a guest room and a morning meal.' Strong: 'Our first customer is a leisure or business guest within 8 km of the centre, who today pays R$ 308 per room-night and complains about delay. We target 54 room-nights a month by month three.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: ten customers are interviewed for bed and breakfast business. Seven say price, two say speed, one says trust. Question: which first message do you write, and which interview record backs it? Model answer: lead with the room-night price and speed promise, keep trust proof as the second line, and cite the seven price answers by date.

Activity. Write the one-sentence customer, a five-row rival table and eight interview records. Share them with a peer for a challenge round.

Standard to meet. Define a specific customer, build a rival table with prices, and trace every claim to an interview or a source.

Module 2 Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil 4 hUnderstand

Brazil asks every trader to be visible and accountable. Know which register, tax regime and sector rules apply before the first sale, and check each against the current official text.

  • In bed and breakfast business: Lodging follows local registration, tax and safety rules; guest registration duties apply.
  • Choose a legal form: micro-entrepreneur (MEI) only if your activity appears on the official MEI list, or a company registered with a CNPJ. Regulated activities need their sector authorisation first. Check the current limits and lists on the official portal.
  • Learn the tax regime that fits your size and write down which taxes you will collect and pay, and when.
  • Find the sector rules for bed and breakfast business: licences, inspections, professional registers. List each with the office that issues it and the renewal date.
  • Personal data of customers falls under Brazil's data protection law (LGPD). Collect only what you need, say why, keep it safe, and name who answers questions.
  • Consumer law gives buyers clear rights on information, returns and complaints. Put your terms in plain Portuguese.
Worked example. Checklist for a new bed and breakfast business trader: 1) legal form (CNPJ, or MEI only if your activity is on the official list); 2) city operating licence; 3) sector licence or register; 4) tax set-up; 5) privacy notice; 6) written terms. Each has an owner and a date.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a neighbour says registration can wait until the first R$ 1.400 of sales. Question: what do you do? Model answer: do not delay; write the compliance table first, confirm the register, tax and sector licence with the official office, and start only where the table shows no open blocker.

Activity. Build your own six-line compliance table for your first location. Mark what is done, what is pending and who confirms it.

Standard to meet. Cite an official source for every line, assume nothing, and date every pending item. Local law always wins over this course.

Module 3 Design the offer 4 hCreate

An offer states what the customer gets, how well, by when and at what price. For a guest room and a morning meal, the offer must make the quality visible and the next step easy.

  • In bed and breakfast business: Sell a stay: room, breakfast, welcome and local tips.
  • Describe the core offer in three lines: what is delivered (a guest room and a morning meal), the standard it meets, and the time it takes.
  • Add one proof for each promise: a sample, a test result, a guarantee or a customer record.
  • Price from cost upward and from customer value downward, then pick a price inside both.
  • Write a simple one-page terms sheet: what is included, what is extra, how to cancel.
  • Create two options at most, so choice stays easy.
Worked example. Offer: 'A guest room and a morning meal, delivered within agreed time, checked against a written standard, price R$ 280 per room-night. Proof: a dated record the customer can check and sign. Changes or cancellation by written agreement before work begins.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a customer asks for a discount from R$ 280 to R$ 252 per room-night. Question: decide with numbers. Model answer: contribution falls from R$ 190 to R$ 162 per room-night; offer an extra of value (for example faster service) before cutting price, and accept only if the lower contribution still clears break-even.

Longer notes. Decide who your house is for, such as visiting consultants, couples on weekends or cultural travellers, and set one standard room-night price that includes breakfast. Cost a night from breakfast food, laundry and cleaning, water, energy and small amenities. Compare your price with similar stays in your neighbourhood and with a hostel and a hotel. Photograph each room honestly and write house rules on noise and check-in. Confirm lodging registration and fire-safety rules with the city before the first guest sleeps over. Put the breakfast menu, check-in times and parking details in a short welcome note that you send the day before arrival, so guests know what to expect and your first message sets the standard.

Extra worked example. A house with 5 rooms offers 150 room-nights a month. One room-night sells for R$ 280. Variable cost is R$ 28 for breakfast, R$ 42 for laundry and cleaning and R$ 20 for water, energy and amenities, so R$ 28 + R$ 42 + R$ 20 = R$ 90. Margin is R$ 190, or 67,9 percent. Fixed cost is R$ 6.000 a month, so break-even is 6.000 / 190 = 31,58, rounded up to 32 room-nights. Cost lines are worked-example figures, not market data. Ninety room-nights leave R$ 11.100 after the cleaner and rent.

Activity. Write the offer card, the proof list and the terms sheet. Test them with three customers and note every point where they hesitated.

Standard to meet. Check that promise, proof and price are consistent, fit the terms on one page, and record three customer reactions.

Module 4 Run the work step by step 5 hApply

Good operations make results repeatable. For bed and breakfast business, write each step so a new person could follow it on their first day, and measure each step.

  • In bed and breakfast business: Housekeeping cycle: check-out, clean, inspect, reset, check-in.
  • Map the process from first contact to paid and closed. Typical stages: enquiry, quote, preparation, delivery of the room-night, check, payment, follow-up.
  • Name the key resource at each stage: clean rooms and a clear booking system. Note what happens if it is missing.
  • Write a one-page standard procedure per stage: who, what, tool, time, record.
  • Choose three measures: speed, quality (errors per 100) and customer rating. Review them every week.
  • Fix the cause of a defect, not the symptom. Ask 'why' five times and record the answer.
Worked example. Stage record: 'Prepare. Owner: lead. Time: 30 min. Tool: checklist. Record: signed sheet. Defect found on 1 of 22 room-nights last week; cause: missing check on clean rooms and a clear booking system; fix: add check at start, review next Friday.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: of 90 room-nights last month, 5 had a defect traced to clean rooms and a clear booking system. Question: what is the defect rate and what is your first action? Model answer: about 6 defects per 100; stop the cause (add a check at the start), correct affected customers, log the cause, and re-measure next week.

Activity. Draw the process map and write procedures for the two riskiest stages. Run each once and time it.

Standard to meet. Write procedures a stranger can follow, record weekly numbers for each measure, and trace one defect to a root cause.

Module 5 Safety, quality and risk 4 hEvaluate

The most serious risk in this field is bad reviews from small failures. A calm, written risk routine protects people first and the business second.

  • In bed and breakfast business: Fire exits, wiring and food hygiene need checks.
  • List ten things that could go wrong. Score each for how likely (1 to 5) and how serious (1 to 5). Multiply and sort.
  • For the top five, choose a control: remove the hazard, replace it, guard it, train people, or use protective gear, in that order of preference.
  • Write who does what in an emergency, and rehearse it once a quarter.
  • Keep an incident and near-miss log. Near-misses are free lessons.
  • Check that insurance and legal duties match the risk you actually carry.
Worked example. Risk row: 'Hazard: bad reviews from small failures. Likelihood 3, severity 5, score 15. Control: written check before each room-night, trained staff, log sheet. Owner: site lead. Review monthly.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a near-miss occurs: bad reviews from small failures nearly happened but no one was hurt. Question: what do you do in the next 24 hours? Model answer: make safe, record the event, ask the five whys, add or tighten one control, brief the team, and set a review date; near-misses count as lessons, not blame.

Activity. Build a ten-row risk register and a one-page emergency plan. Run a ten-minute drill with a peer and note two improvements.

Standard to meet. Rank controls by preference for the top risks, run the drill, and start the log. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.

Module 6 People, skills and service 4 hApply

People deliver the promise. Define roles, train to a standard and give feedback in the open.

  • In bed and breakfast business: Train hosts in welcome and complaint recovery.
  • Write a role card for each person: purpose, five tasks, the standard for each, and who to ask for help.
  • Train by showing, doing with support, then doing alone with a check. Record sign-off per task.
  • Set service rules for answering customers: reply times, tone, how to apologise and fix.
  • Hold a ten-minute team check each day: yesterday's problem, today's plan, one risk.
  • Build a growth path: what a new person can do in week 1, month 3 and month 12.
Worked example. Training record: 'Task: process a complaint. Shown 2 May; done with support 4 May; done alone 8 May, checked: apology, fix, follow-up call. Signed by lead.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a new hire completes training but a customer complaint shows a skipped step. Question: whose gap is it? Model answer: first the system: check the role card, the training sign-off and the checklist; then coach the person and fix the document so the step cannot be skipped.

Longer notes. Teach the housekeeper a fixed room routine: strip, wipe, restock, vacuum and a final look from the doorway. Put a laminated checklist in the cleaning cupboard and walk the first rooms together. Agree who prepares breakfast, who answers late arrivals and who handles repairs. Practise a calm reply to a complaint about noise or a dirty bathroom. Keep a written rota with pay and days off, and confirm which employment rules apply to a housekeeper who also serves breakfast. Walk through a bad review together with the team once a month and ask what could have prevented it. Staff who help find the fix are far more likely to follow the new routine than those who are only told.

Extra worked example. Of 90 room-nights, about 60 are turnovers. A trained cleaner turns a room in 35 minutes instead of 50, saving 15 minutes x 60 = 900 minutes, or 15 hours. At R$ 20 per hour that is R$ 300. Training takes 3 hours, which costs R$ 60. Avoiding one free replacement night after a cleanliness complaint protects R$ 280. The hours, rates and counts here are worked-example figures, not market data. Net gain is R$ 300 + R$ 280 - R$ 60 = R$ 520.

Activity. Write two role cards and a training plan for one new hire. Role-play a hard customer conversation twice and review.

Standard to meet. Write role cards with measurable standards, show the sign-off steps in training, and write the service rules in plain words.

Module 7 Money: cost, price and break-even 5 hAnalyse

A business lives or dies on a few numbers. Know your contribution per unit, your fixed costs and the volume you need.

  • In bed and breakfast business: Occupancy times price; seasons change cash.
  • Contribution per room-night = price minus variable cost. Fixed costs are paid whether you sell or not.
  • Break-even units = fixed cost divided by contribution. Plan to pass it by a safe margin.
  • Make a 12-month cash plan. Cash timing matters as much as profit: customers may pay late, suppliers may want money first.
  • Keep money for business and money for the owner apart.
  • Test the plan: what if sales fall by a quarter, or cost rises by a tenth?
Worked example. Worked example, all figures in reais: price R$ 280, variable cost R$ 90, contribution R$ 190 per room-night. Fixed cost R$ 6.000 a month. Break-even = R$ 6.000 / R$ 190 = 31,58, so 32 room-nights a month. At 90 room-nights a month, profit = R$ 190 x 90 - R$ 6.000 = R$ 11.100. These are teaching figures that belong to this example only; later examples and the case study use their own figures. Use your own.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: sales fall to 67 room-nights a month. Question: what is the monthly result? Model answer: R$ 190 x 67 - R$ 6.000 = R$ 6.730; compare with break-even of 32 room-nights and decide whether to cut fixed cost or raise volume first. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build your own table: price, variable cost, contribution, fixed cost, break-even and a downside case at 75% of sales. Show it to a peer or mentor.

Standard to meet. Calculate correctly, give every figure a source or mark it as an assumption, and show a downside case.

Module 8 Grow, measure and renew 4 hEvaluate

Growth should follow proof. Measure, learn, then add; and keep returning to the customer.

  • In bed and breakfast business: Grow with direct bookings and reviews.
  • Pick one growth lever at a time: new customers, a higher repeat rate, a better price, or a new offer.
  • Set one measurable target for 90 days, such as 'raise repeat customers from 20% to 30%'.
  • Ask each customer how they found you, and record it. Spend more where the record shows results.
  • Review monthly: numbers, defects, complaints, cash. Write three decisions each time.
  • Plan the renewal of licences, equipment and skills a year ahead.
Worked example. 90-day plan: 'Lever: repeat customers. Action: follow-up call after every room-night. Target: repeat rate from 20% to 30%. Check: weekly count. Review date: day 45 and day 90.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: you can add 18 room-nights a month by one of two levers: a follow-up call to past customers or a price rise of R$ 28 to R$ 308. Question: how do you choose? Model answer: test the cheaper reversible lever first (the call), measure for 30 days, and only then try the price change, so you can tell which one worked.

Longer notes. Record every booking with source, nights, room, price and review score. Calculate occupancy by weekday and by month and look for the empty nights. Test a midweek offer for visiting workers and a weekend offer for couples. Ask guests who liked the stay to leave a written review and to book direct next time. Review prices each season against what similar houses charge and against your costs. Keep a notebook of guest requests, such as an extra pillow, a late taxi or a quiet room, and turn the repeats into standard services, because small touches are what guests mention in reviews.

Extra worked example. This month you sold 90 room-nights out of 150, an occupancy of 60 percent. Revenue is 90 x R$ 280 = R$ 25.200 and variable cost is 90 x R$ 90 = R$ 8.100. After R$ 6.000 fixed cost, profit is R$ 11.100. The side scenario is a worked-example figure, not market data. A midweek offer fills 15 more room-nights: 15 x R$ 190 = R$ 2.850, so profit becomes R$ 13.950.

Activity. Write a 90-day plan with one target, three actions and a review date. Present it in five minutes and take questions.

Standard to meet. Set a measurable, dated target, break the actions down small enough to start this week, and put the review date in the calendar.

case study

A case to work through

Situation. the owner runs a five-room bed and breakfast in Sao Paulo that can offer 150 room-nights a month. She charges R$ 280 per room-night with breakfast, and laundry, cleaning, breakfast food and energy cost about R$ 90. Her fixed costs are R$ 6.000 a month for rent, insurance, internet and a part-time cleaner. Last month she sold 90 room-nights. A travel agent proposes a group of 20 room-nights next month at R$ 240 each, and a guest left a poor review about a dirty bathroom. The owner does not know her break-even occupancy or whether the group price is worth taking. She keeps bookings in a calendar on the wall and has no record of how guests found her or what each night really earned.

Task. Say what you would do first, next and last, and what record you would keep.

Model answer. First, margin per room-night is R$ 280 - R$ 90 = R$ 190, or 67,9 percent. Break-even is 6.000 / 190 = 31,58, so 32 room-nights, which is 21,3 percent of capacity. At 90, profit is 90 x R$ 190 - R$ 6.000 = R$ 11.100, with occupancy of 60 percent. Next, the group price gives R$ 240 - R$ 90 = R$ 150 per night. If the nights would otherwise be empty, 20 x R$ 150 = R$ 3.000 is extra profit. If they displace guests at R$ 280, each swap loses R$ 40. The 20-night group at R$ 240 is a worked-example scenario. Fix the bathroom checklist first. Also, move the wall calendar into a sheet with source, nights, price and review score, and read it monthly. Add a housekeeping checklist signed by the cleaner, so a bathroom complaint can be traced to a date and a room. the owner's wall calendar becomes a weekly sheet, so a dirty-bathroom review points to a date, a room and a signed checklist.

Model answer: MEI for micro-entrepreneurs whose activity is on the official list, or a company with a CNPJ (check current limits; regulated activities need sector authorisation first). (Covered in module 2: Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil. To pass that module: Cite an official source for every line, assume nothing, and date every pending item. Q3. Which Brazilian law governs customer personal data?

Model answer: LGPD, the general data protection law. (Covered in module 2: Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil. To pass that module: Cite an official source for every line, assume nothing, and date every pending item. Local law always wins over this course.)

Q4. What does an offer state, and what backs it up?

levels L1 to L7

Where this sits on the ladder

Seven levels, one slide each. Read the slide, then read the speaker notes under it. Credits stack and you can step in and out; this course is L4.

Slide 1 of 7: L1 Basic

  • Describe what a bed and breakfast offers
  • Name the daily tasks of a host
  • Identify basic cleaning and food safety habits
Notes for slide 1

Shadow one room turnover from stripped bed to reset towels, then copy the order of tasks into a labelled sketch of a guest room. Learn why bathroom and kitchen cloths stay separate and why eggs and milk wait in the fridge. Hand in a twenty-word lodging glossary. About six hours of study; accuracy beats speed.

Slide 2 of 7: L2 Awareness

  • Explain how guests find and choose small stays
  • Recognize local rules that may apply to lodging
  • Compare B&Bs, hostels and hotels
Notes for slide 2

Interview a host or visit a small stay in Vila Madalena or Pinheiros, and ask where guests booked, what they asked first and which complaints repeat. Sort three listings by price, photos and house rules. Hand in a one-page comparison of a B&B, a hostel and a hotel, citing the current official text for any licence you mention.

Slide 3 of 7: L3 Foundation

  • Prepare a guest room and breakfast to standard
  • Handle a booking, check-in and check-out
  • Answer guest questions politely
Notes for slide 3

Practise a full cycle on a real or mock room: make the bed with tight corners, test the smoke detector, set the breakfast table, and log arrival and departure times in a booking sheet. Evidence is a checklist signed by a supervisor plus a recorded role-play of a guest asking about late check-in and noise. Allow roughly forty hours.

Slide 4 of 7: L4 Practitioner

  • Plan a bed and breakfast in Sao Paulo
  • Start it with registrations, safety checks and a pricing plan
  • Run bookings, housekeeping, reviews and monthly accounts
Notes for slide 4

Build a launch file for one house: room list, nightly price, cleaning and breakfast cost per room-night, insurance and safety checks, and the registrations to confirm in the current official text. Then run four weeks of bookings in a sheet with review scores and a monthly profit line. A new host should be able to run from your files.

Slide 5 of 7: L5 Specialist

  • Specialize in one guest type such as business or culture travelers
  • Set seasonal prices using occupancy data
  • Build a direct booking channel
Notes for slide 5

Choose one guest type, say consultants visiting for the week, and redesign the stay around them: desk light, fast wifi, early breakfast, laundry. Pull twelve months of occupancy by weekday and set low, normal and peak nightly rates. Evidence is a rate table, a direct-booking page draft and the booking share it captured against portals.

Slide 6 of 7: L6 Advanced

  • Manage staff, suppliers and maintenance schedules
  • Improve guest reviews with feedback loops
  • Control costs and raise occupancy
Notes for slide 6

Run a team of cleaners, a cook and a maintenance contact with a weekly rota and a yearly calendar for boiler, mattresses and extinguishers. Read every review for patterns, log fixes, and show a before-and-after score. Evidence includes a cost per occupied room-night report and an occupancy trend over two quarters, with notes on what changed it.

Slide 7 of 7: L7 Super expert

  • Design a signature guest experience
  • Mentor hosts and managers
  • Advise on buying, expanding or joining a property group
Notes for slide 7

Produce a written guest-journey standard that a new host can follow, a mentoring log for two hosts, and a review of one buying or expansion case with figures on bookings, running costs and borrowing. Owners and peers should be able to challenge your reasoning. Expect a year of work; the bar is repeatable quality across properties, not one star house.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, taxes, data duties and customer rights. This course is written for São Paulo. Check the current official text for your own place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registration: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registration: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registration: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registration: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)
