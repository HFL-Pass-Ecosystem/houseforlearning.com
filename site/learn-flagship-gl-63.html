flagship course

Plastic Alternatives

Understand materials that can replace single-use plastic, how to test them honestly, and how to plan a small product switch.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation27:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain plastic types and the problem of waste.
  • Apply Test an alternative for use and cost.
  • Analyse Compare life-cycle impacts.
  • Evaluate Judge claims such as biodegradable and compostable.
  • Create Plan a product switch for a small business.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Choose one single-use plastic item, test two alternatives, compare cost and impact, and write a switch plan with customer message.

Condition

Samples of items, a scale and basic tools, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. Safe, non-food-contact tests unless approved.

Standard

Tests are repeatable; claims are checked against recognised definitions; cost per use is shown; end-of-life route exists locally; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Shop owners, packaging buyers, students, municipal staff. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The plastic waste problem and your baseline 3 hUnderstand

Single-use plastic is used for minutes and can last for centuries. Before you change anything, measure what you use.

  • Single-use items such as bags, bottles, sachets, cutlery and cups make up a large share of litter.
  • Microplastics from breakdown and from synthetic textiles enter water and food.
  • Order of action: reduce, reuse, recycle, and only then replace.
  • A switch that increases total impact is not an improvement.
Worked example. A cafe counts 300 disposable cups a day. A returnable cup scheme with a deposit cuts that by 70 per cent, more than any new compostable cup could.

Activity. Audit the plastic you or your business use for one week: list items, counts and weight.

Standard to meet. List has at least 15 items with counts, the top three are highlighted and a baseline weight is recorded.

Module 2 Plastic types, codes and what is really recycled 3 hUnderstand

Knowing the type tells you whether it can be recycled where you live.

  • Resin codes 1 to 7 show the polymer type.
  • PET (1) and HDPE (2) are widely collected; films, multilayer packs and black plastics are rarely recycled.
  • Dirty or mixed plastic is hard to sort.
  • Check what your local collectors actually accept.
Worked example. PET bottles are often collected and recycled; a chips packet made of multilayer film usually goes to landfill. The municipal website lists only codes 1 and 2 for kerbside pickup.

Activity. Sort 10 items by code and local recyclability.

Standard to meet. At least 9 sorted correctly and the local rule is cited.

Module 3 Alternative materials and reuse systems 4 hUnderstand

Many options exist and each has trade-offs. Reuse often beats replacement.

  • Paper and card, glass, metal, plant fibres, starch-based plastics and compostable blends.
  • Refill and returnable systems cut waste most.
  • Consider strength, moisture, heat, cost and local end-of-life facilities.
  • Ask suppliers for data, not slogans.
Worked example. A bakery tests paper bags, starch bags and reusable cloth bags. Paper works but costs more; a cloth bag discount scheme halves bag use. A refill station for detergent cuts bottle purchases by 80 per cent.

Activity. Make an options table for one product with at least four options including a reuse option.

Standard to meet. Each option has pros, cons, cost and end-of-life route.

Module 4 Testing alternatives 4 hApply

Test under real conditions before you switch.

  • Tests: strength (load until failure), leak, heat, cold, shelf life and user trial.
  • Repeat each test three times.
  • Record failures with photos and cost per use.
  • Test with real users.
Worked example. A paper cup with a thin lining leaks after 25 minutes with hot tea; a better-lined version lasts an hour. A starch bag tears at 3 kg while paper holds 5 kg.

Activity. Test two alternatives against your current item and record results.

Standard to meet. Table has repeated results and a conclusion.

Module 5 Life-cycle thinking 4 hAnalyse

Look at the whole life: raw material, making, transport, use and end of life.

  • A reusable item wins after enough uses.
  • Local conditions change results, for example electricity mix and recycling rates.
  • State your boundaries and sources.
  • Compare per use, not per piece.
Worked example. A glass jar needs about 12 uses to beat a plastic tub in emissions; a thicker reusable cup needs about 20 uses.

Activity. Do a simple life-cycle comparison for two options and calculate break-even uses.

Standard to meet. Method and sources stated and arithmetic correct.

Module 6 Claims, labels and greenwashing 3 hEvaluate

Avoid being misled by green claims.

  • Biodegradable and compostable are not the same thing.
  • Compostable may need an industrial facility that your area lacks.
  • Look for recognised standards and ask for test reports.
  • Treat words such as eco and natural with caution.
Worked example. A bag labelled eco has no standard number. The buyer asks for a test report and finds it is not compostable in home conditions, so the option is dropped.

Activity. Review three labels and score their claims.

Standard to meet. Each review lists the evidence needed.

Module 7 Customers, cost and communication 3 hEvaluate

A switch must work for customers and the business.

  • Cost per use, not per piece.
  • Deposit or discount schemes.
  • Explain simply and train staff.
  • Track complaints and returns.
Worked example. A refill discount repays the container cost within 20 uses; eight of ten surveyed customers accept a 5 INR deposit on a cup.

Activity. Cost your switch for one month and survey ten customers.

Standard to meet. Cost sheet and survey results are recorded.

Module 8 Switch plan and pilot 3 hCreate

Plan, pilot and review.

  • Steps, dates, owners and measures.
  • Pilot in one store or one product line.
  • Set a numeric goal such as 50 per cent fewer single-use items in 90 days.
  • Report results and decide to scale.
Worked example. Plastic bags fall by 70 per cent in one month with a returnable bag scheme; after 90 days the shop meets a 62 per cent reduction in all single-use items.

Activity. Present the plan with measures and a decision rule.

Standard to meet. Measures are numeric and dates are set.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: test sheet, comparison table, switch plan. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
TestingOne trialRepeatedRepeated and analysed
Claims reviewNoneDoneDone with standards
PlanVagueDatedMeasured and piloted
Honesty and evidenceClaims without proofEvery claim has a recordEvery claim linked to a dated record and checked by a peer
Safety, ethics and local rulesNot consideredChecked and written downChecked, dated and explained to others

ten locations and global

Your place

Packaging, food-contact and waste rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global
