flagship course

Plastic Alternatives

Understand materials that can replace single-use plastic, how to test them honestly, and how to plan a small product switch.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation27:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain plastic types and the problem of waste.
  • Apply Test an alternative for use and cost.
  • Analyse Compare life-cycle impacts.
  • Evaluate Judge claims such as biodegradable and compostable.
  • Create Plan a product switch for a small business.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Choose one single-use plastic item, test two alternatives, compare cost and impact, and write a switch plan with customer message.

Condition

Samples of items, a scale and basic tools, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. Safe, non-food-contact tests unless approved.

Standard

Tests are repeatable; claims are checked against recognised definitions; cost per use is shown; end-of-life route exists locally; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Shop owners, packaging buyers, students, municipal staff. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The plastic waste problem 3 hUnderstand

Single-use plastic is used for minutes and can last for centuries. Understanding the scale helps you choose where to act first.

  • Single-use items such as bags, bottles, sachets and cutlery make up a large share of litter.
  • Microplastics from breakdown and from textiles enter water and food.
  • Order of action: reduce, reuse, recycle, and only then replace.
  • A switch that increases total impact is not an improvement.
Worked example. A cafe counts 300 disposable cups a day. Switching to a returnable cup scheme with a deposit cuts that by 70 per cent, more than any new compostable cup could.

Activity. Audit the plastic you or your business use in one week and list items with counts.

Standard to meet. List has at least 15 items with counts and the top three are highlighted.

Module 2 Plastic types and codes 3 hUnderstand

Knowing the type tells you if it can be recycled.

  • Resin codes 1 to 7 show the polymer type.
  • PET (1) and HDPE (2) are widely recycled; films and multilayer packs are rarely recycled.
  • Mixed, dirty and black plastics are hard to sort.
  • Check local collection rules.
Worked example. PET bottles are often collected and recycled; a chips packet made of multilayer film usually goes to landfill.

Activity. Sort 10 items by code and recyclability in your area.

Standard to meet. At least 9 sorted correctly.

Module 3 Alternative materials 4 hUnderstand

Many options exist, each with trade-offs.

  • Paper, card, glass, metal, plant fibres, starch-based plastics and compostable blends.
  • Reuse systems often beat single-use alternatives.
  • Consider strength, moisture, heat and cost.
  • Consider whether local facilities exist for the end-of-life route.
Worked example. A bakery tests paper bags, starch bags and reusable cloth bags. The paper bag works but costs more; a cloth bag discount scheme reduces bag use by half.

Activity. Make an options table for one product with pros and cons.

Standard to meet. At least four options compared.

Module 4 Testing alternatives 4 hApply

Do real tests before switching.

  • Strength: load until failure; leak test; heat test; shelf life.
  • Repeat each test three times.
  • Record failures and costs.
  • Test with real users.
Worked example. A starch bag tears at 3 kg while a paper bag holds 5 kg; the shop selects paper for heavy items.

Activity. Test two alternatives against your current item and record results.

Standard to meet. Table has repeated results and a conclusion.

Module 5 Life-cycle thinking 4 hAnalyse

Look at the whole life of a product, from raw material to disposal.

  • Stages: raw material, making, transport, use, end of life.
  • A reusable item wins after enough uses.
  • Local conditions change results.
  • State your boundaries.
Worked example. A glass jar needs about 12 uses to beat a plastic tub in emissions; a thicker cup needs 20 uses.

Activity. Do a simple comparison for two options.

Standard to meet. Method and sources stated.

Module 6 Claims and labels 3 hEvaluate

Avoid being misled by green claims.

  • Biodegradable and compostable are not the same.
  • Compostable may need industrial facilities.
  • Look for recognised standards and test reports.
  • Ask for evidence.
Worked example. A bag labelled "eco" has no standard. The buyer asks for a test report and finds it is not compostable in home conditions.

Activity. Review three labels and score their claims.

Standard to meet. Each review lists evidence required.

Module 7 Customers and cost 3 hEvaluate

A switch must work for customers and the business.

  • Cost per use, not per piece.
  • Deposit or discount schemes.
  • Explain simply and train staff.
  • Track complaints.
Worked example. A refill discount repays the cost of the container within 20 uses.

Activity. Cost your switch for one month.

Standard to meet. Currency shown.

Module 8 Switch plan 3 hCreate

Plan and review.

  • Steps, dates, owners, measures.
  • Pilot in one store.
  • Report results.
  • Scale or adjust.
Worked example. Plastic bags fall by 70 per cent in one month with a returnable bag scheme.

Activity. Present the plan.

Standard to meet. Measures are clear.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: test sheet, comparison table, switch plan. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
TestingOne trialRepeatedRepeated and analysed
Claims reviewNoneDoneDone with standards
PlanVagueDatedMeasured and piloted
Honesty and evidenceClaims without proofEvery claim has a recordEvery claim linked to a dated record and checked by a peer
Safety, ethics and local rulesNot consideredChecked and written downChecked, dated and explained to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Order of waste actions?

Model answer: Reduce, reuse, recycle. (Covered in module 1: The plastic waste problem. To pass that module: List has at least 15 items with counts and the top three are highlighted.)

Q2. Why microplastics matter?

Model answer: They enter food and water. (Covered in module 2: Plastic types and codes. To pass that module: At least 9 sorted correctly.)

Q3. Compostable equals biodegradable?

Model answer: No. (Covered in module 3: Alternative materials. To pass that module: At least four options compared.)

Q4. Name a test.

Model answer: Strength, leak, heat or shelf life. (Covered in module 4: Testing alternatives. To pass that module: Table has repeated results and a conclusion.)

Q5. Life-cycle stages?

Model answer: Material, making, transport, use, end of life. (Covered in module 5: Life-cycle thinking. To pass that module: Method and sources stated.)

Q6. Why cost per use?

Model answer: Reuse items spread cost. (Covered in module 6: Claims and labels. To pass that module: Each review lists evidence required.)

Q7. Name a label risk.

Model answer: Greenwashing. (Covered in module 7: Customers and cost. To pass that module: Currency shown.)

Q8. Best first step?

Model answer: Reduce. (Covered in module 8: Switch plan. To pass that module: Measures are clear.)

Q9. Why pilot?

Model answer: Test small first. (Covered in module 8: Switch plan. To pass that module: Measures are clear.)

Q10. Why local end-of-life?

Model answer: Facilities differ. (Covered in module 8: Switch plan. To pass that module: Measures are clear.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Packaging, food-contact and waste rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
