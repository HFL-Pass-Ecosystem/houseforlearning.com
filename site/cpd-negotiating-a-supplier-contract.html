Negotiating a supplier contract

Preparing and running a fair supplier negotiation.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 4 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playBusiness professionals

Preparing and running a fair supplier negotiation.

  • Prepare a negotiation, given a brief, with goal, walk-away point and three trades.
  • Compare two quotes, given a template, on total cost and risk.
  • Conduct a role-play negotiation, given a counterpart, reaching an agreement inside your limits.
  • Record the outcome, given a template, with terms, owner and date.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a supplier negotiation with a target, limit and three trades, conduct role-plays inside the limit, and record the agreed terms on a one-page summary for sign-off.

Condition

Given a brief, a quote template and a role-play partner playing the supplier, working in a four-hour supervised session.

Standard

Reach an agreement inside your limit using at least one trade, compare two offers on total cost and risk, and record terms, owner and date on a one-page summary.

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Target, limit, walk-away 30 min
  • Write numbers down first
  • Limit is the worst deal you accept
  • Never cross the limit
Worked example. Target 100 per unit, walk-away limit 115. The supplier asks 120. That is 5 above the limit, so you cannot accept it. Write the three numbers down before you start.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "target, limit, walk-away": prepare a negotiation, given a brief, with goal, walk-away point and three trades.

Standard to meet. Set a target, a limit and a walk-away point before a negotiation, writing each number down and stating that the limit is the worst deal you will accept.

Module 2 Three trades you can offer 35 min
  • Three trades ready
  • Give something to get something
  • Know what each is worth
Worked example. Trades: longer contract for a lower rate (12 months for 108), faster payment for a discount, volume for free delivery. Each trade is worth something to you and to them; know the value of each.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "three trades you can offer": compare two quotes, given a template, on total cost and risk.

Standard to meet. Choose three trades you can offer, such as a longer term for a lower price, and say what each trade is worth to you.

Module 3 Listening and questions 30 min
  • Ask open questions
  • Listen for real needs
  • Match trades to needs
Worked example. Open question: "What matters most to you in this contract?" Supplier: "Steady orders so we can plan stock." That points to the trade of a 12-month term. Listen more than you speak.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "listening and questions": conduct a role-play negotiation, given a counterpart, reaching an agreement inside your limits.

Standard to meet. Ask open questions and listen closely to find what the other side really needs, so that trades can be matched to those needs.

Module 4 Role-play 1 35 min
  • Stay inside the limit
  • Use at least one trade
  • Check where you land
Worked example. Role-play 1: supplier opens at 120, you open at 100. You offer a 12-month term. They move to 112, you reach 108. 108 is 8 above target and 7 below the limit. Inside the limit, one trade used.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "role-play 1": record the outcome, given a template, with terms, owner and date.

Standard to meet. Carry out a role-play negotiation against a supplier asking for a higher price, staying inside your limit and using at least one trade.

Module 5 Role-play 2 with a twist 40 min
  • Expect a twist
  • Adjust the trade, not the limit
  • Re-check the total
Worked example. Role-play 2 twist: the supplier adds a delivery cost of 6, taking 108 to 114, only 1 below the limit. You trade volume for free delivery, back to 108. Keep to the limit when the situation changes.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "role-play 2 with a twist": record the outcome, given a template, with terms, owner and date. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Carry out a second role-play that includes a twist, such as a new demand, and adjust your trades while keeping to your limit.

Module 6 One-page summary for sign-off 30 min
  • One page
  • Price, term, delivery, payment, review date, signatory
  • Ready for sign-off
Worked example. Summary: price 108; term 12 months; delivery free (volume trade); payment 30 days; review date after 6 months; signs: the budget holder with authority.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "one-page summary for sign-off": record the outcome, given a template, with terms, owner and date. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Write a one-page summary of the agreed price, term, delivery, payment, review date and signatory, ready for sign-off by the person with authority.

Module 7 Assessment 10 min
  • Timed
  • Prepare, negotiate, summarise
  • Check every point
Worked example. Assessment sample: target 50, limit 58. Agreed 54: 4 above target, 4 below limit. Pass needs prepared numbers, a negotiation inside the limit and a complete summary.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "assessment": record the outcome, given a template, with terms, owner and date. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Complete a timed assessment by preparing numbers, negotiating a role-play and producing a summary that meets every checklist point.

Module 8 Second case: an early-payment offer 30 min
  • Work case card 2
  • Work out the cash cost
  • Decide with the numbers
Worked example. Case card 2: the supplier offers 5 percent off if you pay in 10 days instead of 45. On a bill of 2,000 you pay 1,900 instead of 2,000: 100 saved for paying 35 days sooner. 5 / 95 x 365 / 35 = about 55 percent a year. Take it if your cost of funds is lower than that and cash allows.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second case: an early-payment offer": record the outcome, given a template, with terms, owner and date. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Calculate the cash cost of the early-payment offer from the second case card and decide whether to accept it, stating the figures used.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

What is a walk-away limit?

Model answer: The worst deal you will accept. (Covered in module 1: Target, limit, walk-away. To pass that module: Set a target, a limit and a walk-away point before a negotiation, writing each number down and stating that the limit is the worst deal you will accept.)

Name a trade.

Model answer: Longer term for a better price. (Covered in module 2: Three trades you can offer. To pass that module: Choose three trades you can offer, such as a longer term for a lower price, and say what each trade is worth to you.)

What goes in the summary?

Model answer: Price, term, delivery, payment, review date, who signs. (Covered in module 6: One-page summary for sign-off. To pass that module: Write a one-page summary of the agreed price, term, delivery, payment, review date and signatory, ready for sign-off by the person with authority.)

Who signs?

Model answer: The person with authority under your policy. (Covered in module 6: One-page summary for sign-off. To pass that module: Write a one-page summary of the agreed price, term, delivery, payment, review date and signatory, ready for sign-off by the person with authority.)

What is a limit?

Model answer: The worst deal you accept. (Covered in module 1: Target, limit, walk-away. To pass that module: Set a target, a limit and a walk-away point before a negotiation, writing each number down and stating that the limit is the worst deal you will accept.)

Name a trade.

Model answer: Longer term for lower price. (Covered in module 2: Three trades you can offer. To pass that module: Choose three trades you can offer, such as a longer term for a lower price, and say what each trade is worth to you.)

Why summarize in writing?

Model answer: Avoids later disputes. (Covered in module 6: One-page summary for sign-off. To pass that module: Write a one-page summary of the agreed price, term, delivery, payment, review date and signatory, ready for sign-off by the person with authority.)

What if you hit your limit?

Model answer: Pause or leave; do not cross it. (Covered in module 1: Target, limit, walk-away. To pass that module: Set a target, a limit and a walk-away point before a negotiation, writing each number down and stating that the limit is the worst deal you will accept.)

Who signs?

Model answer: The person with authority, per policy. (Covered in module 6: One-page summary for sign-off. To pass that module: Write a one-page summary of the agreed price, term, delivery, payment, review date and signatory, ready for sign-off by the person with authority.)

You agree 108 with a limit of 115 and a target of 100. How much room is left, and how far from target?

Model answer: 7 below the limit and 8 above target. (Covered in module 4: Role-play 1. To pass that module: Carry out a role-play negotiation against a supplier asking for a higher price, staying inside your limit and using at least one trade.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What is a negotiation. Speaker note: A negotiation is two sides agreeing terms that work for both. Learners at this level can say that preparation matters and that you do not have to accept the first offer.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Target and limit. Speaker note: Show target, limit and walk-away on a simple line: 100, 115, and the point where you leave. Learners know why writing the numbers down before the meeting protects them.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Trade, do not just give. Speaker note: Learners practise offering a trade for each request in a short role-play with a mentor, such as a longer term for a lower rate, and say what each trade is worth.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Run a fair supplier negotiation. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner prepares numbers, conducts two role-plays inside the limit, handles a twist, compares offers and writes a one-page summary ready for sign-off.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Complex supplier deals. Speaker note: The specialist negotiates multi-part deals with several suppliers, compares total cost and risk, handles pressure tactics and builds a standard approval route for exceptions that others in the team can follow.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Category strategy. Speaker note: The advanced buyer plans spending across a category, sets targets and limits for the team, tracks savings honestly and reviews supplier performance before the review date.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Procurement leadership. Speaker note: The super expert sets negotiation policy for the organisation: authority levels, ethics, training and measures of fairness and value. They teach staff to protect long-term supplier relationships.
