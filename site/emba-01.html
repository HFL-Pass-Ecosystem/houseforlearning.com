executive MBA subject 01

Managerial Skills

Pre-programme, open term. 3.5 ECTS, about 88 hours, 4 chapters. Free for everyone.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write a 90-day personal leadership plan with one presence goal, one influence goal and one decision habit.

Condition

Given feedback from at least three colleagues and a diary of two working weeks.

Standard

Every goal is backed by evidence from the feedback or the diary and has a measure and a date.

Who it is for: New and future managers who want to lead themselves before they lead others.

how this subject works

Free entry, voice and your learning style

Free for everyone. This pre-programme subject is open to all, with no payment and no sign-in needed. It is a good first step if you are new to study or to computers.

  • Listen. Every part has a Listen button that reads it aloud.
  • Speak or type. Answer each part by talking (press Speak) or by typing. Your answers stay on this device only.
  • Learn your way. Every part has a See, Hear, Do and Read-and-write route. Not sure which suits you? Take the eight-question learning-style check.

4 chapters

The chapters, with worked examples

Chapter 1 Self-leadership and executive presence 22 hUnderstand: describe

Presence is how calm, clear and prepared you seem when it matters. It is built from habits, not from personality.

  • Prepare the first and last sentence of every meeting you lead
  • Slow down: short sentences and pauses read as confidence
  • Keep promises small and on time; reliability is the base of presence

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A team lead promises 10 updates in a month and delivers 6 on time, a 60% promise rate. Next month she promises only 6 and delivers all 6: 100%. Her manager now trusts her dates, even though she promised less.

Activity: Track every promise you make for one week and work out your on-time rate.

Standard: At least 10 promises logged, the rate calculated correctly, and one change written down.

Chapter 2 Communication and influence 22 hApply: plan and deliver

Influence is helping people say yes for their own reasons. Start with what they care about, then show how your idea serves it.

  • Map who decides, who advises and who can block
  • Lead with the benefit to them, then the evidence
  • Ask for one clear action at the end

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. You need two extra staff. The finance head cares about cost per order. Today it is 4.00 on 10,000 orders a month (40,000). Two staff at 3,000 each cut overtime by 9,000, so cost falls to 34,000, or 3.40 per order. You open with "3.40 per order", not with "we are tired".

Activity: Prepare a one-minute pitch for a real request, written in the other person's terms.

Standard: The pitch names the listener's goal, gives one correct number, and ends with one clear ask.

Chapter 3 Decisions under pressure 22 hApply: decide

Under time pressure we jump to the first answer. A short routine stops the worst mistakes without slowing you down much.

  • Name the decision and the deadline in one sentence
  • List at least two real options, including "wait"
  • Ask: what would make this choice wrong, and how would I know?

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A supplier is late. Option A: pay 2,000 for express shipping, sure to arrive. Option B: wait, with a 70% chance it arrives in time; if late, you lose a 10,000 order. Expected loss of B = 30% x 10,000 = 3,000. A costs 2,000, so A is the better choice.

Activity: Apply the three-question routine to one decision you face this week and write it down in five lines.

Standard: Two or more options, one risk named for the choice, and the expected cost worked out where numbers exist.

Chapter 4 Personal development plan 22 hCreate: plan

A plan turns good intentions into dated steps. Keep it short enough to read every Monday.

  • Pick at most three goals
  • Each goal has a measure, a date and a person who will check
  • Review monthly and drop what no longer matters

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Goal: speak up in senior meetings. Measure: at least one prepared point in 8 of the next 10 meetings. Check: your manager, monthly. After month one you managed 3 of 4 meetings, 75%, so you are on track for 8 of 10.

Activity: Write your 90-day plan with three goals and share it with one person who will check it.

Standard: Three goals at most, each with a measure, a date and a named checker.

ten checks

Check yourself

Say or write your answer first, then read the model answer.

  1. Q1. What is a promise rate?
    Model answer: Promises kept on time divided by promises made. (Covered in chapter 1: Self-leadership and executive presence.)
  2. Q2. Why can promising less raise trust?
    Model answer: Because people trust a record of kept dates more than big promises. (Covered in chapter 1: Self-leadership and executive presence.)
  3. Q3. Name one habit that makes you sound calm.
    Model answer: Short sentences and pauses, or preparing the first and last sentence. (Covered in chapter 1: Self-leadership and executive presence.)
  4. Q4. In an influence map, which three groups do you list?
    Model answer: Who decides, who advises and who can block. (Covered in chapter 2: Communication and influence.)
  5. Q5. Cost is 40,000 for 10,000 orders and drops to 34,000. What is the new cost per order?
    Model answer: 3.40. (Covered in chapter 2: Communication and influence.)
  6. Q6. How should a pitch end?
    Model answer: With one clear ask. (Covered in chapter 2: Communication and influence.)
  7. Q7. Option B has a 30% chance of losing 10,000. What is its expected loss?
    Model answer: 3,000. (Covered in chapter 3: Decisions under pressure.)
  8. Q8. Why list "wait" as an option?
    Model answer: Because doing nothing is a real choice with its own cost and benefit. (Covered in chapter 3: Decisions under pressure.)
  9. Q9. How many goals should a 90-day plan have?
    Model answer: Three at most. (Covered in chapter 4: Personal development plan.)
  10. Q10. What three things does each goal need?
    Model answer: A measure, a date and a person who checks. (Covered in chapter 4: Personal development plan.)
