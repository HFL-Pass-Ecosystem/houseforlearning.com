Construction

Run a pilot of construction in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small construction offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: property, construction and smart buildings. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the site, the trades and the words 6 hUnderstand

Construction builds and repairs homes and other structures. Start with the roles, the stages and the basic words.

  • Terms: permit, drawing, scope, estimate, tender, subcontractor, inspection, deficiency.
  • Stages: design, permit, site prep, foundation, frame, services, finish, inspection, handover.
  • Trades include carpenters, electricians, plumbers and labourers; some trades need a licence or certificate.
  • Visit a site from outside or with permission and name each trade you see.
  • Read a simple drawing and find the scale, the dimensions and the notes.
  • Learn the common tools by name and by use: tape, level, square, saw, drill, chalk line.
  • Learn the common materials: lumber, concrete, drywall, insulation, roofing, and what each is used for.
Worked example. A deficiency is a small fault found at the end, such as a missing cover plate, that must be fixed before final payment. If a list shows 12 deficiencies and 9 are fixed, 12 - 9 = 3 remain. Final payment waits until all 12 are signed off.

Activity. Write a one-page stage list for a small project, such as a deck, with the trade for each stage.

Standard to meet. Task: list project stages and trades. Condition: for one small project. Standard: all stages in order, terms used correctly, and trades matched to tasks.

Module 2 Explain how a job moves from idea to handover 6 hApply

Every job needs a clear scope, a plan and a way to handle changes.

  • Scope says what is included and what is not. Write it before you price.
  • A schedule shows which tasks come first and which can run together.
  • A change order is a written, priced and agreed change to the scope.
  • Keep a daily log: weather, who was on site, work done, problems.
  • Break a job into tasks and give each a duration in days.
  • Mark tasks that must wait for another, such as framing before wiring.
  • Write who orders materials, who is on site and who signs off each stage.
Worked example. The owner asks for an extra window. You write: 'Add one window, 1,200 CAD, two extra days.' Both sign before work starts. If the job was 2,420 CAD and 10 days, it becomes 2,420 + 1,200 = 3,620 CAD and 12 days.

Activity. Write a scope, a task list with days, and a change order form for a small job.

Standard to meet. Task: write scope, schedule and change form. Condition: for one real or pretend small job. Standard: scope names inclusions and exclusions; schedule has start, end and order of tasks.

Module 3 Do basic site tasks safely and to a standard 10 hApply

Safety comes first. Then do the task to a measured standard.

  • Complete the required safety training before you work on site. Wear the right protective gear.
  • Know the main hazards: falls, cuts, electrical contact, trench collapse and lifting injuries.
  • Measure twice and check level, square and plumb before you fix.
  • Before you dig, request a locate of buried lines through the local call-before-you-dig service.
  • Check that ladders, scaffolds and tools are in good condition before each use.
  • Mark and cut with a pencil line and a square, and check the first cut against the plan.
  • Keep the work area tidy and put offcuts and waste in the right bin at the end of the day.
Worked example. Check square by measuring both diagonals of a frame. A frame 2.4 m by 1.2 m should have both diagonals about 2.68 m. If one reads 2.68 and the other 2.72, the diagonals differ by 4 cm, so the frame is out of square and must be adjusted.

Activity. Build a small practice item, such as a frame or a wall section, under supervision. Check and record measurements.

Standard to meet. Task: build and check a practice item. Condition: supervised, with safety gear. Standard: measurements within the stated tolerance and no safety breaches.

Module 4 Know permits, codes, licences and liens 8 hEvaluate

Building rules are mostly provincial and municipal. Find what applies before you start.

  • Most building work needs a municipal permit and inspections. The building code is set by the province and is based on national model codes. Check with your municipality.
  • Some trades, such as electrical and gas work, require a licensed person. Check with your provincial trades authority.
  • Workplace safety is enforced by a provincial board or ministry. Check the current rules.
  • Construction lien laws let unpaid workers and suppliers claim against a property. Rules and holdback amounts differ by province; check the current law.
  • Ask the municipality which permit your project needs and how long approval takes.
  • List the inspections you must request and the stage at which each one happens.
  • Keep copies of permits, inspection reports and licences on site.
Worked example. Rules table row: 'Basement suite | municipal building office | permit and inspections before closing walls | before work | permit number on site'. If the permit takes 6 weeks and the build takes 8 weeks, the earliest finish is week 14, not week 8.

Activity. Build a rules table for a small project in your city with the enforcer, action and date checked.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: from official pages. Standard: each row has a dated source; no rule is guessed.

Module 5 Estimate, price and track job costs 8 hApply

A job must cover its real cost and your risk.

  • List materials, labour, equipment, permits, waste and travel. Add a margin for risk and profit.
  • Get written quotes from suppliers and subcontractors.
  • Track actual cost against estimate each week.
  • Do not forget insurance, tools and unpaid time for quoting.
  • Add a contingency for unknowns, such as 10 percent of cost, and say so in the estimate.
  • Include the cost of waste and rework in material quantities.
  • Compare the quote you give with the cost you track, and note the difference on every job.
Worked example. Price 2,420, cost 1,750: margin 670 per job, about 28 percent (670 / 2420). With 9 jobs, margin is 9 x 670 = 6,030 CAD. If a job runs 10 percent over cost (1,925), margin falls to 495. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Estimate a small job with a cost sheet, a price and a plan to track actual cost.

Standard to meet. Task: build an estimate. Condition: current local prices. Standard: units and quantities shown, allowances stated, margin computed, and a mentor checks the arithmetic.

Module 6 Run the site: schedule, people and quality 6 hCreate

Good running keeps jobs on time and safe.

  • Hold a short safety talk at the start of each day.
  • Order materials by lead time so work does not stop.
  • Inspect each stage before the next one covers it.
  • Close out with a list of deficiencies and sign-off.
  • Walk the site at the end of each day for hazards and for materials left out.
  • Check deliveries against the order before you sign.
  • Take dated photos of work before it is covered up.
Worked example. Plumbing is inspected before walls are closed. A failed test is fixed the same day, not hidden. If the fix takes 4 hours and a closed wall would have taken 2 days to reopen, the early check saves 1.5 days or more.

Activity. Run a small project for four weeks with a daily log, stage checks and a close-out list.

Standard to meet. Task: run a small build or repair. Condition: real or training project. Standard: log complete, each stage checked, deficiencies fixed and signed.

Module 7 Win work, write contracts and manage clients 6 hEvaluate

Clear agreements prevent most disputes.

  • A contract states scope, price, payment schedule, start and end dates, change process and warranty.
  • Ask for references and show photos of past work.
  • Keep records of emails, photos and signed changes.
  • Ask a legal adviser to review your contract template.
  • Put the warranty length and what it covers in writing.
  • Send a written summary after each client meeting and ask for a reply.
  • Agree how and when you will be paid before you buy materials.
Worked example. Payment schedule: 20 percent at start, 40 percent at framing, 30 percent at finish, 10 percent after inspection. On a 2,420 CAD job that is 484, 968, 726 and 242, which add to 2,420.

Activity. Write a one-page contract template and a quote letter for a small job.

Standard to meet. Task: draft a contract template and quote. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: all key terms in plain words; payment steps tied to finished work.

Module 8 Manage risk, pilot a job and grow 8 hCreate

Risk is part of building. Plan for it and prove your system on a real job.

  • Risk register: injury, weather, price rises, late delivery, defects, unpaid invoices.
  • Keep insurance current and cash for slow weeks.
  • Pilot a real small job with a stop rule for cost and time overruns.
  • Write a review and a 12-month plan, including training and hiring.
  • Rank the risks by cost and likelihood, and list a control for each.
  • Write who you call first in an injury, a damage claim or a stop-work order.
  • Plan how you will find and train a helper before you need one.
Worked example. Stop rule: 'If cost passes the estimate by 10 percent, stop and agree a change in writing.' On a 1,750 CAD estimate, the limit is 1,750 x 1.10 = 1,925 CAD. At 1,950, the rule has been passed by 25 CAD and work stops.

Activity. Run the job, write the review and the plan, and present to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a pilot job. Condition: licensed or supervised, safe and legal. Standard: costs tracked, no safety breaches, decision supported by numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
