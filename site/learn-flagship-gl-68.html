Space Tourism Awareness

Understand what suborbital and orbital tourism are, the risks, cost, rules and careers, and how to read claims.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2 Awareness16:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain suborbital and orbital flights.
  • Apply Read a flight profile.
  • Analyse Compare cost, risk and environment.
  • Evaluate Judge safety and fairness.
  • Create Write a fact sheet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a two-page fact sheet on space tourism with flight types, risks, costs, environmental impact and careers.

Condition

A computer, public information, two peers and a mentor.

Standard

Facts sourced and dated; risks stated; claims checked; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Students, travel staff, writers, enthusiasts. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What counts as space and why it matters 2 hUnderstand

The edge of space is a convention, not a wall, and the type of flight sets the experience.

  • A common line is 100 km above sea level, though some authorities use a lower altitude.
  • Suborbital flights go up and come back without orbiting; orbital flights circle Earth and need far more energy.
  • Weightlessness on a suborbital flight lasts a few minutes; an orbital stay can last days.
  • Balloons reach about 30 km, which is high but not space.
Worked example. A suborbital flight reaches about 100 km, gives three to four minutes of weightlessness and returns in under an hour. An orbital stay costs many times more.

Activity. Explain suborbital, orbital and balloon flights to a peer using one drawing.

Standard to meet. Show that peer can say which is which and where the line is.

Module 2 Flight types and experience 2 hUnderstand

Each type has a different ride, training need and risk.

  • Rocket planes and capsules give a quick, strong acceleration; balloons give a slow, quiet ride.
  • Training: days or weeks of preparation, including emergency drills.
  • Medical screening checks heart, blood pressure and other conditions.
  • Comfort limits include seat size and height.
Worked example. A passenger spends two days in training, including a centrifuge session that shows high acceleration. Another selects a balloon flight because they are not fit for high acceleration.

Activity. Compare three flight types in a table.

Standard to meet. Show that table has altitude, time, training and key risks.

Module 3 Safety, health and informed consent 2 hEvaluate

Spaceflight carries real risk, and the passenger must understand it.

  • Hazards: acceleration, vibration, radiation, cabin pressure loss and emergency exits.
  • Operators publish safety records and incident reports.
  • Passengers sign an informed consent describing risks.
  • Medical screening can exclude some people.
Worked example. A passenger reads the written risk statement, asks the operator about abort procedures and decides not to fly until further test flights are complete.

Activity. List six risks and a control for each.

Standard to meet. Show that each risk has a control and a source.

Module 4 Cost, value and who can afford it 2 hAnalyse

Costs are far above what most people can pay.

  • Prices are often hundreds of thousands of dollars or more.
  • Costs might fall over time, but not soon.
  • Compare to other life events to give perspective.
  • Alternatives exist: museum, simulator and educational experiences.
Worked example. A seat costing 4 crore INR (an estimate; check your market) equals many lifetimes of average household income. A simulator ride costs 5,000 INR.

Activity. Compare a seat price with local income and an alternative.

Standard to meet. Show that comparison is clear and uses sources.

Module 5 Environment and sustainability 2 hAnalyse

Rocket launches emit gases and soot high in the air.

  • Black carbon and water vapour affect the upper atmosphere.
  • Per-passenger emissions can be many times those of a long flight.
  • Launch frequency matters more than one flight.
  • Fuel type changes impacts.
Worked example. One study estimates per-passenger emissions far above a long-haul flight; another gives a lower range. A fact sheet quotes the range and explains the difference.

Activity. Check two claims and report the range with sources.

Standard to meet. Show range stated with sources and date.

Module 6 Rules, licensing and responsibility 2 hEvaluate

Space tourism is regulated by national authorities.

  • Launch and flight licences, safety reviews and insurance.
  • Informed-consent rules differ by country.
  • Passengers share responsibility for following instructions.
  • Rules will change as the industry grows.
Worked example. In many countries operators need a licence for each launch type and must tell passengers about risks in writing; check the current rule with the national authority.

Activity. Summarise the rules in your country or the nearest one with a source.

Standard to meet. Show that source is an authority and is dated.

Module 7 Careers and ground services 2 hUnderstand

Most jobs are on the ground.

  • Engineers, technicians, safety and medical staff, trainers, guides and hospitality.
  • Data analysts, communicators and finance staff.
  • Skills transfer from aviation, hospitality, healthcare and engineering.
  • Local spaceports and launch sites can create jobs.
Worked example. A hotel catering trainee moves to a spaceport restaurant and a technician from aviation moves into ground support.

Activity. Link ten roles to the skills they need.

Standard to meet. Show that roles and skills are realistic.

Module 8 Write a balanced fact sheet 2 hCreate

Combine what you have learned into a short guide.

  • Use plain language.
  • State limits and unknowns.
  • Test with two readers.
  • Date the sheet and cite each source.
Worked example. A reader can repeat three facts, name one risk and one cost comparison after reading.

Activity. Finish and test the sheet.

Standard to meet. Show that reader can repeat three facts and one risk.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: fact sheet, comparison table. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
AccuracyErrorsCorrectCorrect and sourced
BalanceOne-sidedBothBoth with limits
ClarityHardClearTested
Fact checksStatements from advertisingEach fact cites a dated public sourceEach cost and risk figure compared across two sources
Law and ethicsLicensing not checkedFlight licence, consumer and safety rules are listed with datesRules checked on the regulator's page and the date recorded

ten locations and global

Your place

Space regulation differs by country.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
