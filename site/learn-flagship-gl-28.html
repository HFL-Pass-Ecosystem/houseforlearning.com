flagship course · GL-28

Business Coaching Practice

Coach small-business owners with questions, goals and accountability: agree a contract, run sessions, track progress and know when to refer on.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Coach small-business owners with questions, goals and accountability: agree a contract, run sessions, track progress and know when to refer on.

  • Understand Explain what coaching is and is not, and its ethics.
  • Apply Run a structured session using questions, not advice.
  • Analyse Analyse a client's goals, obstacles and options.
  • Create Create a coaching agreement, session notes and a progress plan.
  • Evaluate Evaluate your own practice with client feedback.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Coach two practice clients through four sessions each, with agreements, notes (private), plans and feedback.

Condition

Two volunteer clients, a quiet space or call, a notes template, a mentor coach and a peer circle for practice. About 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

Written agreement before session one; confidentiality kept; each session ends with a client-chosen action; progress measured on the client's own scale; client feedback 4 of 5 or better; referrals made when needed.

Who it is for: Managers, trainers, advisers, community leaders, business owners. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Coaching is not therapy, legal, tax or medical advice. Keep confidentiality. Refer clients to a qualified professional when issues are outside coaching.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What coaching is 4 hUnderstand

Coaching helps people find their own answers. You will be able to explain the difference between coaching and advising and agree with a client what success looks like.

  • Different from advising, mentoring, training and therapy.
  • The client sets the goal; the coach asks and listens.
  • Trust, confidentiality and respect.
  • Limits and referrals.
  • In the first meeting, explain that coaching helps clients find their own answers, whereas advising tells them what to do, and give one example of each.
  • Agree what a successful outcome looks like for the client, written in their own words, and revisit that statement every few sessions.
  • Practise a one-minute description of your coaching.
  • Write your own limits and when you will refer.
Worked example. Client asks "What should I do?" The coach asks "What options have you thought of?"

Activity. Write a one-page description of coaching in your words.

Standard to meet. Mentor agrees it separates coaching from advice.

Common mistake. Giving advice and calling it coaching.

Second worked example. A client says 'Tell me how to hire.' The coach asks 'What have you tried so far?' and the client lists two things she had forgotten.

Check yourself. Where did you catch yourself advising?

Module 2 Agreement and ethics 6 hUnderstand

Start with clear terms. You will be able to draft a clear coaching agreement and recognise when a client's needs call for a different professional.

  • Purpose, sessions, time, cost if any.
  • Confidentiality and its limits.
  • Data handling and notes.
  • How to end.
  • Put a short written agreement in place covering session length, frequency, confidentiality and what you will not do, and ask the client to sign it before starting.
  • Know your limits: refer the client to a suitable professional when issues go beyond business coaching, and do not present yourself as giving medical or legal advice.
  • Switch phones off in sessions.
  • Use the client's own words in notes.
Worked example. Limit: if someone is at risk of serious harm, the coach must act.

Activity. Draft an agreement and sign with a client.

Standard to meet. Signed before session one.

Common mistake. Vague agreements.

Second worked example. The agreement lists six sessions of 60 minutes, confidential notes, a closing review and how to end early.

Check yourself. What does your agreement promise?

Module 3 Listening 8 hApply

Great coaches talk less. You will be able to listen without interrupting, summarise accurately and reflect a client's feelings back with care.

  • Full attention; no phone.
  • Reflect back in the client's words.
  • Silence is useful.
  • Notice feelings and energy.
  • Practise listening for ten uninterrupted minutes, then summarise the client's main points in two sentences and ask whether you have understood them correctly.
  • Notice words the client repeats and the feelings behind the facts, and reflect them back gently, for example by saying you noticed they sounded worried about cash.
  • Keep session notes short and private.
  • End each session with the client's own summary.
Worked example. Client: "I'm stuck." Coach: "Stuck on what exactly?"

Activity. Practise listening for 10 minutes with a peer.

Standard to meet. Peer feels heard.

Common mistake. Interrupting.

Second worked example. Client says 'I\'m stuck.' The coach reflects and asks 'Stuck on what exactly?' The goal becomes clear in two minutes.

Check yourself. How long was your longest silence?

Module 4 Powerful questions 8 hApply

Open, short, curious. You will be able to ask short open questions, then wait quietly so the client reaches their own conclusions.

  • What do you want? What is in the way? What else? What will you do?
  • Avoid "why" that blames.
  • One question at a time.
  • Ask for specifics and dates.
  • Prefer open questions starting with "what" or "how", such as "What would make this month a success?", and avoid asking several questions at once.
  • Pause for five seconds after asking a question, since silence gives the client room to think and often produces the most useful answer.
  • Ask what the client will do, by when and how you will know.
  • Check in between sessions by short message.
Worked example. Instead of "Why not hire?" ask "What would hiring change for you?"

Activity. Prepare 20 questions and test five.

Standard to meet. Questions are open and neutral.

Common mistake. Closed questions.

Second worked example. Instead of 'Why didn't you finish?' ask 'What got in the way this week?'

Check yourself. Which questions were open?

Module 5 The session shape 8 hAnalyse

A simple structure keeps focus. You will be able to run a timed one-hour session with a clear opening, focus, plan and close.

  • Check-in; goal for today; explore; options; commit to actions; close.
  • Time boxes.
  • Client summarises.
  • Notes after, not during.
  • Structure each hour as a five-minute check-in, ten minutes reviewing last actions, thirty minutes on the focus topic, ten minutes planning and a five-minute close, with a visible clock.
  • End every session by asking the client to restate their key insight and next step, and write both in the shared notes.
  • Never push your own opinion as the answer.
  • Ask for feedback after each session.
Worked example. 60 minutes: 5 check-in, 10 goal, 25 explore, 10 options, 10 commit.

Activity. Run a full practice session.

Standard to meet. Session fits time.

Common mistake. No time boxes.

Second worked example. A 30-minute call is split into check-in (3), goal (5), explore (12), options (5) and commit (5); the coach keeps time.

Check yourself. How long did each part take?

Module 6 Goals and accountability 10 hCreate

Turn talk into action. You will be able to turn a client's goals into dated actions and review missed ones as lessons rather than failures.

  • SMART goals: specific, measurable, agreed, realistic, timed.
  • Scale 1 to 10 for progress.
  • Barriers and supports.
  • Check-in between sessions.
  • Turn goals into specific actions with a number and a date, for example "contact five past customers by Friday", and record them in one shared sheet.
  • At the next meeting, ask what happened before asking why, and treat missed actions as information about obstacles rather than as failure.
  • Keep a log of questions that worked.
  • Practise silence for ten seconds.
Worked example. Goal: "Call 10 past customers by Friday." Progress 3 → 6 after the week.

Activity. Set goals with each client.

Standard to meet. Each goal measurable.

Common mistake. Goals without dates.

Second worked example. Goal: write three offers by Thursday. Progress: 1 on Monday and 3 by Thursday; next week's goal is to show them to two customers.

Check yourself. What is the date?

Module 7 Business tools in coaching 6 hEvaluate

Light tools help clients see. You will be able to introduce a light business tool at the right moment and let the client remain the one doing the work.

  • Wheel of business areas.
  • Simple cash and customer map.
  • Priorities grid.
  • Do not overload.
  • Offer simple tools when they fit, such as a one-page cash forecast or a weekly priorities list, but let the client do the thinking and filling in.
  • Teach each tool in a short practice exercise, ask whether it suits their working style, and drop it if the client does not find it useful.
  • Meet your mentor once a month.
  • Record learning in a reflection diary.
Worked example. Client scores sales 4, finance 3, team 7: choose finance first.

Activity. Use one tool with a client.

Standard to meet. Client chooses a priority.

Common mistake. Too many tools.

Second worked example. A client scores time 3, money 6 and marketing 5 on a wheel and chooses time; the first tool is a weekly planner.

Check yourself. Which tool helped most?

Module 8 Review and growth 4 hCreate

Learn from feedback. You will be able to gather honest feedback, compare progress to original goals and plan your own development as a coach.

  • Client feedback form.
  • Self-reflection on strengths.
  • Mentor review.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Every six sessions, compare the client's original goals with their progress, ask for honest feedback, and note what you would change in your approach.
  • Keep your own growth plan with reading, peer review and one skill to practise each quarter, and record what you learned from each client engagement.
  • Review ethics every quarter.
  • Plan three skills to build in 90 days.
Worked example. Feedback: "Ask less at once." Practice changes.

Activity. Gather feedback and write the plan.

Standard to meet. Plan has three skills and dates.

Common mistake. Ignoring feedback.

Second worked example. Feedback says 'ask fewer questions at once'. The coach plans one question per turn.

Check yourself. What will you change?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. The advice trap

A client begs you to tell them what to do.

Question: How respond?

Model answer: Offer to explore options, ask what they would do if they were sure, and confirm their decision.

Case 2. The sensitive disclosure

A client reveals severe stress and health problems.

Question: What now?

Model answer: Listen, say this is beyond coaching, and refer to a qualified professional.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Coaching versus advice?

Model answer: Coaching draws out the client's own answers; advice tells them. (Covered in module 1: What coaching is. To pass that module: Mentor agrees it separates coaching from advice.)

Q2. Name a limit of confidentiality.

Model answer: Risk of serious harm. (Covered in module 2: Agreement and ethics. To pass that module: Signed before session one.)

Q3. What is a powerful question?

Model answer: Open, short and curious. (Covered in module 4: Powerful questions. To pass that module: Questions are open and neutral.)

Q4. Why sign an agreement first?

Model answer: It sets clear terms and trust. (Covered in module 2: Agreement and ethics. To pass that module: Signed before session one.)

Q5. What does SMART mean here?

Model answer: Specific, measurable, agreed, realistic, timed. (Covered in module 6: Goals and accountability. To pass that module: Each goal measurable.)

Q6. When refer on?

Model answer: When issues are outside coaching. (Covered in module 2: Agreement and ethics. To pass that module: Signed before session one.)

Q7. Why take notes after?

Model answer: Notes during can break attention. (Covered in module 5: The session shape. To pass that module: Session fits time.)

Q8. How to measure progress?

Model answer: On the client's own 1 to 10 scale. (Covered in module 6: Goals and accountability. To pass that module: Each goal measurable.)

Q9. Is coaching therapy?

Model answer: No. (Covered in module 1: What coaching is. To pass that module: Mentor agrees it separates coaching from advice.)

Q10. What ends a session well?

Model answer: A client-chosen action. (Covered in module 5: The session shape. To pass that module: Session fits time.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Coaching is unregulated in many places but advice rules apply: do not give regulated advice. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a Brainport-area small firm.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Partner to practise with: SEBRAE small-business support, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or a SkillsFuture-linked training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a TAFE or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
