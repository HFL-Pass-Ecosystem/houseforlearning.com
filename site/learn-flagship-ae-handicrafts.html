Handicrafts

Learn handicrafts for the UAE: rules, workflow, costing in AED and a recorded 30-day practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 176 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Understand Explain the UAE market, rules and hazards for handicrafts.
  • Apply Run the workflow: design, source materials, make in small batches, finish and pack.
  • Analyse Measure items sold per items made and find the biggest cause of loss.
  • Evaluate Cost and price in AED and judge scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a record pack a supervisor can audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a real or simulated 30-day pilot for hand-made gifts, woven items and decor in the UAE and keep a full record of every step, cost and result.

Condition

About 176 hours over 3 to 6 months. A partner site or a simulated customer, a free spreadsheet, a phone, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Hazards named with controls; every number has a unit and AED; records complete; rules confirmed with the local authority; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better.

Process learning only. Main risk to manage: sharp tools and dust. Follow UAE federal and emirate rules and any licence needed before you charge a customer. Visitors look for authentic, well-told local craft.

Requirements: none. Currency: AED on every number.

lesson by lesson

11 modules with worked examples (176 h guided)

Module 1 Map the market and the people 5 hUnderstand

Start with who needs hand-made gifts, woven items and decor and why. Around tourists, gift shops, hotels and online buyers drive demand in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other emirates.

  • List 10 offers or suppliers in the UAE with price per item in AED and who buys.
  • Interview 5 people from tourists, gift shops, hotels and online buyers: what do they buy now, how often, what do they pay, what annoys them?
  • Learn three terms: Heritage motif (a traditional pattern with cultural meaning); Batch (a group of items made together); Provenance card (a card that tells the maker and origin).
  • Write a one-page brief: user, need, hoped-for price, and the rule that matters most.
Worked example. Five interviews all say delay is the main pain. You write: 'Reliable hand-made gifts, woven items and decor, answer in one day, price near AED 85 per item.'

Activity. Complete the market table, five interview notes and the brief.

Standard to meet. Show ten offers priced; five users heard; one need stated in one sentence.

Module 2 Rules, hazards and licences 6 hApply

Know the rule before the work. Main hazards here: sharp tools and dust. Hazard thinking means list each step, ask what could go wrong, and decide the control.

  • Ask your local authority or free zone which licence, permit or inspection applies. Write the answer and the date you asked.
  • For each step (design; source materials; make in small batches; finish and pack) write the hazard and the control.
  • Use a hierarchy: remove the hazard, replace it, separate people from it, change the way of work, then protective equipment last.
  • Keep a one-page incident rule: who you call, what you stop, what you record.
Worked example. Step 'design': hazard = sharp tools; control = written check before start and a named supervisor; record = signed log line.

Activity. Draw the flow and mark hazard and control at each step.

Standard to meet. Show that every step has a hazard and a control; licence question answered in writing.

Module 3 Core workflow, step by step 12 hApply

The work is a short chain: design, source materials, make in small batches, finish and pack. Learn each link, then link them with records.

  • Write a checklist for each of the 4 steps: input, action, check, output.
  • Time each step three times and note the slowest.
  • Record a defect or delay each time it happens: what, where, cause, fix.
  • Measure items sold per items made; this is your main number.
Worked example. Three timed runs of 'source materials' take 42, 38 and 51 minutes. Average 43.7; the 51-minute run had a missing tool. Fix: a tool tray checked at start.

Activity. Run the chain three times (simulated or real) and fill the log.

Standard to meet. Show that three complete logs; the slowest step has a cause and a fix.

Module 4 Quality and measurement 10 hAnalyse

What you do not measure you cannot improve. Your main measure is items sold per items made.

  • Define items sold per items made: what counts, what does not, and over what period.
  • Draw a simple chart of the last 10 results and mark the average.
  • Find the top two causes of loss with a tally sheet, then fix the biggest one first.
  • Check measuring tools: are they accurate, clean and in date?
Worked example. Tally of 40 faults: 22 from late inputs, 9 from wrong settings, 9 other. Fix late inputs first: that is 55% of faults.

Activity. Collect a tally for one week and make a Pareto chart.

Standard to meet. Show that tally complete; biggest cause named; one fix planned with a date.

Module 5 Costing and pricing in AED 12 hEvaluate

Know the cost of one item before you set the price. Include materials, labour, energy, transport, licence share, waste and a safety margin.

  • Cost per item = total cost divided by items sold (not made).
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs divided by gain per unit.
  • Test the price with two users and write down the answer.
Worked example. At AED 85 per item and cost AED 48, gain is AED 37 and margin 43.5%. At 200 items a month, gross gain is AED 7,400. If fixed costs are AED 1,920, break-even is 52 items.

Activity. Build the cost sheet with AED on every line and have your mentor check it.

Standard to meet. Show no missing cost found; margin calculated twice by different routes.

Module 6 Serve and sell responsibly 12 hApply

Customers return when you keep promises. Promise only what your workflow can deliver.

  • Write a one-page offer: what is included, what is not, price, time, how you fix mistakes.
  • Write a short script for the first call or visit.
  • Agree dates in writing; confirm each change.
  • Handle a complaint in four steps: listen, apologise for the impact, fix, record what changed.
Worked example. Offer: 'Hand-made gifts, woven items and decor delivered within one working day; price AED 85 per item; faults corrected free within 3 days.'

Activity. Present the offer to two users and note changes.

Standard to meet. Show that offer has scope, price, time and fix rule; two users commented.

Module 7 Run the 30-day pilot 55 hCreate

Small, real and recorded. Change one thing at a time and learn quickly.

  • Keep a daily log: date, units, cost, delays, defects, safety notes.
  • Hold a 15-minute review each week with your mentor.
  • Write down every complaint and what you changed.
  • Take photos only where allowed and never of people without consent.
Worked example. Week 2 shows items sold per items made at 71%; week 4 at 84% after the tool tray and an earlier input check.

Activity. Complete 30 days of the log and four weekly reviews.

Standard to meet. Show 30 days logged; each defect has a cause; weekly notes kept.

Module 8 Review, decide and plan 38 hEvaluate

A decision beats a feeling. Compare plan with result and choose to scale, change or stop.

  • Make a before and after table: cost per unit, defects, delays, repeat users.
  • Write three lessons.
  • Decide scale, change or stop, with the numbers behind it.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
Worked example. Result: cost per item fell from AED 48 to AED 45.12; repeat users 6 of 9. Decision: scale to a second site; keep price.

Activity. Present to a review room and collect one written comment from a user.

Standard to meet. Show decision backed by numbers; two peer reviews given.

Module 9 Tell the story honestly 8 hCreate

Buyers pay for origin and care. The story must be true.

  • Write who made it, from what, and how long it took.
  • Use a heritage motif only with respect for its meaning.
  • Photograph on plain backgrounds with natural light.
  • Add a care card.
Worked example. A woven basket card says: 'Palm leaf, hand woven in four hours.' Price rises from AED 60 to AED 85 with the card.

Activity. Write and photograph three products with cards.

Standard to meet. Show that claims are true; photos clear.

Module 10 Pricing time and materials 9 hEvaluate

Handmade work has a time cost that many makers forget.

  • Cost = materials + labour hours at a fair rate + packaging + share of fees.
  • Test two prices with buyers.
  • Offer small and large versions.
  • Track which items sell slowly.
Worked example. Materials AED 18, 3 hours at AED 20 = 60, packaging 7: cost AED 85; price AED 140 gives margin 39%.

Activity. Price five items and justify each.

Standard to meet. Show labour included; margin computed.

Module 11 Selling at markets and online 9 hApply

Market stalls give feedback; online reaches farther.

  • Plan a stall: display height, price signs, change float.
  • List online with measurements and shipping weight.
  • Pack to avoid breakage.
  • Reply to messages within a day.
Worked example. A fragile item is wrapped in two layers and survives shipping; breakage falls from 1 in 10 to 1 in 50.

Activity. Run one stall day or one online listing week and record sales.

Standard to meet. Record complete; one change made.

levels L1 to L7

Slide by slide, with notes

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: What makes a craft authentic

On the slide: L1 Basic · What makes a craft authentic

Notes: Name a heritage motif, a batch and a provenance card and say who buys handicrafts. Notes: use one local craft as the example; the hazards are sharp tools and dust, so keep tools away from children.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Honest stories and respect for motifs

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Honest stories and respect for motifs

Notes: Explain that a product story must be true and that a heritage motif is used with respect for its meaning. Notes: show one true card and one made-up card and ask which is fair; check tool safety and dust masks.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Write a product card and photo

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Write a product card and photo

Notes: Write and photograph three products with true cards on a plain background. Notes: a card might say palm leaf, hand woven in four hours; use natural light and give maker, material and time.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Price five items

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Price five items

Notes: Price five items with materials, labour hours, packaging and fees and compute the margin. Notes: materials AED 18, 3 hours at AED 20 and packaging AED 7 make cost AED 85; a price of AED 140 gives a margin of 39 per cent.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Compare markets and online

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Compare markets and online

Notes: Compare one stall day with one online listing week and decide which to expand. Notes: set the rule first, for example expand if items sold per items made is above 50 per cent; the card raising a basket from AED 60 to AED 85 is a result to test.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Coach makers on batches and packing

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Coach makers on batches and packing

Notes: Lead a small group of makers on batch size, packing and a reply within a day. Notes: sample five packed items for breakage risk; double wrapping cut breakage from 1 in 10 to 1 in 50.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Design a craft brand system

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Design a craft brand system

Notes: Design a design, sourcing, batch, pricing and selling system for a group of makers with an audit and a 12-month plan. Notes: check every story claim and consent for any maker photo; present one risk, one saving and a pricing floor above cost.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.
