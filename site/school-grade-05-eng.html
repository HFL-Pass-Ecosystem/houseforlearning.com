Grade 05: English

8 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 05 English, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 8 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

Unit 1 Reading fiction and non-fiction Weeks 1 to 5Analyse

In this unit you work on 'Reading fiction and non-fiction', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: compare a story and an information text on the same topic. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Reading fiction and non-fiction' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: compare a story and an information text on the same topic, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Main idea in one sentence. Character: what they say and do shows what they are like (she gave away her lunch: generous). Drama: names on the left, stage directions in brackets. Method: Model run for 'Reading fiction and non-fiction'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Compare a story and an information text on the same topic: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 2 Pronouns, conjunctions and clauses Weeks 6 to 10Apply

In this unit you work on 'Pronouns, conjunctions and clauses', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: join ideas with conjunctions and use pronouns to avoid repeating. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Pronouns, conjunctions and clauses' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: join ideas with conjunctions and use pronouns to avoid repeating, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: In 'The girl is visiting Dubai in March', 'girl' is a common noun, 'Dubai' and 'March' are proper nouns and need capital letters. Method: Model run for 'Pronouns, conjunctions and clauses'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Join two sentences with a coordinating conjunction. Two sentences: "I like tea. I don't like coffee." Joined: "I like tea, but I don't like coffee." Use FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so, with a comma before it.
  2. Step 2, Join with a subordinating conjunction. "I took an umbrella because it was raining." "Although it was late, we kept working." Reason: a subordinate clause cannot stand alone; the main clause can.
  3. Step 3, Use a comma after an introductory clause. "When the bell rang, the students left." "If you study, you will pass." Reason: the comma separates the introductory clause from the main clause.
  4. Step 4, Use pronouns to avoid repeating. Repeated: "The girl took the girl's book and gave the girl's book to the girl's friend." Better: "The girl took her book and gave it to her friend." Pronouns: her, it.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Run-on: "I was tired I went to bed." Right: "I was tired, so I went to bed." Fragment: "Because it was raining." Right: "We stayed home because it was raining." A clause beginning with because needs a main clause.
  6. Step 6, Check the pronoun reference. Unclear: "The manager told the clerk that he was late." Who is "he"? Clear: "The manager told the clerk, 'You are late.'" or "The manager told the clerk that the manager was late." Reason: every pronoun must have one clear noun.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Join and shorten: "The girl was hungry. The girl made a sandwich. The sandwich was big." Model answer: "Because the girl was hungry, she made a big sandwich." Or: "The girl was hungry, so she made a big sandwich."

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Join ideas with conjunctions and use pronouns to avoid repeating: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 7 Vocabulary and word origins Weeks 31 to 33Apply

In this unit you work on 'Vocabulary and word origins', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: use prefixes, suffixes and roots to work out meanings. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Vocabulary and word origins' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: use prefixes, suffixes and roots to work out meanings, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Prefix: dis + appear = disappear. Origins: telephone = Greek tele (far) + phone (sound); biology = bios (life) + logos (study). Method: Model run for 'Vocabulary and word origins'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Use a prefix to unlock a meaning. "Pre-" means before: preview is a view before. "Inter-" means between: international is between nations. "Sub-" means under: submarine is under the sea.
  2. Step 2, Use a root to unlock families. Root "port" means carry: transport (carry across), export (carry out), portable (can be carried). Reason: one root makes many words, and knowing it helps you guess unfamiliar words.
  3. Step 3, Use a suffix to find word class. -tion makes a noun: educate becomes education. -able makes an adjective: read becomes readable. -ly makes an adverb: clear becomes clearly. Reason: the ending signals what the word does.
  4. Step 4, Trace an origin. The word "telephone" joins Greek tele (far) and phone (sound): a far sound. Also "telescope" (far + see). Reason: shared roots link words and give clues to meaning.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "The submarine is above the water" (a student thinking sub- means up). Right: "The submarine travels below the water." Check the prefix meaning against the context.
  6. Step 6, Work out an unknown word. Sentence: "The scientist wanted to transport the samples across the country." Clue: trans- (across) + port (carry). Meaning: to carry across. Check: it fits the sentence.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Define "unpredictable" and "export" using parts. Model answer: "unpredictable" = un- (not) + pre- (before) + dict (say) + -able (can be): cannot be said beforehand. "Export" = ex- (out) + port (carry): to carry out.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Use prefixes, suffixes and roots to work out meanings: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 8 Speaking and debate Weeks 34 to 36Understand

In this unit you work on 'Speaking and debate', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: give an opinion with reasons and respond to a different view. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Speaking and debate' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: give an opinion with reasons and respond to a different view, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Motion: 'Homework should be optional.' For: choice and rest. Against: practice and habits. Rebuttal: 'I understand that..., however...' Method: Model run for 'Speaking and debate'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, State your opinion in one sentence. "I believe schools should start later in the morning." Reason: a clear opinion tells the audience what you are arguing.
  2. Step 2, Support with reasons and evidence. "First, teenagers need more sleep; studies show tired students learn less. Second, later starts reduce late arrivals." Reason: each reason needs support so it is not just opinion.
  3. Step 3, Respond to a different view. Opponent: "A later start means a later finish." You: "That's true, but afternoon clubs can adjust their schedules." Reason: acknowledge the point, then answer it.
  4. Step 4, Use debate phrases. "I agree that..., but..." "That is a fair point; however..." "On the other hand..." Reason: these phrases keep a debate respectful and organised.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Weak: "You are wrong because it is just stupid." Better: "I disagree because evidence shows the opposite." Another error: attacking the person. Right: challenge the idea, not the speaker.
  6. Step 6, Close the argument. "In conclusion, a later start improves sleep, learning and punctuality, so I urge the school to change." Reason: a conclusion repeats your opinion and ends strongly.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Give an opinion on homework with two reasons and a reply to a different view. Model answer: "Homework should be short because rest matters, and because family time matters. Some say it builds discipline; however, short tasks can do that too."

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Give an opinion with reasons and respond to a different view: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Letters, sounds and first reading

On the slide: L1 Basic · Letters, sounds and first reading

Notes: Match letters to sounds and read short words and simple sentences aloud. Notes: use the sounds s, a, t, p, i, n; learners blend them into sat, pin and tap, then read a three-word sentence and clap the syllables.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Sentences and simple writing (this course)

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Sentences and simple writing

Notes: Write a clear sentence with a capital letter and full stop, and read a short story with understanding. Notes: give a picture prompt; learners say the sentence first, write it, and check capital, spacing and full stop with a partner before reading it aloud.

This course: Grade 05 English is at this level. Its units include Reading fiction and non-fiction; Pronouns, conjunctions and clauses; Informal and formal letters; Narrative writing and more.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Paragraphs, grammar and reading for meaning

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Paragraphs, grammar and reading for meaning

Notes: Write a paragraph of five connected sentences and identify nouns, verbs and adjectives in a text. Notes: colour nouns blue and verbs red in a short passage; learners then write about their street, using at least two adjectives and one linking word.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Essays, speaking and responding to texts

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Essays, speaking and responding to texts

Notes: Write a 300-word structured essay and give a short talk, quoting evidence from a text to support a view. Notes: model Point, Evidence, Explain; learners write one paragraph about a character, quote one line, and explain what it shows, then speak it aloud.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Analysis, argument and style

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Analysis, argument and style

Notes: Analyse a writer's choices of language and structure and write a sustained argument of 600 words. Notes: take two openings of different short stories; learners compare tone, sentence length and imagery in a table, then argue which opening works better and why.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Literary and linguistic study

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Literary and linguistic study

Notes: Evaluate texts through a critical lens, tracing how language changes across time and context in a researched essay. Notes: compare an older and a modern passage; learners list five differences in vocabulary and syntax and argue in 1,500 words what each reveals about its period.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Research, editing and authorship

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Research, editing and authorship

Notes: Produce an original piece of research or a polished long-form text, with a literature review and defended method. Notes: pick a narrow question about language in one genre; build a corpus of fifty samples, analyse patterns, write 5,000 words, and revise after external review.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

India (Bengaluru)

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Use AED in money problems; deliver in Arabic, English; personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Canada (Toronto)

United States

Singapore
