E-Commerce

Run a real e-Commerce pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small e-Commerce offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: commerce, retail and online selling. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know what e-commerce is 6 hUnderstand

E-commerce means selling goods or services over the internet. Start by learning the parts: a product, a page, a way to pay, and a way to deliver.

  • Name the four parts of a sale online: product, listing, payment, delivery.
  • Describe three ways to sell: your own web shop, a shared marketplace, and social media orders.
  • Explain what a customer sees from first click to parcel at the door.
  • List the words you will use: SKU, stock, order, refund, shipping rate.
  • Compare a web shop and a marketplace by who sets the price, who pays the fees and who keeps the customer list.
  • Trace the money in one order: what the buyer pays and where each part goes.
  • Identify what a shop must show to be trusted: business name, contact details and return policy.
Worked example. A person sells hand-made soap. Product: soap bar. Listing: photo, price in CAD, size. Payment: card. Delivery: small parcel by post. A web shop lets the seller set the price and keep the customer list; a marketplace brings visitors but takes a fee.

Activity. Buy one small item online. Write down every step you saw, from search to delivery.

Standard to meet. Task: list the parts of an online sale. Condition: using one real purchase. Standard: all four parts named with one example each.

Module 2 Choose a product and a customer 6 hApply

A good shop starts with one clear customer and one need. Do not start with a long list of products.

  • Describe your customer in three lines: who, where, what problem.
  • Compare ten similar listings: price, photos, reviews, delivery time.
  • Explain why a small niche can be easier to win than a big one.
  • Check that the product is legal to sell and safe to ship.
  • Calculate how many orders a month you need to cover your costs at a rough margin.
  • Identify one reason a customer would pick you over ten other listings: speed, size, price or story.
  • Test demand before buying stock: show a photo and price to ten people and count how many say they would buy.
Worked example. Customer: new parents in small towns who cannot find cloth wipes nearby. Ten listings range from 18 to 35 CAD with a median of 26 (the middle two prices are 25 and 27). Many have poor photos and slow delivery, so a price of 24 with clear photos and fast shipping fills the gap.

Activity. Fill a comparison table of ten listings and write one sentence on the gap you can fill.

Standard to meet. Task: choose a first product. Condition: with a ten-listing comparison. Standard: customer, gap and legal check written on one page.

Module 3 Follow the rules in Canada 10 hApply

Online sellers must follow tax, consumer and privacy rules. These differ by province, so always check the official source.

  • Explain that sales tax is charged on most sales; check the current small-supplier rule with the federal tax agency.
  • Describe that return, refund and price-display rules are set by each province; check your province.
  • Apply the privacy rule: collect only the customer data you need and keep it safe.
  • Check labelling rules, including French and English on many packaged goods, and import duty for goods from abroad.
  • Check the rules for your product type, such as food, children's goods or cosmetics, with the federal and provincial authority.
  • Show prices in CAD with taxes stated clearly; check the price-display rule in your province.
  • Keep records of sales, tax collected and costs for as long as the tax agency requires.
Worked example. A rules table row: Sales tax | federal tax agency | register when sales pass the limit | check each quarter | registration number saved. If registration takes 2 weeks and the label check takes 1 week, run both together and both are done in week 2.

Activity. Make a rules table for your product and province. Add the official page and the date you checked.

Standard to meet. Task: list the rules for an online shop. Condition: using official pages only. Standard: every row has who enforces it, what to do, and a date.

Module 4 Build a simple shop page 8 hEvaluate

Your listing is your shop window. Plain words, true photos and clear prices build trust.

  • Build a listing with a plain title, three true photos, size, material and price in CAD.
  • Show the delivery cost and time before checkout.
  • Write a short return policy in simple words.
  • Check the page on a phone, because many buyers use one.
  • Describe the product the way a buyer asks questions: size, colour, use and care.
  • Test the checkout from cart to payment confirmation with a small real order.
  • Write a plain-word description for each photo so a screen reader can read it.
Worked example. Title: Cotton wipes, set of 12, white. Photos: front, size next to a hand, in use. Price 24 CAD. Ships in 2 days. Returns accepted for 30 days if unused. Three testers found the price, delivery cost and return rule in 45, 38 and 52 seconds, an average of 45, so the page passes.

Activity. Build one full listing and ask three people to find the price, the delivery cost and the return rule in under one minute.

Standard to meet. Task: build a product listing. Condition: for a phone screen. Standard: three testers find price, delivery cost and returns in 60 seconds.

Module 5 Stock, price and shipping 8 hApply

Many online sellers fail on money, not on sales. Count every cost before you set the price.

  • Compute margin = price minus product cost, payment fee, packing and shipping.
  • Use reorder point = daily sales x supplier lead time + safety stock.
  • Compare shipping options by price, speed and tracking.
  • Analyse which products tie up cash without selling.
  • Calculate break-even orders: fixed costs per month divided by margin per order.
  • Compare three packing options by cost per parcel and damage rate.
  • Set a safety stock level from the longest supplier delay you have seen.
Worked example. Price 24, product 9, packing 1.50, payment fee 1, shipping paid by seller 5. Margin = 24 - 9 - 1.50 - 1 - 5 = 7.50 per order (31%). With 150 of fixed cost a month, break-even is 150 / 7.50 = 20 orders. Reorder point: 3 sales a day x 10 days of supplier delay + 12 safety stock = 42 units. Teaching numbers only.

Activity. Build a cost sheet for three products and find the one with the best margin per order.

Standard to meet. Task: price three products. Condition: all costs counted. Standard: margin per order and margin percent are correct and shown with units.

Module 6 Get found and get the first orders 6 hCreate

Nobody visits a shop they cannot find. Pick two or three routes and run them every week.

  • Apply simple search words: write titles the way buyers type.
  • Use direct contact: friends, local groups, small businesses that share your customer.
  • Build a short email or message list, only with consent.
  • Track the funnel: visits, carts, orders, and improve the weakest step.
  • Write a product title and first line using words from three real buyer searches.
  • Calculate cost per order from any paid promotion and compare it with margin.
  • Review which route brought the most orders and move time toward it.
Worked example. Four weeks: 600 visits, 30 carts, 12 orders. Visit-to-cart is 5%. The weakest step is cart to order (40%); test a clearer delivery price on the cart page. If that lifts cart-to-order to 50%, the same 30 carts give 15 orders, 3 more.

Activity. Run a four-week test of two routes and record visits and orders each week.

Standard to meet. Task: win first orders. Condition: two routes, four weeks. Standard: weekly numbers recorded and one change tested.

Module 7 Serve customers and protect them 6 hEvaluate

Good service brings repeat orders. Safe handling of data and payments keeps trust.

  • Answer messages within one working day and keep a written script for common questions.
  • Handle a late or damaged parcel: apologise, fix, record the cause.
  • Check security basics: strong sign-in, two-step verification, software updates.
  • Explain how you spot and refuse a fake order or chargeback attempt.
  • Write the return steps in order: customer asks, you reply, parcel comes back, refund is sent, with a number of days for each.
  • Review messages and reviews each week and list the top two complaints.
  • Store customer data in one protected place and delete what you no longer need; check the privacy rule.
Worked example. Parcel arrives damaged. Reply within a day, send a replacement, record 'thin box' as the cause, and change the box. If 4 of 40 parcels were damaged (10%) and after the change 1 of 40 is (2.5%), the fix worked.

Activity. Write a reply script for five common problems and a one-page privacy note.

Standard to meet. Task: handle a customer problem. Condition: using your scripts. Standard: reply in one working day with a fix and a recorded cause.

Module 8 Run the shop for real 8 hCreate

Now run the shop with real stock, real orders and a plan. Decide with numbers.

  • Run a 60-day pilot with a stop rule agreed in advance.
  • Review weekly: orders, margin, returns, complaints, cash.
  • Create a 12-month plan with three milestones and a budget.
  • Lead others: write the steps so a helper can pack and ship the same way.
  • Calculate pilot profit: orders x margin minus fixed costs.
  • Compare results with the stop rule on the planned date and write the decision.
  • Identify the next product from what customers asked for.
Worked example. Stop rule: if fewer than 30 orders in 60 days, stop and write lessons. Result: 54 orders at an average of 24 is 1,296 in sales; at 7.50 margin per order that is 405. With 150 of fixed cost, profit is 255, so the decision is to add one product.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and present your 12-month plan to three people.

Standard to meet. Task: run an online shop pilot. Condition: 60 days, real orders. Standard: results compared with the stop rule and a dated plan written.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
