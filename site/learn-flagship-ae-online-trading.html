Online Trading

Learn online trading for the UAE: rules, workflow, costing in AED and a recorded 30-day practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 177 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Understand Explain the UAE market, rules and hazards for online trading.
  • Apply Run the workflow: pick a niche, source, list and price, ship, handle returns.
  • Analyse Measure orders fulfilled without a complaint and find the biggest cause of loss.
  • Evaluate Cost and price in AED and judge scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a record pack a supervisor can audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a real or simulated 30-day pilot for buying and selling goods online in the UAE and keep a full record of every step, cost and result.

Condition

About 177 hours over 3 to 6 months. A partner site or a simulated customer, a free spreadsheet, a phone, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Hazards named with controls; every number has a unit and AED; records complete; rules confirmed with the local authority; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better.

Process learning only. Main risk to manage: fake goods and payment fraud. Follow UAE federal and emirate rules and any licence needed before you charge a customer. This is trading goods, not financial markets. Avoid any promise of quick profit.

Requirements: none. Currency: AED on every number.

lesson by lesson

11 modules with worked examples (177 h guided)

Module 1 Map the market and the people 5 hUnderstand

Start with who needs buying and selling goods online and why. Around online shoppers and small shops drive demand in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other emirates.

  • List 10 offers or suppliers in the UAE with price per order in AED and who buys.
  • Interview 5 people from online shoppers and small shops: what do they buy now, how often, what do they pay, what annoys them?
  • Learn three terms: Marketplace listing (a product page on a selling site); Landed cost (price plus freight, duty and fees); Return rate (the share of orders sent back).
  • Write a one-page brief: user, need, hoped-for price, and the rule that matters most.
Worked example. Five interviews all say delay is the main pain. You write: 'Reliable buying and selling goods online, answer in one day, price near AED 160 per order.'

Activity. Complete the market table, five interview notes and the brief.

Standard to meet. Price ten offers, interview five users, and state one need in one sentence.

Module 2 Rules, hazards and licences 6 hApply

Know the rule before the work. Main hazards here: fake goods and payment fraud. Hazard thinking means list each step, ask what could go wrong, and decide the control.

  • Ask your local authority or free zone which licence, permit or inspection applies. Write the answer and the date you asked.
  • For each step (pick a niche; source; list and price; ship; handle returns) write the hazard and the control.
  • Use a hierarchy: remove the hazard, replace it, separate people from it, change the way of work, then protective equipment last.
  • Keep a one-page incident rule: who you call, what you stop, what you record.
Worked example. Step 'pick a niche': hazard = fake goods; control = written check before start and a named supervisor; record = signed log line.

Activity. Draw the flow and mark hazard and control at each step.

Standard to meet. Name a hazard and a control for every step, and answer the licence question in writing.

Module 3 Core workflow, step by step 12 hApply

The work is a short chain: pick a niche, source, list and price, ship, handle returns. Learn each link, then link them with records.

  • Write a checklist for each of the 5 steps: input, action, check, output.
  • Time each step three times and note the slowest.
  • Record a defect or delay each time it happens: what, where, cause, fix.
  • Measure orders fulfilled without a complaint; this is your main number.
Worked example. Three timed runs of 'source' take 42, 38 and 51 minutes. Average 43.7; the 51-minute run had a missing tool. Fix: a tool tray checked at start.

Activity. Run the chain three times (simulated or real) and fill the log.

Standard to meet. Complete three logs, and give the slowest step a cause and a fix.

Module 4 Quality and measurement 10 hAnalyse

What you do not measure you cannot improve. Your main measure is orders fulfilled without a complaint.

  • Define orders fulfilled without a complaint: what counts, what does not, and over what period.
  • Draw a simple chart of the last 10 results and mark the average.
  • Find the top two causes of loss with a tally sheet, then fix the biggest one first.
  • Check measuring tools: are they accurate, clean and in date?
Worked example. Tally of 40 faults: 22 from late inputs, 9 from wrong settings, 9 other. Fix late inputs first: that is 55% of faults.

Activity. Collect a tally for one week and make a Pareto chart.

Standard to meet. Complete the tally, name the biggest cause, and plan one fix with a date.

Module 5 Costing and pricing in AED 12 hEvaluate

Know the cost of one order before you set the price. Include materials, labour, energy, transport, licence share, waste and a safety margin.

  • Cost per order = total cost divided by orders sold (not made).
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs divided by gain per unit.
  • Test the price with two users and write down the answer.
Worked example. At AED 160 per order and cost AED 110, gain is AED 50 and margin 31.3%. At 200 orders a month, gross gain is AED 10,000. If fixed costs are AED 4,400, break-even is 88 orders.

Activity. Build the cost sheet with AED on every line and have your mentor check it.

Standard to meet. Check that no cost is missing, and calculate the margin twice by different routes.

Module 6 Serve and sell responsibly 12 hApply

Customers return when you keep promises. Promise only what your workflow can deliver.

  • Write a one-page offer: what is included, what is not, price, time, how you fix mistakes.
  • Write a short script for the first call or visit.
  • Agree dates in writing; confirm each change.
  • Handle a complaint in four steps: listen, apologise for the impact, fix, record what changed.
Worked example. Offer: 'Buying and selling goods online delivered within one working day; price AED 160 per order; faults corrected free within 3 days.'

Activity. Present the offer to two users and note changes.

Standard to meet. Write an offer with scope, price, time and a fix rule, and collect comments from two users.

Module 7 Run the 30-day pilot 55 hCreate

Small, real and recorded. Change one thing at a time and learn quickly.

  • Keep a daily log: date, units, cost, delays, defects, safety notes.
  • Hold a 15-minute review each week with your mentor.
  • Write down every complaint and what you changed.
  • Take photos only where allowed and never of people without consent.
Worked example. Week 2 shows orders fulfilled without a complaint at 71%; week 4 at 84% after the tool tray and an earlier input check.

Activity. Complete 30 days of the log and four weekly reviews.

Standard to meet. Log 30 days, give each defect a cause, and keep weekly notes.

Module 8 Review, decide and plan 38 hEvaluate

A decision beats a feeling. Compare plan with result and choose to scale, change or stop.

  • Make a before and after table: cost per unit, defects, delays, repeat users.
  • Write three lessons.
  • Decide scale, change or stop, with the numbers behind it.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
Worked example. Result: cost per order fell from AED 110 to AED 103.40; repeat users 6 of 9. Decision: scale to a second site; keep price.

Activity. Present to a review room and collect one written comment from a user.

Standard to meet. Justify the decision with numbers, and give two peer reviews.

Module 9 Sourcing and landed cost 9 hApply

The cheap price is not the real price.

  • Add freight, duty, fees and returns.
  • Check certification of products.
  • Verify the supplier with a sample order.
  • Avoid fake or copied goods.
Worked example. Unit AED 40 + freight AED 6 + duty AED 2 + fees AED 12 = landed cost AED 60 per unit.

Activity. Compute landed cost for three products.

Standard to meet. Include all costs.

Module 10 Listings, fulfilment and returns 9 hEvaluate

Good listings reduce returns.

  • Accurate photos and sizes.
  • Pack and ship within the promise.
  • Handle returns by clear rules.
  • Track return rate.
Worked example. In one shop, the return rate fell from 12% to 7% after a size chart was added.

Activity. Write two listings and a returns rule.

Standard to meet. Check that the claims are accurate.

Module 11 Disputes, fakes and customs holds 9 hEvaluate

Every trader meets a damaged parcel, a fake claim or a shipment held at the border. A calm, written process protects both the buyer and the business.

  • Keep proof for every order: listing, invoice, packing photo and delivery record.
  • Sell only goods you can show are genuine; refuse anything you cannot trace to a supplier.
  • If a consignment is held, answer the authority in writing with the documents asked for and keep the buyer informed.
  • Settle disputes by the written return policy, not by mood, and record the outcome.
Worked example. A buyer says a phone case arrived cracked. The packing photo shows it intact, but the courier note shows a crushed box. You refund the full AED 45 price, claim AED 45 back from the courier, and add 2 cm more padding to the pack. The refund costs less than a lost review or a chargeback (the bank taking the AED 45 back) plus a bank fee of AED 25, AED 70 in total.

Activity. Write a one-page dispute procedure with a 48-hour reply rule and three example cases.

Standard to meet. State in the procedure the proof needed, who decides, a time limit in hours, and how records are kept.

levels L1 to L7

Slide by slide, with notes

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Name the parts of an online sale

On the slide: L1 Basic · Name the parts of an online sale

Notes: Name the five parts of one online sale: listing, order, payment, shipping and return. Notes: use one real product page; ask learners to point to each part and say that this course is about trading goods, never about financial markets.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Fake goods and payment fraud

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Fake goods and payment fraud

Notes: Describe two signs of a fake product and two signs of a risky payment. Notes: the main hazards are fake goods and payment fraud; show a supplier with no invoice and an order with a different billing and delivery name, and ask what to do before shipping.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Work out landed cost

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Work out landed cost

Notes: Calculate the landed cost of one product from its price, freight, duty and fees. Notes: check the sum on the course example, AED 40 + AED 6 + AED 2 + AED 12 = AED 60 per unit, and make sure every line has a unit and AED.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Run a 30-day trading pilot

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Run a 30-day trading pilot

Notes: Run a real or simulated 30-day pilot that covers pick, source, list, price, ship and return, with a daily log. Notes: the standard is complete records, no fake goods, and the margin worked out twice; at AED 160 per order and AED 110 cost the gain is AED 50, or 31.3 per cent.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Compare two products and decide

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Compare two products and decide

Notes: Compare two products by return rate, margin and orders fulfilled without a complaint, and choose to scale, change or stop. Notes: write the rule first, for example stop if the break-even of 88 orders a month is out of reach; at 200 orders the gross gain is AED 10,000 against AED 4,400 fixed costs.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Coach a team on proof and returns

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Coach a team on proof and returns

Notes: Set a rule that every order keeps four records (listing, invoice, packing photo, delivery record) and coach two packers to follow it. Notes: sample five orders each week and mark them on a checklist; the target is five out of five complete.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Design the trading system

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Design the trading system

Notes: Design a trading system for several product lines with supplier checks, fraud rules, a customs-hold routine and a 12-month plan. Notes: audit supplier traceability and refuse any item you cannot trace; present one risk, one saving and quarterly targets for orders fulfilled without a complaint.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.
