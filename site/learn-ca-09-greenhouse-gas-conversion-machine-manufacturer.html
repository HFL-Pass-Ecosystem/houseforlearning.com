Greenhouse gas conversion machine manufacturer

Run a pilot of greenhouse gas conversion machine manufacturer in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small greenhouse gas conversion machine manufacturer offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: advanced technology and manufacturing. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Understand greenhouse gas conversion 6 hUnderstand

These machines capture or convert gases such as carbon dioxide or methane into fuels, chemicals or solids.

  • Know the main routes: capture and store, convert to fuel, convert to materials, and burn off methane.
  • Learn the key numbers: tonnes captured per year, energy used per tonne and cost per tonne.
  • Buyers include industrial plants, landfills, farms, energy firms and municipalities.
  • Pick one source gas and one buyer type to start.
  • Find a real gas source near you (landfill, manure digester, industrial stack) and write its flow in tonnes per year, with where the figure came from.
  • Compare routes by cost per tonne, energy needed and product value; pick the simplest one that meets the buyer's need.
  • Write what a buyer must believe to buy: the gas flow is steady, the product sells and the permit can be obtained.
Worked example. Landfill gas contains roughly half methane. Using a 100-year factor of 25 (use the factor your reporting rule requires), converting 1,000 t of methane avoids about 1,000 x 25 = 25,000 t carbon-dioxide equivalent, before you subtract any carbon dioxide the process itself releases. If the unit converts only 80% of the 1,000 t, then 800 t x 25 = 20,000 t carbon-dioxide equivalent is avoided.

Activity. Write a one-page problem statement with source, gas, volume and buyer.

Standard to meet. Volumes have units and sources.

Module 2 Know environmental and safety rules 6 hApply

Emissions equipment is regulated.

  • Federal and provincial environmental law set emission limits and reporting.
  • Pressure vessels and gas systems follow technical safety codes and need certified welders and inspectors.
  • Carbon credits and offsets need approved methods and verifiers.
  • Permits for installation and operation come before building.
  • Ask the provincial environment office which permit covers a pilot unit and how long approval takes; write the answer down.
  • Ask the provincial technical safety authority about the code for pressure vessels and list the inspections.
  • Ask a verifier which offset method applies to your gas and what data they need.
Worked example. Row: 'Operate a gas conversion unit | provincial environment ministry | permit and monitoring plan | before start-up | permit on file'. Row: 'Build a pressure vessel | provincial technical safety authority | certified design and inspection | before use | inspection record'. If the permit takes 6 months and the build 4 months, start both together: earliest start-up is month 6, not month 10.

Activity. Build a rules table for a landfill pilot.

Standard to meet. Rows dated and sourced.

Module 3 Design a small conversion unit 10 hApply

Start with mass and energy balance.

  • Draw a block diagram: inlet gas, clean-up, reactor, product and vent.
  • Calculate mass in = mass out for one hour.
  • Choose sensors for flow, temperature, pressure and gas composition.
  • Plan safety with shut-offs, leak detection and venting.
  • Choose units (kg per hour) and use them everywhere on the diagram.
  • Size the reactor by residence time: volume = flow x time. Check one example by hand.
  • List the three worst failures (leak, over-pressure, loss of power) and the safe state for each.
Worked example. Inlet 100 kg per hour gas; product 62 kg; vent 4 kg; unaccounted 100 - 62 - 4 = 34 kg, or 34%, so investigate leaks or measurement error. After fixing a valve: product 90, vent 6, unaccounted 4 = 4%, within the 5% limit. A flow of 20 m3 per hour with 3 minutes of residence needs 20 x 3 / 60 = 1 m3 of reactor.

Activity. Complete a balance for a chosen design.

Standard to meet. Balance within 5%.

Module 4 Cost the machine and the tonne 8 hEvaluate

Customers buy cost per tonne avoided.

  • Spread the capital cost over the years of life.
  • List operating cost: power, catalysts, labour and maintenance.
  • List revenue from product sales and credits.
  • Work out break-even and payback.
  • Separate capital and operating cost lines, and give each a source: quote, price list or stated assumption.
  • Test three scenarios: 50%, 75% and 100% of design capacity, and compute cost per tonne for each.
  • Compare with the buyer's current cost per tonne (for example flaring or a carbon price) to see the gap.
Worked example. Machine 640,000 over a 10-year life = 64,000 a year; operating cost 150,000 a year; total 214,000. At 5,000 tonnes a year the cost is 214,000 / 5,000 = about 43 per tonne; at 75% use (3,750 tonnes) it is 214,000 / 3,750 = about 57. Compare with the credit or product price per tonne. At a sale price of 640,000 and a build cost of 420,000, the maker earns 220,000 per machine (about 34%). Teaching figures in CAD.

Activity. Compute cost per tonne and payback.

Standard to meet. Assumptions listed.

Module 5 Operate, measure and verify 8 hApply

Claims need data.

  • Install calibrated meters on every input and output.
  • Log inputs and outputs daily.
  • Arrange third-party verification of your results.
  • Keep a maintenance schedule and a stock of spares.
  • Write a daily check list: read meters, check alarms, walk the unit for leaks, sign and date.
  • Calibrate each meter on a schedule and keep the certificate; never adjust figures after the fact.
  • Compute uptime = hours running / hours planned and report it monthly.
Worked example. Monthly report: captured 410 t; uptime 92%; verified by auditor. In a 720-hour month, 92% uptime is 720 x 0.92 = about 662 running hours, so the capture rate was 410 / 662 = about 0.62 t per hour.

Activity. Keep a 4-week data log with calibration dates.

Standard to meet. Log complete.

Module 6 Win a pilot site 6 hCreate

Offer a risk-free test.

  • Approach site managers with data on their own gas.
  • Offer a pilot with shared cost.
  • Show your permits and safety plan.
  • Align the pilot with the site's climate targets.
  • Prepare a one-page site brief: gas flow, space, power, permit path and who pays what.
  • Offer a shared-cost pilot: the site gives space and gas, you give the unit and staff, and the data belongs to both.
  • Ask the site for its emissions target and show how the pilot contributes in tonnes.
Worked example. Funnel: 12 sites, 4 visits, 2 pilots; site-to-visit is 4 / 12 = 33% and visit-to-pilot is 2 / 4 = 50%. If a pilot costs 60,000 and the site pays 40%, the site pays 24,000 and you pay 36,000.

Activity. Pitch a pilot to three sites.

Standard to meet. Pitch has numbers.

Module 7 Manage safety, claims and community 6 hEvaluate

Be accurate and safe.

  • Run a hazard review before start-up.
  • Back every claim with verified data.
  • Consult neighbours and communities early.
  • Write an emergency plan and practise it.
  • Run a hazard review with someone outside the design team; log each hazard with a control and an owner.
  • Check each public claim against verified data; if it is not verified, say 'estimated' and give the method.
  • Hold an open meeting with neighbours and record their questions and your answers.
Worked example. Risk: 'Gas leak | 2 x 5 | detectors and shutoff | site lead | weekly test'. Score 2 x 5 = 10 of 25. Testing detectors weekly means 52 tests a year; any failed test triggers a repair within 24 hours.

Activity. Write risk register.

Standard to meet. Owners named.

Module 8 Pilot and scale 8 hCreate

Prove one unit.

  • Run the pilot for 90 days.
  • Compare cost per tonne with the plan.
  • Set a stop rule: if uptime is under 70%, stop and fix.
  • Plan manufacturing of the next unit after the review.
  • Write the run plan: 90 days, daily logs and a weekly review.
  • Compare measured cost per tonne with the plan each month and explain each difference.
  • Outline the manufacturing plan: parts list, suppliers and build time for the next unit.
Worked example. Uptime 88%; proceed. Over 90 days (2,160 hours), 88% is 2,160 x 0.88 = about 1,901 running hours, while the stop rule of 70% would be 2,160 x 0.70 = 1,512 hours.

Activity. Run pilot review.

Standard to meet. Review has decision.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is a mass balance?

Model answer: Mass in equals mass out; gaps show leaks or errors. (Covered in module 3: Design a small conversion unit. To pass that module: Balance within 5%.)

Q2. Why use calibrated meters?

Model answer: Claims need trusted data. (Covered in module 5: Operate, measure and verify. To pass that module: Log complete.)

Q3. Who verifies reductions?

Model answer: An approved third-party verifier. (Covered in module 5: Operate, measure and verify. To pass that module: Log complete.)

Q4. What comes before building?

Model answer: Permits. (Covered in module 2: Know environmental and safety rules. To pass that module: Rows dated and sourced.)

Q5. How compute cost per tonne?

Model answer: Annual cost divided by tonnes avoided. (Covered in module 4: Cost the machine and the tonne. To pass that module: Assumptions listed.)

Q6. What does the uptime figure measure?

Model answer: Hours running divided by hours planned. (Covered in module 5: Operate, measure and verify. To pass that module: Log complete.)

Q7. Why consult neighbours?

Model answer: Trust is needed, and a permit may depend on community input; check the provincial rule. (Covered in module 7: Manage safety, claims and community. To pass that module: Owners named.)

Q8. What triggers a fix?

Model answer: Uptime under 70%. (Covered in module 8: Pilot and scale. To pass that module: Review has decision.)

Q9. Who must build and inspect pressure systems?

Model answer: Certified welders and inspectors, following the technical safety code. (Covered in module 2: Know environmental and safety rules. To pass that module: Rows dated and sourced.)

Q10. What is payback?

Model answer: Capital divided by yearly net gain. (Covered in module 4: Cost the machine and the tonne. To pass that module: Assumptions listed.)

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
