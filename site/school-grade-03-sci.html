Grade 03: Science

7 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2Primary7 units36 teaching weeks36-week school yearBloom levels: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyse
  • Remember Name parts of a plant and their jobs, and describe how seeds spread.
  • Apply Link body features to habitats including desert and sea.
  • Apply Group nutrients and describe the journey of food.
  • Analyse Compare soils and describe how water is used and saved.
  • Understand Explain how shadows form and how they change in length.
  • Understand Describe pushes and pulls and the effect of friction.
  • Apply Link the sun, moon and weather to daily and seasonal change.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 03 Science, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 7 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

unit by unit

7 units with worked examples

Unit 1 Plant life Weeks 1 to 6Remember

In this unit you work on 'Plant life', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: name parts of a plant and their jobs, and describe how seeds spread. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Observe: see, touch, measure or watch a safe demonstration linked to 'Plant life' and write what you notice.
  • Explain: use the key words of the unit to say what is happening and why, in a few lines.
  • Use it: name parts of a plant and their jobs, and describe how seeds spread. Use a diagram, table or short calculation where it fits.
  • Test and conclude: say what evidence supports your answer and what you would change.
Worked example. Solved: Plants need light, water, air and warmth. Test: two seedlings, one in light and one in a dark cupboard, same water. After a week the dark one is pale and weak. Method: Model run for 'Plant life'. Question: what do we want to find out? Prediction: one sentence. Method: three safe steps with the equipment named. Record: a small table with units. Conclusion: one sentence that uses the evidence and the key words of the unit. What a good answer looks like: a fair test, measured values with units, and a conclusion that matches the table.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Name parts of a plant and their jobs, and describe how seeds spread: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 4 Rocks, soil and water Weeks 17 to 21Analyse

In this unit you work on 'Rocks, soil and water', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: compare soils and describe how water is used and saved. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Observe: see, touch, measure or watch a safe demonstration linked to 'Rocks, soil and water' and write what you notice.
  • Explain: use the key words of the unit to say what is happening and why, in a few lines.
  • Use it: compare soils and describe how water is used and saved. Use a diagram, table or short calculation where it fits.
  • Test and conclude: say what evidence supports your answer and what you would change.
Worked example. Solved: Soil layers: topsoil, subsoil, bedrock. Sand drains fast, clay holds water. Pour 100 ml on each and time the drain. Method: Model run for 'Rocks, soil and water'. Question: what do we want to find out? Prediction: one sentence. Method: three safe steps with the equipment named. Record: a small table with units. Conclusion: one sentence that uses the evidence and the key words of the unit. What a good answer looks like: a fair test, measured values with units, and a conclusion that matches the table.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Compare three soils: sandy, clay and loam. Put 100 mL of water on 100 g of each in a funnel with filter paper over a measuring cup. Time 2 minutes and measure the water collected.
  2. Step 2, Record: sandy soil 80 mL, loam 50 mL, clay 20 mL. Sandy soil lets water pass fastest because its grains are big with large gaps. Clay holds water because its grains are tiny and packed tight.
  3. Step 3, Work out how much water each soil kept: sandy 100 - 80 = 20 mL, loam 100 - 50 = 50 mL, clay 100 - 20 = 80 mL. Loam is best for most crops because it drains and holds a good amount.
  4. Step 4, List ways water is used at home: drinking, cooking, washing, toilet, gardening. Tally a family day: bath bucket 20 L, cooking 10 L, drinking 5 L, washing clothes 40 L, total 75 L.
  5. Step 5, Plan saving. Close the tap while brushing, which saves about 6 L. Collect rinse water for plants. If a leaking tap drips 1 L per hour, it wastes 24 L a day. Fix leaks to save water.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: which soil is best for a pond lining and why, and how much is wasted in a week by the 1 L per hour leak? Answer: clay, since it holds water; 24 x 7 = 168 L.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Compare soils and describe how water is used and saved: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Using my five senses

On the slide: L1 Basic · Using my five senses

Notes: Name the five senses and use them to describe a safe object. Notes: place a leaf, a smooth stone and a soft cloth in a bag; learners touch and describe each. Never taste or smell unknown things; teach children to ask first. Grades 1 and 2.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Fair tests (this course)

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Fair tests

Notes: Describe a fair test by naming what is changed, what is measured and what stays the same. Notes: plant two seed pots, one in light and one in the dark, with equal water and soil. Learners say the one changed factor, and draw both pots after a week. Grades 3 and 4.

This course: Grade 03 Science is at this level. Its units include Plant life; Animal life and adaptation; Food and digestion; Rocks, soil and water and more.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Measuring and recording

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Measuring and recording

Notes: Measure length, mass, time and temperature and record them in a table. Notes: a plant grows 2 cm in 4 days, which is 0.5 cm a day. Learners take three readings of 12, 14 and 13 mm, find the mean of 13, and say why repeats help. Grades 5 and 6.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Variables, graphs and density

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Variables, graphs and density

Notes: Apply a formula and draw a graph from a fair test. Notes: density equals mass over volume; a metal block of 54 g and 20 cm3 has density 2.7 g/cm3. Learners plot mass against volume for three blocks and read the gradient. Dry the benchtop before using water. Grades 7 and 8.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Evidence, anomalies and models

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Evidence, anomalies and models

Notes: Analyse data, spot an anomaly and judge how well a model fits the evidence. Notes: readings of 5.1, 5.3, 5.2 and 7.9 hold one anomaly at 7.9, so the mean of the rest is 5.2. Learners suggest a cause, repeat the trial and write a conclusion that cites the numbers. Grades 9 and 10.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Modelling and curve fitting

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Modelling and curve fitting

Notes: Apply least-squares fitting and error reasoning to link data to a model. Notes: the points (1,2.1), (2,3.9), (3,6.2), (4,7.8), (5,10.1) give slope 1.99 and intercept 0.05, with R squared 0.997. Learners explain what the intercept means and whether a straight line is right.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Study design and sample size

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Study design and sample size

Notes: Evaluate and create a study with a clear hypothesis, controls, sample size and ethics approval. Notes: to detect a 5-unit difference with spread 10 at 5 per cent significance and 80 per cent power needs about 63 per group. Learners write a pre-registered plan and list the risks and consent steps.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

India (Bengaluru)

Use INR in money problems; deliver in English, Hindi, Kannada; personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Use AED in money problems; deliver in Arabic, English; personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Use BRL in money problems; deliver in Portuguese (Spanish and English support); personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Canada (Toronto)

Use CAD in money problems; deliver in English, French; personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

United States

Use USD in money problems; deliver in English, Spanish; personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Singapore

Use SGD in money problems; deliver in English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Australia

Use AUD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Use HKD in money problems; deliver in Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Use GEL or ILS in money problems; deliver in Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Use KYD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Data Protection Act; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Mexico

Use MXN in money problems; deliver in Spanish, English; personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; guardian contact for every learner under 18.
