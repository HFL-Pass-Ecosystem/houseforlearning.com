consulting

Run a pilot of consulting in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small consulting offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: finance and accounting. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the words and the job of a consultant 6 hUnderstand

A consultant gives advice to solve a problem for a client. Start with the words and the role.

  • Learn the terms: client, scope, deliverable, proposal, fee, stakeholder, findings, recommendation.
  • A consultant advises; the client decides and acts.
  • Consulting needs listening, clear thinking and honest reporting.
  • Pick a field you know and write who you could help and with which problem.
  • Write a one-line problem statement for a client you could help.
  • Compare giving advice with doing the work and say which one you sell.
  • Rate yourself 1 to 5 on listening, analysing and writing, with one piece of proof for each.
Worked example. A shop owner says sales are flat. The consultant's job is to find out why and suggest options, not to run the shop. Three options costing $400, $900 and $0 let the owner choose with the facts in front of them.

Activity. Define ten words and write one sentence on the problem you could help solve.

Standard to meet. Task: define terms and name a problem. Condition: from memory, then check. Standard: meanings plain and the problem statement fits one sentence.

Module 2 Explain the steps of an engagement 6 hApply

An engagement has steps. If you name them, you can plan time and avoid surprises.

  • The steps: first meeting, scope, proposal, agreement, research, findings, recommendations, handover, follow-up.
  • Scope says what is in and what is out of the work.
  • Ask questions first: What is the problem? What have you tried? What does success look like? By when?
  • Agree how many meetings and reviews are included.
  • Draft a one-page engagement outline with the steps and dates.
  • List what is out of scope and write it in the proposal.
  • Add a buffer of about one hour in every ten to your plan.
Worked example. A client wants a 'full review'. You narrow it to: 'Review the order process and give three options within 30 days.' With 4 meetings of 1 hour, 10 hours of research and 6 hours of writing, the plan is 4 + 10 + 6 = 20 hours.

Activity. Write a question list and run a mock first meeting.

Standard to meet. Task: run a first meeting. Condition: with a pretend client for 30 minutes. Standard: problem, success measure, deadline and scope boundaries are written down.

Module 3 Analyse a problem and write findings 10 hApply

Now you apply a method. Gather facts, find causes, write clearly.

  • Collect facts: interviews, numbers, documents and observation.
  • Separate facts from opinions. Mark what you are sure of.
  • Ask 'why' five times to reach a root cause.
  • Write findings in plain language: what we found, why it matters, what to do, what it costs.
  • Draw a table of facts with a source beside each.
  • Test a cause by asking what would be different if it were true.
  • Compare two options on cost, effort and risk and recommend one.
Worked example. Fact: 30% of orders ship late. Cause: the packing bench is shared. Option: add a second bench, cost $400. If the shop ships 200 orders a month, 30% is 60 late orders; cutting it to 10% leaves 20, which is 40 fewer.

Activity. Complete a mini review of a small business with consent and write a two-page findings note.

Standard to meet. Task: write a two-page findings note. Condition: from at least three sources of facts. Standard: each finding has evidence, a cause and an option with cost.

Module 4 Follow Canadian rules for consulting 8 hEvaluate

Consultants work under business, tax and privacy rules.

  • Business structure and registration are set by provincial and federal rules; check the current rule with the local authority.
  • A consultant is usually self-employed, not an employee. The tax agency has tests for this; check the current rule with the tax agency.
  • Do not claim a licensed title you do not hold. Some fields, such as law, engineering, accounting and health, are regulated; stay inside your lane.
  • Keep client information private and follow privacy law. Sign a confidentiality agreement when asked.
  • Write down the topics you will refer to a licensed professional, and keep the list with your client notes.
  • Describe how you keep business and personal money apart.
  • Check whether you must collect sales tax and ask the tax agency.
Worked example. A client asks for legal advice about a contract. You say that is for a lawyer and offer to prepare the business questions to bring. You write down five questions so the meeting with the lawyer is short.

Activity. Build a table of rule, who sets it, what you must do and where you checked, for ten situations.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: using official public pages. Standard: each row has a dated source; unclear items say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.

Module 5 Price and propose 8 hApply

You are paid for outcomes and time. Work out your fee and write a clear proposal.

  • Time includes meetings, research, writing, reviews and admin.
  • Compare hourly, daily and fixed project fees.
  • A fixed fee needs a tight scope and a change process.
  • A proposal states the problem, the work, deliverables, timeline, fee and what you need from the client.
  • Calculate your hourly break-even from costs and your pay target.
  • Compare a fixed fee with hourly pricing for the same project.
  • Draft a change rule: extra work is quoted before it starts.
Worked example. Project: 24 hours at $70 = $1,680, plus 10% for changes: 1,680 x 0.10 = $168, total $1,848. If the work takes 30 hours, the fixed fee pays 1,848 / 30 = $61.60 an hour, below $70.

Activity. Write a one-page proposal for a pretend client and price it.

Standard to meet. Task: write a proposal. Condition: from your first-meeting notes. Standard: scope, deliverables, dates, fee and exclusions are stated and a mentor can follow the numbers.

Module 6 Find clients and show proof 6 hCreate

Clients hire consultants they trust.

  • Write a clear offer: who you help, what problem, what result.
  • Show a short case note with permission: problem, action, result.
  • Meet local business owners with a specific idea for each, not a general pitch.
  • Keep a list: contact, date, reply, next step. Contact only people who agree to hear from you.
  • Write a 100-word case note with the problem, the action and the result, using numbers.
  • Choose five local businesses and write one idea for each.
  • Track contacts, replies and calls and work out your reply rate.
Worked example. You email a café: 'Your online menu is hard to read on a phone. Here are three fixes. May I send a one-page review?' If 10 messages bring 3 replies and 1 call, the reply rate is 3 / 10 = 30%.

Activity. Write one case note and send ten tailored messages.

Standard to meet. Task: run a two-week outreach. Condition: with consent rules followed. Standard: ten messages sent, replies logged and one lesson written.

Module 7 Protect clients, ethics and quality 6 hEvaluate

Trust is the product. Protect it.

  • Tell the client about any conflict of interest in writing.
  • Do not promise results you cannot control. Report what you found, even if it is not welcome.
  • Keep client files secure and delete them as agreed.
  • Write an agreement: scope, fees, payment dates, confidentiality, ownership of work and how to end it. Ask a lawyer to review larger deals.
  • Describe how you respond if a client asks you to change a finding.
  • List where client files are stored and the date you will delete them.
  • Draft a conflict-of-interest sentence for your agreements.
Worked example. Your agreement says: 'The consultant gives advice; the client decides. The report is owned by the client once paid in full.' It also asks for a deposit of 50% of $1,848, which is $924, before work starts.

Activity. Write an agreement template and a delivery checklist.

Standard to meet. Task: produce a template and checklist. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: scope, fees, confidentiality and ownership are in plain words.

Module 8 Run a real consulting project and decide 8 hCreate

Now you run it: deliver, measure and choose what to do next.

  • Set a pilot goal such as one paid or agreed project with a clear result.
  • Record hours, fee, scope changes and client feedback each week.
  • Ask the client what changed because of your advice.
  • Choose: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Calculate your effective rate as the fee divided by hours.
  • Record each scope change and what you charged for it.
  • Evaluate the result against the success measure agreed at the start.
Worked example. After 45 days: 1 project, 26 hours, fee $1,820, two scope changes agreed. That is 1,820 / 26 = $70 an hour. You change the template to price changes up front.

Activity. Run the project and present a 12-month plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: deliver one project. Condition: real client and signed agreement. Standard: deliverables on time, log complete and the plan uses numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
