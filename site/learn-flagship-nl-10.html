Agriculture and Fishing: Sustainable Practice

Plan and run one small, sustainable growing or catch-handling project and record yields, costs and care for land or water.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 140 h guidedPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Plan and run one small, sustainable growing or catch-handling project and record yields, costs and care for land or water.

  • Understand Explain how soil, water and weather shape a plan.
  • Apply Run a small plot or handling process.
  • Analyse Analyse yield and cost.
  • Create Create a care plan for land or water.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and run one small sustainable production or handling project for 60 days with a care plan, yield record and cost sheet.

Condition

About 140 hours over 3 to 6 months. Small plot, tank or partner holding, free spreadsheet, adviser, mentor and peer circle.

Standard

Site plan; care plan; 60-day log of inputs and yield; cost per kilo; lessons; at least 70% on the check.

Learning only. Follow your local law and get written permission before you work with real customers, premises, data or money.

Requirements: none. Who it is for: learners 16 and over, workers changing field, small-business starters, teams. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (140 h guided)

Module 1 Read the site 10 hUnderstand

Soil, water, light and wind decide what can grow or live on a site. Reading the site before you plan saves money and failed crops. You make a simple map and test the basics.

  • Test soil with a simple kit for acidity (pH) and note texture, for example sandy, loamy or clay, by feeling a moist sample.
  • For water, test or ask a local adviser about temperature, oxygen and clarity, and record the date and place of each test.
  • Map the sun hours, wind direction, slope and places where water collects.
  • Check local rules about land use, water use and wildlife, and check with your local authority before you start.
  • Write a short site note saying which crops or species fit and which do not.
Worked example. Your 10 m2 plot has a soil pH of 6.5, which suits salad leaves, and the north corner stays wet after rain. You choose raised beds for that corner and keep the dry part for direct sowing.

Activity. Build a site map with tests, sun and wind notes, wet and dry areas and a short list of suitable crops.

Standard to meet. Show that the map and tests are dated, rules are checked, and the site note links each choice to a result.

Module 2 Choose and plan 14 hCreate

Small and well-chosen beats big and wild. You pick one crop or species, write a calendar and plan your inputs. A clear plan makes your records easy to compare later.

  • Pick one crop or species that fits your site, season and skills, and avoid starting with several at once.
  • Write a calendar with sowing, care, harvest and sale dates and the number of days between them.
  • Plan inputs such as seed, compost, water and tools, with quantities and costs.
  • Set a target yield and a target cost per kilogram before you start, so that you can judge results later.
  • Ask an adviser to look at the plan and note any change.
Worked example. Salad leaves need about 28 days from sowing to harvest, so you sow a new strip every 14 days. Over 8 weeks that gives 4 sowings, and you set a target of 20 kg from 10 m2.

Activity. Write a plan with a calendar, input list with quantities and costs, and targets for yield and cost.

Standard to meet. Show that the plan has dates, quantities, targets and an adviser comment.

Module 3 Inputs and care 20 hApply

Water, feed and tools are your inputs, and using them wisely saves money and protects the environment. You measure what you use so you can improve it. Clean tools and tanks prevent disease.

  • Measure water by volume each time, for example with a marked can or a meter, and write it in the log.
  • Use compost or other organic feed where possible, and record the weight or volume used.
  • Water at cool times of day, early morning or evening, to reduce loss to the sun.
  • Clean tools, tanks and containers after use, and keep a note of the date.
  • Compare inputs against your plan each week and adjust when use is too high.
Worked example. At 20 L per watering, 3 times a week for 8 weeks, you use 20 x 3 x 8 = 480 L. A drip line cuts this by 40%, so you use 480 x 0.60 = 288 L, saving 192 L.

Activity. Keep a weekly input log with water, feed and cleaning, and compare it to your plan.

Standard to meet. Show that the log shows amounts and units for every week, and differences from the plan are explained.

Module 4 Pests, disease and welfare 16 hApply

Prevention is the first answer to pests, disease and poor welfare. Regular checks find problems early and help you choose the least harmful control. Animals and fish need clear welfare care under local rules.

  • Check a sample of 20 plants or animals weekly and note signs of damage, disease or stress.
  • Use prevention first, such as netting, crop rotation, clean water and good spacing.
  • If control is needed, choose the least harmful method first, and check with your local authority and adviser before using any treatment.
  • For animals or fish, provide space, clean water, suitable feed and shelter, and remove stressed or sick ones as advised.
  • Record every action and the result a week later.
Worked example. In a weekly check of 20 plants, 3 have bird damage, which is 3 / 20 = 15%. Your action level is 10%, so you add netting and the next check shows 1 of 20, which is 5%.

Activity. Complete weekly checks on a sample, record the results as percentages and write one prevention action.

Standard to meet. Show that checks are dated, percentages are correct and each action has a result recorded.

Module 5 Run the 60 days 40 hApply

Running the project means caring every day and recording every week. You weigh harvest and note weather. These records are the evidence for everything you decide afterwards.

  • Follow your calendar and write down any change and the reason.
  • Record daily or weekly water, feed, labour hours and weather, using the same units each time.
  • Weigh each harvest on the same scale and write the date, weight and quality.
  • Calculate yield per square metre or per tank volume at each harvest.
  • Take photos at the start, middle and end to show progress.
Worked example. A harvest of 18 kg from a 10 m2 plot gives a yield of 18 / 10 = 1.8 kg per m2. If you plan 20 kg, you reached 18 / 20 = 90% of your target.

Activity. Run the 60 days with a log of inputs, weather and harvest weights, and calculate yield.

Standard to meet. Show that the log has no long gaps, weights have dates and units, and yield is correct.

Module 6 Cost per kilo 14 hEvaluate

Cost per kilo shows whether the project can pay for itself. You add all costs and divide by the weight sold or used. Comparing with a market price shows whether your result is competitive.

  • List all costs, such as seed, water, tools, packaging and labour hours at a fair rate, with currency on each line.
  • Add the lines to get the total cost.
  • Divide total cost by the kilograms harvested to get cost per kilo.
  • Compare to the local market price per kilo and note the difference.
  • Find the largest cost line and write one idea to reduce it.
Worked example. Seed EUR 6, water EUR 4, tools share EUR 8 and labour EUR 8 give a total of EUR 26. Cost per kilo is 26 / 18 = EUR 1.44, and if the market pays EUR 3 per kilo the gross margin is 3 - 1.44 = EUR 1.56 per kilo.

Activity. Build a cost sheet with all costs, cost per kilo and a comparison to the market price.

Standard to meet. Show that all lines have currency, the division is correct and the comparison is shown.

Module 7 Storage and sale 12 hApply

Fresh produce loses quality fast. Cooling, clean packing and quick sale protect value and food safety. You record how much you sell, share or lose.

  • Cool the harvest quickly and keep it cool, because warmth speeds spoilage.
  • Pack in clean containers and label them with the date and contents.
  • Follow local rules on selling food or fish, and check with your local authority about labelling and hygiene.
  • Sell or share within a few days, and record the amounts sold, given away and wasted.
  • Calculate waste as a percentage and think of one way to reduce it.
Worked example. Of 18 kg harvested, you sell 12 kg at EUR 3, which is EUR 36, share 4 kg and lose 2 kg. Waste is 2 / 18 = 11%, and the cold box keeps leaves fresh for 5 days.

Activity. Sell or share the harvest and record the amounts sold, shared, lost and the income.

Standard to meet. Show that the amounts add up to the harvest weight, income is correct and waste is shown as a percentage.

Module 8 Review and care plan 14 hEvaluate

A review compares plan with result and plans the next season. Good practice leaves land or water in better condition. You write a short care plan for soil or water.

  • Compare planned and actual yield, cost and time in one table with percentages.
  • Write three lessons, each with a number from your records.
  • Plan the next season, such as a different crop to rest the soil, for example beans after leaves.
  • Write a care plan for soil or water, with compost, cover crops, clean water or reduced inputs.
  • Set a date to review the plan and a target number to reach.
Worked example. Planned yield was 20 kg and actual yield was 18 kg, which is 90% of plan. Planned cost per kilo was EUR 1.30 and actual was EUR 1.44, so cost was about 11% higher, and you plan to cut water use.

Activity. Present a review with plan against actual, three lessons and a care plan for next season.

Standard to meet. Show that the table shows percentages, each lesson cites a number and the care plan has dated actions.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: your records, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why test the site before planting?

Model answer: To match the crop or species to the soil or water, so you do not waste seed, time and money. (Covered in module 1: Read the site. To pass that module: The map and tests are dated, rules are checked, and the site note links each choice to a result.)

Q2. Why start with one crop or species?

Model answer: It is easier to learn, record and compare, and mistakes cost less. (Covered in module 2: Choose and plan. To pass that module: The plan has dates, quantities, targets and an adviser comment.)

Q3. Why measure inputs?

Model answer: You cannot improve what you do not measure, and records show waste and savings. (Covered in module 3: Inputs and care. To pass that module: The log shows amounts and units for every week, and differences from the plan are explained.)

Q4. What is the first step against pests?

Model answer: Prevention and regular checks, before any treatment. (Covered in module 4: Pests, disease and welfare. To pass that module: Checks are dated, percentages are correct and each action has a result recorded.)

Q5. What is the yield per m2 for 18 kg from 10 m2?

Model answer: 18 / 10 = 1.8 kg per m2. (Covered in module 5: Run the 60 days. To pass that module: The log has no long gaps, weights have dates and units, and yield is correct.)

Q6. What is the cost per kilo for EUR 26 and 18 kg?

Model answer: 26 / 18 = EUR 1.44 per kilo. (Covered in module 6: Cost per kilo. To pass that module: All lines have currency, the division is correct and the comparison is shown.)

Q7. Why cool the harvest quickly?

Model answer: Cooling slows spoilage, so the produce keeps its quality and value for longer. (Covered in module 7: Storage and sale. To pass that module: The amounts add up to the harvest weight, income is correct and waste is shown as a percentage.)

Q8. Why plan the next season and care for soil or water?

Model answer: Care keeps the land or water healthy so future yields stay good, and planning avoids repeating mistakes. (Covered in module 8: Review and care plan. To pass that module: The table shows percentages, each lesson cites a number and the care plan has dated actions.)

Q9. What is the task of this course, in one sentence?

Model answer: Plan and run one small sustainable production or handling project for 60 days with a care plan, yield record and cost sheet. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. What standard must your final evidence meet?

Model answer: Site plan; care plan; 60-day log of inputs and yield; cost per kilo; lessons; at least 70% on the check. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Shared with schools and community groups. Note for the guide: ask one learner to explain it to a neighbour and check the words are right.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation, Guided tasks with a checklist. Speaker note: Learner does guided tasks from the first three modules (Read the site; Choose and plan; Inputs and care) with a checklist. Evidence is a small set of dated records. Note for the guide: sit beside the learner for the first record, then step back.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner, Run the full task alone (this course). Speaker note: Task: Plan and run one small sustainable production or handling project for 60 days with a care plan, yield record and cost sheet. Standard: Site plan; care plan; 60-day log of inputs and yield; cost per kilo; lessons; at least 70% on the check. Note for the guide: a mentor reviews the records; two peers mark with the rubric above.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist, One narrow area to depth. Speaker note: Learner picks one area, for example Run the 60 days (the longest module at 40 h), and goes deeper than this course asks. Learner then teaches a peer circle of three or four. Note for the guide: check the teaching, not only the work.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced, Lead a team and review evidence. Speaker note: Learner leads a small team or designs a service in this field and marks other people's evidence against the rubric. Roles this points to: Farm assistant; Greenhouse helper; Fish-handling assistant. Note for the guide: sample three records from the team and compare marks.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert, Set and audit the standard. Speaker note: Learner writes the method for Agriculture and Fishing: Sustainable Practice as a short guide, audits other learners' evidence, and publishes worked examples back to learners. Note for the guide: worked examples keep currency and units on every number and are checked by a second expert.

Notes: credits stack and you can step in and out. This course is L4. Aligned with, not accredited by, national credit frameworks.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local custom changes rules, language, currency and who you practise with. Check each rule with your local authority before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
