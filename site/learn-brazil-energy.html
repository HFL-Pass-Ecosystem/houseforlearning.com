Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, start and run energy in São Paulo: serve a home, firm or utility, deliver a power service or installation, keep records, and show a working month of numbers.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. Own phone or computer, real customers or a real practice site, a peer and a mentor for the final review. Local rules and licences checked against official sources.

Standard

Every module standard is met; all claims are true and backed by records; peers and a mentor mark the evidence 'Meets' or better.

Who it is for: students, graduates, career changers, owners, workers and returners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, tracker, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know your customer and market 4 hAnalyse

Every sound business in energy begins with one clear customer and one clear problem. In São Paulo and the wider state, demand is real but crowded, so a small, well-described customer group wins before a broad one.

  • In energy: Customer groups: homes, small firms, and large users; the buying driver is bill reduction, reliability, or both.
  • Write the customer in one sentence: a home, firm or utility. Add the place, the moment of need and what they use today.
  • List five rivals or substitutes within reach. For each, record price, strength, weakness and how people reach it.
  • Interview at least eight real customers with open questions. Record exact words, not your summary.
  • Count the market in units: how many kilowatts installed could one area buy in a month? Use two public sources and your own count, then take the lower figure.
  • Choose one first segment and say why the others wait.
Worked example. Weak: 'Everyone needs a power service or installation.' Strong: 'Our first customer is a home, firm or utility within 8 km of the centre, who today pays R$ 4.620 per kilowatt installed and complains about delay. We target 18 kilowatts installed a month by month three.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: ten customers are interviewed for energy. Seven say price, two say speed, one says trust. Question: which first message do you write, and which interview record backs it? Model answer: lead with the kilowatt installed price and speed promise, keep trust proof as the second line, and cite the seven price answers by date.

Activity. Write the one-sentence customer, a five-row rival table and eight interview records. Share them with a peer for a challenge round.

Standard to meet. Customer is specific; rival table has prices; every claim traces to an interview or a source.

Module 2 Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil 4 hUnderstand

Brazil asks every trader to be visible and accountable. Know which register, tax regime and sector rules apply before the first sale, and check each against the current official text.

  • In energy: Electrical work must follow the national installation standard and use certified equipment; the energy regulator (ANEEL) sets rules for connection to the grid.
  • Choose a legal form: micro-entrepreneur (MEI) only if your activity appears on the official MEI list, or a company registered with a CNPJ. Regulated activities need their sector authorisation first. Check the current limits and lists on the official portal.
  • Learn the tax regime that fits your size and write down which taxes you will collect and pay, and when.
  • Find the sector rules for energy: licences, inspections, professional registers. List each with the office that issues it and the renewal date.
  • Personal data of customers falls under Brazil's data protection law (LGPD). Collect only what you need, say why, keep it safe, and name who answers questions.
  • Consumer law gives buyers clear rights on information, returns and complaints. Put your terms in plain Portuguese.
Worked example. Checklist for a new energy trader: 1) legal form (CNPJ, or MEI only if your activity is on the official list); 2) city operating licence; 3) sector licence or register; 4) tax set-up; 5) privacy notice; 6) written terms. Each has an owner and a date.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a neighbour says registration can wait until the first R$ 21.000 of sales. Question: what do you do? Model answer: do not delay; write the compliance table first, confirm the register, tax and sector licence with the official office, and start only where the table shows no open blocker.

Activity. Build your own six-line compliance table for your first location. Mark what is done, what is pending and who confirms it.

Standard to meet. Every line cites an official source; nothing is assumed; pending items have dates. Local law always wins over this course.

Module 3 Design the offer 4 hCreate

An offer states what the customer gets, how well, by when and at what price. For a power service or installation, the offer must make the quality visible and the next step easy.

  • In energy: Offer a sized design with expected yearly savings shown as a range, not a promise.
  • Describe the core offer in three lines: what is delivered (a power service or installation), the standard it meets, and the time it takes.
  • Add one proof for each promise: a sample, a test result, a guarantee or a customer record.
  • Price from cost upward and from customer value downward, then pick a price inside both.
  • Write a simple one-page terms sheet: what is included, what is extra, how to cancel.
  • Create two options at most, so choice stays easy.
Worked example. Offer: 'A power service or installation, delivered within agreed time, checked against a written standard, price R$ 4.200 per kilowatt installed. Proof: a dated record the customer can check and sign. Changes or cancellation by written agreement before work begins.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a customer asks for a discount from R$ 4.200 to R$ 3.780 per kilowatt installed. Question: decide with numbers. Model answer: contribution falls from R$ 1.200 to R$ 780 per kilowatt installed; offer an extra of value (for example faster service) before cutting price, and accept only if the lower contribution still clears break-even.

Longer notes. Define the offer per kilowatt installed. A typical home system is 5 kilowatts: panels, inverter, cables, mounting and the crew's work. Inspect the roof, the electrical panel and the shade before you quote, and write the expected yearly generation as an estimate to be measured, never as a guarantee. Compute the cost per kilowatt from equipment, crew days, transport and a small allowance for returns. Keep the office, vehicles and salaries as fixed cost. Before you sign, ask the distributor what its grid-connection request requires and confirm the electrical safety rules for the work.

Extra worked example. The price is R$ 4.200 per kilowatt installed. Equipment, crew and transport cost R$ 3.000 per kilowatt, so each kilowatt leaves R$ 1.200, a margin of 28,6%. A 5-kilowatt home, a worked-example size, sells for R$ 21.000 and leaves R$ 6.000. At 30 kilowatts a month, revenue is R$ 126.000 and contribution R$ 36.000. After R$ 20.000 of fixed cost, the result is R$ 16.000. Break-even at 16,7 kilowatts is a little over three such homes, so the six homes of a normal month sit well above it.

Activity. Write the offer card, the proof list and the terms sheet. Test them with three customers and note every point where they hesitated.

Standard to meet. Promise, proof and price are consistent; terms fit one page; three customer reactions are recorded.

Module 4 Run the work step by step 5 hApply

Good operations make results repeatable. For energy, write each step so a new person could follow it on their first day, and measure each step.

  • In energy: Follow a work order: survey, design, approval, install, test, commission, handover pack.
  • Map the process from first contact to paid and closed. Typical stages: enquiry, quote, preparation, delivery of the kilowatt installed, check, payment, follow-up.
  • Name the key resource at each stage: tested equipment and a qualified electrician. Note what happens if it is missing.
  • Write a one-page standard procedure per stage: who, what, tool, time, record.
  • Choose three measures: speed, quality (errors per 100) and customer rating. Review them every week.
  • Fix the cause of a defect, not the symptom. Ask 'why' five times and record the answer.
Worked example. Stage record: 'Prepare. Owner: lead. Time: 30 min. Tool: checklist. Record: signed sheet. Defect found on 1 of 7 kilowatts installed last week; cause: missing check on tested equipment and a qualified electrician; fix: add check at start, review next Friday.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: of 30 kilowatts installed last month, 2 had a defect traced to tested equipment and a qualified electrician. Question: what is the defect rate and what is your first action? Model answer: about 7 defects per 100; stop the cause (add a check at the start), correct affected customers, log the cause, and re-measure next week.

Activity. Draw the process map and write procedures for the two riskiest stages. Run each once and time it.

Standard to meet. Procedures are followable by a stranger; measures have weekly numbers; one defect is traced to a root cause.

Module 5 Safety, quality and risk 4 hEvaluate

The most serious risk in this field is electrical fault, fire or poor design. A calm, written risk routine protects people first and the business second.

  • In energy: Lock-out and tag-out before any work on live circuits; test for dead with a proven tester.
  • List ten things that could go wrong. Score each for how likely (1 to 5) and how serious (1 to 5). Multiply and sort.
  • For the top five, choose a control: remove the hazard, replace it, guard it, train people, or use protective gear, in that order of preference.
  • Write who does what in an emergency, and rehearse it once a quarter.
  • Keep an incident and near-miss log. Near-misses are free lessons.
  • Check that insurance and legal duties match the risk you actually carry.
Worked example. Risk row: 'Hazard: electrical fault, fire or poor design. Likelihood 3, severity 5, score 15. Control: written check before each kilowatt installed, trained staff, log sheet. Owner: site lead. Review monthly.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a near-miss occurs: electrical fault, fire or poor design nearly happened but no one was hurt. Question: what do you do in the next 24 hours? Model answer: make safe, record the event, ask the five whys, add or tighten one control, brief the team, and set a review date; near-misses count as lessons, not blame.

Activity. Build a ten-row risk register and a one-page emergency plan. Run a ten-minute drill with a peer and note two improvements.

Standard to meet. Top risks have controls ranked by preference; drill done; log started. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.

Module 6 People, skills and service 4 hApply

People deliver the promise. Define roles, train to a standard and give feedback in the open.

  • In energy: Train electricians on testing, earthing and working at height; keep their licences current.
  • Write a role card for each person: purpose, five tasks, the standard for each, and who to ask for help.
  • Train by showing, doing with support, then doing alone with a check. Record sign-off per task.
  • Set service rules for answering customers: reply times, tone, how to apologise and fix.
  • Hold a ten-minute team check each day: yesterday's problem, today's plan, one risk.
  • Build a growth path: what a new person can do in week 1, month 3 and month 12.
Worked example. Training record: 'Task: handle a complaint. Shown 2 May; done with support 4 May; done alone 8 May, checked: apology, fix, follow-up call. Signed by lead.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a new hire completes training but a customer complaint shows a skipped step. Question: whose gap is it? Model answer: first the system: check the role card, the training sign-off and the checklist; then coach the person and fix the document so the step cannot be skipped.

Longer notes. Plan crews from the jobs on the calendar. Write for each job the crew size, the days on the roof, the tools, the lockout steps and the final tests. Give new installers a practical test on working at height, harness use, cable crimping and reading a panel diagram before they join a crew. Record near misses and check harnesses before every job. Keep a commissioning sheet signed by the client. Match each task to the electrical-safety and work-at-height training the rules require. Hold a ten-minute safety briefing before every job and sign the sheet.

Extra worked example. A 5-kilowatt home takes a crew of two for two days, which is 4 person-days (worked-example figures, not market data). With 30 kilowatts, the month has 6 homes and 24 person-days of work. Two people working 22 days give 44 person-days, so the crew is used 55% of the time. Adding two homes brings the load to 32 person-days, 73% of capacity. At 8 homes the crew still has 12 person-days free, enough for a ninth home at 36 person-days, 82% of capacity.

Activity. Write two role cards and a training plan for one new hire. Role-play a hard customer conversation twice and review.

Standard to meet. Role cards have measurable standards; training shows sign-off steps; service rules are written in plain words.

Module 7 Money: cost, price and break-even 5 hAnalyse

A business lives or dies on a few numbers. Know your contribution per unit, your fixed costs and the volume you need.

  • In energy: Quote with a margin on materials and labour; include a 5% contingency and the cost of grid approval time.
  • Contribution per kilowatt installed = price minus variable cost. Fixed costs are paid whether you sell or not.
  • Break-even units = fixed cost divided by contribution. Plan to pass it by a safe margin.
  • Make a 12-month cash plan. Cash timing matters as much as profit: customers may pay late, suppliers may want money first.
  • Keep money for business and money for the owner apart.
  • Test the plan: what if sales fall by a quarter, or cost rises by a tenth?
Worked example. Worked example, all figures in reais: price R$ 4.200, variable cost R$ 3.000, contribution R$ 1.200 per kilowatt installed. Fixed cost R$ 20.000 a month. Break-even = R$ 20.000 / R$ 1.200 = 17 kilowatts installed a month. At 30 kilowatts installed a month, profit = R$ 1.200 x 30 - R$ 20.000 = R$ 16.000. These are teaching figures that belong to this example only; later examples and the case study use their own figures. Use your own.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: sales fall to 22 kilowatts installed a month. Question: what is the monthly result? Model answer: R$ 1.200 x 22 - R$ 20.000 = R$ 6.400; compare with break-even of 17 kilowatts installed and decide whether to cut fixed cost or raise volume first. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build your own table: price, variable cost, contribution, fixed cost, break-even and a downside case at 75% of sales. Show it to a peer or mentor.

Standard to meet. Arithmetic is correct; every figure has a source or is marked as an assumption; a downside case is shown.

Module 8 Grow, measure and renew 4 hEvaluate

Growth should follow proof. Measure, learn, then add; and keep returning to the customer.

  • In energy: Grow through maintenance contracts that keep systems producing and customers calling.
  • Pick one growth lever at a time: new customers, a higher repeat rate, a better price, or a new offer.
  • Set one measurable target for 90 days, such as 'raise repeat customers from 20% to 30%'.
  • Ask each customer how they found you, and record it. Spend more where the record shows results.
  • Review monthly: numbers, defects, complaints, cash. Write three decisions each time.
  • Plan the renewal of licences, equipment and skills a year ahead.
Worked example. 90-day plan: 'Lever: repeat customers. Action: follow-up call after every kilowatt installed. Target: repeat rate from 20% to 30%. Check: weekly count. Review date: day 45 and day 90.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: you can add 6 kilowatts installed a month by one of two levers: a follow-up call to past customers or a price rise of R$ 420 to R$ 4.620. Question: how do you choose? Model answer: test the cheaper reversible lever first (the call), measure for 30 days, and only then try the price change, so you can tell which one worked.

Longer notes. Grow by improving the path from quote to signed contract. Record every quote with the roof visit date, the size in kilowatts, the answer and the reason for a no. Send the client the estimate of savings with its assumptions, and visit again after the first month to read the meter together. Ask clients for a neighbour's introduction. Compare quotes by distance from the base, since travel raises cost. Avoid promises of savings you cannot measure, and keep any savings figure inside what energy advertising rules allow. Review lost quotes together each month and note what could change.

Extra worked example. The company sends 25 quotes a month and 24% sign (worked-example rates, not market data), giving 6 homes of 5 kilowatts, or 30 kilowatts. A faster follow-up lifts this to 32%, giving 8 homes and 40 kilowatts, which is 10 more. At R$ 1.200 per kilowatt, the gain is R$ 12.000 a month. Those 10 kilowatts lift the monthly result from R$ 16.000 to R$ 28.000, and the faster follow-up costs only the installer's phone time.

Activity. Write a 90-day plan with one target, three actions and a review date. Present it in five minutes and take questions.

Standard to meet. Target is measurable and dated; actions are small enough to start this week; review date is in the calendar.

case study

A case to work through

Situation. A small solar installer in Sao Paulo fits about 30 kilowatts a month in six homes and sells each kilowatt at R$ 4.200. The plan counts R$ 3.000 of cost per kilowatt and R$ 20.000 of fixed cost, so the planned result is R$ 16.000. This month the foreman reports that four of the six homes needed a second visit: a cable run was longer than measured, an inverter was configured wrongly or the panel had no free breaker. Each return visit cost the crew a day plus travel. An employee must study the six job files, find the causes and propose a pre-installation and commissioning checklist. The owner wants the cost of the returns and a procedure that the crews can follow.

Task. Say what you would do first, next and last, and what record you would keep.

Model answer. First, list the six homes and the cause of each return visit. Suppose each return costs R$ 1.100 (this and the four returns are worked-example figures, not market data), so the extra cost is 4 x R$ 1.100 = R$ 4.400. Spread over 30 kilowatts, that adds R$ 146,67 per kilowatt and the real cost is R$ 3.146,67. The monthly result becomes 30 x R$ 1.053,33 - R$ 20.000 = R$ 11.600 instead of R$ 16.000. Next, write a roof and panel checklist for the quote visit, with measured cable lengths and breaker space, and a commissioning sheet with the inverter settings. Record returns per job. With one return in six jobs the extra cost falls to R$ 1.100, so the checklist has to win back R$ 3.300 a month. Add the cable length and breaker space to the quote sheet so the foreman never discovers them on the roof.

levels L1 to L7

Where this sits on the ladder

Seven levels, one slide each. Read the slide, then read the speaker notes under it. Credits stack and you can step in and out; this course is L4.

Slide 1 of 7: L1 Basic

  • Name the main parts of energy in plain words
  • Identify one energy project in Sao Paulo and what it does
  • Describe a normal day for a energy assistant
Notes for slide 1

No background is needed. In 6 to 10 hours you learn how electricity moves from plant to socket, what a kilowatt and a kilowatt-hour measure, and why wet hands and bare wire are dangerous. Evidence is a short quiz or labelled diagram. You must use units correctly and name the safe distance rules.

Slide 2 of 7: L2 Awareness

  • Explain how energy affects daily life in Sao Paulo
  • List the main risks, such as electrical hazards and supply failures, and who handles them
  • Compare two examples of a energy project and note the differences
Notes for slide 2

For homeowners, small business managers and career changers who deal with installers. In 15 to 25 hours you compare two energy projects in Sao Paulo, such as a rooftop solar system and a grid upgrade, and explain shocks, fires and supply failures and who handles each. A short report is the evidence. Careful, honest explanation is the standard.

Slide 3 of 7: L3 Foundation

  • Use a load and consumption meter to complete a simple task
  • Record energy used per unit of output in a clear table
  • Follow safe and correct steps for a routine energy audit
Notes for slide 3

Trainees read a consumption meter and a load table, take supervised readings and record energy per unit of output. Over 40 to 60 hours you complete a simple audit of a room and keep a portfolio of signed sheets. Required standard: isolation before touching, correct instruments, and readings written down immediately.

Slide 4 of 7: L4 Practitioner: Plan, Start and Run

  • Plan a energy project for Sao Paulo using local demand, costs and a risk list
  • Prepare a budget, timeline and checklist for electricity sector and safety rules; check the current official text
  • Run the first 90 days and review energy used per unit of output each week
Notes for slide 4

The main course, 100 to 150 hours across eight modules. You plan a small installation business for Sao Paulo: system size in kilowatts, equipment, crew days, grid connection and safety rules, then a 90-day start with energy per unit reviewed weekly. Evidence is a plan, a checklist and a review report. Cost per kilowatt and break-even must be calculated.

Slide 5 of 7: L5 Specialist

  • Design a energy system design for a demanding client
  • Analyse energy used per unit of output to find the root cause of a problem
  • Coach junior staff and check their energy audit work
Notes for slide 5

For installers and auditors with a year or more on real roofs and panels. Across 150 to 250 hours you design a system for a demanding client, with string sizing, protections and an estimate of yearly generation, and trace a poor yield to its cause. An expert reviews a case file. Clean drawings, measured results and safe crews are expected.

Slide 6 of 7: L6 Advanced

  • Lead a team across several energy project sites or projects
  • Evaluate options by weighing cost, quality and electrical hazards and supply failures
  • Negotiate with suppliers, clients and public bodies
Notes for slide 6

For project managers and energy heads with budgets and crews. Plan 250 to 400 hours on a project across several sites, weighing equipment cost against hazard and supply risk, and negotiating with utilities and suppliers. Evidence is a project file with measured generation and incident counts. Steady, safe delivery and honest reporting are required.

Slide 7 of 7: L7 Super expert

  • Create new methods or standards for energy
  • Judge complex, uncertain cases and defend the decision in public
  • Mentor leaders and publish lessons for the Sao Paulo community
Notes for slide 7

For recognised experts with many years in energy. Study lasts years: you write standards for installation and testing, judge complex failures and fires in public, and mentor directors. Evidence includes published work, independent review and mentoring. Trusted judgement counts where a wrong call risks lives.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, taxes, data duties and customer rights. This course is written for São Paulo. Check the current official text for your own place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registration: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registration: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registration: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registration: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registration: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registration: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registration: registration with ACRA. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia
