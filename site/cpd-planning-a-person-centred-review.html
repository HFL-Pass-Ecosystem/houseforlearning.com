CPD · Social work and social care

Planning a person-centred review

Running a review that puts the person's own goals first.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 3 hours (180 minutes) of CPDSelf-study plus role-playSocial work and social care

Running a review that puts the person's own goals first.

  • Prepare a review, given a case file, with the person's goals, strengths and questions listed first.
  • Facilitate a short meeting, given a role-play, so the person speaks for at least half the time.
  • Write an action plan, given meeting notes, with each action having an owner and a date.
  • Evaluate a review, given a checklist, scoring each item with one reason.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare, run and record a person-centred review in which the person speaks first and last and every action has an owner and a date

Condition

Given a case file, meeting notes, role-plays and a checklist, in a 3-hour practical session

Standard

Run a short review where the person speaks at least half the time, write actions with a named owner and date, and score the review against a checklist with reasons

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 What person-centred means 25 min
  • Person-centred means the person's goals lead.
  • Strengths come before problems.
  • The person is the expert on their own life.
Worked example. Person H's goal in his own words: "I want to cook my own dinner again." The review starts from that goal, not from the list of falls.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "what person-centred means": prepare a review, given a case file, with the person's goals, strengths and questions listed first.

Standard to meet. Explain what person-centred means and show how the person's own goals, strengths and wishes lead a review.

Module 2 Preparing and inviting 25 min
  • List goals, strengths and questions before the meeting.
  • Send a clear agenda and a welcoming time and room.
  • Invite a support person if wanted.
Worked example. Agenda: welcome (5 min), going well (10), not working (10), goals for 3 months (10), actions (5) = 40 minutes. Invitations sent 7 days before.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "preparing and inviting": facilitate a short meeting, given a role-play, so the person speaks for at least half the time.

Standard to meet. Prepare a review from a case file, listing the person's goals, strengths and questions first, and invite everyone with a clear agenda and a welcoming setting.

Module 3 Running the review: person first and last 40 min
  • The person speaks first and last.
  • Aim for at least half the talking time.
  • Invite them back if someone talks over them.
  • If family disagree with the person, record both views; the person's wishes lead unless local capacity rules apply.
Worked example. In a 40-minute review the person speaks for at least 20 of the 40 minutes. Family member interrupts at minute 12: "Let's hear what Person H thinks."

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "running the review: person first and last": write an action plan, given meeting notes, with each action having an owner and a date.

Standard to meet. Facilitate a short review in a role-play so the person speaks first, last and for at least half the time, redirecting anyone who talks over them.

Module 4 Setting goals with actions, owners, dates 30 min
  • Every action has a named owner.
  • Every action has a date.
  • Set the next review date before you close.
Worked example. Action 1: "Book a falls check" - owner: nurse, date: 12 Dec. Action 2: "Try a short cooking session" - owner: Person H and support worker, date: 15 Dec. Next review: 15 Mar.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "setting goals with actions, owners, dates": evaluate a review, given a checklist, scoring each item with one reason.

Standard to meet. Write an action plan from meeting notes in which every action has a named owner and a date, and set the next review date.

Module 5 Recording and sharing 20 min
  • Use plain language in the person's words.
  • Fill in the review form and care plan.
  • Share a copy with the person.
Worked example. Outcome in the person's words: "I will cook on Tuesdays with my support worker." Recorded on the form and care plan, copy given to Person H the same day.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "recording and sharing": evaluate a review, given a checklist, scoring each item with one reason. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Record the outcome in plain language in the person's own words on the review form and care plan, and share it with the person.

Module 6 Practice 20 min
  • Use Person H and Person N case cards.
  • Plan the agenda and write three actions.
  • Check each action for owner and date.
Worked example. Person N wants a weekly choir; transport is the barrier. 3 actions, 3 owners, 3 dates; 3 of 3 pass the owner-and-date test.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "planning a person-centred review" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (prepare a review; facilitate a short meeting; write an action plan; evaluate a review).

Standard to meet. Plan and run a review for a case card, such as Person H or Person N, and write three actions with owners and dates.

Module 7 Assessment 10 min
  • Score a review against the checklist.
  • Give one reason per item.
  • Name one change for next time.
Worked example. Checklist of 8 items: 7 ticked. Missed: next review date not set. Reason given: "meeting ran out of time." Change: set the date at minute 35.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "assessment": evaluate a review, given a checklist, scoring each item with one reason. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate a review against a checklist, scoring each item with one reason, and name one change for next time.

Module 8 Second worked case and reflection 10 min
  • Compare two cases.
  • Note differences in barriers.
  • Plan one personal improvement.
Worked example. Person H needs a falls check (health barrier); Person N needs transport (travel barrier). 2 cases, 6 actions in total, 6 of 6 with owner and date.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second worked case and reflection": evaluate a review, given a checklist, scoring each item with one reason. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Compare the Person H and Person N reviews, identify the barrier in each, and justify one improvement to your own facilitation.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Who speaks first?

Model answer: The person. (Covered in module 3: Running the review: person first and last. To pass that module: Facilitate a short review in a role-play so the person speaks first, last and for at least half the time, redirecting anyone who talks over them.)

Each action needs what?

Model answer: An owner and a date. (Covered in module 4: Setting goals with actions, owners, dates. To pass that module: Write an action plan from meeting notes in which every action has a named owner and a date, and set the next review date.)

How do you handle a family member talking over the person?

Model answer: Invite the person back, check their wishes. (Covered in module 3: Running the review: person first and last. To pass that module: Facilitate a short review in a role-play so the person speaks first, last and for at least half the time, redirecting anyone who talks over them.)

Where do you record the outcome?

Model answer: The review form and care plan. (Covered in module 5: Recording and sharing. To pass that module: Record the outcome in plain language in the person's own words on the review form and care plan, and share it with the person.)

Who owns an action?

Model answer: A named person. (Covered in module 4: Setting goals with actions, owners, dates. To pass that module: Write an action plan from meeting notes in which every action has a named owner and a date, and set the next review date.)

What if the family disagree with the person?

Model answer: Record both views; the person's wishes lead unless capacity rules apply. (Covered in module 3: Running the review: person first and last. To pass that module: Facilitate a short review in a role-play so the person speaks first, last and for at least half the time, redirecting anyone who talks over them.)

How do you make the room welcoming?

Model answer: Seating, time, quiet, support person. (Covered in module 2: Preparing and inviting. To pass that module: Prepare a review from a case file, listing the person's goals, strengths and questions first, and invite everyone with a clear agenda and a welcoming setting.)

When is the next review set?

Model answer: Before the meeting ends. (Covered in module 4: Setting goals with actions, owners, dates. To pass that module: Write an action plan from meeting notes in which every action has a named owner and a date, and set the next review date.)

How to record outcomes?

Model answer: Plain language in the person's words. (Covered in module 5: Recording and sharing. To pass that module: Record the outcome in plain language in the person's own words on the review form and care plan, and share it with the person.)

What do you do if family members disagree with the person's wishes?

Model answer: Record both views, keep the person's wishes leading unless local capacity rules apply, and tell the manager. (Covered in module 3: Running the review: person first and last. To pass that module: Facilitate a short review in a role-play so the person speaks first, last and for at least half the time, redirecting anyone who talks over them.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What a review is. Speaker note: A review is a meeting to check how a person's care is going. The learner can say that the person is invited, is heard first, and decides what matters most.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Person-centred thinking. Speaker note: The learner knows that goals, strengths and wishes lead the review, and that families and workers support rather than speak for the person. They practise saying "What matters to you?" and listen for the answer before adding their own view.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Prepare well. Speaker note: The learner can read a case file, list the person's goals and questions, and send a clear, welcoming invitation and agenda with help from a template.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Run and record a review. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner runs a short review where the person speaks at least half the time, writes actions with an owner and date, and records the outcome in plain words.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Complex reviews. Speaker note: The specialist handles reviews with several family views, communication barriers or limited capacity, keeping the person central and recording differing views fairly. They plan extra time, a support person or an advocate, and keep the person's own words in the record.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Evaluate and coach. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner scores reviews with a checklist, gives specific feedback and helps colleagues improve agendas, action plans and recording. They check each action has an owner and a date, and help others tighten vague actions into clear ones.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Shape review practice. Speaker note: The super expert designs the review process for a service, sets standards for person-first speaking time, audits outcomes and trains teams across sites.
