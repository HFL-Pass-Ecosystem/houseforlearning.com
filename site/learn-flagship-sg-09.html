SG-09 · full course

Urban and Vertical Agriculture

Grow food in small urban spaces using water and space wisely, and record yield and cost. You learn what plants need, how to choose crops, build a system, keep daily care records, measure yield, work out cost per kilo and sell safely.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 120 h guidedPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Grow food in small urban spaces using water and space wisely, and record yield and cost. You learn what plants need, how to choose crops, build a system, keep daily care records, measure yield, work out cost per kilo and sell safely.

  • Understand Explain light, water and nutrients.
  • Apply Run a small system.
  • Analyse Analyse yield and water.
  • Create Create a plan for a bigger space.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 45-day small urban growing system and report yield per square metre, water use per kilo and cost per kilo, with a plan to scale or stop.

Condition

About 120 hours over 3 to 5 months. A balcony, rooftop or indoor rack, containers, a free spreadsheet, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

System plan; 45-day log; yield and water numbers; cost per kilo; pest plan; at least 70% on the final check.

Learning only. Follow your local law and get written permission before you work with real customers, premises, data or money.

Requirements: none. Who it is for: learners 16 and over, workers changing field, small-business starters, teams. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (120 h guided)

Module 1 Light, water, nutrients 10 hUnderstand

Plants need light, water and nutrients in the right balance. Too little or too much of any one causes poor growth. Measuring each lets you improve them.

  • Light is measured in hours per day, and leaf crops such as lettuce usually do well with about 12 hours.
  • Water should reach the roots without pooling, and you track the litres used each day.
  • Nutrients are fed in a balanced mix, and the strength is checked with a simple meter or test.
  • Indoor lights use power, so a 30 W light on for 12 hours uses 0.36 kWh each day.
  • A common mistake is to add more nutrients when plants look poor, when the real cause is too little light.
Worked example. A 30 W light runs 12 hours a day, which is 30 times 12, or 360 watt-hours, or 0.36 kWh. Over 45 days that is 0.36 times 45, which is 16.2 kWh.

Activity. Survey your growing space and record the light hours, the space size in square metres and the water source, then estimate the power use if you add a light.

Standard to meet. Your survey has measurements and units, and your power estimate is calculated correctly.

Module 2 Choose crops 10 hCreate

The best crops for a small space are fast, compact and in demand. You match the crop to your light, your season and your buyers. Fit the growing time to your 45-day run.

  • Prefer leaf crops and herbs, because they grow quickly and need less depth for roots.
  • Check the days to harvest on the seed packet and make sure it fits your 45-day window.
  • Choose crops that suit your light and temperature, and avoid ones that need a lot of space or support.
  • Ask two possible buyers which crops they would actually buy.
  • A common mistake is to pick a crop because it is fashionable and not because it fits the space.
Worked example. Basil takes about 28 days to harvest and lettuce about 35 days. Both fit inside a 45-day run, with 17 and 10 days to spare, so you can plan one full cycle of either.

Activity. Pick two crops, write the days to harvest, the space each needs and your reasons, and ask two buyers for their view.

Standard to meet. Your two crops both fit the growing window and have at least one reason each supported by a buyer comment or a measurement.

Module 3 Build the system 20 hApply

The system holds the plants, water and light. Good design saves space and water, and stops problems such as rot. You build it safely and document it.

  • Choose containers or racks that are stable, food-safe and the right depth for your crop.
  • Include drainage holes and a tray so water drains away from the roots and can be reused.
  • Place lights at the right height and on a timer, and keep electrical parts dry and away from water.
  • Photograph and sketch the system and label the parts with their sizes.
  • Check with your local authority about rules for balconies, rooftops and electrical work.
Worked example. A rack has 3 tiers of 0.5 square metres each, so it has 3 times 0.5, which is 1.5 square metres of growing area. It takes up only 0.5 square metres of floor.

Activity. Build your system, test it with plain water for a day, and submit photos, a sketch and a list of parts with sizes.

Standard to meet. Your system holds water without leaks, drains properly and has a sketch with labelled measurements.

Module 4 Daily care 24 hApply

Plants need regular care. A simple routine for water, feeding and pest checks prevents most problems. A daily log gives you the data for later.

  • Water on a schedule and record the litres used each day.
  • Check the pH of the nutrient mix each week and keep it in the range for the crop, often about 5.8 to 6.5.
  • Look at the leaves for spots, holes or insects every day, and remove affected leaves.
  • Write the date, litres, pH and any problem in the daily log.
  • A common mistake is to water by habit and not by checking the plant and the mix.
Worked example. A weekly check reads pH 7.2, which is above the 5.8 to 6.5 range. You add a small, measured amount of pH-down solution and retest, until the reading is 6.2, and record the change.

Activity. Run your system for 45 days and submit the daily log with water, pH checks and any pest or problem notes.

Standard to meet. Your log is complete for at least 40 of the 45 days and any out-of-range reading shows a recorded action.

Module 5 Measure yield 16 hAnalyse

Yield is how much food you get from the space you use. You weigh the harvest and compare it with the area and the water used. These numbers tell you if the system is good.

  • Weigh each harvest on a kitchen scale and record the kilograms and date.
  • Calculate yield per square metre as the total weight divided by the growing area.
  • Calculate water per kilo as the total litres used divided by the total kilograms harvested.
  • Compare your numbers with another crop or with an earlier harvest to see changes.
  • A common mistake is to weigh only the best plants.
Worked example. A harvest of 9 kg from 0.5 square metres gives 9 divided by 0.5, which is 18 kg per square metre. If you used 45 litres of water for 9 kg, water per kilo is 5 litres.

Activity. Weigh all harvests, calculate yield per square metre and water per kilo, and present them in a table with one chart.

Standard to meet. Your calculations are correct with units, and the chart shows the totals clearly.

Module 6 Cost per kilo 16 hEvaluate

To know whether growing pays you must add up every cost. Indoor systems use power, so include it. The cost per kilo is the key number.

  • List the cost of seeds, nutrients, power, containers share and your time.
  • Spread the cost of long-lasting items, such as racks, over the harvests you expect.
  • Add all costs and divide by the kilograms harvested to get the cost per kilo.
  • Compare your cost per kilo with the price you can sell at to find the margin.
  • A common mistake is to leave out power and labour.
Worked example. Seeds cost S$5, nutrients S$7, power S$10, the share of the container S$8 and your time S$6, so the total is S$36. With 8 kg harvested, the cost per kilo is 36 divided by 8, which is S$4.50.

Activity. Complete a cost sheet showing every cost, total, kilograms and cost per kilo.

Standard to meet. Your cost per kilo is correct and each cost line has a note, receipt or measurement behind it.

Module 7 Sell and share 14 hApply

Fresh produce sells best when it is clean, cool and local. You handle it hygienically and keep records. Sharing with neighbours builds community.

  • Harvest in the cool of the day, wash with clean water and dry the produce gently.
  • Pack in clean containers, keep it cool and label it with the name, the date and your contact.
  • Sell close by, such as to neighbours or a nearby shop, to keep it fresh and the transport short.
  • Record each sale with the date, weight and price, and keep a small amount to share.
  • Check with your local authority about the rules for selling or giving away food.
Worked example. You sell 8 kg at S$8 per kilo, so you earn S$64. With a cost of S$4.50 per kilo, the margin per kilo is S$3.50, which is 3.50 divided by 8, or about 44%.

Activity. Sell or share your produce, keep a record of each sale and calculate your margin per kilo.

Standard to meet. Your sales record is complete, your margin is calculated correctly and the food was handled and labelled as your local authority requires.

Module 8 Review 10 hEvaluate

The review decides whether to scale up, change or stop. Use your numbers on yield, cost and demand. Write the decision down with reasons.

  • Compare yield, water use and cost with your targets and with the earlier numbers.
  • Note the main lessons, such as what plants did best and what problems recurred.
  • Estimate the benefit and cost of adding more space, using your cost per kilo and margin.
  • Decide to scale, change or stop and set a date for the next step.
  • A common mistake is to scale up before the numbers show it will pay.
Worked example. A second rack costs S$60 and could add 8 kg a cycle. At a margin of S$3.50 per kilo that is 8 times 3.50, or S$28 per cycle, so it pays back in 60 divided by 28, which is 2.1, so three cycles.

Activity. Present a review with your results table, lessons and a scale, change or stop decision with reasons.

Standard to meet. Your decision is supported by at least three numbers from your records and includes a date for the next step.

assessment

Evidence: your records, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name the three things plants need.

Model answer: Light, water and nutrients. (Covered in module 1: Light, water, nutrients. To pass that module: Your survey has measurements and units, and your power estimate is calculated correctly.)

Q2. Why choose leaf crops for a small space?

Model answer: They grow quickly and take little room. (Covered in module 2: Choose crops. To pass that module: Your two crops both fit the growing window and have at least one reason each supported by a buyer comment or a measurement.)

Q3. Why does a container need drainage?

Model answer: It stops water pooling, which would rot the roots. (Covered in module 3: Build the system. To pass that module: Your system holds water without leaks, drains properly and has a sketch with labelled measurements.)

Q4. Why check pH?

Model answer: It affects how well plants take up nutrients. (Covered in module 4: Daily care. To pass that module: Your log is complete for at least 40 of the 45 days and any out-of-range reading shows a recorded action.)

Q5. What is the yield for 9 kg from 0.5 square metres?

Model answer: 9 divided by 0.5 is 18 kg per square metre. (Covered in module 5: Measure yield. To pass that module: Your calculations are correct with units, and the chart shows the totals clearly.)

Q6. Why include power in the cost?

Model answer: Indoor systems use costly power, and leaving it out hides the true cost. (Covered in module 6: Cost per kilo. To pass that module: Your cost per kilo is correct and each cost line has a note, receipt or measurement behind it.)

Q7. Why sell close to where you grow?

Model answer: The produce stays fresher and transport costs and travel are low. (Covered in module 7: Sell and share. To pass that module: Your sales record is complete, your margin is calculated correctly and the food was handled and labelled as your local authority requires.)

Q8. What decides whether to scale up?

Model answer: Yield, cost and demand. (Covered in module 8: Review. To pass that module: Your decision is supported by at least three numbers from your records and includes a date for the next step.)

Q9. What is the task of this course, in one sentence?

Model answer: Run a 45-day small urban growing system and report yield per square metre, water use per kilo and cost per kilo, with a plan to scale or stop. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. What standard must your final evidence meet?

Model answer: System plan; 45-day log; yield and water numbers; cost per kilo; pest plan; at least 70% on the final check. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic, First words, tools and safety. Speaker note: Learner can say what Urban and Vertical Agriculture is about and name the safety rule. Use module 1, Light, water, nutrients, as the first short unit (about 2 to 6 hours). Say this rule aloud: Learning only. Follow your local law and get written permission before you work with real customers, premises, data or money. No record is needed at this level.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness, What the field is and who it serves. Speaker note: Learner explains the idea in plain words: Explain light, water and nutrients. Shared with schools and community groups. Note for the guide: ask one learner to explain it to a neighbour and check the words are right.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation, Guided tasks with a checklist. Speaker note: Learner does guided tasks from the first three modules (Light, water, nutrients; Choose crops; Build the system) with a checklist. Evidence is a small set of dated records. Note for the guide: sit beside the learner for the first record, then step back.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner, Run the full task alone (this course). Speaker note: Task: Run a 45-day small urban growing system and report yield per square metre, water use per kilo and cost per kilo, with a plan to scale or stop. Standard: System plan; 45-day log; yield and water numbers; cost per kilo; pest plan; at least 70% on the final check. Note for the guide: a mentor reviews the records; two peers mark with the rubric above.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist, One narrow area to depth. Speaker note: Learner picks one area, for example Daily care (the longest module at 24 h), and goes deeper than this course asks. Learner then teaches a peer circle of three or four. Note for the guide: check the teaching, not only the work.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced, Lead a team and review evidence. Speaker note: Learner leads a small team or designs a service in this field and marks other people's evidence against the rubric. Roles this points to: Urban grower; Greenhouse assistant; Community garden coordinator. Note for the guide: sample three records from the team and compare marks.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert, Set and audit the standard. Speaker note: Learner writes the method for Urban and Vertical Agriculture as a short guide, audits other learners' evidence, and publishes worked examples back to learners. Note for the guide: worked examples keep currency and units on every number and are checked by a second expert.

Notes: credits stack and you can step in and out. This course is L4. Aligned with, not accredited by, national credit frameworks.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local custom changes rules, language, currency and who you practise with. Check each rule with your local authority before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
