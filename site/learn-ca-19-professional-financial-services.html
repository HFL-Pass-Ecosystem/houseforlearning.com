Professional financial services

Run a real professional financial services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small professional financial services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: finance and accounting. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Understand what clients need from financial services 6 hUnderstand

Clients seek planning, tax, insurance, credit and investing advice.

  • Know the service lines and who can offer each.
  • Interview five clients on their goals and worries.
  • Choose a niche such as newcomers, students or small businesses.
  • Write a service sheet.
Worked example. Newcomers need credit history, tax filing and savings plans in their first year.

Activity. Interview five people and write a service sheet.

Standard to meet. Needs recorded.

Module 2 Know registration and conduct rules 6 hApply

Financial advice is regulated.

  • Provincial securities, insurance and mortgage regulators license advisers.
  • Know-your-client and suitability duties require documented advice.
  • Anti-money-laundering reporting.
  • Privacy law covers client records.
Worked example. Row: 'Recommend a fund | provincial securities regulator | registration and suitability record | each advice | file note'.

Activity. Build a rules table.

Standard to meet. Rows sourced and dated.

Module 3 Build a plan and explain it 10 hApply

Plain-language plans win trust.

  • Collect goals, income, costs, debts, assets.
  • Compute net worth and savings rate.
  • Set steps with dates.
  • Explain risks and fees.
Worked example. Income 4,000, costs 3,200; savings rate 20%.

Activity. Build a plan for a fictional client.

Standard to meet. Numbers check.

Module 4 Price the service 8 hEvaluate

Fees must be clear.

  • Hourly, fixed or percentage fees.
  • Disclose all costs.
  • Time tracking.
  • Break-even.
Worked example. Fee 150 per hour; cost 25; margin 125; fixed cost 3,000 a month → 24 hours. Teaching figures in CAD.

Activity. Build a fee sheet.

Standard to meet. Disclosure written.

Module 5 Run records and reviews 8 hApply

Keep files complete.

  • Client file checklist.
  • Annual review.
  • Complaints process.
  • Supervision.
Worked example. 100% of files had suitability notes after a checklist.

Activity. Audit five mock files.

Standard to meet. Gaps listed.

Module 6 Win clients ethically 6 hCreate

Trust grows from help.

  • Referrals and talks.
  • Free guides.
  • Clear next step.
  • Track funnel.
Worked example. Funnel: 30 talks, 8 meetings, 3 clients.

Activity. Give a short talk.

Standard to meet. Feedback recorded.

Module 7 Manage conflict, fraud and market risk 6 hEvaluate

Protect clients.

  • Disclose conflicts.
  • Fraud signs.
  • Never promise returns.
  • Business continuity.
Worked example. Risk: 'Client scam loss | 3 x 5 | warnings and verification | adviser | quarterly'.

Activity. Write a risk register.

Standard to meet. Owners named.

Module 8 Pilot with mock clients 8 hCreate

Practise.

  • Three mock plans.
  • Peer review.
  • Stop rule: missing disclosure fails.
  • Reflect.
Worked example. All three plans passed disclosure check.

Activity. Run mock pilot.

Standard to meet. Review has decision.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is suitability?

Model answer: Advice fitting the client's goals, risk and situation.

Q2. Who regulates advisers?

Model answer: Provincial regulators.

Q3. What is savings rate?

Model answer: Savings divided by income.

Q4. Why disclose fees?

Model answer: Clients must know costs.

Q5. What is KYC?

Model answer: Know your client.

Q6. Why keep notes?

Model answer: Proof of advice.

Q7. What must you never promise?

Model answer: Returns.

Q8. What is a conflict of interest?

Model answer: A situation where your gain may clash with the client's.

Q9. Why annual reviews?

Model answer: Plans must be updated.

Q10. What triggers fail in the mock?

Model answer: Missing disclosure.

levels

Seven levels, L1 to L7

Use this ladder to see where you are and what to show next. Each level builds on the one below.

Seven levels, L1 to L7
LevelRoleYou canNotes and evidence
L1Finance aware learnerDefine: name the main terms, people and rules of professional financial servicesStart here if new. Evidence: a one-page glossary and a visit or interview note.
L2Clerk or assistantDescribe: explain how professional financial services works from first contact to resultEvidence: a flow chart and a two-minute spoken explanation.
L3Bookkeeper or junior analystApply: carry out a routine task in professional financial services to a written standardEvidence: a supervised task record that meets the standard once.
L4Accountant or adviserAnalyse: diagnose why a professional financial services result missed its target and fix itEvidence: a fault or gap analysis with a fix and a repeat test. This course targets this level.
L5Senior adviser or managerEvaluate: judge options for professional financial services against cost, risk and qualityEvidence: an options paper with numbers and a recommendation defended to a panel.
L6Head of financeCreate: design and pilot a new offer or process in professional financial servicesEvidence: a pilot with measured results over at least 30 days.
L7Partner or chief officerLead: run a team or business in professional financial services and develop othersEvidence: a record of results over a year and of people you have coached to a higher level.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
