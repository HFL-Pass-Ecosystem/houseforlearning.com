Grade 11 Science: Physics

7 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 11 Science Physics, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 7 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

Unit 3 Work, energy, power and systems of particles Weeks 13 to 17Apply

In this unit you work on 'Work, energy, power and systems of particles', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: apply conservation of energy and momentum. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Observe: see, touch, measure or watch a safe demonstration linked to 'Work, energy, power and systems of particles' and write what you notice.
  • Explain: use the key words of the unit to say what is happening and why, in a few lines.
  • Use it: apply conservation of energy and momentum. Use a diagram, table or short calculation where it fits.
  • Test and conclude: say what evidence supports your answer and what you would change.
Worked example. Solved: Power = work / time. 600 J of work in 12 s gives P = 50 W. Potential energy of 2 kg at 5 m = 2 x 10 x 5 = 100 J (g = 10). Method: Model run for 'Work, energy, power and systems of particles'. Question: what do we want to find out? Prediction: one sentence. Method: three safe steps with the equipment named. Record: a small table with units. Conclusion: one sentence that uses the evidence and the key words of the unit. What a good answer looks like: a fair test, measured values with units, and a conclusion that matches the table.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Calculate work with a force at an angle. A 50 N force at 60 degrees pulls a cart 4 m. W = F d cos 60 = 50 x 4 x 0.5 = 100 J.
  2. Step 2, Use the work-energy theorem. A 2 kg block starts at 3 m/s and the net force of 4 N acts for 5 m. Work = 20 J. Initial KE = 0.5 x 2 x 9 = 9 J. Final KE = 29 J, so v = sqrt(2 x 29 / 2) = 5.39 m/s.
  3. Step 3, Conserve mechanical energy. A pendulum bob of 0.5 kg is released from a height 0.8 m above its lowest point. mgh = 0.5 x 10 x 0.8 = 4 J. At the bottom v = sqrt(2 x 10 x 0.8) = 4 m/s.
  4. Step 4, Conserve momentum in a collision. A 2 kg trolley at 3 m/s hits a stationary 1 kg trolley and they stick. Momentum 6 kg m/s. v = 6 / 3 = 2 m/s. KE before 9 J, after 6 J, so 3 J is lost: inelastic.
  5. Step 5, Compute power and centre of mass. A motor lifts 100 kg through 10 m in 20 s: P = mgh/t = 100 x 10 x 10 / 20 = 500 W. The centre of mass of masses 2 kg at x = 0 and 4 kg at x = 3 m is 12 / 6 = 2 m.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: a 1 kg ball moving at 4 m/s hits and sticks to a 3 kg stationary ball. Find the final speed and KE lost. Answer: v = 4 / 4 = 1 m/s; KE lost = 8 - 2 = 6 J.

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Apply conservation of energy and momentum: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

Unit 5 Properties of solids and fluids Weeks 23 to 27Apply

In this unit you work on 'Properties of solids and fluids', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: use stress, strain and Bernoulli's principle. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Observe: see, touch, measure or watch a safe demonstration linked to 'Properties of solids and fluids' and write what you notice.
  • Explain: use the key words of the unit to say what is happening and why, in a few lines.
  • Use it: use stress, strain and Bernoulli's principle. Use a diagram, table or short calculation where it fits.
  • Test and conclude: say what evidence supports your answer and what you would change.
Worked example. Solved: Pressure = force / area: 200 N on 0.5 m squared is 400 Pa. Density = mass / volume: 2 kg in 0.001 m cubed is 2000 kg per m cubed. Method: Model run for 'Properties of solids and fluids'. Question: what do we want to find out? Prediction: one sentence. Method: three safe steps with the equipment named. Record: a small table with units. Conclusion: one sentence that uses the evidence and the key words of the unit. What a good answer looks like: a fair test, measured values with units, and a conclusion that matches the table.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Calculate stress and strain. A steel wire of length 2 m and cross-section 1 mm2 (1e-6 m2) carries a load of 100 N. Stress = 100 / 1e-6 = 1 x 10^8 Pa. With Young's modulus 2 x 10^11 Pa, strain = 5 x 10^-4. Extension = 5e-4 x 2 = 1 mm.
  2. Step 2, Compute pressure in a fluid. In water at depth 5 m, gauge pressure = rho g h = 1000 x 10 x 5 = 5 x 10^4 Pa. With atmosphere 1.0 x 10^5 Pa, absolute pressure is 1.5 x 10^5 Pa.
  3. Step 3, Use the continuity equation A1 v1 = A2 v2. Water in a pipe of 4 cm2 at 2 m/s narrows to 1 cm2. v2 = 4 x 2 / 1 = 8 m/s.
  4. Step 4, Use Bernoulli's principle P + 0.5 rho v^2 + rho g h = constant. Horizontal pipe: v1 = 2 m/s, v2 = 8 m/s. Pressure drop = 0.5 x 1000 x (64 - 4) = 30000 Pa. Higher speed means lower pressure, as in an aerofoil.
  5. Step 5, Surface tension and viscosity: capillary rise h = 2 T cos(theta) / (rho g r). With T = 0.072 N/m, r = 0.5 mm: h = 2 x 0.072 / (1000 x 10 x 0.0005) = 0.0288 m, about 2.9 cm.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: find the speed in a 2 cm2 section if a 6 cm2 section has 1 m/s flow. Answer: v = 6 x 1 / 2 = 3 m/s.

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Use stress, strain and Bernoulli's principle: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

Unit 6 Thermodynamics and kinetic theory Weeks 28 to 32Apply

In this unit you work on 'Thermodynamics and kinetic theory', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: apply the laws of thermodynamics. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Observe: see, touch, measure or watch a safe demonstration linked to 'Thermodynamics and kinetic theory' and write what you notice.
  • Explain: use the key words of the unit to say what is happening and why, in a few lines.
  • Use it: apply the laws of thermodynamics. Use a diagram, table or short calculation where it fits.
  • Test and conclude: say what evidence supports your answer and what you would change.
Worked example. Solved: Q = m c change in T. 2 kg of water (4200 J per kg per K) heated by 10 K needs 2 x 4200 x 10 = 84,000 J. Method: Model run for 'Thermodynamics and kinetic theory'. Question: what do we want to find out? Prediction: one sentence. Method: three safe steps with the equipment named. Record: a small table with units. Conclusion: one sentence that uses the evidence and the key words of the unit. What a good answer looks like: a fair test, measured values with units, and a conclusion that matches the table.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Apply the first law Q = delta U + W. A gas in a cylinder gets 800 J of heat and expands doing 300 J of work. delta U = 800 - 300 = 500 J, so its internal energy increases.
  2. Step 2, Do an isothermal process. For 1 mol of ideal gas at 300 K, expanding from 2 L to 4 L, W = nRT ln(V2/V1) = 1 x 8.314 x 300 x 0.693 = 1729 J. delta U = 0 so Q = 1729 J.
  3. Step 3, Use the second law and the Carnot engine. An engine runs between 600 K and 300 K. Efficiency = 1 - 300/600 = 50 per cent. If it takes 1000 J from the source it rejects 500 J and does 500 J of work.
  4. Step 4, Kinetic theory: average KE per molecule = 3/2 k T. At 300 K, with k = 1.38e-23 J/K: 6.21 x 10^-21 J. The rms speed of nitrogen (M = 0.028 kg/mol) is sqrt(3RT/M) = sqrt(3 x 8.314 x 300 / 0.028) = 517 m/s.
  5. Step 5, Use specific heats. For a monatomic gas Cv = 3/2 R = 12.47 J per mol per K and Cp = 5/2 R, ratio gamma 1.67. Heat 2 mol by 20 K at constant volume: Q = 2 x 12.47 x 20 = 499 J.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: find the efficiency of a Carnot engine between 500 K and 400 K. Answer: 1 - 400/500 = 20 per cent.

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Apply the laws of thermodynamics: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

Unit 7 Oscillations and waves Weeks 33 to 36Understand

In this unit you work on 'Oscillations and waves', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: describe simple harmonic motion and wave speed. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Observe: see, touch, measure or watch a safe demonstration linked to 'Oscillations and waves' and write what you notice.
  • Explain: use the key words of the unit to say what is happening and why, in a few lines.
  • Use it: describe simple harmonic motion and wave speed. Use a diagram, table or short calculation where it fits.
  • Test and conclude: say what evidence supports your answer and what you would change.
Worked example. Solved: Wave speed = frequency x wavelength. 50 Hz and 6.8 m gives 340 m/s. Period = 1 / frequency = 0.02 s. Method: Model run for 'Oscillations and waves'. Question: what do we want to find out? Prediction: one sentence. Method: three safe steps with the equipment named. Record: a small table with units. Conclusion: one sentence that uses the evidence and the key words of the unit. What a good answer looks like: a fair test, measured values with units, and a conclusion that matches the table.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Describe SHM x = A sin(omega t) with A = 0.05 m and period T = 2 s. omega = 2 pi / T = 3.14 rad/s. Maximum speed = A omega = 0.157 m/s and maximum acceleration = A omega^2 = 0.49 m/s2.
  2. Step 2, Spring-mass system. m = 0.5 kg and k = 200 N/m. T = 2 pi sqrt(m/k) = 2 pi sqrt(0.0025) = 2 pi x 0.05 = 0.314 s, so f = 3.18 Hz.
  3. Step 3, Simple pendulum. L = 1 m gives T = 2 pi sqrt(L/g) = 2 pi sqrt(1/9.8) = 2.01 s. Quadrupling L to 4 m doubles T to 4.01 s.
  4. Step 4, Energy in SHM. For the spring system with A = 0.1 m: total energy = 0.5 k A^2 = 0.5 x 200 x 0.01 = 1 J. At x = 0.05 m, PE = 0.5 x 200 x 0.0025 = 0.25 J, so KE = 0.75 J.
  5. Step 5, Wave speed v = f lambda. A wave of f = 50 Hz and lambda = 6.8 m travels at 340 m/s. On a string, v = sqrt(T/mu): with T = 100 N and mu = 0.01 kg/m, v = 100 m/s. Fundamental on 1 m string: f = v / 2L = 50 Hz.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: find the period of a 0.25 m pendulum (g = 9.8). Answer: 2 pi sqrt(0.25/9.8) = 1.00 s.

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Describe simple harmonic motion and wave speed: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Pushes and pulls

On the slide: L1 Basic · Pushes and pulls

Notes: Name forces as pushes or pulls and say how they change movement. Notes: roll a toy car down a ramp onto the floor and mark where it stops; learners push it again with a bigger start and compare. Keep the floor clear so nobody trips.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Energy and simple circuits

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Energy and simple circuits

Notes: Describe energy stores and build a simple circuit that lights a bulb. Notes: use one 1.5 V cell, one small bulb and two leads; learners draw the loop and say what happens when it is broken. Use only cells or batteries at low voltage, never mains sockets.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Speed, distance and time

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Speed, distance and time

Notes: Calculate speed from distance and time and read a distance-time graph. Notes: practice case is 100 m in 12.5 s, so speed is 8 m/s. Learners time a walked 20 m with a phone stopwatch, repeat three times, find the mean, and then work out the speed.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Force, mass and acceleration

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Force, mass and acceleration

Notes: Apply F = ma to find an unknown force, mass or acceleration. Notes: a 1200 kg car with a net force of 3000 N has acceleration 2.5 m/s2. Learners then find the net force when the engine gives 3500 N and drag is 500 N, and check that the answer matches.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Series and parallel circuits (this course)

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Series and parallel circuits

Notes: Analyse resistor networks using Ohm law and the rules for current and voltage. Notes: 24 ohm and 12 ohm in parallel give 8 ohm, since 1/24 plus 1/12 is 1/8. On 6 V, current is 0.75 A. Check power ratings and keep currents low before building; use a meter to verify.

This course: Grade 11 Science Physics is at this level. Its units include Units, measurement and motion in a straight line; Motion in a plane and laws of motion; Work, energy, power and systems of particles; Rotational motion and gravitation and more.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Oscillations and waves

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Oscillations and waves

Notes: Apply the simple harmonic motion model and solve for period, frequency and energy. Notes: a 0.50 kg mass on a spring of constant 200 N/m has period 2 pi times the square root of 0.0025, about 0.314 s. Learners measure ten swings with a stopwatch and compare, keeping the mass low so it cannot hurt a hand.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Uncertainty and fitting

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Uncertainty and fitting

Notes: Evaluate measurements and models with uncertainty propagation, fitting and goodness of fit. Notes: a pendulum with L of 1.000 m and T of 2.007 s gives g equal to 4 pi squared L over T squared, 9.80 m/s2. Learners propagate a 0.002 s timing error, plan repeats, and decide if a model is supported. Check clamps and stand stability.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.
