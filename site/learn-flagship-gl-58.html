Electric Car and EV Basics

Understand how electric vehicles work, charge, cost and stay safe, and how to advise buyers and fleets honestly.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation27:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain motor, battery, charger and range.
  • Apply Estimate running cost and charging time.
  • Analyse Compare EV and petrol costs.
  • Evaluate Judge battery health, safety and resale.
  • Create Write a buyer or fleet advice sheet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce an advice sheet comparing one EV with one petrol car for a given user, with cost, range, charging and safety notes.

Condition

A computer, public specifications, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. No high-voltage work is done.

Standard

Maths is correct and in your currency; assumptions are stated; safety rules for high-voltage parts are included; claims are sourced; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Sales staff, drivers, fleet clerks, technician trainees. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How an electric vehicle works 3 hUnderstand

An electric vehicle stores energy in a battery and uses an electric motor to turn the wheels.

  • Parts: battery pack, inverter, motor, onboard charger, controller, thermal system.
  • Regenerative braking returns some energy to the battery.
  • Energy use is measured in kWh per 100 km.
  • No exhaust emissions at the vehicle; total emissions depend on how electricity is made.
Worked example. A car that uses 15 kWh per 100 km and has a 60 kWh battery can travel about 400 km in ideal conditions.

Activity. Draw a block diagram of an EV and label each part.

Standard to meet. All main parts named correctly.

Module 2 Batteries 3 hUnderstand

The battery is the most costly and sensitive part.

  • Capacity in kWh; usable capacity is less than total.
  • Heat, frequent fast charging and long periods at full or empty age batteries.
  • Warranty usually covers years and distance, with a minimum capacity.
  • Cold weather reduces range.
Worked example. Keeping daily charge between 20 and 80 per cent when possible can slow ageing. Preheating the cabin while plugged in saves range.

Activity. Summarise battery care in five tips.

Standard to meet. Tips are accurate and practical.

Module 3 Charging levels and time 4 hApply

Charging speed depends on power.

  • Slow home charging (around 3 to 7 kW), faster wallbox and public DC fast chargers.
  • Time = energy needed / charging power plus losses.
  • Plug types and compatibility vary by region.
  • Charging to 80 per cent is fastest; the last 20 per cent is slow.
Worked example. To add 30 kWh at 7 kW takes about 4.3 hours plus 10 per cent losses, so roughly 4.7 hours.

Activity. Calculate three charge times for different chargers.

Standard to meet. Maths correct.

Module 4 Running cost comparison 4 hAnalyse

Compare fairly with a petrol or diesel car.

  • EV cost per 100 km = kWh per 100 km x price per kWh.
  • Petrol cost per 100 km = litres per 100 km x price per litre.
  • Add service, insurance, tax and depreciation.
  • Include home charger cost and public charging mix.
Worked example. EV: 15 kWh x 8 INR = 120 INR per 100 km. Petrol: 7 L x 100 INR = 700 INR per 100 km.

Activity. Compare for your place and daily distance.

Standard to meet. Currency shown and sources listed.

Module 5 Real-world range 3 hEvaluate

Label range is not what you get every day.

  • Speed, temperature, load, hills and tyres change range.
  • Plan trips with charger stops and keep a reserve.
  • Use route planners that include chargers.
  • Understand winter effects.
Worked example. Cold weather and highway driving cut range by about 25 per cent. A 400 km range car plans for 300 km on a winter highway trip.

Activity. Plan a 300 km trip with stops.

Standard to meet. Charging stops and reserve listed.

Module 6 Safety 4 hApply

High voltage demands respect.

  • Orange cables are high voltage; only trained technicians work on them.
  • Rescue sheets help emergency services.
  • Never charge with damaged cables or connectors.
  • Keep a fire plan for charging areas.
Worked example. A warning light for battery damage means stop and call the maker or a trained technician.

Activity. Write a one-page safety card.

Standard to meet. Mentor approves the card.

Module 7 Battery end of life 3 hEvaluate

Plan for reuse and recycling.

  • Second-life use in storage for buildings.
  • Recycling recovers metals.
  • Check collection programmes.
  • Avoid illegal dumping.
Worked example. An old pack with 70 per cent capacity powers a shop's backup system for several more years.

Activity. Describe three end-of-life options.

Standard to meet. Options are realistic.

Module 8 Write the advice sheet 3 hCreate

Give honest advice to a buyer or fleet.

  • State fit: daily distance, home charging, budget.
  • List cons as well as pros.
  • Show assumptions and sources.
  • Review after six months.
Worked example. The advice says an EV suits a commuter with home charging but not a user with no parking.

Activity. Write the sheet.

Standard to meet. Reader can decide.

ten locations and global

Your place

Standards, subsidies and tax differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
