ESL level E2: English as a second language

11 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in ESL level E2 English as a second language, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 11 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

11 units with worked examples

Unit 1 Daily routines Weeks 1 to 4Apply

In this unit you work on 'Daily routines', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: talk about my day using the present simple. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Daily routines' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: talk about my day using the present simple, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'I wake up at 6:30. I eat breakfast at 7:00. I go to school at 7:30.' Ask a partner: 'What time do you wake up?' Method: Model run for 'Daily routines'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Write the day in time order using the present simple. Model: "I get up at six. I have breakfast at seven. I go to school at eight." Reason: the present simple describes habits, and each sentence names one action and one time.
  2. Step 2, Add "s" for he, she and it. Model: "My brother gets up at seven. He brushes his teeth. She goes to school by bus." Reason: the third-person singular takes "-s", and "go" becomes "goes", "watch" becomes "watches".
  3. Step 3, Use frequency words before the main verb. Model: "I usually walk to school. I never eat lunch at home." Reason: usually, often, sometimes and never sit before the verb but after "be": "I am always late."
  4. Step 4, Make negatives and questions with do and does. Model: "I don't play games on Monday." "She doesn't eat meat." Question: "What time do you get up?" "Does he walk to school?" After does, the verb stays plain.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "She don't goes to school on Sunday." Right: "She doesn't go to school on Sunday." The helper does carries the "s", so the main verb returns to its base form.
  6. Step 6, Write a short paragraph. "On school days I get up at six. I wash and have breakfast. Then I go to school by bus. After school I do my homework. I go to bed at nine." Reason: time words like then and after link events.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Write four sentences about a friend's day using he or she and one frequency word. Model answer: "My friend gets up at six. She usually walks to school. She doesn't watch TV on weekdays. She goes to bed at ten."

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Talk about my day using the present simple: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

Unit 2 Places in town Weeks 5 to 8Apply

In this unit you work on 'Places in town', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: ask for and give simple directions. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Places in town' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: ask for and give simple directions, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'Excuse me, where is the library?' 'Go straight, turn left at the bank. It is opposite the park.' Method: Model run for 'Places in town'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Learn place words: bank, post office, library, market, hospital, park, bus station. Model: "The library is next to the park." Reason: "next to", "opposite", "between" and "behind" place one building by another.
  2. Step 2, Ask for directions politely. Model: "Excuse me, where is the post office?" or "How do I get to the bank?" Reason: "Excuse me" gets attention, and the question is clear and short.
  3. Step 3, Give directions with imperatives. Model: "Go straight on. Turn left at the traffic lights. Then turn right. The bank is on your left." Reason: verbs at the start of a sentence give direct, clear instructions.
  4. Step 4, Use distance and landmark words. Model: "Walk for two minutes. It's opposite the market." Reason: a landmark, such as the market, helps the listener check they are going the right way.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "Turn to left and go straight." Right: "Turn left and go straight." We say "turn left" without "to". Another error: "It is in front the bank." Right: "It is in front of the bank."
  6. Step 6, Practise a full dialogue. A: "Excuse me, where is the library?" B: "Go straight on. Turn right at the park. It's next to the school." A: "Thank you very much." B: "You're welcome."
  7. Step 7, Your turn. You are at the bus station. Give directions to the hospital: straight, then left at the bank, opposite the park. Model answer: "Go straight on. Turn left at the bank. The hospital is opposite the park."

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Ask for and give simple directions: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

Unit 3 Hobbies and sports Weeks 9 to 12Remember

In this unit you work on 'Hobbies and sports', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: say what I like and can do. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Hobbies and sports' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: say what I like and can do, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'I enjoy swimming and I am good at drawing. I play football on Saturdays.' Method: Model run for 'Hobbies and sports'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Say what you like with verb plus -ing or a noun. Model: "I like swimming." "I love football." "I don't like running." Reason: after like, love and hate we often use the -ing form for activities.
  2. Step 2, Say what you can do with can plus the base verb. Model: "I can play chess." "She can swim very well." Reason: "can" never changes and the next verb has no "s" or "-ing".
  3. Step 3, Ask and answer about hobbies. A: "What do you do in your free time?" B: "I play cricket and I read stories." A: "Can you play the guitar?" B: "No, I can't, but I can sing."
  4. Step 4, Link with and, but and because. Model: "I like tennis, but I can't play it well." "I like painting because it's relaxing." Reason: "but" shows contrast and "because" gives the reason.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "She can swims fast." Right: "She can swim fast." Another wrong: "I like play football." Right: "I like playing football" or "I like to play football."
  6. Step 6, Write a short paragraph. "My name is Ravi. I like cycling and swimming. I play cricket on Saturday. I can't play tennis, but I can play table tennis. I like sport because it's fun."
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Write four sentences about your own hobbies including one can and one but. Model answer: "I like drawing. I play football on Friday. I can swim, but I can't dive. I like music because it is relaxing."

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Say what I like and can do: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

Unit 4 Shopping and money Weeks 13 to 15Apply

In this unit you work on 'Shopping and money', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: ask the price and buy something. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Shopping and money' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: ask the price and buy something, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 3 books at 45 each: 3 x 45 = 135. Paid 200, so change = 200 - 135 = 65. Method: Model run for 'Shopping and money'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Ask the price with "How much". Model: "How much is this shirt?" for one item and "How much are these shoes?" for plural items. Reason: use is for singular and are for plural.
  2. Step 2, Say the answer with the price. Model: "It's twelve dollars." "They are twenty-five dollars." Read prices as words: 4.50 is "four dollars fifty". Reason: say the big unit, then the small one.
  3. Step 3, Ask for what you want politely. Model: "I'd like a notebook, please." "Can I have two pens?" Reason: "I'd like" is the polite short form of "I would like".
  4. Step 4, Pay and check change. Customer: "Here is ten dollars." Seller: "Thank you. Here's your change, three dollars." Customer: "Thank you." Reason: "change" is the money returned, and it is never plural.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "How much cost this bag?" Right: "How much is this bag?" or "How much does this bag cost?" Another error: "I want a bread." Right: "I'd like some bread."
  6. Step 6, Practise the full shopping dialogue. A: "Hello, I'd like an orange juice." B: "That's two dollars." A: "How much are those apples?" B: "They're three dollars a kilo." A: "I'll take one kilo."
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Write a four-line dialogue buying a pen that costs one dollar fifty with a five-dollar note. Model answer: "How much is this pen?" "It's one dollar fifty." "Here is five dollars." "Thank you. Here's your change, three dollars fifty."

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Ask the price and buy something: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

Unit 5 Past events Weeks 16 to 18Understand

In this unit you work on 'Past events', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: tell a simple story about yesterday using the past simple. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Past events' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: tell a simple story about yesterday using the past simple, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'Yesterday I visited my aunt. We played a game and ate lunch.' Regular past: play, played; visit, visited. Irregular: go, went; eat, ate. Method: Model run for 'Past events'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Use the past simple for finished actions with a time word like yesterday. Model: "Yesterday I played football." Regular verbs add "-ed": play, played; walk, walked; clean, cleaned.
  2. Step 2, Learn irregular verbs. Go becomes went, eat becomes ate, see becomes saw, have becomes had. Model: "We went to the market and saw a monkey." Reason: these common verbs change form and must be remembered.
  3. Step 3, Make negatives and questions with did. Model: "I didn't watch TV." "Did you finish your homework?" Answer: "Yes, I did." Reason: after did and didn't the verb returns to the base form.
  4. Step 4, Order a story with sequence words. "First I got up late. Then I ate breakfast. After that I went to school. Finally I came home." Reason: first, then, after that and finally show the order of events.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "Yesterday I go to the park." Right: "Yesterday I went to the park." Another error: "She didn't went." Right: "She didn't go." The past mark is on did only.
  6. Step 6, Write a short story. "Yesterday was Sunday. I got up at eight and ate rice. Then I visited my grandmother. We cooked dinner together. I went to bed early because I was tired."
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Write four sentences about what you did yesterday using two irregular verbs. Model answer: "Yesterday I went to school. I saw my friend. After lunch I played cricket. In the evening I did my homework."

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Tell a simple story about yesterday using the past simple: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

Unit 6 Describing people and things Weeks 19 to 21Apply

In this unit you work on 'Describing people and things', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: use adjectives and comparatives. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Describing people and things' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: use adjectives and comparatives, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'My cousin is tall and has short black hair. She is kind and funny.' Use is/has and two describing words. Method: Model run for 'Describing people and things'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Describe people with adjectives after "be": tall, short, kind, funny, quiet. Model: "My uncle is tall and funny." Put the adjective before a noun too: "He is a kind man."
  2. Step 2, Describe things with size, then shape, then colour. Model: "I have a small round red ball." Reason: size comes before shape, and shape before colour. An opinion word comes first of all: "a nice small ball."
  3. Step 3, Make short comparatives with "-er than". Model: "Anil is taller than Bina." "This bag is lighter than that bag." Reason: for one-syllable adjectives add "-er" and use "than" before the second thing.
  4. Step 4, Make longer comparatives with "more". Model: "The film is more interesting than the book." "A car is more expensive than a bicycle." Reason: adjectives of two or more syllables use "more" instead of "-er".
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "She is more tall than me." Right: "She is taller than me." Another: "This is more good." Right: "This is better." Irregular: good, better; bad, worse. For "big", double the g: bigger.
  6. Step 6, Compare two things in a paragraph. "My dog is bigger than my cat. The cat is quieter than the dog. The dog is friendlier, but the cat is cleaner."
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Compare two animals using three sentences with one -er and one more. Model answer: "A horse is faster than a cow. A cow is heavier than a goat. A horse is more beautiful than a cow, in my opinion."

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Use adjectives and comparatives: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

Unit 7 Health and feelings Weeks 22 to 24Remember

In this unit you work on 'Health and feelings', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: say how I feel and what is wrong. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Health and feelings' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: say how I feel and what is wrong, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'I have a headache. I feel tired. I should drink water and rest.' Ask: 'How do you feel?' Method: Model run for 'Health and feelings'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Say how you feel using "I feel" or "I am" plus an adjective: happy, sad, tired, angry, nervous. Model: "I feel tired today." "I am happy because my friend is here."
  2. Step 2, Say what is wrong with "have a" or "my ... hurts". Model: "I have a headache." "I have a sore throat." "My stomach hurts." Reason: English uses "have a" for common ailments.
  3. Step 3, Talk to a doctor. Doctor: "What's the matter?" Patient: "I have a fever and a cough." Doctor: "How long have you felt like this?" Patient: "Since yesterday." Reason: "since" names the start time.
  4. Step 4, Give advice with should. Model: "You should rest." "You should drink water." "You shouldn't go to school today." Reason: "should" is followed by the base verb and is used for advice.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "I am headache." Right: "I have a headache." Another wrong: "You should to rest." Right: "You should rest." There is no "to" after should.
  6. Step 6, Practise a full dialogue. A: "You look sick. What's the matter?" B: "I have a bad cold." A: "You should go home and rest." B: "Yes, I think so. Thank you."
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Write three lines saying how you feel, what is wrong, and one piece of advice. Model answer: "I feel terrible. I have a sore throat. I should drink warm water and rest."

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Say how I feel and what is wrong: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

Unit 10 Listening for information Weeks 31 to 33Apply

In this unit you work on 'Listening for information', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: take notes from a short audio. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Listening for information' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: take notes from a short audio, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Read the questions first. Listen for names, numbers and times. Note: bus 7:45, platform 3. Then answer in a full sentence. Method: Model run for 'Listening for information'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Preview the topic before the audio. If the audio is about a bus timetable, expect times, places and numbers. Reason: knowing what to expect helps you notice the key details instead of every word.
  2. Step 2, Take notes with short words and symbols. Hear: "The bus to the lake leaves at half past eight from stop five." Note: "bus - lake - 8.30 - stop 5." Reason: notes keep only the facts and drop the extra words.
  3. Step 3, Listen for numbers carefully. Fifteen and fifty sound alike: fif-TEEN has the stress at the end, and FIF-ty at the start. Model note: "15 min" or "50 min". Replay the line to check.
  4. Step 4, Write answers from notes in full sentences. Notes: "bus - lake - 8.30 - stop 5." Answer: "The bus to the lake leaves at eight thirty from stop five." Reason: a sentence made from notes sounds complete and accurate.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong note: "bus 8.13" when the speaker said "eight-thirty". Hint: "-teen" sounds end strongly, but "thirty" starts strongly. Check with another detail, such as "half past".
  6. Step 6, Practise with a short script. Audio: "The market opens at nine on Tuesday and Friday. Entry is free. It's near the old bridge." Notes: "market - 9 - Tue, Fri - free - old bridge."
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Audio: "The swimming class is on Wednesday at four. Bring a towel. The teacher is Mr Khan." Take notes and write two answers. Model answer: notes "swim - Wed - 4 - towel - Mr Khan"; "The class is on Wednesday at four." "The teacher is Mr Khan."

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Take notes from a short audio: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

Unit 11 Review and showcase Weeks 34 to 36Apply

In this unit you work on 'Review and showcase', taught in a way that is short, spoken, with pictures and real objects. The target is clear: present a short project. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Review and showcase' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: present a short project, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Read each question twice, underline the command word (describe, explain, compare), plan three points, and leave two minutes to check. Method: Model run for 'Review and showcase'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Pick a project topic you can describe: "My favourite place". Plan three parts: introduction, three details, ending. Reason: a clear plan keeps your talk short and easy to follow.
  2. Step 2, Write the introduction. "Hello, everyone. My project is about my favourite place, the lake near my village. I'm going to tell you where it is, what I do there, and why I like it."
  3. Step 3, Write the details with present simple and past simple. "It is five minutes from my house. On Sundays I swim and fish. Last summer I saw a white bird there." Reason: present simple gives habits and past simple gives a memory.
  4. Step 4, Add reasons with because and a comparison. "I like it because it is quiet. It is cleaner than the river." Reason: reasons and comparisons make the talk more interesting than simple facts.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "I am going to tell you about my place is nice." Right: "I'm going to tell you about my place. It is nice." Keep each sentence to one main verb.
  6. Step 6, Finish and invite questions. "Thank you for listening. Does anyone have a question?" Audience: "How do you get there?" Answer: "I go on foot. It takes about five minutes." Reason: a closing line signals the end and invites response.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Write a five-sentence introduction and ending for a project on your favourite food. Model answer: "Hello, everyone. My project is about rice. I eat it every day because it is cheap and tasty. Thank you. Any questions?"

Activity. About 15 to 25 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a voice note or one page of work.

Standard to meet. Present a short project: at least 4 of 5 attempts understood by a listener who does not know you.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: First words and survival phrases (this course)

On the slide: L1 Basic · First words and survival phrases

Notes: Say hello, give your name and ask for help in simple English. Notes: teach Hello, My name is... and Can you help me? with gestures and pictures; learners practise each phrase with three partners and point to objects as they name them.

This course: ESL level E2 English as a second language is at this level. Its units include Daily routines; Places in town; Hobbies and sports; Shopping and money and more.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Everyday words and short sentences

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Everyday words and short sentences

Notes: Understand and write short sentences about family, food and daily routine, using present simple. Notes: show I eat rice, she eats bread; learners write five true sentences about their day, read them to a partner, and fix the verb ending together.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Questions, past tense and short texts

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Questions, past tense and short texts

Notes: Ask and answer questions and write a short paragraph about last weekend using past simple. Notes: contrast I go and I went; learners interview a partner using What did you do?, then write six sentences from the answers and underline each past verb.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Real-life communication and listening

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Real-life communication and listening

Notes: Follow a two-minute conversation and take part in a phone call, a shop visit or a clinic visit. Notes: play a short recording with a booking at a clinic; learners note three key facts, then role-play the same call with their own details and ask for repetition politely.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Academic and workplace English

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Academic and workplace English

Notes: Write a 250-word report or email and give a short talk with clear structure, linking words and few errors. Notes: teach First, However, As a result; learners rewrite a plain paragraph with three linkers, then present it in two minutes with a peer giving feedback.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Language analysis and teaching methods

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Language analysis and teaching methods

Notes: Explain why learners make common errors and design a lesson that targets them using evidence from learner writing. Notes: collect ten samples; learners classify errors such as article, tense or word order, rank them by frequency, and plan a thirty-minute fix for the top two.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Research and curriculum design

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Research and curriculum design

Notes: Design a classroom-based study or a full course plan for adult learners, with measures and ethics set out. Notes: choose a question such as whether peer feedback improves writing; define the sample, the pre-test and post-test, and one risk, then present the plan for critique.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.
