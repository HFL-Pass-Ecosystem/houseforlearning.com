Solar Power Design and Installation Basics

Learn to size, design and plan a small solar system with correct safety, cost, layout and handover.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation33:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain how panels, inverters and batteries work together.
  • Apply Size a small system from a load list.
  • Analyse Check roof, shade and orientation.
  • Evaluate Judge safety, cost and payback.
  • Create Produce a design pack and handover sheet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design a small rooftop solar system for a practice home or shop, with load list, size, layout, safety plan, cost, payback and handover sheet.

Condition

A computer, a spreadsheet, a floor plan or satellite view, two peers and a licensed electrician mentor. Installation on live wiring is done only by licensed people.

Standard

Load list and size are correct; roof and shade checks are done; protective devices and earthing are listed; cost and payback shown; a licensed person signs the design; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Trainee electricians, technicians, small shop owners, community energy workers. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How a solar system works 3 hUnderstand

Panels make direct current, an inverter changes it to usable power, and protection keeps people and wiring safe.

  • Panels produce power from light; output falls with shade, heat and dust.
  • Grid-tied systems feed the building and the grid; off-grid systems need batteries.
  • The inverter matches voltage and frequency and shuts down if the grid fails.
  • Meters, breakers and earthing complete the system.
Worked example. A 3 kW array in a sunny place makes about 4 kWh per kW per day, so around 12 kWh a day.

Activity. Draw a block diagram of a grid-tied system.

Standard to meet. Diagram shows panels, isolator, inverter, protection, meter and load in order.

Module 2 Load list 4 hApply

List every device before you size anything.

  • Record watts and hours used each day for each device.
  • Add starting surge for motors.
  • Separate essential loads from others.
  • Total energy per day = sum of watts x hours.
Worked example. A shop has 6 lights at 10 W for 10 hours (0.6 kWh), 2 fans at 60 W for 8 hours (0.96 kWh) and a fridge using 1.5 kWh a day. Total is 3.06 kWh a day.

Activity. Build a load list for a practice shop.

Standard to meet. Every device is listed with watts and hours and the total is correct.

Module 3 Site survey 4 hAnalyse

The roof and the wiring decide what is possible.

  • Measure usable roof area, tilt and direction.
  • Check shade at morning, noon and afternoon.
  • Inspect roof strength and old wiring.
  • Photograph and record the main panel.
Worked example. A roof with 16 usable square metres facing south fits about eight panels of 2 square metres; a water tank shades two panels in the afternoon and they are moved.

Activity. Complete a survey sheet for a practice roof.

Standard to meet. Area, tilt, shade and panel condition are recorded with photos or sketches.

Module 4 Sizing the system 5 hApply

Match energy need, sunshine and losses to a panel count.

  • Array kW = daily kWh / (sun hours x 0.75 efficiency).
  • Choose an inverter close to array size.
  • Check voltage and current limits of the inverter.
  • Check the roof can carry the number of panels.
Worked example. A shop using 3.06 kWh a day with 5 sun hours needs 3.06 / (5 x 0.75) = 0.82 kW, so two panels of 450 W (0.9 kW) cover it on paper. The owner adds margin for dust and ageing and chooses three panels, 1.35 kW.

Activity. Size the array and inverter for your load list.

Standard to meet. Formula, numbers and margin are shown and limits are checked.

Module 5 Electrical protection and earthing 5 hApply

Protection prevents fire and shock.

  • Direct current isolator, alternating current breaker and surge protection.
  • Correct cable size and colour for the current and distance.
  • Earth the frames and the system to the correct standard.
  • Label every switch and cable.
Worked example. A cable too thin for the current heats up; the design uses the next size up and keeps the voltage drop within the allowed limit.

Activity. Draw a single line diagram with protection.

Standard to meet. All protective devices are present and sized, and earthing is shown.

Module 6 Safety on site 4 hEvaluate

Work at height and live electricity can kill.

  • Use harness, edge protection and safe ladders.
  • Isolate and lock out before any work; test for dead.
  • Never work alone on the roof; watch for heat and rain.
  • Only licensed people connect to the main supply.
Worked example. A team stops work when wind rises because a panel acts like a sail and can pull a worker off the roof.

Activity. Write a site safety plan for one install day.

Standard to meet. Plan covers height, electrical, weather, roles and emergency steps.

Module 7 Cost and payback 4 hEvaluate

Show the owner what it costs and when it pays back.

  • List panels, inverter, mounting, cables, protection and labour.
  • Annual saving = kWh made x price per kWh.
  • Payback = cost / annual saving.
  • Include maintenance and inverter replacement.
Worked example. A 1.35 kW system costing 90,000 INR (an estimate; check your market) makes about 1,800 kWh a year, but the shop uses only about 1,100 kWh (3.06 x 365), so it saves 1,100 kWh x 8 INR per kWh = 8,800 INR a year. Payback is about 10 years.

Activity. Build the cost and payback table.

Standard to meet. Costs add up, assumptions are listed and payback is correct.

Module 8 Design pack and handover 4 hCreate

Hand over clear documents so the owner can use and maintain the system.

  • Include load list, layout, single line diagram and cost.
  • Give a user guide and a cleaning and check schedule.
  • Record serial numbers and warranties.
  • Explain what to do if the system trips.
Worked example. The owner receives a folder and a one-page card by the inverter listing who to call and what lights mean.

Activity. Assemble the full design pack.

Standard to meet. All documents are present, consistent and understandable to a non-technical owner.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: load list, sizing sheet, layout drawing, safety plan, cost sheet, handover sheet. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
SizingWrongCorrectCorrect with checks
ProtectionMissingListedListed and approved
CostIncompleteCompleteComplete with sensitivity
System numbersSizes given with no workingLoad list, kWh per day and panel count shown step by stepWorking checked by a second person, with margin and losses explained
Electrical code and permitsCode not checkedWiring code, permit and inspector steps for the place are written downCode rules cited and a licensed installer has signed off the design

ten locations and global

Your place

Electrical and net-metering rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global
