CPD · Financial and insurance services

Identifying conflicts of interest

Spotting and declaring conflicts before they cause harm.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 3 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playFinancial and insurance services

Spotting and declaring conflicts before they cause harm.

  • Recognise a conflict, given 8 short cases, with at least 7 correct.
  • Declare a conflict, given your policy, using the right form and person.
  • Manage a conflict, given a scenario, choosing an action that protects the client.
  • Record the decision, given a template, with reasons and date.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Recognise conflicts of interest in short cases, declare them with the firm's wording before advice, and record the decision and named approver in the register.

Condition

Given eight short cases, the firm's disclosure wording, a conflicts register template and a named approver, working in a three-hour supervised session.

Standard

Classify at least 7 of 8 cases correctly, declare in writing before any advice, and record the conflict, action and approver so the decision can be traced.

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 What a conflict is, with everyday examples 20 min
  • A conflict is a clash between your interest and the client's
  • Spot it, then declare it
  • A conflict is not always a breach
Worked example. Worked-example figures: an adviser earns a bonus of 150 if a client picks product P. Personal gain could affect the duty to the client, so it is a conflict. Everyday match: choosing a supplier owned by your cousin.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "what a conflict is, with everyday examples": recognise a conflict, given 8 short cases, with at least 7 correct.

Standard to meet. Define a conflict of interest in plain words and recognise it in everyday examples where personal or firm gain could affect your duty to the client.

Module 2 Eight short cases 30 min
  • Mark conflict or not conflict
  • Name the action: declare, avoid or ask
  • Give a reason in one line
Worked example. Case 3: adviser holds shares in fund F and is about to suggest fund F. Conflict: declare. Case 5: adviser has lunch with a client at the client's own cost. Not a conflict by itself. Target: 7 of 8 correct is 87.5 percent.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "eight short cases": declare a conflict, given your policy, using the right form and person.

Standard to meet. Judge eight short cases, marking each as a conflict or not a conflict and naming the action that fits, such as declaring, avoiding or asking.

Module 3 Disclosure wording 20 min
  • Plain words
  • Written, before advice
  • Approved wording only
Worked example. Sample wording (use your firm's approved text): "Before I advise you, I must tell you that I receive a bonus if you choose product P. You may ask about other options." Given in writing, before advice.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "disclosure wording": manage a conflict, given a scenario, choosing an action that protects the client.

Standard to meet. Use the firm's approved disclosure wording to tell a client about a conflict before giving any advice, in writing.

Module 4 Recording the decision and approver 15 min
  • Date, conflict, action, approver
  • Approver is named, not the adviser
  • So it can be traced later
Worked example. Register line: 12 May | Conflict: bonus of 150 on product P | Action: disclosed in writing, alternatives offered | Approver: team manager | Decision: proceed with disclosure.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "recording the decision and approver": record the decision, given a template, with reasons and date.

Standard to meet. Record each conflict, the action taken and the named approver in the register, so the decision can be traced later.

Module 5 Practice 10 min
  • Decide, disclose, record
  • Use a new case
  • Correct the missed step
Worked example. Practice case: a fund run by your relative. Step 1 decide: conflict. Step 2 declare to client and approver. Step 3 record. A learner who forgot step 3 adds the register line and ticks all 3 steps.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "identifying conflicts of interest" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (recognise a conflict; declare a conflict; manage a conflict; record the decision).

Standard to meet. Practise deciding, disclosing and recording on a new case, such as a fund run by a relative, and correct any step you missed.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Short and written
  • Three points
  • Say it in your own words
Worked example. Check: define a conflict (when personal or firm gain could affect duty to the client); first step (declare); record (conflict, action, approver). 3 of 3 required.

Practise: Take the end check for "identifying conflicts of interest": show that you can record the decision, given a template, with reasons and date, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Show in a short check that you can define a conflict, take the first step of declaring it and record it with the approver.

Module 7 Second case: a gift and a relative 45 min
  • Work case card 2 and a gift case
  • Apply the three steps
  • Check the register line
Worked example. Case card 2: you are asked to recommend a fund managed by a company your cousin runs. Decide: conflict. Disclose to client and approver before advice. Record. Gift case: a client offers a gift worth 80 and the firm limit is 50, so 30 over the limit; declare it to the approver, decline or log it.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second case: a gift and a relative": record the decision, given a template, with reasons and date. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Decide, disclose and record the decision for the cousin case and a gift case, then check each register line for the conflict, action and named approver.

Module 8 Teach-back and peer check 35 min
  • Explain the three steps in one minute
  • Peer checks each case
  • Give feedback using the list
Worked example. Teach-back: "A conflict is when personal or firm gain could affect my duty to the client. I declare it first, in writing, before advice. I record the conflict, my action and the approver." Peer marks 3 of 3 on the list.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "teach-back and peer check": record the decision, given a template, with reasons and date. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Explain the define, declare and record steps aloud in one minute so that a peer can confirm all three are present and in the right order.

When disclose?

Model answer: Before advice, in writing. (Covered in module 3: Disclosure wording. To pass that module: Use the firm's approved disclosure wording to tell a client about a conflict before giving any advice, in writing.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What is a conflict of interest. Speaker note: A conflict happens when what is good for you or your firm could pull against what is good for the client. Learners at this level can say this in one sentence and give one everyday example.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Spotting the signs. Speaker note: Show everyday examples: a bonus on one product, a relative's company, a gift. Learners know the words declare, avoid and ask and understand that spotting a conflict is not an accusation.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Mark the case. Speaker note: Learners mark simple written cases as conflict or not conflict, with a mentor beside them. They read the firm's policy and find who the named approver is.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Decide, declare, record. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner judges eight cases, uses approved wording to disclose before advice, and records the conflict, action and approver in the register so the decision can be traced.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Hard cases. Speaker note: The specialist handles layered cases: several small interests together, interests of colleagues, or gifts over time. They advise colleagues on grey areas and escalate quickly when unsure.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Managing the register. Speaker note: The advanced worker reviews the register for patterns, checks that approvers decide on time, reports trends to senior managers and updates the policy when the same case keeps appearing.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: A culture that speaks up. Speaker note: The super expert designs the firm's conflicts framework: policy, wording, training, review and speaking-up routes. They measure success by early declaration, not by the number of breaches hidden.

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Credits stack; aligned with, not accredited by, any framework.

Your place

Use your own body's forms, units and currency. Local professional bodies set the rules for CPD hours; check them first.

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Credit and levels: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Credit and levels: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Credit and levels: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Credit and levels: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Credit and levels: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Credit and levels: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Credit and levels: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Mexico
