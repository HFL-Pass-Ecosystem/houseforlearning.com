Nanotechnology In Medicine: Hong Kong deep edition

Nanotechnology In Medicine planned in detail for Hong Kong: sector lessons plus local rules, jobs and HKD examples. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real. All amounts are estimates for planning, not quotes. The module lines use rounded sample numbers; the Local example in module 6 uses separate local estimates.

Task, condition and standard

Task

a study plan and paper review for one narrow nanomaterial use

Condition

Over 6 hours as a planning study only, with checks by a mentor and a peer. Check local rules with an expert.

Standard

The plan has one question, a control, 4 dated rule rows and 3 named weaknesses in a paper; it makes no clinical claim.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read your local market 4 hUnderstand

Every plan starts with the place. Read Hong Kong as your customer does before you choose a sector.

  • Pick one narrow area to study, for example a coating, a test strip, or a delivery material. Plan it as a study only.
  • A dense, trade-led city. Space is costly, so online and shared-space models fit well.
  • Money moves fast by bank transfer and fast-payment apps; customers expect instant confirmation.
  • Three languages in daily use: your page in two (Traditional Chinese and English) is the minimum.
  • Close to the wider region: many small firms serve customers across the border, so shipping and data rules matter early. Check with a local expert.
Worked example. Study page: one question, one material, one use; no clinical claim and no patient work by you. | Local example: Market page for Hong Kong: size in one line, three languages or fewer, main payment methods, three public holidays that affect delivery, and one risk. Each line has a source you can open and a date.

Activity. Write a one-page market page with sources and dates. Ask two local people to correct it.

Standard to meet. Cite a source and a date for every line, have two local readers confirm the facts, and name one risk with a response.

Module 2 Choose your sector 4 hAnalyse

The global list has 75 sectors in 8 clusters. Pick by local fit, not by fashion.

  • Compare three models: partner with a licensed lab, license an existing method, or serve as a research service. Judge by time and cost. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster A, Online selling and marketing: Strong fit: customers shop on phones and expect delivery within a day. Compete on speed and service, not price.
  • Cluster B, Technology and digital services: Fit for services sold to small firms: IT support, testing, privacy. Offices are small, so offer monthly plans. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster C, Health, care and wellbeing (regulated): Regulated. Practise only with the right licence and registration. This course teaches planning, not practice. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster D, Mobility, aerial and space (regulated): Regulated and space-limited. Plan around airspace and road rules; start with charging points or shared mobility on paper. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster E, Energy, building and environment: High-rise living drives smart-energy, water and home-sharing ideas. Check building rules and landlord approval first. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster F, Food and farming: Land is scarce: vertical farms, ghost kitchens and alternative proteins suit small floor areas.
  • Cluster G, Money services (regulated): Regulated money services. Plan as a partner of a licensed firm; never hold customer money without a licence. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster H, People and business support: Coaching, coworking and outsourcing fit well; many one-person firms need flexible support.
Worked example. Lab partner HKD 4,000 per run, license HKD 1,600 per month, service HKD 2,400 per report: compare by speed and risk. | Local example: Scoring example: pick three sectors, score each 1 to 5 on local demand, skills you hold, start-up cost, rules and partner access. A sector that scores 5 on demand but 1 on rules is a later project, not a first one. Pick the sector with the highest total and a rules score of 3 or more.

Activity. Rank six sectors from at least three clusters. Choose one and write the reason in three lines.

Standard to meet. Score six sectors on five criteria, state the reason for your choice, and mark any regulated sector as planning only.

Module 3 Register and license 4 hApply

Rules differ by place. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real.

  • Check medical device, drug, ethics and lab safety rules. Partner with a licensed firm for all testing and trials. Check with a local expert.
  • Register the business with the tax authority (business registration) before you trade; companies are also listed with the companies registry. Check with a local expert.
  • Choose sole trader, partnership or company: a company separates personal and business risk, but has yearly filing duties. Check with a local expert.
  • Some activities need an extra licence (food, health, money, education centres). List yours before you take a first order. Check with a local expert.
  • Keep a registered address and a named contact; a shared-office address is common for a first year.
Worked example. Rules table: lab licence, ethics approval, device class, safety rule; each has a date checked. | Local example: Licence list for a small Nanotechnology In Medicine business: registration type, tax registration, address, any activity licence, insurance. Next to each, write who issues it, what it costs in HKD (from their public page), how long it takes, and the date you checked.

Activity. Build a licence table for your chosen sector with issuer, cost, time and the date checked. Do not apply; this is a plan.

Standard to meet. Complete the table with a public source for every row, put the steps in a logical order, and write one question for an adviser.

Module 4 Protect data and customers 3 hApply

Trust is the real product. Protect customer data and keep your promises in writing.

  • Keep trial data clean: record who, what and when, keep an audit trail, and protect any patient data.
  • The personal data ordinance sets six principles, from collection and use to security and access.
  • Say in plain words what you collect and why; give a way to see and correct it.
  • Direct marketing needs a clear choice for the customer to say no; keep that record.
  • Sending data outside the city may need a safeguard; check with a local expert and list every tool that stores customer data.
Worked example. Data log: each sample has an ID, date, handler and storage place; no patient names. | Local example: Privacy notice in five lines: who we are; what we collect; why; who sees it; how to ask for a copy, correction or removal. Plus a data map listing each tool and where it stores data.

Activity. Write the five-line notice and a data map for your plan. Ask a peer to find any gap.

Standard to meet. Write the notice in plain words, list every tool in the data map, and write and date the refund and complaint steps.

Module 5 Find and serve customers 4 hCreate

Go where your customers already are, in the language they use. Language: Cantonese, Mandarin and English; write the page in Traditional Chinese and English.

  • Gather proof from partner results: a peer-reviewed method, a lab report, and a test date. Never claim a cure.
  • Channels: messaging apps, social media and marketplaces are where customers already are.
  • Language: show both languages side by side; keep prices in HKD only.
  • Payments: bank transfer and fast-payment apps first; give an instant receipt.
  • Trust: put the business registration number, a real address and a refund note on every page. Check with a local expert.
Worked example. Proof sheet: 3 lab runs, same result 3 times, report dated and signed by the lab. | Local example: Test plan: show the offer to 20 real people through two channels over two weeks; count enquiries, replies and first orders. Example result: 20 shown, 6 enquiries, 2 orders: a 10% order rate. Change one thing (price or wording) and test again.

Activity. Test two channels with 20 people. Record results in a table and write the next change.

Standard to meet. Test has a start and end date; results are counted, not guessed; one change is chosen and justified.

Module 6 Price, tax and money 4 hEvaluate

Know your numbers in HKD. Tax rule in one line: No sales tax or VAT; profits tax has a lower first-band rate, so read the current table.

  • Plan the cost of research: lab runs, materials, review and a reserve. Calculate the total.
  • Write HKD next to every number.
  • Contribution per unit = price minus the cost of each unit.
  • Break-even units = fixed monthly cost divided by contribution per unit; round up.
  • Add tax and duty explicitly; never bury them in a price. Check with a local expert.
  • Check against a worse case: if sales are 25% lower, do you still cover costs?
Worked example. 6 runs x HKD 4,000 = HKD 24,000. Materials HKD 3,200 review HKD 2,400 so HKD 29,600. Reserve 10% of HKD 29,600 = HKD 2,960: total HKD 32,560. | Local example: For a small Nanotechnology In Medicine business (all amounts are estimates): price HKD 28,000 per unit, cost HKD 18,000 per unit, fixed cost HKD 90,000 per month. Contribution = 28,000 - 18,000 = 10,000. Break-even = 90,000 / 10,000 = 9.0, so 9 units per month (one unit = one test batch). If sales fall 25% from 18, you sell 13 and the margin is 40,000.

Activity. Build your own sheet in HKD: price, unit cost, fixed cost, contribution, break-even and a 25% worse case. Put the tax and duty rules in a separate line with a source.

Standard to meet. Complete all six lines of the sheet in HKD, have a peer check the arithmetic, and show the worse case.

Module 7 People and partners 3 hApply

Plans become real through people. Hire, partner and write every promise down.

  • List partners: a licensed lab, a clinical adviser, a regulatory adviser, and a university group. Write who signs off what. Check with a local expert.
  • Employers usually pay a compulsory retirement-fund contribution for staff; budget it from the first hire.
  • Written terms, rest days and paid leave follow the employment law; keep signed copies. Check with a local expert.
  • Freelancers are common; write scope, price and date in one page before work starts.
  • Partner with a college, training body or business chamber to find helpers and to practise.
Worked example. Sign-off table: lab signs results, adviser signs the safety review, you sign the plan. | Local example: Role card for a first helper: tasks, hours, pay in HKD, trial period, review date, and one measure of success. Signed by both sides.

Activity. Write one role card and one partner letter (what each side gives). Role-play a first meeting for 10 minutes.

Standard to meet. Write a role card with pay in local currency, hours and a review date, balance the partner letter, and keep signed copies of both.

Module 8 Launch plan and review 4 hCreate

Finish with a one-page launch plan and a plan to review it every month.

  • Decide a go or no-go rule: a signed lab partner, a safety review passed, and a funded study plan before any spend. Check with a local expert.
  • Dates: first test, first order, first review.
  • One measure per week: enquiries, orders or revenue; pick one.
  • A monthly review: what we planned, what happened, what we change.
  • Keep a risk list with a response for each; include a rules change. Check with a local expert.
  • Local caution: A city with high costs: test one small offer before you sign a lease.
Worked example. Check: partner signed, review passed, HKD 32,560 funded: go; review open: no-go. | Local example: Launch page for Hong Kong: Week 1 licence table done; Week 2 test with 20 people; Week 3 numbers sheet checked; Week 4 launch decision with a go or no-go rule written in advance.

Activity. Write the launch page and present it to two people for 10 minutes. Record their questions and your answers.

Standard to meet. Plan has dates, one weekly measure, a go or no-go rule and a risk list; presenter answers three questions with evidence.

jobs

Where this takes you

  • Nanotechnology In Medicine assistant. supports a small Nanotechnology In Medicine business in Hong Kong.
  • Online shop operator. runs product pages, orders and delivery for a small seller.
  • Digital marketing assistant. plans posts, reports results and tests offers.
  • IT support technician. fixes and monitors small-office systems.
  • Coworking community coordinator. runs events and member support.
  • Business coach assistant. prepares plans, trackers and follow-ups for clients.

Six steps from entry to senior

  1. Nanotechnology In Medicine assistant (L2, Entry). helps with set tasks, keeps records and follows the checklist.
  2. Junior Nanotechnology In Medicine coordinator (L3, Junior). runs small jobs end to end and reports each week.
  3. Nanotechnology In Medicine practitioner (L4, Practitioner). plans and delivers the work alone, with a mentor check.
  4. Nanotechnology In Medicine specialist (L5, Specialist). goes deep in one area, such as rules, numbers or customers.
  5. Nanotechnology In Medicine team lead or manager (L6, Advanced). leads people and partners, owns the plan and the data.
  6. Senior Nanotechnology In Medicine adviser or mentor (L7, Senior). designs the whole system, reviews others and publishes cases.

See how these steps connect to study and work in Career pathways and Pathways.
