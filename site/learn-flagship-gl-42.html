flagship course

Fitness Technology

Choose, set up and use wearables, apps and simple sensors to plan safe training and track progress honestly.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner40:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain what common fitness sensors measure and where they are weak.
  • Apply Set up a tracker and app for one training goal.
  • Analyse Read a week of data and spot patterns and errors.
  • Evaluate Judge whether a device or app is accurate, private and worth its cost.
  • Create Build a 4-week safe training plan with a data log.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Build and follow a 4-week training plan for one willing adult or yourself, using one tracker and one app, with a weekly data review.

Condition

A phone, one wearable or the phone sensors, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. A qualified coach or doctor is asked first when health conditions exist.

Standard

Plan is safe and written with a start check; data is logged on at least 24 of 28 days; at least two device errors are found and explained; privacy settings are reviewed; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Fitness instructors, gym staff, teachers, community sport leaders and anyone training with a device. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Goals and a safe start 4 hUnderstand

Technology helps only when the goal is clear and the start is safe.

  • Write one goal with a number and a date, such as walk 8,000 steps on five days a week for four weeks.
  • Do a start check: ask about illness, injury, medicines, pregnancy; if any apply, ask a doctor or qualified coach before you plan.
  • Never push through chest pain, dizziness or sharp pain; stop and seek help.
Worked example. Aarti wants to run 5 km. She notes a knee injury last year, asks a physiotherapist first, and chooses a walk-run plan for four weeks.

Activity. Write your goal and start check.

Standard to meet. Goal has a number and date; any risk is checked with a qualified person.

Module 2 What sensors measure 4 hUnderstand

Each sensor measures something indirectly, and every one has error.

  • Steps come from motion; heart rate from light on the skin or a chest strap; sleep from movement and heart rate; calories are estimates.
  • Errors grow with dark skin tone or tattoos for light sensors, cold hands, loose straps and unusual movement.
  • Compare to a simple truth: count 100 steps by hand, or a 60-second pulse by finger.
Worked example. A wrist band says 112 steps for a hand-counted 100: error 12%. Chest strap and band show 148 and 141 beats per minute in the same minute.

Activity. Test your device against two hand checks and record the error.

Standard to meet. Error percentage is calculated and written next to each check.

Module 3 Set up app, device and data 5 hApply

Good set-up avoids bad data.

  • Fit the device snugly; set height, weight and age correctly; choose the right activity type.
  • Sync daily; keep software updated; charge before sessions.
  • Decide what you will log: sessions, minutes, effort from 1 to 10, sleep hours, soreness.
Worked example. Ravi finds his steps double count while pushing a stroller; he turns that activity mode off and notes it.

Activity. Set up and write a one-page set-up record.

Standard to meet. Settings recorded; test sync passes; the log has seven fields.

Module 4 Training plan basics 6 hCreate

A plan has progress, rest and variety.

  • Increase load slowly, about 10% a week; include two rest or light days.
  • Mix cardio, strength and mobility; warm up and cool down.
  • Write the plan as a week table with minutes, effort and the data to log.
Worked example. Week 1: 3 walks of 30 minutes, 2 strength sessions of 20 minutes. Week 2: walks of 33 minutes.

Activity. Write a 4-week plan table.

Standard to meet. Weekly increases are 10% or less; two rest days each week.

Module 5 Reading the data 6 hAnalyse

Data is useful when you look at trends, not single days.

  • Use weekly averages; ignore one odd day.
  • Look for patterns: poor sleep before low effort days, high resting heart rate after illness.
  • Mark missing days and device errors; do not fill gaps with guesses.
Worked example. The weekly average of resting heart rate rises from 62 to 68 after a cold; the plan eases for three days.

Activity. Review your first two weeks and write three findings.

Standard to meet. Findings use averages and name at least one data error.

Module 6 Privacy, ethics and fairness 5 hEvaluate

Health data is sensitive.

  • Read what the app collects and shares; turn off location sharing and public maps near your home.
  • Never share another person's data without clear consent; a coach or employer must not force data sharing.
  • Avoid comparing bodies in ways that shame; celebrate effort.
Worked example. A school club shares only team totals and not names after pupils object.

Activity. Complete a privacy checklist for your app.

Standard to meet. At least five settings reviewed and written down.

Module 7 Choosing and costing a device 5 hEvaluate

More expensive does not mean better.

  • List needs first: battery, water resistance, sensors, app support.
  • Compare 3 options on cost, battery days, accuracy tests, repair and resale; add the yearly cost of any subscription.
  • Prefer products with clear data export so you are not locked in.
Worked example. Option B costs 6,000 INR with 10-day battery; option C costs 4,000 INR plus 1,200 INR a year. After two years B is cheaper.

Activity. Make a comparison table for 3 devices with the currency written.

Standard to meet. Totals over two years are correct.

Module 8 Review, share and sustain 5 hCreate

Finish the 4 weeks and plan the next.

  • Compare results to the goal; write what worked and what to change.
  • Present to your peer circle; invite one improvement.
  • Plan device reuse or recycling at end of life.
Worked example. Goal met on 4 of 5 weeks; sleep log shows late phone use; next plan adds a screen-off time at 10 pm.

Activity. Present your results.

Standard to meet. Results use real data; one change is planned.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: goal sheet, start check, device test notes, 28-day log, weekly reviews, privacy checklist. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name two sensor errors.

Model answer: Loose strap and dark skin or tattoo for light sensors; step double counts.

Q2. Why ask a doctor first for some people?

Model answer: Illness, injury or medicines can make some exercise unsafe.

Q3. How can you test a step counter?

Model answer: Count 100 steps by hand and compare.

Q4. What weekly increase is a common safe guide?

Model answer: About 10%.

Q5. Why use weekly averages?

Model answer: They hide single-day noise and show trends.

Q6. Name two privacy settings.

Model answer: Location sharing and public activity maps.

Q7. What is subscription lock-in?

Model answer: You lose data or features when you stop paying.

Q8. How do you treat missing days?

Model answer: Mark them; do not invent numbers.

Q9. Why include rest days?

Model answer: The body adapts and recovers during rest.

Q10. What do you do on sharp pain?

Model answer: Stop and seek qualified help.

ten locations and global

Your place

Rules on health data differ by place. Use only consented data and keep it private.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
