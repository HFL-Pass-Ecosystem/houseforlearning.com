Electric car charging station provider

Run a real electric car charging station provider pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small electric car charging station provider offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: energy, environment and resource work. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Find the right sites for chargers 6 hUnderstand

Chargers earn where cars stop for a while and power is available.

  • Types: home, workplace, destination (1-4 hours), fast (20-40 minutes).
  • Site scores: dwell time, traffic, nearby amenities, grid power, safety, cost to connect.
  • Visit three sites; count cars parked per hour.
  • Write a one-page site pitch to an owner.
  • Ask the local utility early for the power available at the site and the cost to connect.
  • Count cars at three times of day to see the peak and the quiet periods.
  • Ask the owner what they gain: footfall, rent or a share of revenue.
Worked example. Shopping centre: average stay 90 minutes, 60 EV cars a day, grid connection 150 kW available; score 4.2/5. If 25% of the cars would charge, 60 x 0.25 = 15 sessions a day is the ceiling to test. At 50 kW a 28 kWh session takes 28 / 50 = 0.56 hour, about 34 minutes, which fits inside the 90-minute stay.

Activity. Score three sites and choose one.

Standard to meet. Counts recorded.

Module 2 Know electrical and utility rules 6 hApply

Charging involves electricity and public safety.

  • Electrical work follows the provincial code and licensed electricians; permits and inspections are required.
  • Utility connection agreements set capacity and timing.
  • Accessibility and signage rules apply.
  • Selling electricity by the kWh is regulated by province; check how to charge.
  • Ask the provincial electrical authority which permit and inspection steps apply and who must hold the licence.
  • Ask the utility for the connection timeline and write it into your plan.
  • Check the provincial rule on whether you may charge by kWh, by time or by session.
Worked example. Row: 'Install a fast charger | provincial electrical authority | permit and inspection | before energising | inspection certificate'. Row: 'Charge by the kWh | provincial measurement and energy rules | check allowed method | before opening | written answer filed'. If the utility needs 12 weeks and the permit 4 weeks and they run in parallel, plan 12 weeks, not 16.

Activity. Build a rules table for one site.

Standard to meet. Rows sourced and dated.

Module 3 Plan the installation 10 hApply

Capacity and layout come first.

  • Compute power: number of chargers x kW, with load management to share capacity.
  • Plan cables, trenching, lighting and drainage.
  • Choose connectors and payment methods that suit local cars.
  • Plan remote monitoring.
  • Have a licensed electrician size the cable and breaker; do not guess.
  • Lay out the bays so every cable reaches and none crosses a walkway.
  • Choose the load-management setting (fair share or first come) and write it down.
Worked example. 4 chargers of 50 kW on a 150 kW supply with load sharing: no more than 3 run at full power at once (3 x 50 = 150). If all 4 are in use, each gets 150 / 4 = 37.5 kW, so a 28 kWh session takes 28 / 37.5 = 0.75 hour, or 45 minutes.

Activity. Draw a site layout and load plan.

Standard to meet. Power sums checked.

Module 4 Price charging and prove the business 8 hEvaluate

Revenue per kWh, minus power cost and fees.

  • Energy margin = selling price - energy price - network fees.
  • Add idle fees to free the bay.
  • Include maintenance, software and site lease.
  • Compute payback.
  • List the cost lines (hardware, install, connection, signs, software) and sum them.
  • Set the price per kWh from the margin you need, then compare with nearby prices.
  • Run three cases: low, medium and high sessions per day.
Worked example. Installed cost 18,000. High case: 12 sessions a day x 28 kWh x 0.18 margin = 60.48 a day, about 1,800 a month; less 400 for site, software and upkeep leaves 1,400, so payback = 18,000 / 1,400 = about 13 months. Low case: 5 sessions a day gives 25.20 a day, about 756 a month; less 400 leaves 356, so payback = 18,000 / 356 = about 51 months. Teaching figures in CAD.

Activity. Build a payback model with a low-use case.

Standard to meet. Three cases shown.

Module 5 Operate reliably 8 hApply

Uptime is the product.

  • Monitor remotely; alert on faults.
  • Respond within a set time.
  • Clean and inspect monthly.
  • Track uptime and sessions.
  • Write a fault response plan: who is called, in what order and within what time.
  • Keep spare parts for common faults (connector, cable) on a shelf.
  • Check payment terminals and screens weekly and record the result.
Worked example. Uptime 97% target; fault repaired within 24 hours. In a 30-day month (720 hours), 3% downtime allows only 720 x 0.03 = 21.6 hours, so one 24-hour fault alone breaks the target; spare parts on the shelf prevent this.

Activity. Keep a 4-week uptime log.

Standard to meet. Faults and fixes recorded.

Module 6 Win site hosts and drivers 6 hCreate

Partners host; drivers return.

  • Offer revenue share to site owners.
  • List on charging maps.
  • Offer fleet plans.
  • Collect feedback.
  • Prepare a one-page revenue-share offer showing the owner's income at low, medium and high use.
  • List the site on charging maps and check that its details are right.
  • Ask five drivers what would make them return and record the answers.
Worked example. Funnel: 20 sites, 6 meetings, 2 agreements; that is 6 / 20 = 30% to a meeting and 2 / 6 = 33% to an agreement. If the owner gets 10% of a 1,800 monthly margin, the owner earns 180 and you keep 1,620.

Activity. Pitch three site owners.

Standard to meet. Pitch has numbers.

Module 7 Manage safety and grid risk 6 hEvaluate

Plan for hazards and demand peaks.

  • Cable and trip hazards, vandalism, weather.
  • Peak demand charges.
  • Cyber security for chargers.
  • Insurance.
  • Walk the site at night and list hazards (lighting, trip points, vehicle impact) with a fix for each.
  • Ask the utility how demand charges are set and compute the worst month.
  • Change default passwords, apply updates and limit who can control the chargers.
Worked example. Risk: 'Peak demand charge | 3 x 4 | load management | ops | monthly'. Suppose the utility charged 12 per kW of monthly peak (an assumed rate; check the real tariff): a 150 kW peak costs 150 x 12 = 1,800, while limiting load to 100 kW costs 100 x 12 = 1,200, a saving of 600 a month.

Activity. Write risk register.

Standard to meet. Owners named.

Module 8 Pilot and expand 8 hCreate

Prove one site.

  • 90 days.
  • Measure sessions, kWh, uptime.
  • Stop rule: under 3 sessions a day after 60 days means move or adjust price.
  • Plan roll-out.
  • Set up counters for sessions, kWh, uptime and revenue before day one.
  • Review at day 30, 60 and 90 and adjust price or signs using the data.
  • Write the roll-out plan with site criteria, budget and order.
Worked example. 5.2 sessions per day; proceed. Over 90 days that is 5.2 x 90 = 468 sessions, and at 28 kWh each, 468 x 28 = 13,104 kWh. The stop rule of 3 sessions a day would be 3 x 90 = 270 sessions, so the site clears it easily.

Activity. Run pilot review.

Standard to meet. Review has decision.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why share load across chargers?

Model answer: To stay within site capacity.

Q2. What is dwell time?

Model answer: How long a car stays parked.

Q3. Who may do the electrical work?

Model answer: Licensed electricians with permits and inspection.

Q4. What is energy margin?

Model answer: Selling price minus energy price and fees.

Q5. Why charge idle fees?

Model answer: To free the bay for others.

Q6. What should uptime target be?

Model answer: About 97% or better, with repair inside 24 hours.

Q7. Why monitor remotely?

Model answer: To catch faults early.

Q8. What is a demand charge?

Model answer: A fee based on peak power drawn.

Q9. What triggers a move?

Model answer: Under 3 sessions a day after 60 days.

Q10. Why insure?

Model answer: Liability and equipment damage.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
