Entrepreneur Streams

Run a pilot of entrepreneur Streams in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small entrepreneur Streams offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: professional, scientific and community services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the streams and the words 6 hUnderstand

An entrepreneur stream is a route to earn through your own work or business. You may use more than one at a time. Learn the main routes.

  • Routes: side income, self-employment, a small business, a partnership, a social enterprise.
  • Terms: income stream, customer, margin, fixed cost, variable cost, risk.
  • One stream is simple; several streams spread risk but cost more attention.
  • List your skills, time, money and limits in one page.
  • Identify one customer who would pay for each stream and write what they pay for.
  • Compare a steady stream with an uneven one by writing a normal month and a worst month of income.
  • Describe your limits in numbers: hours per week, money you can afford to lose, days you cannot work.
Worked example. A person has 10 hours a week, 2,000 in savings and skills in repair and writing. The list gives three ideas: weekend repairs, a short how-to guide and tutoring, each with a named customer. The person keeps the two that fit within 10 hours.

Activity. Write a one-page profile with skills, hours per week, savings and limits, and list three possible streams.

Standard to meet. Task: write a profile and three stream ideas. Condition: honest numbers. Standard: hours and money shown; each idea names a customer.

Module 2 Explain how each stream makes money 6 hApply

Each stream has customers, a way to earn and a way to fail.

  • For each stream write: who pays, for what, how often, how much.
  • Find the main cost and the main risk.
  • Mark which streams are active (time for money) and which can grow without your time.
  • Compare two streams side by side in a table.
  • Calculate monthly income for each stream as customers x price x times per month.
  • Identify the one cost that grows when sales grow, such as materials or travel.
  • Explain which stream stops earning when you stop working and which keeps earning for a while.
Worked example. Stream A: 20 repair jobs x 80 = 1,600 a month, minus 20 x 20 = 400 in costs, leaves 1,200 for 40 hours of work. Stream B: 10 sales x 25 = 250, minus 10 x 3 = 30 in costs, leaves 220 for 3 hours. A pays more in total; B pays more per hour and can grow without extra time.

Activity. Build a comparison table for three streams with income, costs, time and risk.

Standard to meet. Task: compare three streams. Condition: using real local prices. Standard: table complete with units; assumptions listed.

Module 3 Do the core work of your first stream to a standard 10 hApply

Pick one stream and deliver it well before you add another.

  • Write the method for the core service or product.
  • Deliver it twice and measure time, quality and cost.
  • Fix the weak step and run it again.
  • Keep a record of customer feedback.
  • Draft a checklist for the core work so a second person could repeat it.
  • Measure hours, materials and money spent on each delivery, using the same units each time.
  • Ask each customer what worked and what to change, and record the answers.
Worked example. The first job took 5 hours; the second took 3.5 hours with a checklist. Time fell by 1.5 hours, which is 1.5 / 5 = 30 percent. If your own time is valued at 20 an hour, the time cost per job fell from 100 to 70.

Activity. Complete the core task twice with records and an improvement.

Standard to meet. Task: deliver the core work twice. Condition: real or practice customers. Standard: method written, second run better, feedback recorded.

Module 4 Know Canadian rules for working for yourself 8 hEvaluate

Your status affects tax, benefits and rules. Check before you start.

  • Self-employed income must be reported, and you may need to register for sales tax once taxable sales pass the small-supplier limit. Check the current rules with the tax agency.
  • Self-employed people usually do not have employer-paid benefits; plan for insurance, savings and pension.
  • Some work needs a licence or regulated title; some places need a local business licence.
  • Newcomers on a work or study permit: your permit conditions may limit self-employment. Check the current rules with the immigration authority before you start, and ask about any entrepreneur programs on official pages only.
  • Ask the provincial business registry what name registration your type of business needs and write the answer with the date.
  • Ask a licensed adviser whether your work needs insurance for customers' property or injuries.
  • Keep receipts and a log of income and costs from the first day, in the way the tax agency requires.
Worked example. Rules table row: 'Self-employment on a permit | immigration authority | confirm your permit allows it | before first sale | written confirmation kept'. If the permit answer takes 3 weeks and the registry answer takes 2 weeks, ask both at once: all rows are done in week 3, not week 5.

Activity. Build a rules table for your situation and province with sources and dates.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has enforcer, action and date checked; unclear cases say to check with the authority.

Module 5 Price each stream and count real income 8 hApply

Income is what remains after costs and tax, not what you bill.

  • Margin per unit = price minus variable cost. Break-even = fixed cost divided by margin.
  • Set aside money for tax and for slow months.
  • Compute income per hour for each stream. Drop the weakest one when you can.
  • Keep one account for the business and keep records.
  • Calculate the break-even number of jobs for any tool or course you must buy first.
  • Set aside a share of each margin for tax and slow months; state the share as an assumption and check it with the tax agency.
  • Compare income per hour with what you could earn elsewhere in the same hours.
Worked example. Stream A earns 80 per job with 20 in costs and 2 hours: 60 / 2 = 30 per hour. Stream B earns 25 per sale with 3 in costs and 0.3 hours: 22 / 0.3 = about 73 per hour. A 300 tool for stream A pays back after 300 / 60 = 5 jobs. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build a spreadsheet with income per hour for each stream and a break-even for each.

Standard to meet. Task: build a stream income sheet. Condition: your own time log. Standard: units shown, formulas visible, three what-if cases, mentor checks arithmetic.

Module 6 Run your week across streams 6 hCreate

Several streams need a calendar and clear limits.

  • Block fixed times for each stream and for admin.
  • Pick three weekly measures: income, hours and customer messages answered.
  • Say no to work that breaks your limits.
  • Review monthly: keep, change or stop each stream.
  • Schedule admin time (invoices, records, messages) as a real block, not leftover time.
  • Track hours per stream each week and compare them with the plan.
  • Choose one weekly rest day and protect it.
Worked example. Week plan: Monday and Tuesday repair jobs, 5 hours each; Wednesday guide updates, 3 hours; Thursday sales messages, 2 hours; Friday records, 2 hours. Total 5 + 5 + 3 + 2 + 2 = 17 hours, which leaves 3 of the 20 planned hours as a buffer.

Activity. Run a four-week schedule and log results for each stream.

Standard to meet. Task: run a four-week multi-stream log. Condition: real or practice. Standard: weekly pages complete; one stream decision made from numbers.

Module 7 Find customers and build trust 6 hEvaluate

Each stream needs a route to customers.

  • Pick at most three channels: direct contact, referrals, local partners, online listing.
  • Write a one-page offer: who, what, result, proof, price basis, how to start.
  • Follow consent rules for email and texts and keep customer data safe.
  • Offer service in English and French where the customer or the law needs it.
  • Choose one proof for each offer, such as a photo of finished work or a short customer note used with permission.
  • Calculate the funnel: contacts, replies, conversations, jobs, and name the weakest step.
  • Reply to each message within one working day and write down the answer you gave.
Worked example. Offer: 'Home repairs under 3 hours, same week, fixed price, photo of finished work.' Twenty contacts gave 6 replies (30%), 5 conversations (5/6, about 83% of replies) and 2 jobs (40% of conversations). The weakest step is contact to reply, so test a shorter first message.

Activity. Contact twenty possible customers with consent and book five conversations.

Standard to meet. Task: run a two-week outreach. Condition: consent-based. Standard: funnel numbers recorded; one test and its result written.

Module 8 Manage risk, pilot and plan for growth 8 hCreate

Test your plan before you commit more time and money.

  • Risk register: income gap, illness, one big customer, rule change, burnout, tax debt.
  • Build a cash buffer for slow months and ask a licensed adviser which insurance options fit your work.
  • Run a 60 to 90 day pilot with a stop rule.
  • Write the review and a 12-month plan; decide which stream to grow, keep or stop.
  • Calculate a cash buffer equal to three months of personal and business costs.
  • Evaluate each stream after the pilot as keep, change or stop, with the number that supports the choice.
  • Write the first three actions of your 12-month plan with dates.
Worked example. Stop rule: 'If a stream earns under 15 per hour after 60 days, we stop or change it.' Stream C earned 210 over 20 hours: 210 / 20 = 10.50 per hour, which is below 15, so it is changed or stopped. A three-month buffer at 1,500 a month is 4,500.

Activity. Run the pilot and present the review and plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: pilot your streams. Condition: legal operation under your rules table. Standard: measures tracked, decisions supported by numbers, plan clear.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
