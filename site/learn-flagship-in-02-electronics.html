flagship course

Electronics Assembly and Testing

Build and test electronic circuits and boards, read schematics, solder safely, and report faults with measured proof.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 41 h lessons plus practice

Build and test electronic circuits and boards, read schematics, solder safely, and report faults with measured proof.

  • Define voltage, current, resistance and power with units.
  • Apply safe soldering and assembly to a written standard.
  • Analyse a circuit with a multimeter and an oscilloscope.
  • Evaluate a board for fit to its specification.
  • Create a small tested product and its build record.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Assemble a small electronic board from a schematic, test it against its specification and record the results.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. Bench with soldering station, multimeter, power supply, component kit, safety glasses.

Standard

Joints are clean and sound, readings sit inside the stated limits, and every step is recorded so another person can rebuild the board.

Who it is for: Diploma and degree students, hobbyists, repair technicians, small manufacturers. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Bench safety and electricity basics 4 hDefine
  • Voltage is push, current is flow, resistance is opposition; Ohm law links them.
  • Power equals voltage times current; know the heat it makes.
  • Static damage: wrist strap, mat and bags protect chips.
  • Hot iron, fumes and small parts: ventilate, rest the iron in its stand.
Worked example. A 5 V supply across a 250 ohm resistor gives 20 mA and 0.1 W; a quarter-watt resistor is safe.

Activity. Solve five Ohm law problems and write six bench rules.

Standard to meet. All answers include units and are correct; rules cover heat, fumes, static, eyes, power and tidiness.

Module 2 Components and schematics 5 hExplain
  • Resistors, capacitors, diodes, LEDs, transistors, chips: what each does.
  • Read symbols, reference marks and values; colour codes and markings.
  • Polarity: diodes, electrolytic capacitors and chips have a right way round.
  • A bill of materials lists every part with its quantity.
Worked example. A schematic shows R1 at 330 ohm in series with an LED; the bill of materials lists one resistor and one LED for the stage.

Activity. Annotate a given schematic and make its bill of materials.

Standard to meet. Every part is named, valued and counted; polarity marked.

Module 3 Soldering and assembly 6 hApply
  • Tin the tip, heat the pad and lead together, feed solder, remove solder then iron.
  • A good joint is shiny and shaped like a small cone.
  • Fit low parts first, then taller ones; trim leads after soldering.
  • Inspect with a magnifier for bridges and cold joints.
Worked example. A cold joint looks dull and ball-shaped; the technician reheats it with a little fresh solder until it flows.

Activity. Assemble a kit board with at least 30 joints.

Standard to meet. No bridges, no cold joints on inspection, and all parts correct.

Module 4 Measurement and testing 6 hApply
  • Use a multimeter for voltage, resistance and continuity.
  • Power up slowly with a current limit and watch for heat.
  • Check test points against expected values in the spec.
  • Use an oscilloscope for signals: set scale, trigger and read the period.
Worked example. A 1 kHz square wave shows 1 ms per cycle on the scope; the technician sets 0.5 ms per division and counts two divisions.

Activity. Test your board at five points and record expected and measured values.

Standard to meet. All readings are inside limits or the difference is explained.

Module 5 Fault finding 6 hAnalyse
  • Start with visual checks, then power, then signal path.
  • Split the circuit in halves to find which half fails.
  • Compare to a working board.
  • Write the root cause, not only the fix.
Worked example. Board dead: supply reads 0 V at the regulator output; the input is fine, so the regulator or its load fails; a short on the load is found.

Activity. Find a seeded fault and write the root cause.

Standard to meet. Root cause is proved with a reading and the fix is checked.

Module 6 Quality, standards and records 5 hEvaluate
  • Work to a workmanship standard with clear accept and reject examples.
  • Record batch, date, test results and who built it.
  • Rework policy: fix once, then scrap or escalate.
  • Safe handling and disposal of electronic waste.
Worked example. A batch of 40 boards: 38 pass first time, 2 reworked; yield is 95% first pass.

Activity. Build a one-page record sheet and use it on five boards.

Standard to meet. Sheet is complete for all five boards and the yield is calculated.

Module 7 Small product design 5 hCreate
  • Choose a simple need such as a night light or a water alarm.
  • Draw the schematic, choose parts, estimate cost.
  • Build a prototype and test.
  • Write a short user note.
Worked example. A water-level alarm using a transistor and buzzer; cost estimate 140 rupees; tested at three levels.

Activity. Design, build and test a small product.

Standard to meet. Prototype works to spec, cost is stated, and the user note is clear.

Module 8 Capstone: build and report 4 hCreate
  • Take a given schematic and build to the end.
  • Test and fix any faults.
  • Write a build record and a one-page summary.
  • Present to peers.
Worked example. A final board with a record showing 3 tests, 1 fault fixed, and 2 hours total.

Activity. Build, test and present.

Standard to meet. Board meets spec, record is complete, and the talk is clear.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What does Ohm law link?

Model answer: Voltage, current and resistance.

Q2. Which parts need correct polarity?

Model answer: Diodes, LEDs, electrolytic capacitors and chips.

Q3. What does a good joint look like?

Model answer: Shiny and cone-shaped.

Q4. Why power up with a current limit?

Model answer: To avoid damage if there is a short.

Q5. What is the first fault-finding step?

Model answer: Look carefully, then check power.

Q6. How is first-pass yield found?

Model answer: Boards passing first time divided by boards built.

Q7. What goes in a user note?

Model answer: What it does, how to use it safely, and how to reset it.

Q8. What proves a board meets its spec?

Model answer: Test readings inside the stated limits.

Q9. Name two static protection items.

Model answer: Wrist strap and anti-static mat.

Q10. Why keep a bill of materials?

Model answer: To buy and check the right parts every time.

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Name basic components and bench rules.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain what circuits do.
  3. L3 Foundation. Solder and test a kit under supervision.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Build, test and fix boards alone.
  5. L5 Specialist. Design boards, set test plans and handle high-volume work.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead production quality and train others.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set product standards and supply plans across sites.
