CPD · Accountancy

Reconciling accounts to a month-end standard

Matching records to statements and clearing differences before close.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 4 hours of CPDSelf-study plus role-playAccountancy

Matching records to statements and clearing differences before close.

  • Reconcile a bank account, given a statement and a ledger, with all differences explained and signed.
  • Identify timing and error differences, given 10 items, with at least 9 classified correctly.
  • Prepare a reconciliation sheet, given a template, with preparer, reviewer and date complete.
  • Escalate an unexplained difference, given a threshold, within the agreed time.

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

7 modules

Module 1 Why reconcile and what "done" means 20 min

Learn: a short explanation with a worked example from the sector, then the rule or checklist you will use.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 1.

Standard to meet. Explain why a bank reconciliation is done every month and describe what a finished, fully explained reconciliation looks like, naming how it helps catch errors and fraud early.

Module 2 Reading a bank list and a ledger list 30 min

Learn: a short explanation with a worked example from the sector, then the rule or checklist you will use.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 2.

Standard to meet. Read a bank list and a ledger list side by side and identify each line, so that every entry on both lists is correctly understood before matching begins.

Module 3 Matching and timing differences 45 min

Learn: a short explanation with a worked example from the sector, then the rule or checklist you will use.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 3.

Standard to meet. Match the lines of a bank list to a ledger list and classify each difference as a timing item, such as an uncleared cheque or a deposit not yet recorded.

Module 4 The reconciliation statement layout 35 min

Learn: a short explanation with a worked example from the sector, then the rule or checklist you will use.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Complete the reconciliation statement in the set layout, adjusting both balances with timing and ledger items until the difference after reconciling is zero.

Module 5 Unusual items: spotting and explaining 30 min

Learn: a short explanation with a worked example from the sector, then the rule or checklist you will use.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Spot unusual items such as unexplained small amounts, round sums or duplicates, then explain each one, record it against the set limit and escalate when required.

Module 6 Full practice file 40 min

Learn: a short explanation with a worked example from the sector, then the rule or checklist you will use.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Reconcile a full practice file of twelve bank lines and twelve ledger lines, finding all three planted differences and finishing with a correct signed statement.

Module 7 Assessment 10 min

Learn: a short explanation with a worked example from the sector, then the rule or checklist you will use.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Complete a timed assessment reconciliation by matching items, writing the statement and explaining every difference, meeting the checklist with no unexplained balance left.

practice

Worked example and case cards

Worked example

Bank balance 10,000; ledger cash balance 9,700. Items: cheque issued, not yet cleared (400); deposit received, not yet in ledger (100). Check: 10,000 - 400 = 9,600; 9,700 - 100 = 9,600. Difference after reconciling: 0.

Layout

balance per bank; add/less timing items; adjusted balance; balance per ledger; add/less ledger items; adjusted balance; difference.

Case card

a practice file of 12 bank lines and 12 ledger lines with 3 planted differences.

Case card 2

Bank shows 14,820.00; ledger shows 15,240.00. A cheque of 600.00 is unpresented and a fee of 180.00 is unrecorded. Learners find the remaining gap and write the statement.

checks

9 check questions with model answers

Why reconcile monthly?

Model answer: To catch errors and fraud early. (Covered in module 1: Why reconcile and what "done" means. To pass that module: Explain why a bank reconciliation is done every month and describe what a finished, fully explained reconciliation looks like, naming how it helps catch errors and fraud early.)

A cheque issued but not cleared affects which balance?

Model answer: Bank balance side (deduct it). (Covered in module 3: Matching and timing differences. To pass that module: Match the lines of a bank list to a ledger list and classify each difference as a timing item, such as an uncleared cheque or a deposit not yet recorded.)

What do you do with an unexplained 50?

Model answer: Investigate, record it and escalate if above the limit. (Covered in module 5: Unusual items: spotting and explaining. To pass that module: Spot unusual items such as unexplained small amounts, round sums or duplicates, then explain each one, record it against the set limit and escalate when required.)

Who reviews?

Model answer: A second person, per your policy. (Covered in module 1: Why reconcile and what "done" means. To pass that module: Explain why a bank reconciliation is done every month and describe what a finished, fully explained reconciliation looks like, naming how it helps catch errors and fraud early.)

What is a timing difference?

Model answer: An item in one record this month and the other next month. (Covered in module 3: Matching and timing differences. To pass that module: Match the lines of a bank list to a ledger list and classify each difference as a timing item, such as an uncleared cheque or a deposit not yet recorded.)

What do you do with an unexplained 5.00?

Model answer: Check against the set limit; record it and note it. (Covered in module 5: Unusual items: spotting and explaining. To pass that module: Spot unusual items such as unexplained small amounts, round sums or duplicates, then explain each one, record it against the set limit and escalate when required.)

Why keep the statement signed and dated?

Model answer: It proves who checked and when. (Covered in module 4: The reconciliation statement layout. To pass that module: Complete the reconciliation statement in the set layout, adjusting both balances with timing and ledger items until the difference after reconciling is zero.)

Name one unusual item.

Model answer: A round-sum payment, a duplicate, a payment on a weekend. (Covered in module 5: Unusual items: spotting and explaining. To pass that module: Spot unusual items such as unexplained small amounts, round sums or duplicates, then explain each one, record it against the set limit and escalate when required.)

Who approves a write-off?

Model answer: The person named in the approval card, never the preparer. (Covered in module 5: Unusual items: spotting and explaining. To pass that module: Spot unusual items such as unexplained small amounts, round sums or duplicates, then explain each one, record it against the set limit and escalate when required.)
