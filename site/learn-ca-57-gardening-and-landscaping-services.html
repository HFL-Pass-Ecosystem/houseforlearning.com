Gardening And Landscaping Services

Run a real gardening And Landscaping Services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small gardening And Landscaping Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: local, logistics and field services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the yard, the plants and the tools 6 hUnderstand

Gardening and landscaping care for outdoor spaces. First learn the words, the parts of a yard and the tools.

  • Name the parts of a yard: lawn, beds, trees, shrubs, paths, edges and drainage.
  • Explain the difference between annual plants, perennials, shrubs and trees.
  • Name basic tools and what each is for: spade, rake, pruner, edger, mower, trimmer.
  • Explain what a hardiness zone is and why it matters in Canada's cold winters.
  • Identify common weeds and local native plants using a local guide.
  • Describe how poor drainage shows up: puddles, wet patches and moss.
  • Clean and dry tools after use and explain why sharp tools are safer.
Worked example. A client wants 'a low-maintenance garden' for a shady corner. You note the shade, the damp soil and the zone, and pick plants that suit those, not just plants that look nice. You then list the tools needed: a spade, a pruner and a rake.

Activity. Draw a simple map of one yard and label ten parts, then list the tool for each job.

Standard to meet. Task: map a yard. Condition: one real yard and a notebook. Standard: ten parts correctly named and each paired with a suitable tool.

Module 2 Read soil, sun, water and seasons 6 hApply

Plants live or die by soil, light and water. Check them before you plant anything.

  • Check sun by noting hours of direct light in the morning, midday and afternoon.
  • Feel soil: sandy drains fast, clay holds water, loam is in between.
  • Explain why mulch helps hold water and slow weeds.
  • Describe the Canadian garden year: spring clean-up, summer care, fall clean-up and winter protection.
  • Check the local last-frost and first-frost dates with a local source before you plant tender plants.
  • Test soil with a jar of soil and water: let it settle to see sand, silt and clay layers.
  • Write a soil note to guide compost use; check a local soil test service for nutrients.
Worked example. A bed gets four hours of sun and has heavy clay. You choose plants that list part shade or part sun and add compost so water drains better. Your week of notes shows 2 hours of direct light in the morning and 2 in the afternoon, which is 4 hours in total.

Activity. Test and record the sun, soil and water of one bed over one week.

Standard to meet. Task: survey a bed. Condition: one week of notes. Standard: sun hours, soil type and drainage are recorded and one plant choice is justified from them.

Module 3 Do routine garden jobs safely 10 hApply

Mowing, trimming, weeding and planting are the core jobs. Do them safely and the same way each time.

  • Mow with sharp blades at a suitable height and never cut more than about a third of the grass at once.
  • Weed by pulling out the roots, not only the tops, and do it when the soil is damp so the roots come out whole.
  • Prune with clean, sharp tools and only after you know the plant's right season.
  • Wear gloves, eye and ear protection and sturdy shoes; check the area for stones and cords first.
  • Keep a short job sheet for each visit that records the date, the tasks done, the time spent and any problems found.
  • Check that blades, guards and trimmer line are fitted before starting.
  • Clean tools after each job so plant disease does not spread.
Worked example. Before mowing, a worker walks the lawn and removes toys and stones. She sets the mower height, mows in straight lines and writes the time on the job sheet. If the grass is 9 cm tall, cutting a third means removing at most 3 cm and leaving 6 cm.

Activity. Complete a full routine visit on a practice yard with a written step list.

Standard to meet. Task: do a routine visit. Condition: a checklist and protective gear. Standard: every step done in order, the area left tidy and the job sheet filled in.

Module 4 Know the rules and look after the environment 8 hEvaluate

Rules about water, chemicals, noise and waste differ across Canada. Learn which ones apply.

  • Check the current rule with the local authority for watering limits, noise hours and yard waste collection.
  • Explain why many towns and provinces limit lawn chemicals, and check your province and town rules before you use any product.
  • Read every product label and keep the safety data sheet for each product.
  • Dispose of clippings, soil and trimmings the way your town requires.
  • Know that digging near buried lines needs a locate request first; call before you dig.
  • Describe invasive plants and why to check local lists before planting.
  • Store and carry fuel only in approved containers.
Worked example. A crew plans to plant a tree. Before digging they request a locate of buried lines. The marked lines show a cable two metres from the planned spot, so they keep the hole clear and record the locate request number.

Activity. Make a rules table for your town: rule, who enforces it, what you do, source and date checked.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: official local pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a dated source; nothing is guessed.

Module 5 Design a small garden plan 8 hApply

A plan turns a wish into a list of work, plants and costs.

  • Measure the space and draw it to scale on graph paper or a simple drawing tool.
  • Choose plants for height, bloom time and care level so something looks good in each season.
  • Place tall plants at the back or centre, low plants at the front, and leave room to grow.
  • Write a plant list, a materials list and a work order.
  • Check the plan with the client before you buy anything.
  • Compute the area of a bed (length x width) to find how much mulch or soil you need.
  • Split the work into phases so the client can approve each one.
Worked example. A 4 m by 2 m bed is 8 square metres. Three tall perennials go at the back, five medium shrubs in the middle and a low edge plant at the front. Compost 5 cm deep needs 8 x 0.05 = 0.4 cubic metres.

Activity. Design a small bed plan with a plant list, a materials list and a cost estimate.

Standard to meet. Task: design a bed. Condition: real measurements. Standard: the plan is to scale, plants suit sun and soil, and quantities are worked out and checked.

Module 6 Quote, price and plan your work 6 hCreate

A good quote covers your time, materials, travel and a small margin.

  • Quote from a checklist: size, condition, access, frequency and extras.
  • Price = labour hours x hourly cost + materials + travel + margin.
  • Add a buffer for weather; Canadian seasons give you fewer working days than you expect.
  • Offer clear options such as weekly, every two weeks or one-time work.
  • Ask a licensed accountant about sales tax and income reporting.
  • Compute your margin percentage: margin divided by price.
  • Write a weather rule: a rain day moves the job date, not the price.
Worked example. A clean-up takes 3 hours at a cost of 30 CAD per hour = 90, plus materials 15 and travel 10. Total cost is 115. A quote of 150 CAD gives a margin of 35, which is about 23% of the price.

Activity. Build a quote sheet and quote three real or practice jobs.

Standard to meet. Task: quote jobs. Condition: a checklist and a spreadsheet. Standard: each quote shows time, cost, margin and what is left out, and a mentor checks the arithmetic.

Module 7 Find clients, insure and set terms 6 hEvaluate

Clients want proof you are careful and reliable. Terms in writing protect both sides.

  • Show photos of finished work and write a short note on each: problem, action, result.
  • Check what insurance a business like yours is asked to hold; talk to a licensed insurance adviser.
  • Check whether your province asks you to register for workplace injury cover when you hire others.
  • Write simple terms: what is included, price, payment date, weather and cancellation.
  • Ask for referrals from neighbours after good work.
  • Take before-and-after photos from the same angle each time.
  • Write a follow-up message that asks the client how the work looks after one week.
Worked example. A new business posts five before-and-after photos with short notes in the local group and gets 6 enquiries, 3 quotes and 2 jobs in a month. That is 2 jobs from 6 enquiries, or one in three.

Activity. Build a five-photo portfolio, a one-page offer and a terms sheet.

Standard to meet. Task: prepare to sell. Condition: real or practice jobs. Standard: each portfolio piece has a case note, the offer fits one page and the terms are plain and complete.

Module 8 Run a season of landscaping work 8 hCreate

Now run it: schedule, crew, quality and cash across a Canadian season.

  • Plan routes by area to cut travel time.
  • Plan the year: spring and summer for growing work, fall for clean-up and bulbs, winter for planning or related work if allowed.
  • Check tools and vehicles weekly.
  • Ask for a rating after every job and act on the low ones.
  • Write a 12-month plan with milestones, a budget and a risk list.
  • Work out profit per day as the day's income minus its costs, then compare the days to see which jobs pay best.
  • Plan a winter task list for tools: clean, sharpen, repair and order.
Worked example. A small crew of two plans five stops a day. They group stops within a 15 minute drive and gain one extra stop per week, which adds about 150 CAD a week at 150 CAD per stop. Over a 20-week season that is 20 x 150 = 3,000 CAD.

Activity. Run a 60-day pilot with real clients and write the review.

Standard to meet. Task: run a landscaping pilot. Condition: 60 days. Standard: the log has volume, quality and cash each week and the review decides what to stop, change or grow with reasons.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What are three things to check before you plant?

Model answer: Sun hours, soil type and drainage.

Q2. Why does mulch help?

Model answer: It holds water and slows weeds.

Q3. How much of the grass should you cut at one time?

Model answer: No more than about a third.

Q4. Why call before you dig?

Model answer: Buried lines may be there, and a locate request shows where.

Q5. How do you find the price of a job?

Model answer: Labour hours times cost, plus materials, travel and margin.

Q6. Why read the product label and safety data sheet?

Model answer: They tell you safe use, protective gear and disposal.

Q7. What is a hardiness zone?

Model answer: A map-based rating of how cold winters get, used to choose plants.

Q8. Why use a job sheet?

Model answer: It records what was done and when, so quality can be checked.

Q9. Who sets watering and noise rules?

Model answer: Mostly the town or municipality; check the current rule with the local authority.

Q10. What should you do if you are unsure about a rule or insurance?

Model answer: Ask the official body or a licensed adviser and keep the answer in writing.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
