Laundry and Facility Care Services

Set up a small, safe laundry or cleaning service and measure quality, turnaround and cost. You learn fabric care, chemical safety, process steps, stain handling, quality checks and costing.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 110 h guidedPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Set up a small, safe laundry or cleaning service and measure quality, turnaround and cost. You learn fabric care, chemical safety, process steps, stain handling, quality checks and costing.

  • Understand Explain fabric care and cleaning basics.
  • Apply Run washing or cleaning batches.
  • Analyse Analyse quality and cost.
  • Create Create a clear service sheet.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a small laundry or cleaning service for 30 days with quality checks, a turnaround record and cost per item, then report redo rate and complaints.

Condition

About 110 hours over 3 to 5 months. Partner premises with machines, a free spreadsheet, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Process sheet; safety plan; quality checks; turnaround log; cost per item; at least 70% on the final check.

Learning only. Follow your local law and get written permission before you work with real customers, premises, data or money.

Requirements: none. Who it is for: learners 16 and over, workers changing field, small-business starters, teams. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (110 h guided)

Module 1 Care and fibres 8 hUnderstand

Different fabrics need different care. Care labels show the safe temperature and cycle. Reading them avoids damage and unhappy customers.

  • Learn the common fibres such as cotton, wool and synthetics, and how each reacts to heat and water.
  • Read the care label on every item and group items by the temperature and cycle they allow.
  • Separate dark and light colours to avoid dye running onto other items.
  • Record any item with a missing or unreadable label and ask the owner before washing it.
  • A common mistake is to wash a mixed load at the hottest setting to save time.
Worked example. A wool jumper is 50 cm wide across the chest. A hot wash shrinks it by 8%, which is 50 times 0.08, or 4 cm, so it ends at 46 cm and no longer fits.

Activity. Sort 20 items by fibre, temperature and colour, and record the cycle you would choose for each group and why.

Standard to meet. Your groups match the care labels for at least 19 of the 20 items and you give a reason for each cycle.

Module 2 Safety and chemicals 14 hApply

Cleaning products are chemicals and can harm skin, eyes and lungs. You store them properly, wear protection and never mix them. A written plan keeps everyone safe.

  • Keep every product in its original container with its label, stored away from food and out of reach of children.
  • Wear gloves and eye protection and keep the room ventilated when you handle strong products.
  • Never mix cleaning products, because some combinations give off toxic gas.
  • Measure concentrates with a marked cup and write the dilution on the container.
  • Check with your local authority about the rules for storing and disposing of cleaning chemicals.
Worked example. A concentrate is diluted 50 mL into a 1 L total solution, so the strength is 50 divided by 1,000, which is 5%. Marking this on the bottle stops others from guessing and mixing wrongly.

Activity. Write a one-page chemical safety plan covering storage, protection, dilution, spills and disposal, and have your supervisor review it.

Standard to meet. Your plan covers all five areas, lists each product used and is signed by the supervisor.

Module 3 Process steps 14 hApply

A good process makes sure every item comes back to its owner clean and undamaged. You follow the same steps every time. Tags and checks stop items getting lost.

  • Follow the sequence of receive, tag, check pockets, wash, dry, press and pack for every order.
  • Tag each order with a number that links to the customer record and not to the customer name.
  • Check pockets and note any damage or stains with the customer when you receive the item.
  • Inspect each item at the end against the checklist before it is packed.
  • A common mistake is to skip the pocket check, which can ruin a load.
Worked example. You run 10 orders with 4 to 8 items each and tag every one. All 10 orders are returned with the right number of items, so there are 0 lost items and 0 mix-ups.

Activity. Run at least 10 orders through your process and keep a record for each with the tag number, the items in and the items out.

Standard to meet. No items are lost or mixed up across the 10 orders, and your records match the items received and returned.

Module 4 Stain handling 12 hApply

Treating a stain without knowing what it is can set it forever. You identify the stain, test the method on a hidden spot and record the result. Patience beats rushing.

  • Ask the owner or inspect the stain to identify it, such as oil, ink, food or blood.
  • Choose a method that suits both the stain and the fabric, and check the care label first.
  • Test the method on a hidden area, for example an inside seam, before treating the visible stain.
  • Record the stain, the method, the result and the time taken for every treatment.
  • A common mistake is to use hot water on a protein stain, which can set it.
Worked example. You treat 3 stains and 2 come out fully, while 1 fades but is still visible. Your success is 2 of 3, or about 67%, and the third is explained to the owner.

Activity. Treat at least three stains on practice cloth or approved items and keep a record with method and result for each.

Standard to meet. Each record names the stain and method, the hidden-area test was done first and the result is described honestly.

Module 5 Quality checks 12 hAnalyse

Quality means the item comes back clean, dry and neatly folded. A checklist and photos give evidence. The redo rate shows how well your process works.

  • Check each item against a list that includes clean, dry, no stains, no damage and neatly folded.
  • Take a photo of any problem so you can show the cause and the fix.
  • Record each redo, which is an item that needs washing again, with the reason.
  • Calculate the redo rate as the redos divided by the items checked.
  • A common mistake is to pass items quickly without checking, which moves the problem to the customer.
Worked example. From 30 items checked, 1 needs a redo. The redo rate is 1 divided by 30, or about 3.3%, and the cause was a stain that was not seen before washing.

Activity. Check at least 30 finished items against your checklist and report the redo rate with causes.

Standard to meet. Your checklist is used on each item, your redo rate is correct and every redo has a recorded cause.

Module 6 Costs 14 hEvaluate

Knowing your cost helps you set a price that works. You include water, energy, chemicals and your time. Cost per kilogram and cost per item are the key numbers.

  • Record the water, energy and chemicals used for a sample of loads and put a money value on each.
  • Add the cost of your time and any machine wear.
  • Calculate cost per kg as the total cost of a load divided by its weight in kg.
  • Convert to cost per item using the average item weight and compare with your price.
  • A common mistake is to leave out your own time, which makes the service look cheaper than it is.
Worked example. A load of 20 kg costs S$22 in water, energy, chemicals and time. Cost per kg is 22 divided by 20, which is S$1.10. An item of 0.5 kg costs 0.5 times 1.10, which is S$0.55.

Activity. Complete a cost sheet using at least three real loads, with cost per kg and cost per item.

Standard to meet. Your cost per kg is correct to the nearest cent and each cost line has a source or measurement.

Module 7 30-day run 30 hApply

You now run the service for 30 days. Keep records for each order. The turnaround and complaint numbers tell you how well it works.

  • Log the date and time each order arrives and the date and time it is ready.
  • Calculate turnaround as the ready time minus the arrival time for each order.
  • Report the median turnaround, which is the middle value when the times are put in order.
  • Record every complaint with the cause and what you did about it.
  • A common mistake is to report only the best turnaround times.
Worked example. Five orders take 18, 24, 24, 30 and 48 hours. In order the middle value is 24, so the median is 24 hours. The mean would be 144 divided by 5, which is 28.8 hours, and the one slow order pulls it up.

Activity. Run the service for 30 days and submit the log of items in and out, turnaround times, the median, and the complaint list.

Standard to meet. Your log has all orders, the median is calculated correctly and every complaint has a cause.

Module 8 Review 6 hEvaluate

The review uses your records to choose one or two improvements. You check results again after making them. Share what you learn.

  • Find the most common cause of redos or complaints from your logs.
  • Choose a fix that deals with that cause, such as sorting items before washing.
  • Set a target such as halving the redo rate and a date to check.
  • Share your findings with your mentor and peer circle so they can learn too.
  • A common mistake is to stop after a plan without repeating the measurement.
Worked example. 6 of 200 items needed a redo, which is 3%. A pre-sort step should catch most of the stain problems, so you aim to halve the rate to 1.5%, which is 3 redos in 200.

Activity. Present a short review with the top cause, your fix, your target and a date to re-measure.

Standard to meet. Your review names one top cause with data, a specific fix, a numeric target and a date.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why read care labels?

Model answer: To choose the safe temperature and cycle and avoid damage. (Covered in module 1: Care and fibres. To pass that module: Your groups match the care labels for at least 19 of the 20 items and you give a reason for each cycle.)

Q2. Why must you never mix cleaning products?

Model answer: Some mixtures produce toxic gases that can cause serious harm. (Covered in module 2: Safety and chemicals. To pass that module: Your plan covers all five areas, lists each product used and is signed by the supervisor.)

Q3. Why tag each order?

Model answer: To stop mix-ups and keep every item linked to its owner. (Covered in module 3: Process steps. To pass that module: No items are lost or mixed up across the 10 orders, and your records match the items received and returned.)

Q4. Why test a method on a hidden area first?

Model answer: To avoid damaging the visible part of the item. (Covered in module 4: Stain handling. To pass that module: Each record names the stain and method, the hidden-area test was done first and the result is described honestly.)

Q5. What is the redo rate when 1 in 30 items needs rewashing?

Model answer: 1 divided by 30 is about 3.3%. (Covered in module 5: Quality checks. To pass that module: Your checklist is used on each item, your redo rate is correct and every redo has a recorded cause.)

Q6. What is the cost per kg for a load of 20 kg that costs S$22?

Model answer: 22 divided by 20 is S$1.10 per kg. (Covered in module 6: Costs. To pass that module: Your cost per kg is correct to the nearest cent and each cost line has a source or measurement.)

Q7. What is the median?

Model answer: The middle value when the numbers are placed in order. (Covered in module 7: 30-day run. To pass that module: Your log has all orders, the median is calculated correctly and every complaint has a cause.)

Q8. What do you do after your review?

Model answer: Fix the top cause and measure again. (Covered in module 8: Review. To pass that module: Your review names one top cause with data, a specific fix, a numeric target and a date.)

Q9. What is the task of this course, in one sentence?

Model answer: Run a small laundry or cleaning service for 30 days with quality checks, a turnaround record and cost per item, then report redo rate and complaints. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. What standard must your final evidence meet?

Model answer: Process sheet; safety plan; quality checks; turnaround log; cost per item; at least 70% on the final check. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation, Guided tasks with a checklist. Speaker note: Learner does guided tasks from the first three modules (Care and fibres; Safety and chemicals; Process steps) with a checklist. Evidence is a small set of dated records. Note for the guide: sit beside the learner for the first record, then step back.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner, Run the full task alone (this course). Speaker note: Task: Run a small laundry or cleaning service for 30 days with quality checks, a turnaround record and cost per item, then report redo rate and complaints. Standard: Process sheet; safety plan; quality checks; turnaround log; cost per item; at least 70% on the final check. Note for the guide: a mentor reviews the records; two peers mark with the rubric above.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist, One narrow area to depth. Speaker note: Learner picks one area, for example 30-day run (the longest module at 30 h), and goes deeper than this course asks. Learner then teaches a peer circle of three or four. Note for the guide: check the teaching, not only the work.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced, Lead a team and review evidence. Speaker note: Learner leads a small team or designs a service in this field and marks other people's evidence against the rubric. Roles this points to: Laundry attendant; Facility cleaner; Housekeeping assistant. Note for the guide: sample three records from the team and compare marks.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert, Set and audit the standard. Speaker note: Learner writes the method for Laundry and Facility Care Services as a short guide, audits other learners' evidence, and publishes worked examples back to learners. Note for the guide: worked examples keep currency and units on every number and are checked by a second expert.
