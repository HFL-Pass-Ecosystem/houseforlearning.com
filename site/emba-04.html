executive MBA subject 04

Global Management

Year 1, term A. 5.0 ECTS, about 125 hours, 6 parts. Part 1 is free.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write a two-page brief on how a firm should organise and lead its operations in a new country.

Condition

Given public country data and a short description of a mid-sized firm.

Standard

The brief covers culture, rules, risk, structure and team leadership, and each recommendation is backed by evidence.

Who it is for: Managers whose firm sells, buys or employs people in more than one country.

how this subject works

Free entry, voice and your learning style

Part 1 is free to try. Read it, listen to it and answer it before you decide. Join the programme, or this subject on its own, to open the other parts.

6 parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 Globalisation and the firm 21 hUnderstand: explain

Firms go abroad to find customers, cheaper inputs, skills or to follow clients. Each reason leads to a different way of operating.

  • Market seeking, cost seeking, skill seeking, client following
  • Trade flows of goods, money, data and people
  • Not every industry is equally global

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A firm earns 30m at home and 20m abroad: foreign share = 20 / 50 = 40%. If foreign sales grow 25% and home stays flat, foreign becomes 25m of 55m, about 45%.

Activity: For a firm you know, name its main reason for going abroad and its foreign share of sales.

Standard: The reason matches the evidence and the share is calculated correctly.

Part 2 Culture and cross-border management 21 hAnalyse: compare

Culture shapes how people treat hierarchy, time, risk and directness. Managers who read these differences avoid needless conflict.

  • Hierarchy: who may speak up and how
  • Direct or indirect feedback
  • Time: fixed deadlines or flexible relationships

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A manager from a direct culture gives blunt feedback to a team in an indirect culture; 4 of 10 staff ask to change team within a month. After switching to private, framed feedback, requests drop to 1.

Activity: Compare two countries on hierarchy, directness and time, and write one rule for leading in each.

Standard: Three dimensions compared with examples and one practical rule per country.

Part 3 Institutions and political risk 21 hAnalyse: score risk

Laws, courts, tax and politics decide how safe it is to invest. Weak institutions raise costs that do not appear on a price list.

  • Check contract enforcement, tax stability and permits
  • Score each risk by chance and impact
  • Plan how to reduce the largest risks

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Tax change: 30% chance, impact 2m, so 0.6m. Permit delay: 50% chance, impact 1m, so 0.5m. Total expected cost 1.1m a year.

Activity: Score three institutional risks for one country.

Standard: Each risk has a chance, an impact and a correct expected cost.

Part 4 Global organisation design 21 hEvaluate: choose a structure

A firm can run abroad by country, by product, by function or as a mix. The right choice balances local response with global efficiency.

  • Country structure: strong local response, duplicated cost
  • Global product structure: efficient, less local
  • Matrix: both, but slower decisions

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Five countries each run their own finance team of 4 people: 20 staff. A shared regional centre needs 12. Saving 8 staff at 30,000 each = 240,000 a year, against slower local answers.

Activity: Recommend a structure for a firm in five countries and give the main trade-off.

Standard: One structure chosen, one benefit and one cost quantified.

Part 5 Leading global teams 21 hApply: lead

Global teams need trust built on purpose, reliable updates and early wins, because people rarely meet.

  • Start with a face-to-face or video kick-off
  • Share one goal and visible progress
  • Rotate meeting times so the same people do not always stay late

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A team across 3 time zones holds 12 meetings a quarter. Rotating times means each zone hosts 4 at an awkward hour instead of one zone taking all 12.

Activity: Plan a quarter of meetings for a three-zone team with fair rotation.

Standard: Twelve meetings planned, burden shared within one meeting between zones.

Part 6 Global management integrative case 21 hCreate: recommend

The final chapter brings everything together: reason for going abroad, culture, risk, structure and leadership in one recommendation.

  • Answer first, then evidence
  • Use numbers from earlier chapters
  • Name what would change your mind

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Brief opening: "Enter country B through a regional hub; expected risk cost 1.1m a year is covered by 3m profit." Each figure traces back to the earlier chapters.

Activity: Write the two-page brief for the case firm.

Standard: Two pages, answer first, at least four correct figures from earlier chapters.

ten checks

Check yourself

Say or write your answer first, then read the model answer.

  1. Q1. Name four reasons firms go abroad.
    Model answer: Markets, lower cost, skills, following clients. (Covered in part 1: Globalisation and the firm.)
  2. Q2. 20m of 50m sales are foreign. What share?
    Model answer: 40%. (Covered in part 1: Globalisation and the firm.)
  3. Q3. Name three cultural dimensions a manager should read.
    Model answer: Hierarchy, directness, time. (Covered in part 2: Culture and cross-border management.)
  4. Q4. Why give feedback privately in an indirect culture?
    Model answer: Public criticism can cause loss of face and damage trust. (Covered in part 2: Culture and cross-border management.)
  5. Q5. A 50% chance of a 1m delay: expected cost?
    Model answer: 0.5m. (Covered in part 3: Institutions and political risk.)
  6. Q6. Name one sign of weak institutions.
    Model answer: Poor contract enforcement, unstable tax or slow permits. (Covered in part 3: Institutions and political risk.)
  7. Q7. What is the main weakness of a matrix structure?
    Model answer: Slower decisions. (Covered in part 4: Global organisation design.)
  8. Q8. Saving 8 staff at 30,000 each saves how much a year?
    Model answer: 240,000. (Covered in part 4: Global organisation design.)
  9. Q9. Why rotate meeting times?
    Model answer: So the same zone does not always carry the late hours. (Covered in part 5: Leading global teams.)
  10. Q10. What should a brief start with?
    Model answer: The answer or recommendation. (Covered in part 6: Global management integrative case.)
