executive programme

Module 5: Semi-globalisation, distance modelling and trade gravity

A 2.5 ECTS executive module, 75 hours.

what you take

Module 5

  • Size

    2.5 ECTS, 75 hours of study.

  • Credit

    Counts in 15 countries. See the table.

how it is marked

Assessment

  • Econometric audit 30%
  • Allocator simulation 35%
  • Board presentation 35%

Marking bands: A, B or fail. Presentations use at most 10 slides, with a short live defence.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task, condition and standard

Task

Use a simple trade-gravity model to estimate how much two markets should trade, find the gap to what they do trade, and recommend where to put resources.

Condition

Given a table of country sizes, distances, trade values and a short regression result for at least six country pairs.

Standard

Predictions, percentage changes and gaps are calculated correctly, and the recommendation is backed by the numbers and a stated limit of the model.

Who it is for: Strategy, finance and planning leaders who allocate money across foreign markets.

eight parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 Semi-globalisation: how far borders still matter 9 hUnderstand: explain and measure

The world is not flat and not closed. Most activity is still local, with a smaller but real share crossing borders. We measure that share.

  • Border-crossing share = cross-border flow / total flow
  • Compare with a no-border benchmark
  • A low share means borders still matter

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency: a country has total sales of 800 across its firms, of which 160 go to other countries. Share = 160 / 800 = 20%. If it traded with the world in proportion to the world's size, and it holds 4% of world output, then foreign buyers would be 96% of the market. Actual share 20% against 96% means it sells a fifth as much abroad as a no-border world would predict. Semi-global, not flat.

Activity: Calculate the cross-border share for three countries from a table and compare each with a no-border benchmark.

Standard: Three shares and three benchmarks are correct, with one sentence on what each says.

Part 2 The gravity idea: size up, distance down 10 hApply: calculate

Bigger economies trade more. Farther ones trade less. The gravity model puts both in one line.

  • Trade is about size A x size B divided by distance
  • Use an index to compare pairs
  • Equal pairs should give equal answers

Worked example: Pair 1: GDP 2,000 and 1,000, distance 500 km. Index = 2,000 x 1,000 / 500 = 4,000. Pair 2: GDP 500 and 500, distance 250 km. Index = 500 x 500 / 250 = 1,000. Pair 1 should trade 4 times as much as pair 2. If pair 1 actually trades 3 and pair 2 trades 1 in the same units, pair 1 is below the model by 25%: 3 against 4.

Activity: Build a gravity index for six pairs and rank them from most to least expected trade.

Standard: Six indexes correct and ranked, with the largest gap between actual and expected named.

Part 3 Logs and elasticities 9 hApply: transform and interpret

Models use logs so that changes are percentages. An elasticity tells how much trade changes when distance or size changes by 1%.

  • Elasticity of -1 means doubling distance halves trade
  • A percent change = new / old - 1
  • Log lines are easier to fit

Worked example: Distance elasticity of -1. Distance rises 10%, from 500 to 550 km. Trade changes by 1/1.10 - 1 = -9.1%, not -10%. With elasticity -1.2, factor = 1.10 to the power -1.2 = about 0.892, so trade falls 10.8%. A firm with 12 million (in your currency) of trade on that lane loses 1.3 million at the second elasticity.

Activity: Compute the effect on trade of a 10%, 20% and 50% rise in distance at two elasticities.

Standard: Six percentage changes are correct to one decimal place.

Part 4 The border effect 9 hAnalyse: compare

Even at equal distance, trade inside a country is larger than trade across a border. The ratio is called the border effect.

  • Compare same-distance pairs
  • Border effect = home flow / foreign flow
  • Ratio shows the weight of everything that is not distance

Worked example: Two regions in the same country, 400 km apart, average flow 50. A region and a foreign region, also 400 km apart and of equal size, average flow 8. Border effect = 50 / 8 = 6.25. Meaning: borders act like a barrier that cuts trade to 16% of home levels (8 / 50). If a new agreement lifts the foreign flow to 20, the effect becomes 2.5.

Activity: Calculate border effects for three pairs and rank them by how much a border hurts trade.

Standard: Three ratios correct and ranked, with the reciprocal percentage for each.

Part 5 Reading a regression output 9 hAnalyse: audit

A regression fits a line to data. A short regression table says how strong each factor is and how sure we can be. We learn to audit it.

  • Coefficient: size of effect
  • Standard error: how uncertain
  • t-value = coefficient / standard error

Worked example: Output: size of exporter 0.9 (SE 0.1), size of importer 0.8 (SE 0.1), distance -0.9 (SE 0.3). t-values: 9, 8, -3. A t-value beyond about 2 in size is reliable. Distance -0.9 with SE 0.3 means the true value is between about -1.5 and -0.3 (plus or minus two SE: -0.9 - 0.6 and -0.9 + 0.6). Audit note: the range is wide, so do not plan on exactly -0.9.

Activity: Audit a given output: compute t-values, state the reliable ones and write the range for each.

Standard: All t-values correct, the ranges correct, and one named weakness.

Part 6 Language, borders, agreements and other shifters 9 hApply: estimate effects

Sharing a language, a border or a trade agreement raises trade. In logs, a coefficient of c means a change of e to the power c minus 1 (e is about 2.718).

  • Dummy variable: 1 if the feature is present
  • Effect = e^c - 1
  • Compare features by this percentage

Worked example: Coefficient for shared language 0.4. Effect = e^0.4 - 1 = 1.49 - 1 = 49% more trade. Coefficient for a trade agreement 0.25: e^0.25 - 1 = 1.284 - 1 = 28%. Predicted trade for a pair with both = base x 1.49 x 1.284 = base x 1.91. With a base of 20, expected = 38.2, so 91% above base.

Activity: For four pairs, work out the predicted change from language, border and agreement features.

Standard: Four predictions correct and the feature with the largest effect named.

Part 7 Finding trade potential and the allocator simulation 10 hEvaluate: decide

Potential is predicted trade minus actual trade. Where the gap is large and the cost to close it is low, the money goes.

  • Gap = predicted - actual
  • Return = gap / cost
  • Rank by return, then fund until the budget ends

Worked example: Budget 10m. Market P: predicted 60, actual 40, gap 20, cost 4m, return 5 per m. Market Q: predicted 90, actual 45, gap 45, cost 9m, return 5 per m. Market R: predicted 30, actual 28, gap 2, cost 1m, return 2 per m. Fund P (4m) and then 6m to Q at 5 per m, which gives 30. Gap closed = 20 + 30 = 50. Market R is poor value despite low cost, so it gets nothing.

Activity: Allocate a 12m budget across four markets in a simulation and defend the ranking by return.

Standard: Returns correct, budget fully used or reasons stated, and total gap closed is calculated.

Part 8 Board presentation: the case and the limits 10 hCreate: present

The board presentation puts the model, the gaps and the allocation into ten slides with one clear ask and clear limits.

  • One ask on slide 1
  • Sensitivity: what if the elasticity is wrong
  • State what the model leaves out

Worked example: Ask: move 5m to Markets P and Q. Sensitivity: a new port cuts distance to P by 20%, so trade should rise by 1/0.8 - 1 = 25% at elasticity -1, which is 40 x 0.25 = 10 units. If the true elasticity is -0.5, the gain is 0.8 to the power -0.5 - 1 = 11.8%, or 4.7 units. The answer is a range of 4.7 to 10. Limits: model ignores tariffs, politics and product mix.

Activity: Present a 10-slide board case and take three questions in a 5-minute defence.

Standard: At most 10 slides, one ask, a sensitivity range with correct numbers and at least two limits.

ten checks

Check yourself

  1. A country sells 160 abroad out of total sales of 800. What is the cross-border share?
    160 / 800 = 20%. (Covered in part 1: Semi-globalisation: how far borders still matter. To pass that part: Three shares and three benchmarks are correct, with one sentence on what each says.)
  2. Pair A has GDP 2,000 and 1,000 at 500 km. What is its gravity index?
    2,000 x 1,000 / 500 = 4,000. (Covered in part 2: The gravity idea: size up, distance down. To pass that part: Six indexes correct and ranked, with the largest gap between actual and expected named.)
  3. Pair B has index 1,000. How much more should pair A trade than pair B?
    4 times as much. (Covered in part 2: The gravity idea: size up, distance down. To pass that part: Six indexes correct and ranked, with the largest gap between actual and expected named.)
  4. With a distance elasticity of -1, distance rises 10%. By how much does trade fall?
    1/1.10 - 1 = about -9.1%. (Covered in part 3: Logs and elasticities. To pass that part: Six percentage changes are correct to one decimal place.)
  5. Same-distance home flow is 50 and foreign flow is 8. What is the border effect?
    50 / 8 = 6.25. (Covered in part 4: The border effect. To pass that part: Three ratios correct and ranked, with the reciprocal percentage for each.)
  6. A coefficient is -0.9 with standard error 0.3. What is the t-value?
    -3. (Covered in part 5: Reading a regression output. To pass that part: All t-values correct, the ranges correct, and one named weakness.)
  7. A shared language has a coefficient of 0.4. How much more trade does it predict?
    e^0.4 - 1 = about 49%. (Covered in part 6: Language, borders, agreements and other shifters. To pass that part: Four predictions correct and the feature with the largest effect named.)
  8. Predicted 90, actual 45, cost 9m. What is the return per m?
    Gap 45 / 9m = 5 per m. (Covered in part 7: Finding trade potential and the allocator simulation. To pass that part: Returns correct, budget fully used or reasons stated, and total gap closed is calculated.)
  9. A new port cuts distance by 20% at elasticity -1. By how much should trade rise?
    1/0.8 - 1 = 25%. (Covered in part 8: Board presentation: the case and the limits. To pass that part: At most 10 slides, one ask, a sensitivity range with correct numbers and at least two limits.)
  10. Why present a range instead of one figure?
    The elasticity is uncertain, so the gain could be 4.7 to 10 units, and the board should see both. (Covered in part 8: Board presentation: the case and the limits. To pass that part: At most 10 slides, one ask, a sensitivity range with correct numbers and at least two limits.)

slides L1 to L7

The seven-level ladder, slide by slide

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: Why borders still matter. Most trade and investment is still local. Border-crossing share shows how much is not. Speaker note: Say: ask what share of your own spending crosses a border. Check: compute one share. Mistake: saying the world is flat.
  2. Slide 2: L2 The gravity index. Size of A times size of B divided by distance gives an index of expected trade. Speaker note: Say: do one pair on the board. Check: learners rank two pairs. Mistake: using distance in the wrong place.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Percent changes and elasticities. An elasticity turns a change in distance or size into a change in trade. Speaker note: Say: show 10% distance rise. Check: compute 1/1.1. Mistake: assuming a 10% rise gives a 10% fall.
  4. Slide 4: L4 The border effect. At the same distance, home flows are larger. The ratio shows how much a border weighs. Speaker note: Say: use two pairs of regions. Check: compute the ratio and its reciprocal. Mistake: mixing up the direction.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Auditing the regression. Coefficient, standard error and t-value. Range equals coefficient plus or minus twice the error. Speaker note: Say: calculate a range live. Check: who trusts the distance figure? Mistake: ignoring the error.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Potential and allocation. Gap equals predicted minus actual. Rank by gap per money spent. Speaker note: Say: run the P, Q, R case. Check: total gap closed. Mistake: funding the biggest gap regardless of cost.
  7. Slide 7: L7 The board case. One ask, ten slides, a range and the limits of the model. Speaker note: Say: demonstrate a sensitivity range. Check: is the ask on slide 1? Mistake: presenting one number as certain.

jobs this leads to

Where this takes you

  • Market analyst. Builds models of expected trade and finds under-served markets.
  • Strategy and planning manager. Decides how to split a budget across countries.
  • Trade finance manager. Plans funding for cross-border sales and watches the risks.
  • Economic adviser. Explains trade and distance effects to boards and leaders.
