English · Grade 3 · TEACHER ANSWER KEY

Answers are shown in green. Accept any correct answer in the learner's own words. Marking: 1 mark for each question unless stated.

Two limericks by Edward Lear (1812 to 1888; in the public domain)

There was an Old Man with a beard,
Who said, "It is just as I feared!
Two Owls and a Hen,
Four Larks and a Wren,
Have all built their nests in my beard!"

There was a Young Lady whose chin
Resembled the point of a pin;
So she had it made sharp,
And purchased a harp,
And played several tunes with her chin.

1.How many lines does a limerick have?
Answer: Five
2.Which lines rhyme in the AABBA pattern?
Answer: Lines 1, 2 and 5 rhyme with each other; lines 3 and 4 rhyme with each other.
3.Write the rhyming word pair from the first limerick: beard, ___
Answer: feared
4.In the second limerick, which word at the end of line 2 rhymes with "chin"?
Answer: pin
5.Which animals nested in the old man's beard?
Answer: Two owls, a hen, four larks and a wren.
6.Give two words that rhyme with "hen".
Answer: Any two, for example ten, pen, men, then.
7.Why is the limerick funny?
Answer: Because birds building nests in a beard is impossible and silly (accept any fair reason).
8.Complete the rhyme: There once was a cat on a mat / Who wore a very big ___
Answer: hat (accept any word rhyming with mat)
9.Write the last word of a line that rhymes with "sea".
Answer: Any word rhyming with sea, for example tree.
10.Write your own limerick of five lines.
Answer: Marked with the poetry rubric: five lines, AABBA rhyme, a clear funny idea.
