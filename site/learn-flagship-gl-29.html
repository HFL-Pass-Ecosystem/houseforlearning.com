flagship course · GL-29

Business Process Outsourcing Services

Set up and run a small outsourcing service: define a process, write procedures, protect client data, hit quality and speed targets and report clearly.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Set up and run a small outsourcing service: define a process, write procedures, protect client data, hit quality and speed targets and report clearly.

  • Understand Explain what can be outsourced, risks, and how contracts and service levels work.
  • Apply Document a process step by step.
  • Analyse Analyse volume, time and error data to set targets.
  • Create Create a procedure, quality checks and a client report.
  • Evaluate Evaluate the service against agreed targets.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 4-week pilot of one outsourced process (for example data entry, invoice processing or customer email) for a practice client with measured quality and speed.

Condition

A practice client and sample non-personal data, a spreadsheet and a free workflow tool, a mentor with operations experience and a peer circle. About 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

Agreement and data rules signed; procedure written and followed; accuracy of 98% or better on a sample; 95% of tasks done within the target time; client report sent weekly; no real personal data used in training.

Who it is for: Operations staff, small-business owners, freelancers, team leads, students. Requirements: Basic spreadsheet skills. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Protect client data. Use sample data in training. Sign confidentiality terms. Never share logins.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What outsourcing is 4 hUnderstand

Handing a repeatable process to a service.

  • Examples: data entry, bookkeeping support, customer support, design support.
  • Why firms outsource: cost, focus, skill, hours.
  • Risks: quality, security, dependence.
  • What not to outsource.
  • Check data rules for the client's country.
  • Compare in-house cost.
Worked example. A shop sends 200 supplier invoices a month to a processing service for 1.20 each.

Activity. List five outsourceable processes.

Standard to meet. Reasons for each.

Common mistake. Outsourcing a process you cannot describe.

Second worked example. Write the steps first; then price.

Check yourself. Which process did you choose and why?

Module 2 Define the process 6 hUnderstand

If it is not written, it is not repeatable.

  • Inputs, steps, outputs, owner.
  • Flow chart with decision points.
  • Exceptions and who decides.
  • Examples of good and bad output.
  • Add screenshots of each step.
  • Name a process owner.
Worked example. Invoice process: receive, check, enter, match, approve, file; exception: missing PO number goes to the client.

Activity. Map one process.

Standard to meet. Chart has decisions and exceptions.

Common mistake. Skipping exceptions.

Second worked example. Missing purchase order goes back to the client within a day.

Check yourself. What are your exceptions?

Module 3 Contract and service levels 8 hApply

Agree what good means.

  • Scope, price, term, exit.
  • Service levels: turnaround, accuracy, hours.
  • Penalties and credits.
  • Data protection and confidentiality.
  • Agree how to change the contract.
  • Include a review date.
Worked example. SLA: 95% of invoices processed in 24 hours; accuracy 98%.

Activity. Draft a one-page agreement.

Standard to meet. Targets are measurable.

Common mistake. Service levels that cannot be measured.

Second worked example. "Fast" becomes "within 24 hours".

Check yourself. Which targets are numbers?

Module 4 Procedures and training 8 hApply

Write for a new person.

  • Short numbered steps with screenshots.
  • Checklists.
  • Practice with sample data.
  • Sign-off on competence.
  • Run a dry run with sample items.
  • Update the procedure after the pilot.
Worked example. New staff reach target accuracy after 2 days using the checklist.

Activity. Write the procedure and train a peer.

Standard to meet. Peer completes tasks alone.

Common mistake. Training with real data.

Second worked example. Use sample data with names removed.

Check yourself. What data did you train with?

Module 5 Quality control 8 hAnalyse

Check before the client does.

  • Sample checks: 10% of items.
  • Error types and causes.
  • Fix causes, not just errors.
  • Second-person check on risky steps.
  • Record error types by cause.
  • Share results with the team.
Worked example. 100 items, 3 errors found = 97%: below 98%, so add a check on amounts.

Activity. Check a sample of 50.

Standard to meet. Accuracy calculated.

Common mistake. Checking only at the end.

Second worked example. Daily sample of 10% finds errors earlier.

Check yourself. What is your sample size?

Module 6 Volume, time and capacity 10 hCreate

Plan staff by numbers.

  • Time per task x volume = hours.
  • Peaks and buffers.
  • Capacity per person per day.
  • Cost per task.
  • Keep a buffer of ten percent.
  • Track overtime.
Worked example. 300 tasks x 6 minutes = 1,800 minutes = 30 hours; one person does 6 hours a day: 5 person-days.

Activity. Build a capacity sheet.

Standard to meet. Hours match volume.

Common mistake. Ignoring peaks.

Second worked example. Month-end volume doubles; plan extra hours.

Check yourself. When is your peak?

Module 7 Security and privacy 6 hEvaluate

Trust is the business.

  • Access only for those who need it.
  • No data on personal devices unless agreed.
  • Log access.
  • Report incidents at once.
  • Use unique logins.
  • Remove access on leaving.
Worked example. A staff member needs only the invoice folder: grant that, not the bank files.

Activity. Write a security checklist.

Standard to meet. Checklist used on the pilot.

Common mistake. Everyone has admin rights.

Second worked example. Grant only what each role needs.

Check yourself. Who has what access?

Module 8 Reporting and improvement 4 hCreate

Clients buy results.

  • Weekly report: volumes, speed, accuracy, issues.
  • Root-cause fixes.
  • Suggest improvements.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Hold a monthly review.
  • Keep a list of improvements.
Worked example. Report: 640 done, 97.5% on time, 98.4% accurate; fix: add a drop-down for tax codes.

Activity. Send four weekly reports.

Standard to meet. Reports are one page and accurate.

Common mistake. Reports full of numbers, no advice.

Second worked example. Each report ends with one suggestion.

Check yourself. What did you suggest?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. Quality drop

Accuracy falls to 95%.

Question: Response?

Model answer: Sample errors, find the cause, retrain or change the form, recheck.

Case 2. Sudden volume

Volume triples for a week.

Question: What do you do?

Model answer: Tell the client, add temporary hours, protect quality and report.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: process map, agreement, procedure, quality sample, capacity sheet, security checklist and four weekly reports. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name two outsourceable processes.

Model answer: Data entry and invoice processing.

Q2. What is an SLA?

Model answer: A service-level agreement with measurable targets.

Q3. Why map a process?

Model answer: To make it repeatable and trainable.

Q4. How compute accuracy?

Model answer: Correct items divided by checked items.

Q5. Capacity formula?

Model answer: Time per task times volume gives hours.

Q6. Why use sample data in training?

Model answer: It protects real personal data.

Q7. What to do on a missing input?

Model answer: Follow the exception rule, usually return to the client.

Q8. Why sample checks?

Model answer: They find errors early at low cost.

Q9. What goes in a weekly report?

Model answer: Volume, speed, accuracy and issues.

Q10. Name one security control.

Model answer: Least access or access logging.

ten locations and global

Your place

Data export, labour and tax rules differ by place; check contracts and cross-border data rules. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a Brainport-area small firm.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Partner to practise with: SEBRAE small-business support, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or a SkillsFuture-linked training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a TAFE or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico (Central America)

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
