flagship course · GL-25

Inclusive and Minority-Owned Business Growth

Help under-served owners grow a business: assess where they stand, open fair routes to customers, finance and mentors, and track real progress.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Help under-served owners grow a business: assess where they stand, open fair routes to customers, finance and mentors, and track real progress.

  • Understand Explain barriers owners face and the ways to remove them respectfully.
  • Apply Assess a business with a simple, strengths-first health check.
  • Analyse Analyse supplier, customer and finance routes for fairness and fit.
  • Create Create a 90-day growth plan with the owner.
  • Evaluate Evaluate progress with agreed measures, led by the owner.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Work with one owner (or a practice case) to produce a health check, a 90-day growth plan and a progress report.

Condition

An owner who agrees to take part, a spreadsheet, public programme lists, a mentor with business-support experience and a peer circle. About 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

The owner agrees the plan and measures in writing; no private data shared beyond agreed people; at least three realistic routes researched; plan has dated actions; progress measured at day 30 and 60.

Who it is for: Business advisers, community workers, mentors, small-business owners, students. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Respect, consent and privacy come first. Do not label people or make assumptions. Give no legal or tax advice beyond your role; refer to a qualified adviser.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Respect and listening 4 hUnderstand

The owner leads; you support. You will be able to listen first, agree working terms openly, and support an owner's decisions without taking over their business.

  • Ask what success means to them.
  • Strengths first: skills, products, customers, community.
  • Avoid assumptions and labels.
  • Agree how information is stored and shared.
  • Begin the first meeting by asking the owner to tell their story in their own words for ten minutes, taking notes and repeating back what you heard before suggesting anything.
  • Agree how you will work together, including how often you meet, what stays private and who makes final decisions, and write this down in a short plain-language note.
  • Meet the owner in a place they choose.
  • Use the owner's words in every document.
Worked example. An owner says "I want steady orders, not a big shop": the plan aims at repeat orders.

Activity. Hold a listening session and write the owner's goals in their words.

Standard to meet. Confirm the summary with the owner.

Common mistake. Arriving with a plan before listening.

Second worked example. A weaver says 'I want fair pay, not mass sales'; the plan sets a floor price of 18 a scarf and 3 fixed buyers instead of a market stall. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. What did the owner say success looks like?

Module 2 Health check 6 hUnderstand

Know where the business stands. You will be able to score six business areas on a simple scale, giving the owner an honest starting picture to measure against.

  • Money in and out; margins; debts.
  • Customers: who, how many, repeat rate.
  • Operations: capacity, quality, suppliers.
  • Skills, tools, records, legal basics.
  • Score six areas from 1 to 5, such as sales, cash, records, customers, people and tools, to give the owner a simple picture they can understand at a glance.
  • Take the two lowest scores and ask the owner what changed in the last year, since a recent drop often points to a cause that can be fixed.
  • Note strengths before problems.
  • Ask what help they have already tried.
Worked example. Monthly sales 1,800; costs 1,350; profit 450 (25%); two clients give 70% of sales, so losing one would hurt badly (concentration risk). Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Complete the health check.

Standard to meet. Score each area with evidence.

Common mistake. Only listing weaknesses.

Second worked example. A tailor's monthly sales are 2,400 and costs 2,000: profit 400 (17%). Rent is half of costs, so the first action is to ask for a rent review. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. What are three strengths?

Module 3 Barriers and routes 8 hApply

Barriers are often access, not ability. You will be able to separate barriers of money, knowledge and access, and match each with a practical route the owner can actually use.

  • Common barriers: credit, networks, information, language, procurement rules.
  • Routes: programmes, networks, supplier schemes, mentors.
  • Check eligibility and cost.
  • Prefer fair, transparent routes.
  • List every barrier under three headings (money, knowledge and access) and pair each with a realistic route, such as a local support scheme, a mentor or a shared tool.
  • Ask the owner which barrier hurts most today and start there, because early progress on their biggest worry builds trust.
  • Explain every term in plain language.
  • Check eligibility rules before suggesting a scheme.
Worked example. A supplier-diversity scheme lists simple steps: register, document, get invited to quotes.

Activity. Research five routes with steps and costs.

Standard to meet. Rank routes by fit.

Common mistake. Naming barriers as if they were the owner's fault.

Second worked example. A scheme to buy from small firms asks for a tax number, a bank letter and a sample; the owner finds she already has two of the three and starts the third.

Check yourself. Which barrier is about access, not ability?

Module 4 Customers and markets 8 hApply

Growth needs buyers. You will be able to identify an owner's best customers, test one low-cost new market, and judge whether demand is real.

  • Who buys now and who else could.
  • Offers and prices that fit.
  • Online and local channels.
  • Reviews and referrals.
  • Help the owner name their ten best customers, what each buys and how often, then count how many arrived through recommendation to see which channels work.
  • Test one new market cheaply, such as a market stall or an online listing for two weeks, and record the number of enquiries and sales it produces.
  • Never promise funding you do not control.
  • Keep a list of free local mentors.
Worked example. Owner sells to neighbours; a local canteen could buy weekly: draft a simple price list.

Activity. Draft two outreach messages and a price list.

Standard to meet. Write specific messages.

Common mistake. Sending mass messages.

Second worked example. A woman who bakes for her street asks the nearby clinic if staff would pay 3 a day for lunch boxes; she tests with 10 boxes. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. Whom will you contact first?

Module 5 Money and finance 8 hAnalyse

Fair finance helps; unfair finance harms. You will be able to build a monthly cash table and cost per sale, helping owners judge fair finance and avoid harmful borrowing.

  • Know the full cost of the money: rate, fees and length of loan.
  • Grants, small loans, savings groups, supplier credit.
  • Avoid pressure and hidden fees.
  • Keep simple records.
  • Draw up a simple monthly cash table showing money in, money out and what is left, using real bank figures from the last three months.
  • Show the owner the true cost of one sale and the smallest price rise that would increase profit, using an actual product from their own sales.
  • Offer to read forms together.
  • Record the owner's decisions, not yours.
Worked example. A loan of 2,000 at 18% (an estimate; check your market) for a year costs 360 in interest; compare with a grant needing no repayment. Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Compare three finance options.

Standard to meet. Total cost shown for each.

Common mistake. Taking the first loan offered.

Second worked example. A grant of 1,500 needs a 6-week application and a report; a loan of 1,500 at 12% for a year costs 180 in interest. The owner picks the loan because the shop must restock in 3 weeks. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. What is the total cost of each option?

Module 6 Skills and tools 10 hCreate

Small upgrades make big differences. You will be able to match skills gaps to short training or peers, and teach one simple tool that saves the owner time.

  • Records, pricing, online listing, safety.
  • Free training and mentors.
  • A simple tool stack.
  • Plan learning time.
  • Identify two skill gaps that block growth, such as pricing or online selling, and match each to one short training session or a peer who can demonstrate the skill.
  • Choose one free or low-cost tool, such as a basic spreadsheet or an online booking page, and teach the owner to use it in a single practice session.
  • Share only agreed information with others.
  • Celebrate small wins in writing.
Worked example. Owner learns to price with margin: raises prices 8% and keeps customers.

Activity. Pick two skills and a learning plan.

Standard to meet. Plan fits owner's time.

Common mistake. Training that is too long.

Second worked example. A cobbler tracks costs for the first time, finds one repair sells at a 5% margin, and raises its price from 8 to 10; 2 of 30 customers complain and the rest stay. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. Which skill will be used this week?

Module 7 The 90-day plan 6 hEvaluate

A short, dated plan beats a long wish list. You will be able to write a dated 90-day plan with three numbered goals and weekly actions that fit around a busy owner's week.

  • Three goals; each with three actions.
  • Owner, helper, date for each.
  • Measures: sales, orders, customers, savings.
  • Review day 30 and 60.
  • Set three goals for the 90 days, each with a number and a date, for example 10 new customers by day 60, and display them where the owner will see them daily.
  • Break each goal into weekly actions of under two hours each, and agree a ten-minute check-in call every Friday to review progress.
  • Review progress with the owner every two weeks.
  • Ask what support is still needed at the end.
Worked example. Goal: 5 repeat customers by day 60. Actions: call 10 past buyers; offer a bundle; track orders.

Activity. Write the plan with the owner.

Standard to meet. Obtain the owner's sign-off.

Common mistake. Plans with ten goals.

Second worked example. Goal: 8 new regular buyers by day 45. Actions: visit 15 shops, leave a sample, follow up after 3 days, record each visit on one sheet.

Check yourself. Which three goals did the owner choose?

Module 8 Progress and handover 4 hCreate

Measure honestly and hand over power. You will be able to compare day-one and day-ninety scores, then hand over a summary so the owner keeps control after you leave.

  • Compare with the health check.
  • Celebrate what worked; adjust what did not.
  • Document what the owner can repeat.
  • Plan next steps and who supports.
  • At day 90, compare the starting health-check scores with the new ones and show the owner the changes in a simple table so progress is clearly visible.
  • Hand over a one-page summary with next steps, useful contacts and the date of a follow-up call, so the owner can continue without your help.
  • Hand over every file and template.
  • Write a short lessons note with the owner.
Worked example. Day 60: 4 repeat customers against 5; profit up 5 percentage points; keep going with the bundle.

Activity. Write the progress report.

Standard to meet. Report uses numbers and owner quotes.

Common mistake. Taking credit for the result.

Second worked example. Day 45: 6 new regular buyers against 8; average order up from 40 to 52; next step is a second visit to the 9 shops that said maybe. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. What did the owner say?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. The big contract

A large buyer offers a contract that needs three times current capacity.

Question: What do you advise?

Model answer: List what is needed, test a smaller first order, discuss financing and refer to a finance adviser.

Case 2. The scheme with fees

A "support programme" asks for an upfront fee.

Question: What do you check?

Model answer: Verify the organisation, ask for written terms and warn the owner about pressure.

Glossary

Barrier
Something that makes it harder to reach a fair outcome.
Concentration risk
Relying on few customers.
Margin
Money left after costs.
Procurement
How organisations buy goods and services.
Mentor
A person who shares experience.
Health check
A simple review of a business.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: owner goals, health check, route research, finance comparison, 90-day plan and progress report. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Who leads the plan?

Model answer: The owner. (Covered in module 1: Respect and listening. To pass that module: Confirm the summary with the owner.)

Q2. Why start with strengths?

Model answer: It builds confidence and shows assets to use. (Covered in module 1: Respect and listening. To pass that module: Confirm the summary with the owner.)

Q3. What is concentration risk?

Model answer: Too much income from few customers. (Covered in module 2: Health check. To pass that module: Score each area with evidence.)

Q4. Name three barriers.

Model answer: Credit, networks, information, language or procurement rules. (Covered in module 3: Barriers and routes. To pass that module: Rank routes by fit.)

Q5. How to compare finance?

Model answer: By total cost, fees and terms. (Covered in module 5: Money and finance. To pass that module: Total cost shown for each.)

Q6. What does a good plan include?

Model answer: Goals, actions, owners, dates and measures. (Covered in module 7: The 90-day plan. To pass that module: Obtain the owner's sign-off.)

Q7. Why get consent?

Model answer: It protects privacy and trust. (Covered in module 1: Respect and listening. To pass that module: Confirm the summary with the owner.)

Q8. When do you review?

Model answer: At day 30 and day 60. (Covered in module 7: The 90-day plan. To pass that module: Obtain the owner's sign-off.)

Q9. Should you give legal advice?

Model answer: No; refer to a qualified adviser. (Covered in module 1: Respect and listening. To pass that module: Confirm the summary with the owner.)

Q10. Why document learning?

Model answer: So the owner can repeat it. (Covered in module 8: Progress and handover. To pass that module: Report uses numbers and owner quotes.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Programmes, definitions and legal protections differ by place: use local schemes and respect local law on equality. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Partner to practise with: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

ladder L1 to L7

Slide by slide, with notes

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Basics of enterprise

On the slide: L1 Basic · Basics of enterprise

Notes: Describe what a small enterprise is and what it needs to survive. Notes: talk to one local owner. Standard: a list of three needs they named.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Awareness

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Awareness

Notes: Describe barriers small owners face (finance, access to buyers, rules, networks) and which are fixable. Notes: sort ten barriers into fixable and structural. Standard: reasons given for each choice.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Foundation

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Foundation

Notes: Carry out a business health check on a practice case: sales, costs, records, customers and risks. Notes: use a standard checklist. Standard: five findings ranked by effect.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Practitioner

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Practitioner

Notes: Plan 30 days of action for one practice owner with a budget, a buyer list and a measure. Notes: agree each action with the owner. Standard: every action has an owner, a date and a measure.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Specialist

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Specialist

Notes: Work with one owner or a practice case to produce a health check, a 90-day growth plan and a progress report. Notes: include access to buyers and finance. Standard: report shows the baseline, the actions done and the change in a chosen measure.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Advanced

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Advanced

Notes: Lead a support programme for several owners with referral partners, fair selection rules and results reporting. Notes: add an annual fairness review. Standard: selection rules published and results reported for all groups served.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Super expert

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Super expert

Notes: Set the fair-opportunity method for business growth (access to buyers, finance, mentors) and teach the advisers who use it. Notes: review who was reached and who was missed each year and change the outreach.
