enterprise software course

Enterprise Database and Finance Applications Practice

Learn relational data, queries, and the core finance flows of a large business application suite.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L1 to L7 ladder29:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Remember Remember : data in tables.
  • Apply Apply : asking questions with sql.
  • Understand Understand : the general ledger and chart of accounts.
  • Apply Apply : payables, receivables and cash.
  • Analyse Analyse : reporting and month-end close.
  • Evaluate Evaluate : controls, security and audit.
  • Create Create : integration and performance design.
  • Create Create : capstone: build and defend a finance data solution.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Learn relational data, queries, and the core finance flows of a large business application suite. Finish all 8 modules on a practice set-up.

Condition

A free trial or practice account, practice data only (never real customer data), a peer circle and a mentor for the final review.

Standard

Every module standard is met; records are kept; a peer and a mentor rate the work 'Meets' or better on the rubric.

Who it is for: Accountants, data staff, junior developers, finance operations staff, career changers. Requirements: a computer and internet. Materials: slides with notes, worked examples, activities, quiz, rubric.

How the ladder works: modules climb from L1 (remember) to L7 (create); the ladder section shows each level as a slide. Take them in order; the first three suit new learners, 4 and 5 suit practitioners, 6 and 7 suit advanced staff.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Data in tables 2 hRememberL1 Basic

A database is a set of tables linked by keys.

  • Table, row, column, key.
  • Types: text, number, date.
  • Why we do not store the same fact twice.
Worked example. A customer table and an invoice table are linked by customer number.

Activity. Sketch three linked tables for a small shop.

Standard to meet. Keys named; no repeated facts.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Tables and keys. Speaker note: Draw two tables joined by a key.
  2. Slide 2: One fact, one place. Speaker note: Show a repeated-data problem.
Module 2 Asking questions with SQL 3 hApplyL2 Awareness

SQL asks for exactly the rows and columns you need.

  • SELECT, FROM, WHERE, ORDER BY.
  • JOIN two tables.
  • GROUP BY with totals and counts.
  • Read before write: never run change commands on live data without a test.
Worked example. A query returns total sales per region for last month in one table.

Activity. Write six queries on the practice data set.

Standard to meet. Six correct results; no write commands on live data.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Four words. Speaker note: SELECT FROM WHERE ORDER.
  2. Slide 2: Join. Speaker note: Show matching rows.
Module 3 The general ledger and chart of accounts 3 hUnderstandL3 Foundation

The ledger is the book where every money event lands.

  • Chart of accounts: assets, liabilities, equity, income, expense.
  • Double entry: debit equals credit.
  • Periods, open and close.
Worked example. Buying a laptop for 1,000 debits equipment and credits bank.

Activity. Post ten practice journals and balance the trial.

Standard to meet. Trial balance balances to zero.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Debit and credit. Speaker note: Two columns always equal.
  2. Slide 2: Open and close a period. Speaker note: Why we lock.
Module 4 Payables, receivables and cash 4 hApplyL4 Practitioner

Two flows run the business: what we owe and what we are owed.

  • Purchase to pay: order, receipt, invoice, match, pay.
  • Order to cash: quote, order, invoice, receipt, match.
  • Three-way match and approval limits.
  • Ageing report and follow-up.
Worked example. An invoice differs from the order by 3%; the match rule sends it for approval.

Activity. Process five purchase invoices and five sales invoices; run an ageing report.

Standard to meet. All matched or explained; ageing report correct.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Two flows. Speaker note: Show both on one page.
  2. Slide 2: Three-way match. Speaker note: Order, receipt, invoice.
Module 5 Reporting and month-end close 4 hAnalyseL5 Specialist

A calm close is a checked list, done the same way each month.

  • Close calendar and owners.
  • Accruals, reconciliations, review.
  • Management reports and variance notes.
Worked example. Close takes 5 days instead of 9 because reconciliations run weekly.

Activity. Run a practice month-end close with a check list.

Standard to meet. List fully ticked; variances explained in plain words.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Close calendar. Speaker note: Day-by-day.
  2. Slide 2: Variance note. Speaker note: Write one in plain words.
Module 6 Controls, security and audit 4 hEvaluateL6 Advanced

Controls stop mistakes and fraud without stopping work.

  • Segregation of duties.
  • Approval limits and logs.
  • Backup, restore and access reviews.
Worked example. The person who creates a supplier cannot also pay that supplier.

Activity. Review a practice setup and list five control gaps with fixes.

Standard to meet. Five real gaps, each with a fix and owner.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Never one person alone. Speaker note: Examples.
  2. Slide 2: Logs. Speaker note: What to record.
Module 7 Integration and performance design 5 hCreateL7 Super expert

At the top you design how systems talk and how fast they answer.

  • Interfaces: files, services, messages.
  • Indexes, query plans, batch windows.
  • Data migration plan with reconciliation.
  • Disaster recovery targets.
Worked example. A nightly interface moves 200,000 rows in 20 minutes after an index is added.

Activity. Write a design for a finance integration with migration and recovery plan.

Standard to meet. Design reviewed; recovery time and data loss limits stated.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Interface map. Speaker note: Boxes and arrows.
  2. Slide 2: Index. Speaker note: Before and after timing.
Module 8 Capstone: build and defend a finance data solution 4 hCreateL7 Super expert

The final task joins data, reports and controls in one small solution. You design the tables, write the queries, post and reconcile the entries, and then explain and defend your design to a reviewer.

  • Design: sketch the tables and keys, then list the reports the finance team needs.
  • Build: write queries for totals by month and by customer, and check them against the ledger.
  • Control: list who can create, approve and pay, and show that no one person does all three.
  • Defend: present the design in five minutes, including risks, test results and a restore plan.
Worked example. A small trading firm asks for monthly sales by region that matches the ledger. Your query total differs by 40, and you trace it to one invoice posted in the wrong period, then fix the entry and document the cause.

Activity. Design and test a small finance reporting solution on the practice data set, reconcile it to the ledger and present it to a reviewer.

Standard to meet. Query totals equal ledger totals; three control checks pass; the reviewer finds the design clear and the recovery plan realistic.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Capstone: build and defend a finance data solution: the idea. Speaker note: The final task joins data, reports and controls in one small solution. You design the tables, write the queries, post and reconcile the entries, and then explain and defend your design to a reviewer.
  2. Slide 2: Capstone: build and defend a finance data solution: your turn. Speaker note: Design and test a small finance reporting solution on the practice data set, reconcile it to the ledger and present it to a reviewer.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. A customer table and an invoice table are linked. What is the link called and why not repeat the customer address on every invoice?

Model answer: They are linked by a key, such as customer number. Storing the address once avoids mistakes when it changes. (Covered in module 1: Data in tables. To pass that module: Keys named; no repeated facts.)

Q2. Write what this query does: SELECT region, SUM(amount) FROM sales GROUP BY region.

Model answer: It adds up the sales amount for each region and returns one row per region. (Covered in module 2: Asking questions with SQL. To pass that module: Six correct results; no write commands on live data.)

Q3. In the capstone your query total for sales differs from the ledger by 40. What do you do?

Model answer: Trace the difference to its source, for example an invoice posted in the wrong period, fix the entry and write down the cause. Then re-run the reconciliation until the totals agree. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: build and defend a finance data solution. To pass that module: Query totals equal ledger totals; three control checks pass; the reviewer finds the design clear and the recovery plan realistic.)

Q4. Buying a laptop for 1,000 by bank transfer: which accounts are debited and credited?

Model answer: Debit the equipment account by 1,000 and credit the bank account by 1,000. Debits equal credits. (Covered in module 3: The general ledger and chart of accounts. To pass that module: Trial balance balances to zero.)

Q5. A trial balance shows debits of 52,300 and credits of 51,900. What does this tell you?

Model answer: The books do not balance, with a difference of 400. Look for a one-sided entry or a typing error. (Covered in module 3: The general ledger and chart of accounts. To pass that module: Trial balance balances to zero.)

Q6. An invoice for 1,030 arrives for an order of 1,000 and the match allows 2 percent. What happens?

Model answer: The 3 percent difference is over the limit, so the invoice goes for approval. A person decides whether to pay. (Covered in module 4: Payables, receivables and cash. To pass that module: All matched or explained; ageing report correct.)

Q7. What three documents are compared in a three-way match?

Model answer: The purchase order, the goods receipt and the supplier invoice. They must agree before payment. (Covered in module 4: Payables, receivables and cash. To pass that module: All matched or explained; ageing report correct.)

Q8. Why does running reconciliations weekly shorten the month-end close?

Model answer: Problems are found and fixed early, so fewer surprises remain at month end. The close then follows a calm checklist. (Covered in module 5: Reporting and month-end close. To pass that module: List fully ticked; variances explained in plain words.)

Q9. Give one example of segregation of duties in payables.

Model answer: The person who creates a supplier is not the person who pays that supplier. This reduces the chance of fraud. (Covered in module 6: Controls, security and audit. To pass that module: Five real gaps, each with a fix and owner.)

Q10. A nightly interface of 200,000 rows takes too long. Name one fix and one thing to include in the recovery plan.

Model answer: Add an index on the column used to find rows and check the query plan. The plan should state recovery time and the limit of data loss. (Covered in module 7: Integration and performance design. To pass that module: Design reviewed; recovery time and data loss limits stated.)

levels L1 to L7

The ladder, slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: L1 Basic

Remember the words table, row, column and key. Point to them on a small data example and say why one fact is stored once.

Speaker notes. Draw two linked tables on the board. Common mistake: copying the same customer details into many rows.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: L2 Awareness

Understand how SQL asks for chosen rows and columns, and why you read before you write. Explain the difference between a query and a change command.

Speaker notes. Run a simple query together and ask what it returns. Common mistake: running update commands on live data.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: L3 Foundation

Apply double entry to post journals and balance a trial. Explain how a period is opened and closed.

Speaker notes. Check that debits equal credits on every entry. Common mistake: posting one side only.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: L4 Practitioner

Apply the purchase-to-pay and order-to-cash flows with a three-way match and an ageing report. Resolve differences with a clear note.

Speaker notes. Ask learners to trace one invoice from order to payment. Common mistake: paying before the goods receipt.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: L5 Specialist

Analyse a month-end close by using a calendar, reconciliations and plain-word variance notes. Find why numbers moved.

Speaker notes. Review a variance note for clarity. Common mistake: notes that repeat the number without giving the reason.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: L6 Advanced

Evaluate a setup for control gaps such as missing segregation of duties, weak approvals and untested backups. Rank the gaps by risk.

Speaker notes. Ask for a fix and an owner for each gap. Common mistake: controls so strict that staff avoid them.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: L7 Super expert

Create a full design for interfaces, indexes, migration and recovery, and defend it to a reviewer. State recovery time and data loss limits.

Speaker notes. Check that the design has numbers, not only words. Common mistake: no test of the restore plan.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local law changes how you store customer data and issue invoices. Check it for your place first.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Use INR for amounts. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Use EUR for amounts. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Use AED for amounts. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Use BRL for amounts. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Use CAD for amounts. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Use USD for amounts. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Use SGD for amounts. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Use AUD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Use HKD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Use GEL or ILS for amounts. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Use KYD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Use MXN for amounts. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
