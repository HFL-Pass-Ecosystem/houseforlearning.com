campus standards · 3

Modular shell

A campus is built from one kind of steel module. It arrives flat, is bolted together in hours and can be moved.

picture

Four modules make two floors

  • Plan per module: about 14 m2
  • Standard stack: 2 storeys
  • Rail height: 1.1 m or more
  • Wall panel: about 50 mm

parts

What the shell is made of

Shell parts
PartStandard
ModuleA flat-pack steel-framed unit, about 2.4 m wide, 5.8 m long and 2.9 m high. Four corner posts, a top frame and a bottom frame bolted together.
StackingTwo storeys is the standard. A third storey needs an engineer's check. The bottom frame carries the unit above through the corner posts.
Roof and ceilingInsulated roof sheet with a flat ceiling panel under it. Water leaves through downpipes in the four corners.
FloorCement-fibre board on the frame with a hard-wearing vinyl finish. Wet rooms get an extra waterproof layer.
WallsInsulated sandwich panel about 50 mm thick, with a mineral-wool core for fire resistance. Single-sided wall panels close an open side.
OpeningsA steel door about 0.97 m x 1.97 m for service doors, sliding glass doors about 1.8 m x 2.8 m for learning rooms, and sliding windows about 0.93 m x 1.2 m.
Glass wallA framed glass curtain wall for the learning studio front. Plan about 5 to 6 m2 of glass per studio for light, and add shading.
Terrace and railA terrace the size of one module on the upper storey, with a guard rail at least 1.1 m high.
StairsStraight single flight for one storey, a wider dog-legged or L-shaped stair for a busy building, and a spiral stair only as a secondary route.
Ground and anchorsA level base with water running away from it. The foundation is designed for local wind, soil and earthquake rules by an engineer.

Planning values for teaching and early design. A licensed local engineer and the local authority confirm every real project.
