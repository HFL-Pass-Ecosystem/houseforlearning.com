flagship course

Home Sharing and Short-Stay Hosting

Plan, run and review a safe, legal and honest short-stay home, from first listing to repeat guests.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner44:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain the local rules, safety duties and guest expectations for a short-stay home.
  • Apply Prepare a compliant listing, house manual and turnover checklist.
  • Analyse Compare nightly price, occupancy and costs to find true profit per month.
  • Evaluate Judge guest reviews and fix the three most common complaints.
  • Create Launch a full hosting plan with pricing, calendar, safety and guest journey.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare and run one short-stay home (or room) for a 30-day pilot with a complete listing, house manual, safety checks, pricing sheet and guest review log.

Condition

A real room or home you may legally share (or a practice case supplied by your mentor), a phone, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. About 8 weeks part-time.

Standard

Written permission or rule check is on file; every safety item passes the checklist; price covers costs with a margin you can show; the guest review average and complaint log are recorded honestly; peers rate the evidence Meets or better.

Who it is for: Home owners, tenants with permission, property caretakers, small hosts, hospitality trainees and returners. Requirements: none; you must have the right to host or use the practice case. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Is hosting right for you, and are you allowed? 4 hUnderstand

Hosting is a small business inside a home. Before photos or prices, confirm that you are allowed to host and that your household agrees.

  • Check three things: owner or landlord permission, building or society rules, and local registration, tax and safe-stay rules.
  • Ask the household: noise, shared spaces, privacy, who cleans, who greets guests.
  • Write a one-page go or no-go note with the rule you checked and the date.
Worked example. A tenant in a flat finds the lease bans sub-letting. She writes to the owner, receives written permission for short room stays, files the paper, and only then takes photos. Go decision dated 3 March.

Activity. Write your go or no-go note with three rules checked and one household agreement.

Standard to meet. Each rule has a source (document or office) and a date; no guess is written as a fact.

Module 2 Knowing your guest 5 hAnalyse

A listing wins when it fits a real guest: a worker on a short job, a family visiting relatives, a student on an exam trip.

  • Read 10 public listings near you; record price per night, what is promised, and what guests praise or complain about.
  • Pick one main guest type and write three needs (quiet, kitchen, near transport).
  • Write a promise you can keep every time.
Worked example. Ten listings near a hospital show most complaints are about noise and late check-in. The host chooses visiting relatives as guests and promises: quiet after 9 pm, self check-in from 2 pm, a kettle and a tea set.

Activity. Make your table of 10 listings and your one-line promise.

Standard to meet. Table has price with currency; promise is specific and testable by a guest.

Module 3 Safety first 6 hApply

A pleasant stay is a safe stay. Safety items are not optional extras.

  • Fit and test smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms where relevant; show exits; keep a first-aid box and emergency numbers on the wall.
  • Check electrics, gas, locks, stairs, balcony rails and water heating; log the date and who checked.
  • Protect guest privacy: tell guests about any cameras at the door and never place them inside rooms.
Worked example. A host tests the alarm on 5 May, finds a dead battery, replaces it and writes the new date on the checklist. A loose stair rail is tightened and photographed before the next guest.

Activity. Complete the safety checklist for your space and attach photos of each fix.

Standard to meet. All items pass; every fix has a date; no hidden cameras anywhere in guest areas.

Module 4 The listing and honest photos 6 hCreate

Words and photos are a promise. Honest, bright and specific beats fancy.

  • Title: place type, area, one benefit. Description: the first three lines answer who, where, what is included.
  • Photos: daylight, tidy, each room, outside entrance, and the view from the bed. No editing that hides flaws.
  • List house rules kindly and clearly: quiet hours, smoking, visitors, check-in and check-out.
Worked example. Weak title: "Lovely home". Strong title: "Quiet room 8 minutes from the hospital, own bath, self check-in". The description ends with: "Street is busy until 8 pm; the room faces the garden."

Activity. Write the title, a 120-word description, a photo list of 12 shots and five house rules.

Standard to meet. A stranger can picture the stay; nothing is promised that you cannot deliver.

Module 5 Pricing and true profit 6 hEvaluate

Price is not what you hope for; it is what covers cost, fits the market and leaves a margin.

  • Costs per month: cleaning, laundry, power, water, internet, supplies, platform fee, tax, repairs, your time.
  • Break-even nights = monthly cost divided by profit per night. Compare to realistic occupancy from your 10 listings.
  • Use weekday, weekend and long-stay prices; review every two weeks.
Worked example. Costs are 18,000 INR a month; a nightly price of 2,000 INR leaves 1,300 INR profit after cleaning (an estimate; check your market). Break-even is 14 nights. The area average is 12 nights, so the host raises the long-stay discount carefully and watches for 30 days.

Activity. Build a monthly sheet with all costs, three prices and break-even nights; write the currency next to every number.

Standard to meet. Sheet totals are correct; break-even is compared with real local occupancy.

Module 6 The guest journey: before, during, after 6 hApply

Guests judge the whole journey, not only the room.

  • Before: a welcome message, directions, check-in time, rules. During: a quick check-in message and one way to reach you. After: thanks, a review request and lost-items check.
  • Self check-in must be secure: change codes every guest and never share master keys.
  • A turnover checklist: strip, wash, clean, restock, test, photo, lock.
Worked example. A guest arrives late; the host's directions include a photo of the gate and a phone number. Next morning the host sends a 2-line message: "Everything ok? Tell me by 10 am." The guest reports a slow shower; fixed that day.

Activity. Write your four standard messages and a turnover checklist.

Standard to meet. Messages are polite, under 60 words each, and name one clear action.

Module 7 Reviews, problems and fairness 5 hEvaluate

Problems will happen. Fair, calm replies protect your name and the guest.

  • Answer every review within two days: thank, state facts, say what you changed.
  • For damage or disputes, keep dated photos and messages; use the platform process; stay respectful.
  • Treat all guests equally; never refuse a guest for protected reasons such as race, faith or disability (check what your local rules protect), and never discriminate.
Worked example. A guest writes that the towels were thin. The host replies: "Thank you; we replaced the towels with new ones on 12 May." The next three reviews mention clean, soft towels.

Activity. Write replies to two real or practice reviews and a one-page problem protocol.

Standard to meet. Replies are factual, polite and show a change; the protocol names who does what within 24 hours.

Module 8 Pilot, measure and improve 6 hCreate

Run a 30-day pilot and turn data into a better stay.

  • Log nights booked, income, costs, guest scores and complaints.
  • Fix the top 3 complaints; repeat what guests praise.
  • Write a 90-day plan: price, calendar, upgrades, and one sustainability step such as saving water or helping a neighbour start hosting.
Worked example. After 30 days: 11 nights booked, average score 4.6 of 5, top complaint is hard-to-find entrance. A sign and a new photo are added; the next 5 guests find it with no call.

Activity. Present your pilot log and 90-day plan to your peer circle.

Standard to meet. Numbers are real and totals match; three fixes and one improvement plan are dated.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: rules check, listing, house manual, safety checklist, pricing sheet, 30-day guest log. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name three checks before you list a home.

Model answer: Permission (owner or landlord), building rules, and local registration, tax and safety rules. (Covered in module 1: Is hosting right for you, and are you allowed?. To pass that module: Each rule has a source (document or office) and a date; no guess is written as a fact.)

Q2. Why must cameras never be inside guest rooms?

Model answer: It breaks privacy and is unlawful in many places and always a breach of trust; check the local rule, and use only a declared door camera outside. (Covered in module 3: Safety first. To pass that module: All items pass; every fix has a date; no hidden cameras anywhere in guest areas.)

Q3. Define break-even nights.

Model answer: Monthly cost divided by profit per night: the number of nights you must book to cover your cost. (Covered in module 5: Pricing and true profit. To pass that module: Sheet totals are correct; break-even is compared with real local occupancy.)

Q4. Give two items on a safety checklist.

Model answer: Smoke alarm tested, gas or electrics checked, first-aid box, exits shown, stair rails safe. (Covered in module 3: Safety first. To pass that module: All items pass; every fix has a date; no hidden cameras anywhere in guest areas.)

Q5. What belongs in the first three lines of a listing?

Model answer: Who the stay suits, where it is, and what is included. (Covered in module 4: The listing and honest photos. To pass that module: A stranger can picture the stay; nothing is promised that you cannot deliver.)

Q6. How do you handle a poor review?

Model answer: Reply within two days with thanks, facts and what you changed. (Covered in module 7: Reviews, problems and fairness. To pass that module: Replies are factual, polite and show a change; the protocol names who does what within 24 hours.)

Q7. Why change door codes for every guest?

Model answer: It keeps the home secure and guests safe. (Covered in module 6: The guest journey: before, during, after. To pass that module: Messages are polite, under 60 words each, and name one clear action.)

Q8. Name one fair-treatment rule.

Model answer: Treat guests equally; never refuse for race, faith, disability or another protected reason. (Covered in module 7: Reviews, problems and fairness. To pass that module: Replies are factual, polite and show a change; the protocol names who does what within 24 hours.)

Q9. What do you log in a 30-day pilot?

Model answer: Nights, income, costs, guest scores and complaints. (Covered in module 8: Pilot, measure and improve. To pass that module: Numbers are real and totals match; three fixes and one improvement plan are dated.)

Q10. Why write the currency next to every number?

Model answer: Readers in other places cannot compare or check numbers without it. (Covered in module 5: Pricing and true profit. To pass that module: Sheet totals are correct; break-even is compared with real local occupancy.)

  • Property turnover manager. Cleans, restocks and inspects between guests.
  • Guest experience coordinator. Handles messages and problems.
  • Listing and photography assistant. Writes honest listings and photos.
  • Small hospitality owner. Grows from one room to several.

ten locations and global

Your place

Short-stay rules differ by city: registration, tax, night limits and building rules change. Check yours first and write the source next to each rule.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.
