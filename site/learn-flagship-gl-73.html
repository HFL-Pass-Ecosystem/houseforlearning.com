flagship course

3D Printing

Learn to design, slice, print, finish and cost 3D-printed parts safely and sell them honestly.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation27:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain printer types and materials.
  • Apply Prepare a model and slice it.
  • Analyse Fix common failures.
  • Evaluate Judge strength, safety and cost.
  • Create Print and finish a useful part.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design or select a part, print it, finish it, test it against a requirement and cost it for sale.

Condition

Access to a printer (own, makerspace or partner), a computer, free software, two peers and a mentor.

Standard

Part meets the dimensions within 0.3 mm; print log shows settings; failure analysis written; safety rules followed; cost per part shown; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Makers, students, product designers, small manufacturers. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Printers, materials and what suits what 3 hUnderstand

The printer and material decide strength, detail, cost and safety. Choose from the need of the part.

  • Filament printers melt plastic and lay it in layers; resin printers cure liquid with light and give fine detail.
  • PLA is easy and stiff but softens in heat; PETG is tougher and handles moisture; ABS resists heat but gives off fumes and needs ventilation; flexible filaments make gaskets and grips.
  • Resins need gloves, washing and curing; waste resin is hazardous.
  • Check the maker's data sheet. Do not assume a printed part is food safe or load safe.
Worked example. A shelf bracket is printed in PETG for toughness. A jewellery prototype is printed in resin for detail. A phone stand is printed in PLA because strength is not critical.

Activity. Compare four materials in a table for strength, heat, ease, cost and safety.

Standard to meet. Check that the table is accurate and cite data sheets.

Module 2 Designing parts for printing 4 hApply

A model designed for printing prints well; a model copied from a metal part may not.

  • Wall thickness of at least two nozzle widths; avoid thin spikes.
  • Avoid overhangs steeper than about 45 degrees or add supports.
  • Layers pull apart most easily, so orient the part so loads run along the layer lines, not across them.
  • Add 0.2 to 0.4 mm clearance for moving or fitted parts.
Worked example. A bracket printed on its side is much stronger than one printed upright because layer lines run along the load.

Activity. Design or choose a part, list dimensions, orientation and clearances.

Standard to meet. Justify dimensions and orientation in writing.

Module 3 Slicing and settings 4 hApply

The slicer turns the model into printer instructions.

  • Layer height (0.1 to 0.3 mm), infill (10 to 40 per cent), walls, speed, temperature and supports.
  • Preview the tool path layer by layer before printing.
  • Save profiles for each material.
  • Check the first layer closely; most failures start there.
Worked example. A part is sliced at 0.2 mm layers, 20 per cent infill and 3 walls. Print time is 2.5 hours and mass 38 g.

Activity. Slice your part and record all settings.

Standard to meet. Record settings and check that the preview shows no gaps.

Module 4 Printing safely 4 hApply

Printers are hot, moving and sometimes unattended.

  • Place on a stable surface with good ventilation; keep flammable items away.
  • Do not touch the nozzle or bed when hot; tie back hair and loose clothes.
  • Do not leave long prints unattended without safeguards such as a smoke alarm.
  • Resin: wear gloves and eye protection and dispose of waste properly.
Worked example. A smoke alarm and a fire blanket are kept near the printer; the print room has a window for airflow.

Activity. Print your part and keep a log of start, end, issues and temperatures.

Standard to meet. Log is complete and safety kit is in place.

Module 5 Troubleshooting failures 3 hAnalyse

Failures teach. Change one setting at a time and write down what happens.

  • Warping: use a heated bed, brim or enclosure.
  • Stringing: lower temperature or tune retraction.
  • Poor adhesion: clean the bed and level it.
  • Clogs: clean the nozzle and check filament moisture.
Worked example. A large flat part warps at the corners. A heated bed at 60 degrees Celsius and a brim fix it on the next print.

Activity. Fix two issues and record the cause, change and result.

Standard to meet. Note one change at a time.

Module 6 Post-processing and finishing 3 hApply

Finishing makes a part usable and professional.

  • Remove supports, sand in steps, prime and paint where needed.
  • Resin parts: wash and cure fully before handling.
  • Check fit and trim.
  • Use a mask when sanding and spraying.
Worked example. Sanding progressively from 120 to 400 grit gives a smooth surface suitable for painting.

Activity. Finish your part and photograph before and after.

Standard to meet. Check that the surface is smooth and fits.

Module 7 Testing the part 3 hEvaluate

A part is good only if it meets a requirement.

  • Measure with calipers and compare to the design.
  • Load test within safe limits; start low.
  • Test three copies to see variation.
  • Document the results.
Worked example. Three bracket copies hold 4.8, 5.1 and 5.0 kg for 10 minutes without deflection.

Activity. Test three copies and report average and spread.

Standard to meet. Record results with units.

Module 8 Costing and selling honestly 3 hCreate

Price covers material, time and risk of failures.

  • Material cost, power, machine wear, labour and failed prints.
  • Add 10 to 20 per cent for waste.
  • Disclose limits and intended use; do not sell safety-critical parts without testing.
  • Respect designs and licences; do not copy protected products.
Worked example. Cost 62 INR for material and power (an estimate; check your market) plus 40 INR labour is 102 INR; adding 10 per cent for waste gives about 112 INR. The part sells for 150 INR.

Activity. Cost one part and write a short product note with limits.

Standard to meet. Cost sheet is correct and the note states intended use.

ten locations and global

Your place

Product-safety and IP rules differ by place; do not copy protected designs.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
