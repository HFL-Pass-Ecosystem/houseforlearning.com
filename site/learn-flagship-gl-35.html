flagship course · GL-35

Online Library Building

Build a small online library: choose a collection, respect copyright, describe items so they can be found, make it accessible and measure use.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Build a small online library: choose a collection, respect copyright, describe items so they can be found, make it accessible and measure use.

  • Understand Explain collection policy, copyright and lending basics.
  • Apply Select and describe items with clear details.
  • Analyse Analyse searches and loans to improve the collection.
  • Create Create a catalogue, access rules and a user guide.
  • Evaluate Evaluate use, fairness and gaps.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Build a catalogue of at least 50 openly licensed or owned items with descriptions, access rules, a user guide and a 4-week use report.

Condition

A free catalogue or spreadsheet tool, openly licensed or owned materials, a simple web page, a mentor with library experience and a peer circle. About 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

Every item has a licence note; descriptions follow one template; accessibility checks done; no copyrighted material without permission; search finds the right item for 8 of 10 test questions.

Who it is for: Teachers, librarians, community groups, small publishers, students. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Respect copyright and licences. Protect reader privacy: do not publish who borrowed what. Check items for suitability and safety.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Purpose and users 4 hUnderstand

A library serves a defined community.

  • Who uses it and why.
  • Languages, ages, reading levels.
  • What they cannot get now.
  • Goals and limits.
  • Name your main users, such as students, local families or researchers, and write three questions each group should be able to answer using your library within two minutes.
  • Set a modest first target, such as 200 well-described items in six months, so the project stays achievable and the quality of every record can be checked.
  • Visit a small library and ask how they choose and remove items.
  • Write one sentence about who the library is for and one about who it is not for.
Worked example. A village learning group needs farm-skills guides in two languages.

Activity. Interview five users.

Standard to meet. Needs list written.

Common mistake. Collecting everything.

Second worked example. A village learning group needs farm skills in two languages. You choose 50 items: 30 English, 20 in the local language, each rated easy or practical.

Check yourself. What does your community need?

Module 2 Collection policy 6 hUnderstand

Decide what goes in and what stays out.

  • Scope, quality, balance.
  • Donations: accept or decline politely.
  • Removing outdated items.
  • Fairness and inclusion.
  • Write a collection policy listing what you accept, what you reject and who decides, and apply it to ten sample items to see whether the rules are clear.
  • Review the collection once a year and remove or replace items that are out of date, damaged, duplicated or no longer within the stated scope.
  • Keep a folder with permission emails and licence notes.
  • Check that every creator is credited the way the licence asks.
Worked example. A 2008 health guide is retired; a newer one added.

Activity. Write a one-page policy.

Standard to meet. Policy has include and exclude rules.

Common mistake. No policy for donations.

Second worked example. A 2008 health guide is retired and a 2023 guide is added; the record lists the date and the reason.

Check yourself. What is in your policy?

Module 3 Copyright and licences 8 hApply

Know what you may share.

  • Public domain, open licences, owned, permission.
  • Check and record each licence.
  • Credit creators as required.
  • Never upload what you may not.
  • Record the licence or permission for each item in a dedicated field, for example 'public domain', 'open licence' or 'permission granted on 5 May', so rights are never guessed.
  • Keep a copy of the written permission emails, and take an item down promptly if a rights holder asks, then note the date and reason in your log.
  • Use plain words in descriptions; avoid jargon.
  • Group items into no more than ten simple categories.
Worked example. An item under a share-alike licence needs a credit and the same licence for changes.

Activity. Check 20 items and record the licence.

Standard to meet. Record complete.

Common mistake. Guessing licences.

Second worked example. You check licences for 20 items: 12 public domain, 6 open licence with credit, 2 need permission. Two are held back until permission arrives.

Check yourself. How many are recorded?

Module 4 Describe items 8 hApply

Good descriptions make items findable.

  • Title, creator, date, language, level, subject, format.
  • Short summary in plain words.
  • Keywords people use.
  • Consistent template.
  • Use the same required fields for every record, such as title, creator, date, format, subject and a two-sentence description, so searching works consistently.
  • Choose a controlled list of subject terms with about 30 to 50 entries at the start, and add a new term only when no existing one fits.
  • Test the catalogue with someone who has never used it.
  • Offer the library in more than one language if users need it.
Worked example. "Seed saving for beginners, 2019, English, easy, 24 pages, keywords: seeds, garden".

Activity. Describe 50 items.

Standard to meet. All follow the template.

Common mistake. Inconsistent descriptions.

Second worked example. Description: 'Seed saving for beginners. 2019. English. Easy. 24 pages. Keywords: seeds, garden.' All 50 items follow this template.

Check yourself. Does yours match?

Module 5 Organise and search 8 hAnalyse

Help people find things.

  • Categories and tags.
  • Simple search; filters.
  • Test with real questions.
  • Fix zero-result searches.
  • Arrange items into no more than seven top-level categories, then test the structure by asking three people to find one named item and noting how long they take.
  • Add filters for format, date and subject, and check that a search for a common misspelling still returns useful results or offers a suggestion.
  • Provide a way to ask for items that are missing.
  • Keep a small budget for scanning or copying with permission.
Worked example. "tomato" finds nothing but "tomatoes" does: add both.

Activity. Run 10 test searches.

Standard to meet. 8 of 10 succeed.

Common mistake. Unhelpful search.

Second worked example. Search 'tomato' returns nothing but 'tomatoes' does; adding both words and a tag for 'vegetables' fixes it. 8 of 10 test searches now succeed.

Check yourself. Which searches failed?

Module 6 Access for all 10 hCreate

Make it usable by everyone.

  • Readable fonts, contrast, alt text.
  • Low-data options.
  • Offline copies where allowed.
  • Translations or summaries.
  • Provide descriptions for images and transcripts for audio, and check that headings, text size and colour contrast work for people using screen readers or small screens.
  • Test the library on a basic phone with a slow connection, and keep file sizes small enough that a typical page opens in a few seconds.
  • Protect any user accounts with strong sign-in.
  • Do not use any item you are unsure about.
Worked example. A text-only page loads in 2 seconds on a slow phone.

Activity. Run an access checklist.

Standard to meet. Fixes made.

Common mistake. Small, grey text.

Second worked example. A text-only page loads in 2 seconds on a slow phone; images have alt text and the font is large with strong contrast.

Check yourself. What did you fix?

Module 7 Lending and privacy 6 hEvaluate

Short loans and privacy.

  • Loan period; return; reminders.
  • Do not share who borrowed what.
  • Keep minimum data.
  • Delete after the loan.
  • Collect only the personal data you need for lending, such as a name and email, store it securely, and delete borrowing records after a stated period like 90 days.
  • Write a plain-language privacy notice and loan rules stating loan length, renewal limits and what happens when an item is late, and show them at sign-up.
  • Review use every month and note rising topics.
  • Archive items rather than deleting when allowed.
Worked example. Loan records are removed 30 days after return.

Activity. Write loan and privacy rules.

Standard to meet. Rules fit in one page.

Common mistake. Tracking borrowers.

Second worked example. Loans last 7 days; reminders go on day 5; records are removed 30 days after return. No list of what each person borrowed is kept.

Check yourself. What do you store?

Module 8 Use, improve, grow 4 hCreate

Measure and learn.

  • Visits, searches, loans, requests.
  • Ask what is missing.
  • Add items steadily.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Track simple measures each month, such as searches, items opened, searches with no result and requests for new items, and use them to decide what to add next.
  • Invite five regular users to a short feedback call every quarter, and publish a brief list of what you changed because of their suggestions.
  • Write a short guide on how to use the library in three steps.
  • Share lessons with other community libraries.
Worked example. 120 visits, 60 loans, 14 requests: add three requested topics.

Activity. Write the use report.

Standard to meet. Report names three changes.

Common mistake. Ignoring requests.

Second worked example. 120 visits, 60 loans and 14 requests in four weeks lead to three requested topics being added.

Check yourself. What was requested?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. The uncertain licence

You cannot find a licence for a popular guide.

Question: What do you do?

Model answer: Do not publish; ask the creator or replace it.

Case 2. The zero results

A common search returns nothing.

Question: Fix?

Model answer: Add synonyms and tags, then retest.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is a collection policy?

Model answer: Rules for what to include and remove.

Q2. Name licence types.

Model answer: Public domain, open licence, owned and by permission.

Q3. Why record licences?

Model answer: To prove you may share the item.

Q4. What goes in a description?

Model answer: Title, creator, date, language, level, subject and format.

Q5. Why test searches?

Model answer: To find items users cannot find.

Q6. Why protect borrower privacy?

Model answer: Reading choices are personal.

Q7. What is alt text?

Model answer: A text description of an image.

Q8. What to do with a donation you cannot use?

Model answer: Decline politely.

Q9. How check success?

Model answer: Visits, searches and loans.

Q10. Why retire items?

Model answer: Outdated items can mislead.

ten locations and global

Your place

Copyright terms and lending rules differ by place; check before you share digital copies. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a Brainport-area small firm.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Partner to practise with: SEBRAE small-business support, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or a SkillsFuture-linked training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a TAFE or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico (Central America)

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
