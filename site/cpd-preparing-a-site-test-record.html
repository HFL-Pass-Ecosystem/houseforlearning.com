CPD · Engineering

Preparing a site test record

Recording tests clearly so a third party can accept them.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 3 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playEngineering

Recording tests clearly so a third party can accept them.

  • List tests required, given a scope, in the order of work.
  • Record results, given sample readings, with unit, instrument and date.
  • Compare results to limits, given a table, marking pass or fail correctly.
  • Sign off, given a checklist, only when every line is complete.

Task, condition and standard

Task

List the tests required for a scope, record readings on a test sheet with unit, instrument and date, judge each against its limit, and sign off only a complete sheet

Condition

Given a scope of work, a blank test sheet, readings and limits for six circuits, and a sample instrument record, in a 3-hour practical session

Standard

Fill every field of the sheet, mark every pass or fail correctly including a reading just under the limit, and sign off only when all six lines are complete and the instrument details are recorded

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Which tests and why 20 min
  • List tests in the order of work.
  • Give a reason for each test.
  • Scope tells you which tests apply.
Worked example. Scope: six new final circuits. Sample order (your standard and procedure set the real list): 1 continuity, 2 insulation resistance, 3 polarity, 4 earth loop impedance, 5 trip test. Reason for 2: to show the cable insulation is not damaged. 6 circuits x 5 tests = 30 results.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "which tests and why": list tests required, given a scope, in the order of work.

Standard to meet. List the tests required for a given scope, in the order of work, and state the reason for each test.

Module 2 Filling the test sheet 30 min
  • One line per circuit and test.
  • Write the unit and the instrument serial every time.
  • Blank is not allowed: write not applicable with a reason.
Worked example. Line 1: circuit C1 | lighting | insulation resistance | 120 MOhm | sample limit at least 1.0 MOhm | pass | tester T1 | 2026-03-14 | instrument T-1042, calibrated 2026-01-10.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "filling the test sheet": record results, given sample readings, with unit, instrument and date.

Standard to meet. Fill in the test sheet, given sample readings, completing every field with unit, instrument serial and calibration date, and writing not applicable with a reason where a field does not apply.

Module 3 Pass or fail against limits 20 min
  • Compare the reading with the limit as written.
  • At least 1.0 means 1.0 passes.
  • Record every fail; never hide it.
Worked example. Sample limit on the sheet: at least 1.0 MOhm. Readings 120 MOhm pass; 1.0 MOhm pass; 0.96 MOhm fail by 0.04 MOhm. The failed line is marked fail, flagged to the responsible person and kept on the sheet.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "pass or fail against limits": compare results to limits, given a table, marking pass or fail correctly.

Standard to meet. Compare each reading with the given limit and mark pass or fail correctly, including a reading just under the limit, and record any failure without hiding it.

Module 4 Signing and storing 15 min
  • Sign only when every line is complete.
  • The tester signs; the checker signs where required.
  • File the sheet as the project and rules require.
Worked example. Count before signing: 6 lines, 5 complete, 1 missing the instrument serial. Do not sign. Complete the serial, recount 6 of 6, then sign and file one copy with the project record.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "signing and storing": sign off, given a checklist, only when every line is complete.

Standard to meet. Sign off the sheet only when every line is complete, with the tester and checker named, and file the record as the project and rules require.

Module 5 Practice 10 min
  • Work through all six circuits in order.
  • Judge each line as you write it.
  • Check for blanks before you hand in.
Worked example. Six circuits, sample limit at least 1.0 MOhm: C1 120, C2 85, C3 0.96, C4 50, C5 200, C6 1.2 MOhm. Expected result: 5 pass, 1 fail (C3).

Practise: Run one full practice round for "preparing a site test record" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (list tests required; record results; compare results to limits; sign off).

Standard to meet. Complete a sheet for six circuits, given readings and limits, with every field filled and every pass or fail judged correctly.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Check the sheet like a reviewer.
  • Look for blanks, missing instrument details and wrong judgements.
  • Report each problem with its line number.
Worked example. A finished sheet: line 2 reads 0.85 MOhm against a limit of at least 1.0 but is marked pass; line 5 has no instrument serial. Two problems found: a wrong judgement and a blank field.

Practise: Take the end check for "preparing a site test record": show that you can sign off, given a checklist, only when every line is complete, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Show that a finished sheet is complete, correct and ready for sign-off, finding any blank field, missing instrument detail or wrong judgement.

Module 7 Second worked case: a reading just under the limit 40 min
  • Do not round a reading up to pass.
  • Record the fail, flag it and tell the responsible person.
  • Keep the first reading when you retest.
Worked example. Case card 2: insulation reading 0.98 MOhm against a sample limit of at least 1.0 MOhm is a fail by 0.02 MOhm. After the fix the retest reads 2.3 MOhm, a pass. Both lines stay on the record with dates.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second worked case: a reading just under the limit": sign off, given a checklist, only when every line is complete. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Judge a reading just under its limit as a fail, record and flag it, and add a dated retest line without deleting the first reading.

Module 8 Teach-back and peer check 40 min
  • Swap finished sheets with a partner.
  • Check each line for unit, instrument and judgement.
  • Give one strength and one correction in plain words.
Worked example. Pairs of 2: each partner checks a six-line sheet in 10 minutes. Target: all 6 lines checked, every planted error named by line number, and a clear sign or do-not-sign decision.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "teach-back and peer check": sign off, given a checklist, only when every line is complete. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate a partner's six-line test sheet line by line, name each blank or wrong judgement, and decide whether the sheet is ready for sign-off.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Why record calibration?

Model answer: Readings are only valid with a calibrated instrument. (Covered in module 2: Filling the test sheet. To pass that module: Fill in the test sheet, given sample readings, completing every field with unit, instrument serial and calibration date, and writing not applicable with a reason where a field does not apply.)

Blank fields allowed?

Model answer: No; write "N/A" with a reason. (Covered in module 2: Filling the test sheet. To pass that module: Fill in the test sheet, given sample readings, completing every field with unit, instrument serial and calibration date, and writing not applicable with a reason where a field does not apply.)

What if a reading fails?

Model answer: Mark fail, tell the responsible person, retest after fixing. (Covered in module 3: Pass or fail against limits. To pass that module: Compare each reading with the given limit and mark pass or fail correctly, including a reading just under the limit, and record any failure without hiding it.)

Who signs?

Model answer: The tester and, where needed, the reviewer. (Covered in module 4: Signing and storing. To pass that module: Sign off the sheet only when every line is complete, with the tester and checker named, and file the record as the project and rules require.)

Can a field stay blank?

Model answer: No; write "not applicable" with reason. (Covered in module 2: Filling the test sheet. To pass that module: Fill in the test sheet, given sample readings, completing every field with unit, instrument serial and calibration date, and writing not applicable with a reason where a field does not apply.)

What if a reading fails?

Model answer: Record it, flag it, do not hide it. (Covered in module 3: Pass or fail against limits. To pass that module: Compare each reading with the given limit and mark pass or fail correctly, including a reading just under the limit, and record any failure without hiding it.)

Who signs?

Model answer: The tester and the checker. (Covered in module 4: Signing and storing. To pass that module: Sign off the sheet only when every line is complete, with the tester and checker named, and file the record as the project and rules require.)

Why record the instrument?

Model answer: Calibration and traceability. (Covered in module 2: Filling the test sheet. To pass that module: Fill in the test sheet, given sample readings, completing every field with unit, instrument serial and calibration date, and writing not applicable with a reason where a field does not apply.)

How long are records kept?

Model answer: Per project and legal rule. (Covered in module 4: Signing and storing. To pass that module: Sign off the sheet only when every line is complete, with the tester and checker named, and file the record as the project and rules require.)

Circuit 4 failed at 0.98 MOhm and reads 2.3 MOhm after repair. What happens to the first reading?

Model answer: It stays on the record as a fail; add a dated retest line marked pass. (Covered in module 7: Second worked case: a reading just under the limit. To pass that module: Judge a reading just under its limit as a fail, record and flag it, and add a dated retest line without deleting the first reading.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: Why we test. Speaker note: Tests show that an installation is safe before it is used. At this first step the learner can say what a test reading is, what a limit is, and why a record must exist for each circuit.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Instruments and records. Speaker note: The learner knows that readings only count when the instrument is calibrated and recorded. They can name the main fields on a test sheet: circuit, test, reading, limit, result, tester, date and instrument.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Read and compare. Speaker note: The learner can read a value with its unit and compare it with a given limit, with help. They follow the worked example for one circuit and see why a blank field is never allowed.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Complete and sign a sheet. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner lists the tests for a scope, fills a six-circuit sheet, judges each pass or fail, records failures openly and signs only when every line is complete.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Awkward results. Speaker note: The specialist handles readings close to a limit, retests after repair, missing instrument records and results from different testers on one sheet. They keep the full history instead of overwriting earlier lines.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Review for acceptance. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner reviews other testers' sheets for blanks, wrong judgements and missing calibration dates, and prepares the record so a third party can accept it without questions.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Design the record system. Speaker note: The super expert designs test sheet templates, sets retention rules and audits records across projects. They train testers in traceability and decide when a pattern of fails needs a wider investigation.
