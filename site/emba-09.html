executive MBA subject 09

Marketing

Year 1, term C. 3.5 ECTS, about 88 hours, 4 parts. Part 1 is free.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task

Write a marketing plan for one product in one market.

Condition

Given customer data, competitor information and a budget.

Standard

Segments, target, positioning, mix and measures are consistent and the budget adds up.

Who it is for: Managers who must win and keep customers.

how this subject works

Free entry, voice and your learning style

Part 1 is free to try. Read it, listen to it and answer it before you decide. Join the programme, or this subject on its own, to open the other parts.

  • Listen. Every part has a Listen button that reads it aloud.
  • Speak or type. Answer each part by talking (press Speak) or by typing. Your answers stay on this device only.
  • Learn your way. Every part has a See, Hear, Do and Read-and-write route. Not sure which suits you? Take the eight-question learning-style check.

4 parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 Developing marketing plans 22 hApply: plan

A marketing plan answers: where are we, where do we want to be, how do we get there, and how will we know.

  • Situation: customers, competitors, company
  • Objectives: specific and measurable
  • Actions, budget and measures

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Goal: grow sales from 2.0m to 2.4m, a 20% rise. Average order 400, so 1,000 more orders are needed.

Activity: Write SMART objectives for a product and turn one into a number of orders.

Standard: Objectives are specific and measurable and the order count is correct.

Part 2 Segmentation, targeting, positioning and customer behaviour 22 hAnalyse: choose

Not every customer is worth chasing. Split the market, choose the segments you can serve best, and give them a clear reason to choose you.

  • Segment by needs and behaviour, not only by age
  • Target where you can win and earn
  • Positioning: for whom, what benefit, why believe

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Segment A: 5,000 buyers, 40% reachable, margin 50 each. Segment B: 20,000 buyers, 10% reachable, margin 30. A gives 5,000 x 0.4 x 50 = 100,000; B gives 20,000 x 0.1 x 30 = 60,000. Target A.

Activity: Score three segments by size, reach and margin and choose one.

Standard: Scores correct and the choice follows from them.

Part 3 Marketing mix and brand strategy 22 hApply: design

Product, price, place and promotion must fit the target and the positioning. A brand is the promise that ties them together.

  • Price fits value and positioning
  • Place is where the target buys
  • Promotion says the same promise everywhere

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A premium positioning with a discount price confuses buyers. Raising price from 80 to 95 and adding a guarantee keeps the premium promise; sales fall 5% but margin per unit rises from 20 to 35.

Activity: Design the four parts of the mix for your chosen segment.

Standard: Each part fits the positioning and one trade-off is explained.

Part 4 Managing the customer and digital marketing 22 hEvaluate: measure

Keeping customers usually costs less than finding new ones. Digital channels let you measure cost per customer and lifetime value.

  • Customer acquisition cost = spend / new customers
  • Lifetime value = margin per year x years kept
  • Grow where lifetime value is well above acquisition cost

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Spend 10,000 to win 200 customers: 50 each. Each brings 30 margin a year and stays 4 years: lifetime value 120. 120 is well above 50, so keep investing.

Activity: Calculate acquisition cost and lifetime value for two channels.

Standard: Both figures correct and the better channel chosen.

ten checks

Check yourself

Say or write your answer first, then read the model answer.

  1. Q1. Name the four questions a plan answers.
    Model answer: Where are we, where to go, how to get there, how will we know. (Covered in part 1: Developing marketing plans.)
  2. Q2. Grow 2.0m to 2.4m with 400 per order. Extra orders?
    Model answer: 1,000. (Covered in part 1: Developing marketing plans.)
  3. Q3. Why not segment only by age?
    Model answer: Needs and behaviour predict buying better. (Covered in part 2: Segmentation, targeting, positioning and customer behaviour.)
  4. Q4. 5,000 buyers, 40% reach, margin 50. Value?
    Model answer: 100,000. (Covered in part 2: Segmentation, targeting, positioning and customer behaviour.)
  5. Q5. Three parts of a positioning statement?
    Model answer: For whom, what benefit, why believe. (Covered in part 2: Segmentation, targeting, positioning and customer behaviour.)
  6. Q6. Name the four parts of the mix.
    Model answer: Product, price, place, promotion. (Covered in part 3: Marketing mix and brand strategy.)
  7. Q7. Why can a low price hurt a premium brand?
    Model answer: It contradicts the promise and confuses buyers. (Covered in part 3: Marketing mix and brand strategy.)
  8. Q8. 10,000 spent for 200 customers. Acquisition cost?
    Model answer: 50. (Covered in part 4: Managing the customer and digital marketing.)
  9. Q9. 30 margin a year for 4 years. Lifetime value?
    Model answer: 120. (Covered in part 4: Managing the customer and digital marketing.)
  10. Q10. When should you keep investing in a channel?
    Model answer: When lifetime value is well above acquisition cost. (Covered in part 4: Managing the customer and digital marketing.)
