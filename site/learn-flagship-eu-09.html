Construction and Built Environment

Prepare a bill of quantities, a cost estimate and a safety plan for one small real or model project.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 142 hLearning Course, 3-6 months
  • Understand Explain how a drawing, a bill of quantities and a safety plan fit together.
  • Analyse Break the project into elements, quantities and hazards.
  • Apply Measure quantities from the drawing and price them with stated rates.
  • Evaluate Judge the estimate against its tolerance, contingency and exclusions.
  • Create Produce a bill of quantities, estimate and safety plan, with a 90-day plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a bill of quantities, a cost estimate and a safety plan for one small real or model project.

Condition

One small real or model project with a drawing, a mentor who knows site work, free tools, currency written on every number. This course teaches process and safety awareness only; building codes and the local authority set the rules.

Standard

A mentor reviews the pack; quantities are within 5% of a second person's count; the safety plan names the hazards of this project; at least 70% on the check; two peer reviews given.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Find the client and the job 6 hUnderstand

Small clients want a clear price, a safe site and a job finished on time.

  • Observe small jobs from the public footpath only; never enter a site without permission.
  • Note materials, price per square metre, time taken and safety measures.
  • Talk to three builders or owners.
  • Choose a small project: a 3 m × 4 m room extension or a boundary wall.
Worked example. Local price for a simple 12 m² room extension: EUR 600 to 900 per m².

Activity. Write a one-page brief with scope and assumptions.

Standard to meet. Scope is clear and has exclusions.

Module 2 Site safety 6 hAnalyse

The main causes of harm are falls, falling objects, digging, electricity, lifting, dust and noise.

  • Plan controls for each hazard.
  • Use protective equipment; hold a short talk before work; stop work if unsafe.
  • Keep walkways clear and tools stored.
  • You only plan and observe; people work on site only with proper training and permission.
Worked example. Falls: edge protection and safe access. Digging: mark services and keep edges stable. Lifting: use correct equipment and a team lift for heavy loads (check the local weight limit).

Activity. Write a task-by-task safety plan for your project.

Standard to meet. Each task has hazards and controls.

Module 3 Read the drawing 35 hApply

A drawing tells what to build, where and with what.

  • Scale, plan, section, elevation, symbols and notes.
  • Check dimensions add up.
  • List work items: excavation, foundation, walls, roof, finishes.
  • Raise queries when the drawing is unclear.
Worked example. Plan 3 m × 4 m. Wall height 2.7 m. Door 0.9 × 2.1, window 1.2 × 1.2.

Activity. Read your drawing and write a list of work items.

Standard to meet. List matches the drawing.

Module 4 Measure quantities 10 hApply

Quantity = length × width × height for volumes, length × width for areas.

  • Excavation volume, concrete volume, wall area less openings, plaster area.
  • Add waste: 5 to 10% for many materials.
  • Use consistent units.
  • Check your totals twice.
Worked example. Wall area: perimeter 14 m × 2.7 m = 37.8 m²; less door 1.89 and window 1.44 = 34.47 m².

Activity. Build a bill of quantities table.

Standard to meet. Within 5% of a peer's result.

Module 5 Cost estimating 35 hAnalyse

Cost = quantity × rate. Add labour, equipment, delivery, overhead and contingency.

  • Use local rates and note the source and date.
  • Contingency 5 to 10% for unknowns.
  • State what is not included.
  • Compare with local price per m².
Worked example. Wall plaster 34.47 m² × EUR 6 = EUR 206.8; masonry 34.47 × EUR 25 = EUR 861.8; total of all items EUR 9,420; overhead 10% = EUR 942; contingency 5% = EUR 471; total EUR 10,833.

Activity. Price the bill of quantities.

Standard to meet. Every line has a rate and currency; exclusions listed.

Module 6 Quality and programme 14 hEvaluate

Quality means built to the drawing and standard.

  • Check list: level, plumb, dimensions, finish.
  • Tolerances: write the allowed error.
  • Programme: order of work and days.
  • Record problems and fixes.
Worked example. Wall plumb tolerance ±5 mm over 2 m; measured by spirit level.

Activity. Write a checklist and a simple programme in days.

Standard to meet. Checklist has tolerances; programme is realistic.

Module 7 Cost control and handover 28 hCreate

Keep cost within budget.

  • Compare actual to estimate weekly.
  • Variation: record changes and cost.
  • Handover pack: drawings, certificates, guarantees.
  • Defects list.
Worked example. Week 3: spent EUR 3,200 vs plan EUR 3,000: investigate the 6.7% overspend.

Activity. Make a cost tracking sheet.

Standard to meet. Sheet shows plan vs actual.

Module 8 Review room and next step 8 hCreate

Present and check.

  • Show drawing, BOQ, cost, safety, quality.
  • Peer feedback.
  • Take the check.
  • Next: Industrial or Agriculture.
Worked example. Score sheet, each line marked 0 to 5 (3 or more meets the standard): drawing reading 4, quantities 4, cost 5, safety plan 3, quality 4. Total 20 of 25 = 80%, above the 70% line. Safety plan at 3 is the weakest line, so it gets a task-specific hazard list next.

Activity. Present for 8 minutes, then answer: which quantity was hardest to measure, how much contingency you kept and why, and which hazard worries you most.

Standard to meet. All rubric lines at 3 or more; the weakest line has a dated fix.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Main causes of site harm?

Model answer: Falls, falling objects, digging, electricity, lifting, dust and noise. (Covered in module 2: Site safety. To pass that module: Each task has hazards and controls.)

Q2. Wall area 14 m × 2.7 m minus 3.33 m² openings?

Model answer: 34.47 m². (Covered in module 4: Measure quantities. To pass that module: Within 5% of a peer's result.)

Q3. Why add a contingency?

Model answer: For unknowns and price changes. (Covered in module 5: Cost estimating. To pass that module: Every line has a rate and currency; exclusions listed.)

Q4. What is a tolerance?

Model answer: The allowed error in a measurement. (Covered in module 6: Quality and programme. To pass that module: Checklist has tolerances; programme is realistic.)

Q5. Name three drawing types.

Model answer: Plan, section and elevation. (Covered in module 3: Read the drawing. To pass that module: List matches the drawing.)

Q6. Why state exclusions?

Model answer: So the client knows what is not in the price. (Covered in module 5: Cost estimating. To pass that module: Every line has a rate and currency; exclusions listed.)

Q7. What is waste allowance?

Model answer: Extra material for cuts and loss, often 5 to 10%. (Covered in module 4: Measure quantities. To pass that module: Within 5% of a peer's result.)

Q8. What if the drawing is unclear?

Model answer: Raise a query before pricing or building. (Covered in module 3: Read the drawing. To pass that module: List matches the drawing.)

Q9. Do you work on site in this course?

Model answer: No: only plan and observe; work needs training and permission. (Covered in module 2: Site safety. To pass that module: Each task has hazards and controls.)

Q10. What should the safety talk cover?

Model answer: Today's tasks, hazards and controls. (Covered in module 2: Site safety. To pass that module: Each task has hazards and controls.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Use local units, rules and authorities.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Business set-up: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Product and food rules: FSSAI.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Business set-up: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Product and food rules: NVWA.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Business set-up: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Product and food rules: the municipality food-control team (for example Dubai Municipality).

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Business set-up: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Product and food rules: ANVISA and MAPA.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Business set-up: federal or Ontario business registration. Personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Product and food rules: the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA).

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Business set-up: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Product and food rules: the FDA and the state health department.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Business set-up: registration with ACRA. Personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Product and food rules: the Singapore Food Agency (SFA).

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Business set-up: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Product and food rules: Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) and the state health authority.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Business set-up: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Product and food rules: the Centre for Food Safety.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Business set-up: local business registration in the country where you work. Personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Product and food rules: the national food-safety authority.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Business set-up: a trade and business licence or company registration. Personal data: the Data Protection Act. Product and food rules: the public health authority.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Business set-up: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Product and food rules: the federal health-risk authority.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
