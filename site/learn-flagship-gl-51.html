flagship course

Senior At-Home Care

Plan and deliver safe, respectful at-home support for older adults: daily living, safety, medicines reminders, dignity and when to call for help.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner33:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain the needs, rights and dignity of an older person at home.
  • Apply Assist with daily routines safely.
  • Analyse Find fall, fire and medicine risks in a home.
  • Evaluate Judge when a situation needs a professional or emergency help.
  • Create Build a written care routine and a family communication plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a person-centred home care plan for a practice case, with a home safety check, daily routine, emergency card and carer wellbeing plan.

Condition

A practice case or a consenting family, a phone, forms, two peers and a qualified nurse or care mentor. Nursing procedures are outside scope.

Standard

Plan respects the person's wishes; safety check finds and fixes at least 8 risks; emergency card is complete; no clinical task is done outside training; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Family carers, home-care assistants, community volunteers, care entrepreneurs. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Respect and dignity first 4 hUnderstand

Good care starts with the person, not the task. Older adults have a lifetime of skills, habits and choices that care must protect.

  • Ask how the person likes to be addressed and what matters to them.
  • Offer choices; do not decide for them when they can decide.
  • Protect privacy in washing, dressing and toilet care.
  • Speak slowly and clearly, face to face, without talking over them.
Worked example. Mrs A prefers tea before washing. The carer keeps this order and tells her each step. Her morning takes ten minutes longer and she is calmer and more willing to help.

Activity. Write a one-page "about me" profile for a practice case.

Standard to meet. Profile has routines, likes, fears and decisions the person makes alone.

Module 2 Home safety check 5 hAnalyse

Most harm to older people at home is preventable. A structured check finds risks before an accident.

  • Falls: rugs, loose cables, wet floors, poor light, steps without rails.
  • Bathroom: non-slip mat, grab rails, raised seat.
  • Fire and gas: working smoke alarm, safe cooking habits, switch-off reminders.
  • Access: keys for helpers, clear path for emergency crews, phone within reach.
Worked example. A check finds a loose rug, a dark stair and no bathroom rail. The rug is removed, a night light is fitted and a rail is ordered, with dates and who is responsible.

Activity. Complete a room-by-room safety checklist and a fix list.

Standard to meet. Every room is checked, each risk has a fix, a date and an owner.

Module 3 Daily living support 5 hApply

Support independence rather than replacing it.

  • Break tasks into steps and help with the step that is hard.
  • Offer enough time; rushing causes accidents and loss of confidence.
  • Use aids: long-handled tools, easy-grip cutlery, pill boxes.
  • Watch eating, drinking, skin and sleep and record changes.
Worked example. Mr B used to skip lunch. A simple routine of a set time, a favourite dish and a drink by the chair raises his daily intake and he regains weight in a month.

Activity. Design a daily routine for a practice case.

Standard to meet. Routine covers meals, drinks, washing, movement, rest and social time with timings.

Module 4 Medicines and health reminders 4 hApply

Carers remind and observe; clinicians decide.

  • Use a weekly pill box and a written list from the doctor or pharmacist.
  • Never change doses or stop medicines on your own.
  • Note side effects such as dizziness, confusion or rash and report them.
  • Keep medicines locked away from others in the house.
Worked example. The carer notices a new sleepiness after a medicine change, records the time and calls the clinic, which adjusts the dose.

Activity. Create a medicine reminder chart and a reporting note.

Standard to meet. Chart shows names, times and checks; note shows what, when and who was told.

Module 5 Mind, mood and memory 4 hApply

Loneliness, low mood and forgetfulness are common but not inevitable.

  • Keep routine, light, daily walks and social contact.
  • Use calendars, clear labels and a daily board for memory.
  • Respond calmly to repeated questions; do not argue.
  • Seek advice if mood or memory changes quickly.
Worked example. A weekly phone call from a grandchild and a neighbour tea visit reduce low mood; a clock with the day and date reduces repeated questions.

Activity. Plan a week of social and mental activity.

Standard to meet. Plan has at least five activities, one with another person each day.

Module 6 Emergencies and when to call 4 hEvaluate

Know the line between watching and acting.

  • Call at once for chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke signs, serious bleeding or a head injury.
  • Call the clinic the same day for fever, new confusion or a bad fall without injury.
  • Keep numbers, medical history and medicine list on a card by the phone.
  • Practise the plan with the family.
Worked example. After a fall with no visible injury, the carer notes new confusion, which is a warning sign, and calls for help instead of waiting.

Activity. Write the one-page emergency card.

Standard to meet. Card has numbers, conditions, medicines, allergies and clear call-now signs.

Module 7 Family and carer wellbeing 3 hEvaluate

Carers burn out when they carry everything alone.

  • Share tasks and agree a rota.
  • Take breaks, sleep and eat properly; ask for respite.
  • Join a carer group or call a helpline.
  • Watch for stress signs such as anger, tiredness and withdrawal.
Worked example. A daughter who was caring alone adds two relatives to a rota. She gets two free evenings a week and her stress falls.

Activity. Make a carer wellbeing plan with a weekly rota.

Standard to meet. Plan has shared tasks, breaks and a named support contact.

Module 8 Review and improve 4 hCreate

Care plans change as needs change.

  • Review monthly or after any health change.
  • Ask the person and the family what is working.
  • Record incidents and learn from them.
  • Update the plan and tell everyone involved.
Worked example. After two near-falls the review adds a walking aid and physiotherapy advice, and the next month shows no falls.

Activity. Produce the full care plan pack with a review date.

Standard to meet. All sections are present, personal details are respected, and the next review is scheduled.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: care plan, home safety checklist, routine, emergency card, carer plan. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why use their words?

Model answer: It keeps dignity and choice. (Covered in module 1: Respect and dignity first. To pass that module: Profile has routines, likes, fears and decisions the person makes alone.)

Q2. Name three fall risks.

Model answer: Loose rugs, poor light, wet floors. (Covered in module 2: Home safety check. To pass that module: Every room is checked, each risk has a fix, a date and an owner.)

Q3. Who decides doses?

Model answer: The prescriber. (Covered in module 4: Medicines and health reminders. To pass that module: Chart shows names, times and checks; note shows what, when and who was told.)

Q4. When call emergency?

Model answer: Chest pain, stroke signs, serious fall, breathing trouble. (Covered in module 6: Emergencies and when to call. To pass that module: Card has numbers, conditions, medicines, allergies and clear call-now signs.)

Q5. What is "do with, not for"?

Model answer: Support independence by helping only as needed. (Covered in module 3: Daily living support. To pass that module: Routine covers meals, drinks, washing, movement, rest and social time with timings.)

Q6. Name one mood tip.

Model answer: Daily activity and social contact. (Covered in module 5: Mind, mood and memory. To pass that module: Plan has at least five activities, one with another person each day.)

Q7. What is sudden confusion a sign of?

Model answer: Possible illness; seek help. (Covered in module 6: Emergencies and when to call. To pass that module: Card has numbers, conditions, medicines, allergies and clear call-now signs.)

Q8. What goes on an emergency card?

Model answer: Numbers, allergies, medicines, contacts. (Covered in module 6: Emergencies and when to call. To pass that module: Card has numbers, conditions, medicines, allergies and clear call-now signs.)

Q9. Why carer breaks?

Model answer: To avoid burnout. (Covered in module 7: Family and carer wellbeing. To pass that module: Plan has shared tasks, breaks and a named support contact.)

Q10. How often review?

Model answer: Monthly or after any event. (Covered in module 8: Review and improve. To pass that module: All sections are present, personal details are respected, and the next review is scheduled.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Care rules, licences and background checks differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.
