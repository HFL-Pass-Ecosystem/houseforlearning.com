flagship course

Senior At-Home Care

Plan and deliver safe, respectful at-home support for older adults: daily living, safety, medicines reminders, dignity and when to call for help.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner33:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain the needs, rights and dignity of an older person at home.
  • Apply Assist with daily routines safely.
  • Analyse Find fall, fire and medicine risks in a home.
  • Evaluate Judge when a situation needs a professional or emergency help.
  • Create Build a written care routine and a family communication plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a person-centred home care plan for a practice case, with a home safety check, daily routine, emergency card and carer wellbeing plan.

Condition

A practice case or a consenting family, a phone, forms, two peers and a qualified nurse or care mentor. Nursing procedures are outside scope.

Standard

Plan respects the person's wishes; safety check finds and fixes at least 8 risks; emergency card is complete; no clinical task is done outside training; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Family carers, home-care assistants, community volunteers, care entrepreneurs. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Respect and dignity 4 hUnderstand

Care starts with the person, not the task.

  • Ask what they want; offer choices; use their name.
  • Protect privacy in washing, dressing and toilet care.
  • Never treat an adult like a child.
Worked example. Mr Rao prefers tea at 7; the carer plans around it and keeps the bathroom door closed.

Activity. Write a "what matters to me" page with the person.

Standard to meet. Page uses their words.

Module 2 Home safety check 5 hAnalyse

Most harm at home is preventable.

  • Falls: loose rugs, poor light, wet floors, no rails.
  • Fire and electrical hazards; gas safety.
  • Easy-to-reach items and clear paths.
Worked example. A carer removes a loose mat and fits a night light, ending night-time stumbles.

Activity. Complete a checklist for one home.

Standard to meet. At least 8 risks found, each with a fix and date.

Module 3 Daily living support 5 hApply

Support independence, not dependence.

  • Meals, drinks and nutrition; assist with mobility; hygiene.
  • Do with, not for, whenever possible.
  • Safe lifting: ask for training; use aids.
Worked example. The carer sets out clothes in order and lets Mrs Iyer dress herself.

Activity. Plan a day routine with times.

Standard to meet. Routine covers meals, fluids, movement, rest and social time.

Module 4 Medicines and health reminders 4 hApply

Carers remind; clinicians decide.

  • Use a pill organiser prepared by a pharmacist or nurse.
  • Record what was taken; never change doses.
  • Report new symptoms promptly.
Worked example. A weekly box with morning and evening slots reduces missed doses.

Activity. Create a reminder chart and record sheet.

Standard to meet. Chart matches the prescriber's list.

Module 5 Mind, mood and memory 4 hApply

Loneliness and forgetfulness are common.

  • Keep routine; use calendars and clocks; encourage activity.
  • Be patient; do not argue about facts when kind redirection works.
  • Watch for sudden confusion, which can mean illness.
Worked example. A daily 15-minute walk and a call to a grandchild lifts mood.

Activity. Plan a weekly social schedule.

Standard to meet. Includes five activities.

Module 6 Emergencies and when to call 4 hEvaluate

Know the line between watch and act.

  • Call emergency services for chest pain, stroke signs, serious fall, breathing trouble.
  • Call the doctor for fever, new pain, poor eating.
  • Keep an emergency card at the door.
Worked example. Face drooping and slurred speech: call emergency now, note the time.

Activity. Make an emergency card.

Standard to meet. Includes numbers, allergies, medicines, contacts.

Module 7 Family and carer wellbeing 3 hEvaluate

Carers burn out.

  • Share the load; take breaks; ask for respite.
  • Keep a shared log or group message.
  • Seek local support groups.
Worked example. Siblings take alternate weekends; stress drops.

Activity. Write a carer plan.

Standard to meet. Plan names who, when, what.

Module 8 Review and improve 4 hCreate

Care plans change as needs change.

  • Review monthly or after any event.
  • Track weight, sleep, mood, falls.
  • Celebrate good days.
Worked example. After a fall, a rail and a physiotherapy visit are added.

Activity. Present your plan review.

Standard to meet. Changes are dated and agreed with the person.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: care plan, home safety checklist, routine, emergency card, carer plan. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why use their words?

Model answer: It keeps dignity and choice.

Q2. Name three fall risks.

Model answer: Loose rugs, poor light, wet floors.

Q3. Who decides doses?

Model answer: The prescriber.

Q4. When call emergency?

Model answer: Chest pain, stroke signs, serious fall, breathing trouble.

Q5. What is "do with, not for"?

Model answer: Support independence by helping only as needed.

Q6. Name one mood tip.

Model answer: Daily activity and social contact.

Q7. What is sudden confusion a sign of?

Model answer: Possible illness; seek help.

Q8. What goes on an emergency card?

Model answer: Numbers, allergies, medicines, contacts.

Q9. Why carer breaks?

Model answer: To avoid burnout.

Q10. How often review?

Model answer: Monthly or after any event.

ten locations and global

Your place

Care rules, licences and background checks differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global
