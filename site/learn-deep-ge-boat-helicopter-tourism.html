Boat & Helicopter Tourism: Georgia and Israel deep edition

Boat & Helicopter Tourism planned in detail for Georgia and Israel: sector lessons plus local rules, jobs and GEL or ILS examples. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read your local market 4 hUnderstand

Every plan starts with the place. Read Georgia and Israel as your customer does before you choose a sector.

  • Plan one route for one visitor group first, such as a 2-hour harbour trip for families. Planning only; a licensed operator runs the craft.
  • Two different countries, two sets of rules. Pick the country first, then use that country’s list in every module. Check with a local expert.
  • Both have strong technology and start-up activity; many small firms sell abroad from day one.
  • Languages differ by customer: write the page in the local language plus English.
  • Public holidays and the working week differ; put the working days on every delivery promise.
Worked example. Draft 3 routes on a map, ask 20 visitors to pick one, and keep the top route. | Local example: Market page for Georgia and Israel: size in one line, three languages or fewer, main payment methods, three public holidays that affect delivery, and one risk. Each line has a source you can open and a date.

Activity. Write a one-page market page with sources and dates. Ask two local people to correct it.

Standard to meet. Every line has a source and a date; two local readers confirm the facts; one risk is named with a response.

Module 2 Choose your sector 4 hAnalyse

The global list has 75 sectors in 8 clusters. Pick by local fit, not by fashion.

  • Compare three models: seat resale for a licensed operator, a shared charter, a private charter. Pick by margin per seat.
  • Cluster A, Online selling and marketing: Strong fit in both: small firms sell online to the region. Check delivery rules and returns for each country. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster B, Technology and digital services: Both countries have a technology culture: security, privacy, cloud and testing services sell to small firms. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster C, Health, care and wellbeing (regulated): Regulated in both. Practise only under the right licence. This course teaches planning, not practice. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster D, Mobility, aerial and space (regulated): Regulated. Airspace and port rules are strict in the region; plan on paper and with a licensed partner. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster E, Energy, building and environment: Sunny and windy sites make energy services a fit; hold permits and grid rules before any build. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster F, Food and farming: Strong farming and food tradition: ghost kitchens, vertical farms and food science fit well; check food-safety rules. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster G, Money services (regulated): Regulated money services; plan as a partner of a licensed firm. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster H, People and business support: Coaching, coworking and outsourcing fit; many founders need flexible support in more than one language.
Worked example. Resale GEL 30 per seat, shared charter GEL 54 per seat, private charter GEL 90 per seat but fewer bookings: compare by seats sold. | Local example: Scoring example: pick three sectors, score each 1 to 5 on local demand, skills you hold, start-up cost, rules and partner access. A sector that scores 5 on demand but 1 on rules is a later project, not a first one. Pick the sector with the highest total and a rules score of 3 or more.

Activity. Score six sectors from at least three clusters. Choose one and write the reason in three lines.

Standard to meet. Six sectors scored on five criteria; the choice has a stated reason; any regulated sector is marked as planning only.

Module 3 Register and license 4 hApply

Rules differ by place. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real.

  • Check the operator licence, crew certificates, craft safety checks, insurance and weather rules. Partner only with a licensed firm. Check with a local expert.
  • Name the country first. Each has its own registration route: an individual-entrepreneur or small-business type in one, a dealer type or a company in the other. Check with a local expert.
  • Compare the types by tax, liability and filing work before you choose; write the table. Check with a local expert.
  • Licences vary by activity (food, health, money, education centres); list yours before the first order. Check with a local expert.
  • Keep a local address and a named contact in the country where you trade.
Worked example. Licence table row: operator permit, crew papers, insurance, safety check; each has an expiry date and a checked date. | Local example: Licence list for a small Boat & Helicopter Tourism business: registration type, tax registration, address, any activity licence, insurance. Next to each, write who issues it, what it costs in GEL or ILS (from their public page), how long it takes, and the date you checked.

Activity. Build a licence table for your chosen sector with issuer, cost, time and the date checked. Do not apply; this is a plan.

Standard to meet. Table is complete; every row has a public source; the order of steps is logical; one adviser question is written.

Module 4 Protect data and customers 3 hApply

Trust is the real product. Protect customer data and keep your promises in writing.

  • Collect only the data a booking needs: name, phone, group size, weight limit where the operator asks, date.
  • Both countries have a personal-data law; read the one for the country where the customer lives. Check with a local expert.
  • Say in plain words what you collect and why; give a way to see and correct it.
  • Keep marketing consent records; customers must be able to say no.
  • Moving data between the two countries or to a third place needs a written safeguard; keep a list of tools.
Worked example. A booking form with 5 fields; the weight field is shared only with the operator. | Local example: Privacy notice in five lines: who we are; what we collect; why; who sees it; how to ask for a copy, correction or removal. Plus a data map listing each tool and where it stores data.

Activity. Write the five-line notice and a data map for your plan. Ask a peer to find any gap.

Standard to meet. Notice is plain words; data map lists every tool; refund and complaint steps are written and dated.

Module 5 Find and serve customers 4 hCreate

Go where your customers already are, in the language they use. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, Russian and English depending on the country and customer.

  • Show proof: a short clip, honest ratings and the operator licence number on the page. Check with a local expert.
  • Channels: messaging apps and social media are strong in both; add a local marketplace.
  • Language: one page in the local language, one in English; never mix currencies on one price.
  • Payments: bank transfer and local payment methods; show the currency code next to every price.
  • Trust: registration number, address, delivery days and a refund note on every page. Check with a local expert.
Worked example. Page test: 100 visitors, 6 send an enquiry, 2 book: a 2% booking rate; add the licence line and test again. | Local example: Test plan: show the offer to 20 real people through two channels over two weeks; count enquiries, replies and first orders. Example result: 20 shown, 6 enquiries, 2 orders: a 10% order rate. Change one thing (price or wording) and test again.

Activity. Run a two-channel test with 20 people. Record results in a table and write the next change.

Standard to meet. Test has a start and end date; results are counted, not guessed; one change is chosen and justified.

Module 6 Price, tax and money 4 hEvaluate

Know your numbers in GEL or ILS. Tax rule in one line: Local sales tax and income tax differ in each country; a small-business regime may apply in one and a dealer type in the other.

  • Count cost per seat: operator fee, fuel share, insurance share, payment fee; then set the price.
  • Write GEL or ILS next to every number.
  • Contribution per unit = price minus the cost of each unit.
  • Break-even units = fixed monthly cost divided by contribution per unit; round up.
  • Add tax and duty explicitly; never bury them in a price. Check with a local expert.
  • Check against a worse case: if sales are 25% lower, do you still cover costs?
Worked example. Operator fee GEL 180 insurance share GEL 15 payment fee GEL 9 other GEL 6: cost GEL 210; a price of GEL 285 leaves GEL 75 per seat. | Local example: For a small Boat & Helicopter Tourism business: price GEL 285 per unit, cost GEL 210 per unit, fixed cost GEL 9,000 per month. Contribution = 285 - 210 = 75. Break-even = 9,000 / 75 = 120.0, so 120 seats per month. If sales fall 25% from 240, you sell 180 and the margin is 4,500.

Activity. Build your own sheet in GEL or ILS: price, unit cost, fixed cost, contribution, break-even and a 25% worse case. Put the tax and duty rules in a separate line with a source.

Standard to meet. Sheet has all six lines in GEL or ILS; arithmetic is checked by a peer; the worse case is shown.

Module 7 People and partners 3 hApply

Plans become real through people. Hire, partner and write every promise down.

  • Name partners: licensed operators, guides, hotels that refer guests; write a safety and cancellation page.
  • Employment rules, notice and social contributions differ; read the one for the country of the worker. Check with a local expert.
  • Written terms and signed copies for every helper, even part-time.
  • Freelancers are common: write scope, price, language and date in one page.
  • Partner with a local college, chamber or training body to find helpers and to practise.
Worked example. Weather cancel rule: refund in full if the operator cancels; reschedule within 14 days. | Local example: Role card for a first helper: tasks, hours, pay in GEL or ILS, trial period, review date, and one measure of success. Signed by both sides.

Activity. Write one role card and one partner letter (what each side gives). Role-play a first meeting for 10 minutes.

Standard to meet. Role card has pay in local currency, hours and a review date; partner letter is balanced; both are signed copies.

Module 8 Launch plan and review 4 hCreate

Finish with a one-page launch plan and a plan to review it every month.

  • Launch with a go or no-go rule: 40 seats sold in 6 weeks at a margin above 20%, with zero safety issues. Check with a local expert.
  • Dates: first test, first order, first review.
  • One measure per week: enquiries, orders or revenue; pick one.
  • A monthly review: what we planned, what happened, what we change.
  • Keep a risk list with a response for each; include a rules change. Check with a local expert.
  • Local caution: The final country choice is still to be confirmed. Keep every page tagged by country.
Worked example. Week 6: 46 seats at 24% and no issues: go; 22 seats: fix the route and price, do not add dates. | Local example: Launch page for Georgia and Israel: Week 1 licence table done; Week 2 test with 20 people; Week 3 numbers sheet checked; Week 4 launch decision with a go or no-go rule written in advance.

Activity. Write the launch page and present it to two people for 10 minutes. Record their questions and your answers.

Standard to meet. Plan has dates, one weekly measure, a go or no-go rule and a risk list; presenter answers three questions with evidence.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is the first move for a Boat & Helicopter Tourism business in Georgia and Israel?

Model answer: Plan one route for one visitor group first, such as a 2-hour harbour trip for families. Planning only; a licensed operator runs the craft.

Q2. Which model comparison belongs to this sector?

Model answer: Compare three models: seat resale for a licensed operator, a shared charter, a private charter. Pick by margin per seat.

Q3. Which clusters are marked regulated?

Model answer: Health and care, mobility and aerial, and money services.

Q4. What do you do if a sector is regulated and you hold no licence?

Model answer: Treat it as planning only and partner with a licensed firm.

Q5. Name three items in a licence table.

Model answer: Issuer, cost, time, and the date checked (any three).

Q6. Name the five lines of a privacy notice.

Model answer: Who we are, what we collect, why, who sees it, how to ask for a copy, correction or removal.

Q7. How do you test an offer?

Model answer: Show it to about 20 real people through two channels and count enquiries and orders.

Q8. Price GEL or ILS 285, unit cost 210, fixed 9,000. What is the contribution per unit?

Model answer: 285 - 210 = 75.

Q9. With the same numbers, what is break-even per month?

Model answer: 9,000 / 75 = 120.0, rounded up to 120.

Q10. What goes on a role card?

Model answer: Tasks, hours, pay, trial period, review date and one measure of success.
