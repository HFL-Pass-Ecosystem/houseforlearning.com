Biofuel Production Overview: Compare feedstocks by yield, cost and emissions.

  • Understand Explain who needs Biofuel Production Overview and name one real first user, with evidence from your own market scan.
  • Apply Build and run the practice task: Compare feedstocks by yield, cost and emissions.
  • Analyse Record every step, cost and problem, then compare the plan with what really happened.
  • Evaluate Judge your result against your goal using numbers, and write three lessons learned.
  • Create Present your evidence to a review room and give two peers written feedback.
  • Create Plan your next 90 days and contribute one template or worked example back to learners.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Compare feedstocks by yield, cost and emissions.

Condition

About 60 hours over 1-3 months. Own tools, free spreadsheet and form tools, and a real practice partner. Mentor and peer circle available. Currency written on every number.

Standard

Evidence reviewed by a mentor; at least 70% on the 10-question check; two written peer reviews given; every number carries its currency and unit.

Who it is for, requirements and materials

  • Who it is for: learners aged 16 and over, small-business starters, workers moving to a new field, and teams (see B2B, B2C, D2C).
  • Requirements: none; start here.
  • Materials included: lesson text, worked examples, case exercise, 10-question check, templates in My page.
  • Teaching team: a trainer from our network, a mentor who reviews your evidence, and a peer circle. Names are given at enrolment.

lesson by lesson

9 modules, in the order you do them

Module 1 What biofuels are and where they come from IdeaStudy · 2 hUnderstand

Goal: name the main types and choose three feedstocks to compare.

  1. Describe in your own words: biodiesel from oils and fats, ethanol from sugars and starch, biogas from wet waste, and other advanced routes.
  2. List feedstocks used near you (used cooking oil, sugar crops, grain, farm waste, food waste, wood residue).
  3. Choose three feedstocks to compare in the module.
  4. Write a one-page question: which is best here and by what measure?

Evidence you keep: notes and your three feedstocks.

Module 2 Kick-off circle IdeaShare and mentoring · 3 hUnderstand

Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.

  1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
  2. Peers ask three questions: does it compete with food, how far is the supply, what does it really save in emissions.
  3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a comparison table with sources, a process note, an emissions comparison and a risk note.

Evidence you keep: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

Module 3 Build the feedstock comparison ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Goal: compare yield, cost and emissions with public data.

  1. Find public figures for fuel yield per tonne or per hectare for each feedstock. Record source, date and whether it is a typical or best case.
  2. Find a cost per litre or per energy unit (with currency) from public energy reports.
  3. Find a life-cycle emissions range per energy unit compared with fossil fuel; note that results depend on land use and method.
  4. Mark each number 'reported' or 'estimate'.

Evidence you keep: the comparison table with sources.

Module 4 Processes, sustainability and safety ActionStudy · 2 hApply

Short modules:

  • Processes: transesterification for oils, fermentation and distillation for sugars, anaerobic digestion for wet waste. Know the inputs and outputs of each.
  • Sustainability: land use, water use, food competition, waste use, and indirect land-use change. Waste-based feedstocks usually have lower risk.
  • Safety: alcohol vapour, flammable liquids, strong chemicals, pressure and methane in digestion; trained staff and approved sites only.
  • Rules: fuel quality standards and blending limits are set nationally; find them.

Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

Module 5 Trace one supply chain ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Goal: follow one feedstock from source to fuel.

  1. Pick one local feedstock and sketch its chain: source, collection, transport, processing, quality test, use.
  2. Estimate transport distance and cost per tonne-kilometre.
  3. List who does each step and what permits or records they need.
  4. Mark the weak link in the chain.

Evidence you keep: the supply chain sketch and the weak link.

Module 6 Case-debate circle ValueShare and mentoring · 6 hEvaluate

Goal: argue the best option for a named region.

  1. Groups are given the same region and asked to recommend one option.
  2. Each group defends its choice using only sourced numbers.
  3. A mentor from energy or agriculture challenges the weakest claim.
  4. Vote, then explain why you changed or kept your view.

Evidence you keep: debate notes and the vote.

Module 7 Write the recommendation brief ValuePractice · 14 hEvaluate

Goal: turn the comparison into a short brief.

  1. Rank the feedstocks by yield, cost, emissions and supply risk.
  2. State the recommendation and its conditions.
  3. List three things that would change it.
  4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.

Evidence you keep: the brief.

Module 8 Review room EmpowermentShare and mentoring · 3 hCreate

Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.

  1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the comparison, the chain, the recommendation and the risks.
  2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
  3. Get a written note from a farmer, a fuel user or an energy adviser.

Evidence you keep: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

Module 9 Check and next step RegenerationStudy · 2 hCreate

Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.

  1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
  2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Electric Car and EV Basics, Greenhouse Gas Auditing, Plastic Alternatives.
  3. Contribute back: share your comparison table or supply chain template so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).

Evidence you keep: check result and the 90-day plan.

practice material and assessment

Worked examples, case, check and local law pointers

Worked examples
  1. Biodiesel from used cooking oil: 1,000 litres of oil gives about 900 litres of fuel (typical, check source). Yield = 90%.
  2. Ethanol: 1 tonne of sugar crop gives 80 litres (typical, check source). A 50-tonne harvest = 4,000 litres.
  3. Cost comparison: feedstock cost 0.60 per litre of fuel, processing 0.25, transport 0.05, margin 0.10 = 1.00 per litre (currency shown).
  4. Transport: 30 km round trip, 10 tonnes, rate 0.15 per tonne-km = 30 x 10 x 0.15 = 45.00 per load.
Case exercise

A farm produces 40 tonnes a month of crop waste and a nearby plant could use it for biogas. Questions: What is the weak link in the supply chain? Does it compete with food? What are the safety hazards? Compare it with selling the waste as animal feed. Write a one-page brief with the sources you used.

Check questions (draft set, 10)
  1. Name three kinds of biofuel. 2. Which feedstocks have lower food risk? 3. Why do emissions results vary? 4. Name the process for ethanol. 5. What is anaerobic digestion? 6. Why is transport distance important? 7. Name two safety hazards. 8. Why mark numbers 'reported' or 'estimate'? 9. What would change your recommendation? 10. What is your decision?

Rules for this course

No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. No home production of fuel. Cite every figure with source and date. Follow local fuel, waste and safety laws.
