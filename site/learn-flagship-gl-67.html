flagship course

3D Home Building

Understand how large-scale 3D printing of walls and homes works, its safety, codes, costs and where it suits.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation28:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain how a construction printer builds walls.
  • Apply Estimate material and print time.
  • Analyse Compare with conventional building.
  • Evaluate Judge codes, safety and quality.
  • Create Write a feasibility note for a small building.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write a feasibility note for a small printed building with design, quantities, time, cost, code checks and risks.

Condition

A computer, public information, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor builder. No printer operation is done.

Standard

Quantities and time are calculated; code and permit checks are listed; risks and controls are stated; cost compared with conventional building; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Builders, architects in training, housing groups, students. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How a construction printer works 3 hUnderstand

A construction 3D printer builds walls by laying a cement-like mix in layers, following a digital path.

  • A gantry or robotic arm moves a nozzle over the site, or parts are printed off site and lifted into place.
  • Walls are printed in layers of about 15 to 40 mm; foundations, doors, windows, roof and services are added by trades.
  • A design file controls the path; changing the file changes the building.
  • Printing is fast for walls, but the whole building still needs foundations, roof and finishes.
Worked example. A printer lays a 40 mm bead in a double-wall pattern with hollow cavities. The cavities are later filled with reinforcement and insulation. A precast printed planter is made off site in two hours.

Activity. Draw the process from design file to finished wall and compare on-site and off-site printing.

Standard to meet. Order the steps and compare both options.

Module 2 Printable materials and curing 3 hUnderstand

The mix decides success.

  • Cement-based mixes with additives, earth mixes and recycled aggregates.
  • The mix must flow through the nozzle, hold its shape under the next layer and gain strength.
  • Hot, dry or windy weather dries layers too fast; cold slows curing.
  • Test every batch for flow and strength.
Worked example. On a hot day a retarder is added so layers bond before drying. A 1 m test wall is printed and cut open; poor bonding is found at low temperature.

Activity. Compare two mixes in a table and describe a test wall plan.

Standard to meet. List the properties and limits.

Module 3 Design, engineering and building codes 4 hApply

Printed buildings follow the same safety rules as other buildings.

  • Structural design by a qualified engineer, including reinforcement, openings and loads such as wind and earthquake.
  • Fire, energy, accessibility and ventilation codes.
  • Foundations and services as usual.
  • Permits and inspections before, during and after printing.
Worked example. Steel bars are placed in wall cavities before they are filled, and an engineer signs the design. Vertical steel bars (rebar) are added at the spacing the engineer specifies for an earthquake zone.

Activity. List code checks and loads for a small building in your place.

Standard to meet. List the sources and name the loads.

Module 4 Quantities, time and cost drivers 4 hAnalyse

Count the bead and the hours.

  • Wall height / layer height = layers; layers x wall length = bead length to print.
  • Volume x density = material mass; add an allowance for waste, for example 10 per cent.
  • Print speed gives hours; add set-up and clean-up time.
  • Include transport of the machine and mix.
Worked example. 60 m of wall at 3 m high with 20 mm layers needs 150 layers; at 4 minutes a layer that is 10 hours plus 1 hour set-up.

Activity. Calculate quantities and time for a small room.

Standard to meet. Calculate the maths correctly, with waste and set-up included.

Module 5 Site safety 4 hEvaluate

People and machines share the site.

  • Exclusion zones, emergency stop and lifting plans.
  • Dust and silica protection when cutting or mixing.
  • Weather plan and training.
  • A trained operator and a spotter at all times.
Worked example. A fence marks the print zone and two workers are assigned: operator and spotter.

Activity. Write a site safety plan with roles.

Standard to meet. Plan is approved by a mentor.

Module 6 Cost and comparison with conventional building 3 hEvaluate

See where it pays.

  • Labour saved versus machine, material and transport cost.
  • Speed and waste reduction.
  • Compare per square metre of wall and of the whole building.
  • Doors, roof and finishes cost the same either way.
Worked example. Printed walls cost 12 per cent less, but doors and roofing cost the same, so the whole building saves about 4 per cent. Transport of the machine adds 8 per cent to the first project.

Activity. Build a comparison table for one small building.

Standard to meet. Show the currency and list the assumptions.

Module 7 Quality control and records 3 hApply

Test and record.

  • Sample cubes for strength and a slump or flow test for every batch.
  • Check layer bonding and tolerances.
  • Record mix temperature, weather and batch numbers.
  • Fix defects before covering.
Worked example. A cube fails the strength test and the batch is rejected; the log shows mix water was too high.

Activity. Write a quality control plan with tests and records.

Standard to meet. Name the tests and define the records.

Module 8 Feasibility note 4 hCreate

Bring it all together.

  • Fit: housing, shelters, sheds and small buildings.
  • Risks, permits and next steps.
  • List ten questions for a mentor builder.
  • Recommend go, pilot or stop.
Worked example. The note recommends a small pilot hut before a house, and a builder advises testing in your climate first.

Activity. Write and present the note.

Standard to meet. Write the note so that the reader can decide from it.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: quantity sheet, cost comparison, risk list, note. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
QuantitiesErrorsCorrectCorrect with margin
Codes and safetyMissingListedListed and approved
Cost comparisonNoneDoneDone with sensitivity
Quantity workingVolumes guessedVolumes and material mass worked from the drawingQuantities compared with a conventional wall of the same size
Building code and permitsCode not checkedBuilding code, permit and engineer sign-off for the place are written downCode cited by clause and an engineer's note filed

Q2. What controls the path?

Model answer: A design file. (Covered in module 1: How a construction printer works. To pass that module: Order the steps and compare both options.)

Q3. Who designs structure?

Model answer: An engineer. (Covered in module 3: Design, engineering and building codes. To pass that module: List the sources and name the loads.)

Q4. Layers formula?

Model answer: Height / layer height. (Covered in module 4: Quantities, time and cost drivers. To pass that module: Calculate the maths correctly, with waste and set-up included.)

Q5. Silica danger?

Model answer: Dust harms lungs. (Covered in module 5: Site safety. To pass that module: Plan is approved by a mentor.)

Q6. Why cube tests?

Model answer: Check strength. (Covered in module 7: Quality control and records. To pass that module: Name the tests and define the records.)

Q7. What stays manual?

Model answer: Roof and services. (Covered in module 1: How a construction printer works. To pass that module: Order the steps and compare both options.)

Q8. Weather effect?

Model answer: Curing changes. (Covered in module 2: Printable materials and curing. To pass that module: List the properties and limits.)

Q9. Cost comparison unit?

Model answer: Per m2. (Covered in module 6: Cost and comparison with conventional building. To pass that module: Show the currency and list the assumptions.)

Q10. Why pilot?

Model answer: Learn small. (Covered in module 8: Feasibility note. To pass that module: Write the note so that the reader can decide from it.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Building codes and permits differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
