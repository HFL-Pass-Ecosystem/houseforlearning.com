flagship course

3D Home Building

Understand how large-scale 3D printing of walls and homes works, its safety, codes, costs and where it suits.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation28:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain how a construction printer builds walls.
  • Apply Estimate material and print time.
  • Analyse Compare with conventional building.
  • Evaluate Judge codes, safety and quality.
  • Create Write a feasibility note for a small building.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write a feasibility note for a small printed building with design, quantities, time, cost, code checks and risks.

Condition

A computer, public information, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor builder. No printer operation is done.

Standard

Quantities and time are calculated; code and permit checks are listed; risks and controls are stated; cost compared with conventional building; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Builders, architects in training, housing groups, students. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How it works 3 hUnderstand

Layers of material.

  • A gantry or arm prints a cement-like mix in layers.
  • Walls are printed; roofs and services are added by hand.
  • A design file controls the path.
Worked example. A printer lays a 40 mm bead in a double-wall pattern.

Activity. Draw the process.

Standard to meet. Steps labelled.

Module 2 Materials 3 hUnderstand

The mix decides success.

  • Cement mixes with additives; earth mixes; recycled aggregates.
  • Must flow, hold shape and cure.
  • Weather affects curing.
Worked example. A hot day needs a retarder.

Activity. Compare two mixes.

Standard to meet. Properties listed.

Module 3 Design and codes 4 hApply

Printing is not exempt.

  • Structural design by an engineer; reinforcement and openings.
  • Fire, energy and accessibility codes.
  • Foundations and utilities as usual.
Worked example. Steel bars are placed in wall cavities.

Activity. List code checks.

Standard to meet. Sources noted.

Module 4 Quantities and time 4 hAnalyse

Count the bead.

  • Wall length x height / layer height = layers.
  • Volume x density = material.
  • Print speed gives hours.
Worked example. 60 m of wall at 3 m high needs 150 layers of 20 mm.

Activity. Calculate for a small room.

Standard to meet. Maths correct.

Module 5 Site and safety 4 hEvaluate

People and machine.

  • Exclusion zones, emergency stop, lifting.
  • Dust and silica protection.
  • Weather plan.
Worked example. A fence marks the print zone.

Activity. Write a site safety plan.

Standard to meet. Plan approved by mentor.

Module 6 Cost and comparison 3 hEvaluate

Where it pays.

  • Labour saving vs machine and material cost.
  • Speed and waste reduction.
  • Compare per m2.
Worked example. Walls cost 12% less but doors cost the same.

Activity. Build a table.

Standard to meet. Currency shown.

Module 7 Quality control 3 hApply

Test it.

  • Sample cubes for strength.
  • Check layer bonding and tolerances.
  • Keep records.
Worked example. A cube fails; the batch is rejected.

Activity. Write a QC plan.

Standard to meet. Tests named.

Module 8 Feasibility note 4 hCreate

Pull it together.

  • Fit: housing, shelters, small buildings.
  • Risks and permits.
  • Next steps.
Worked example. The note recommends a small pilot hut.

Activity. Write and present.

Standard to meet. Reader can decide.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: quantity sheet, cost comparison, risk list, note. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

ten locations and global

Your place

Building codes and permits differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
