flagship course

Wind Energy Fundamentals

Learn how wind turbines turn wind into power, how sites are assessed, and the safety, cost and community basics.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation30:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain how a turbine converts wind into electricity.
  • Apply Estimate energy from wind speed and rotor size.
  • Analyse Compare sites and turbine types.
  • Evaluate Judge cost, noise, wildlife and safety.
  • Create Write a simple feasibility note for a small wind project.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a feasibility note for a small wind installation, with a wind data table, energy estimate, cost and payback, safety and community checks.

Condition

A computer, public wind data for a chosen site, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor engineer. No work at height is done in this course.

Standard

Energy estimate uses stated formulas and units; payback is calculated in your currency; safety and community points are listed; assumptions are written; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Technicians, engineering students, community energy groups, small farm owners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How a wind turbine works 3 hUnderstand

A wind turbine turns the push of moving air into electricity. Knowing the parts and the physics helps you judge any project.

  • Blades capture wind and turn a shaft; a generator converts rotation to electricity; a tower lifts the rotor into stronger wind; a controller manages speed and safety.
  • Key speeds: cut-in (starts), rated (full power) and cut-out (stops for safety).
  • Power grows with the cube of wind speed, so a small increase in speed gives a large increase in power.
  • Larger blades sweep more area, which also raises power.
Worked example. Doubling the wind speed from 5 to 10 m/s gives about eight times more power. A turbine producing 1 kW at 5 m/s could produce about 8 kW at 10 m/s, until it reaches its rated power.

Activity. Label a turbine diagram and write one sentence on what each part does.

Standard to meet. All main parts are named and the cube rule is stated correctly.

Module 2 Wind resource and sites 4 hAnalyse

Good wind makes or breaks a project, and wind data decides whether to continue.

  • Wind speed rises with height; trees, buildings and hills cause turbulence.
  • Use at least a year of hourly or ten-minute averages from a mast or a trusted dataset.
  • Choose open land away from obstacles, with road access and a grid or load nearby.
  • Check land rights, distance from homes and any aviation or radar limits.
Worked example. A hilltop averages 6.5 m/s at 30 m height; a sheltered valley averages 4.2 m/s. The hilltop produces roughly three times the energy from the same turbine.

Activity. Tabulate monthly average wind speed for a real or practice site with the data source.

Standard to meet. Table has twelve months, units and source.

Module 3 Estimating energy 5 hApply

A simple estimate tells you if a project is worth a closer look.

  • Swept area = pi x radius squared.
  • Power in the wind = 0.5 x air density x area x speed cubed; real turbines capture only a fraction.
  • Annual energy = rated power x 8,760 hours x capacity factor.
  • Small turbines often reach 15 to 30 per cent capacity factor on good sites.
Worked example. A 5 kW turbine at 22 per cent capacity factor produces 5 x 8,760 x 0.22 = about 9,636 kWh per year.

Activity. Calculate annual energy for your case and show every step with units.

Standard to meet. Units and steps are correct, and the result is checked against the capacity factor range.

Module 4 Turbine types and sizes 3 hAnalyse

Choose the right size and type for the site and the need.

  • Horizontal-axis turbines are most efficient; vertical-axis turbines are sometimes used where wind is turbulent, but usually produce less.
  • Small turbines suit remote sites; medium and large turbines suit farms and grids.
  • Grid-tied systems send extra power to the network; off-grid systems need batteries.
  • Compare cost per kW, noise, service needs and warranty.
Worked example. A village chooses a grid-tied 10 kW turbine to reduce diesel use, rather than a 3 kW unit with batteries that would not meet its evening load.

Activity. Compare three turbines in a table with rated power, rotor diameter, cost per kW and notes.

Standard to meet. Table is complete with sources and dates.

Module 5 Cost and payback 4 hEvaluate

Count the full cost and the full benefit.

  • Costs: turbine, tower, foundation, wiring, permits, installation, maintenance, insurance.
  • Benefit: electricity bills avoided or sold; value of backup.
  • Simple payback = total cost / yearly benefit.
  • Test with higher costs and lower wind to see how safe the case is.
Worked example. Cost 6,50,000 INR; yearly benefit 85,000 INR; payback 7.6 years. If wind is 10 per cent lower, energy falls about 27 per cent and payback stretches past 10 years.

Activity. Build a payback sheet with a base case and a low-wind case.

Standard to meet. Maths correct and the currency is shown.

Module 6 Safety and maintenance 4 hApply

Heights, rotating parts and electricity are serious hazards.

  • Only trained and certified people work at height or on electrical systems.
  • Lock out and tag out before maintenance; never climb in strong wind.
  • Regular checks: bolts, blades, brakes, wiring and tower.
  • Keep the public away with fencing and signs.
Worked example. A crew has a rule that nobody climbs if wind exceeds a set speed. The rule is posted at the base and checked on a log sheet.

Activity. Write a safety checklist and a monthly maintenance schedule.

Standard to meet. Mentor engineer confirms the lists.

Module 7 Environment and community 3 hEvaluate

Projects work best when neighbours and nature are considered from the start.

  • Noise, shadow flicker, birds and bats, land use and views.
  • Consult neighbours early and follow permit rules.
  • Plan decommissioning and recycling at end of life.
  • Share benefits such as cheaper power or local jobs.
Worked example. Seasonal shutdowns during peak bird migration, radar checks and a community benefit fund reduce objections.

Activity. List impacts and controls for a practice site.

Standard to meet. Each impact has a control and an owner.

Module 8 Write the feasibility note 4 hCreate

Bring it together into a clear decision note.

  • One-page summary with site, wind data, energy, cost and payback.
  • List assumptions and risks.
  • State what you do not know yet.
  • Have a mentor review.
Worked example. The note recommends a one-year wind monitoring mast before buying a turbine, saving the risk of a poor site.

Activity. Write and present the note.

Standard to meet. A peer can follow every number.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: wind data table, energy and payback sheet, safety checklist, feasibility note. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Cost and paybackIncompleteCompleteComplete with options
Safety and communityMissingListedListed with controls

ten locations and global

Your place

Permits, grid rules and safety law differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
