flagship course

Wind Energy Fundamentals

Learn how wind turbines turn wind into power, how sites are assessed, and the safety, cost and community basics.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation30:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain how a turbine converts wind into electricity.
  • Apply Estimate energy from wind speed and rotor size.
  • Analyse Compare sites and turbine types.
  • Evaluate Judge cost, noise, wildlife and safety.
  • Create Write a simple feasibility note for a small wind project.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a feasibility note for a small wind installation, with a wind data table, energy estimate, cost and payback, safety and community checks.

Condition

A computer, public wind data for a chosen site, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor engineer. No work at height is done in this course.

Standard

Energy estimate uses stated formulas and units; payback is calculated in your currency; safety and community points are listed; assumptions are written; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Technicians, engineering students, community energy groups, small farm owners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How a wind turbine works 3 hUnderstand

A wind turbine turns the push of moving air into electricity. Knowing the parts and the physics lets you judge any site or machine.

  • Blades turn a rotor; a generator (with or without a gearbox) makes electricity.
  • Power in wind = 0.5 x air density x swept area x wind speed cubed, so doubling speed gives eight times the power.
  • No turbine captures more than about 59 per cent of wind power; real machines reach 35 to 45 per cent.
  • Turbines start at about 3 m/s, reach rated power near 12 m/s and stop in storms for safety.
Worked example. A rotor 4 m across sweeps 12.6 square metres. In a 6 m/s wind the air carries 0.5 x 1.2 x 12.6 x 216 = 1,633 W. At 40 per cent the turbine gives about 650 W. At 3 m/s it gives only about 80 W.

Activity. Label a turbine diagram and calculate power at 4, 6 and 8 m/s.

Standard to meet. Parts are correct; three power values are right and the cube effect is explained in one sentence.

Module 2 Wind resource and sites 4 hAnalyse

Good wind makes or breaks a project, and wind data decides whether to continue.

  • Use at least one year of data at hub height; short records mislead.
  • Trees, buildings and hills cause turbulence; put the rotor well above them.
  • Record average speed, direction and the share of time in each speed band.
  • Check the distance to the load and to the grid connection.
Worked example. A hilltop averages 6.5 m/s at 30 m height; a sheltered valley averages 4.2 m/s. The hilltop produces nearly four times the energy from the same turbine.

Activity. Tabulate monthly average wind speed for a real or practice site with the data source.

Standard to meet. Table has twelve months, units and source.

Module 3 Estimating energy 5 hApply

A simple estimate tells you if a project is worth a closer look.

  • Annual energy = rated power x 8,760 hours x capacity factor.
  • Small wind capacity factors are often 10 to 25 per cent.
  • Take the lower value if the data is poor.
  • Compare with the yearly use of the building.
Worked example. A 5 kW turbine at 18 per cent capacity factor gives 5 x 8,760 x 0.18 = 7,884 kWh a year. A home using 6,000 kWh a year would cover most of its need; at 10 per cent the output falls to 4,380 kWh.

Activity. Estimate yearly energy for low, medium and high capacity factors.

Standard to meet. Three results are correct and compared with the user load.

Module 4 Turbine types and sizes 3 hAnalyse

Choose the right size and type for the site and the need.

  • Horizontal axis machines are common and efficient; vertical axis machines suit gusty, low-height sites but usually produce less.
  • Match rated power to the load and to the wind speed at the site.
  • Tower height matters more than a bigger rotor in rough terrain.
  • Grid-tied and battery-backed designs have different cost and control.
Worked example. For a farm needing 20 kWh a day, a 3 kW machine at 20 per cent factor gives 14 kWh a day, so a 5 kW machine is the better fit.

Activity. Choose a turbine for a given load and site and justify it.

Standard to meet. Choice shows size, type, tower height and one reason for each.

Module 5 Cost and payback 4 hEvaluate

Count the full cost and the full benefit.

  • Include turbine, tower, foundation, electrical work, permits and labour.
  • Include maintenance (about 2 to 3 per cent of cost a year) and inverter replacement.
  • Annual saving = kWh produced x price per kWh.
  • Payback = cost / annual saving; test weak-wind cases.
Worked example. A 5 kW system costs 600,000. It makes 7,884 kWh at 8 per kWh, saving 63,072 a year, less 15,000 maintenance. Net saving 48,072 gives a payback of 12.5 years. At 10 per cent factor payback passes 25 years and the project is not worth it.

Activity. Build the cost and payback table for two wind cases.

Standard to meet. Costs add up, both cases are shown and a go or no-go is stated.

Module 6 Safety and maintenance 4 hApply

Heights, rotating parts and electricity are serious hazards.

  • Work at height only with training, harness and a rescue plan.
  • Lock out the turbine and electrical supply before work.
  • Check bolts, blades, brakes and cables on a schedule.
  • Keep people clear of blade throw and ice fall zones.
Worked example. A yearly check finds a loose tower bolt and a cracked blade tip; the turbine is stopped and repaired before it runs again.

Activity. Write a one-page maintenance and safety plan.

Standard to meet. Plan lists checks, frequency, who does them and stop rules.

Module 7 Environment and community 3 hEvaluate

Projects work best when neighbours and nature are considered from the start.

  • Noise, shadow flicker and view concerns need honest discussion.
  • Check bird and bat routes and apply local rules.
  • Keep a safe distance of at least the tower height plus blade length from homes and roads.
  • Share benefits where possible.
Worked example. Residents are shown a noise estimate and a map of the safe distance. The turbine is moved 80 m away after they ask.

Activity. List four community and nature checks with an action for each.

Standard to meet. All four are practical and each has an owner.

Module 8 Write the feasibility note 4 hCreate

Bring it together into a clear decision note.

  • State the need and the site.
  • Show the wind table, energy estimate and payback.
  • Cover safety, permits and community.
  • End with go, no-go or measure more, with the reason.
Worked example. The note recommends one more year of measurement because the first-year data covers only seven months.

Activity. Write the two-page feasibility note.

Standard to meet. All parts are present, numbers agree and the decision is justified.

ten locations and global

Your place

Permits, grid rules and safety law differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text.
