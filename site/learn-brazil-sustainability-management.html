Sustainability Management

Eight modules take you from the first customer to a running, measured service in sustainability management.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, start and run sustainability management in São Paulo: serve a firm or city office, deliver a measured plan to cut waste and emissions, keep records, and show a working month of numbers.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. Own phone or computer, real customers or a real practice site, a peer and a mentor for the final review. Local rules and licences checked against official sources.

Standard

Every module standard is met; all claims are true and backed by records; peers and a mentor mark the evidence 'Meets' or better.

Who it is for: students, graduates, career changers, owners, workers and returners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, tracker, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know your customer and market 4 hAnalyse

Every sound business in sustainability management begins with one clear customer and one clear problem. In São Paulo and the wider state, demand is real but crowded, so a small, well-described customer group wins before a broad one.

  • In sustainability management: Customers want to cut cost and prove claims; start with one site and its bills.
  • Write the customer in one sentence: a firm or city office. Add the place, the moment of need and what they use today.
  • List five rivals or substitutes within reach. For each, record price, strength, weakness and how people reach it.
  • Interview at least eight real customers with open questions. Record exact words, not your summary.
  • Count the market in units: how many assessments could one area buy in a month? Use two public sources and your own count, then take the lower figure.
  • Choose one first segment and say why the others wait.
Worked example. Weak: 'Everyone needs a measured plan to cut waste and emissions.' Strong: 'Our first customer is a firm or city office within 8 km of the centre, who today pays R$ 9.900 per assessment and complains about delay. We target 4 assessments a month by month three.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: ten customers are interviewed for sustainability management. Seven say price, two say speed, one says trust. Question: which first message do you write, and which interview record backs it? Model answer: lead with the assessment price and speed promise, keep trust proof as the second line, and cite the seven price answers by date.

Activity. Write the one-sentence customer, a five-row rival table and eight interview records. Share them with a peer for a challenge round.

Standard to meet. Customer is specific; rival table has prices; every claim traces to an interview or a source.

Module 2 Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil 4 hUnderstand

Brazil asks every trader to be visible and accountable. Know which register, tax regime and sector rules apply before the first sale, and check each against the current official text.

  • In sustainability management: Environmental licences and reporting duties apply; avoid claims that cannot be proven.
  • Choose a legal form: micro-entrepreneur (MEI) only if your activity appears on the official MEI list, or a company registered with a CNPJ. Regulated activities need their sector authorisation first. Check the current limits and lists on the official portal.
  • Learn the tax regime that fits your size and write down which taxes you will collect and pay, and when.
  • Find the sector rules for sustainability management: licences, inspections, professional registers. List each with the office that issues it and the renewal date.
  • Personal data of customers falls under Brazil's data protection law (LGPD). Collect only what you need, say why, keep it safe, and name who answers questions.
  • Consumer law gives buyers clear rights on information, returns and complaints. Put your terms in plain Portuguese.
Worked example. Checklist for a new sustainability management trader: 1) legal form (CNPJ, or MEI only if your activity is on the official list); 2) city operating licence; 3) sector licence or register; 4) tax set-up; 5) privacy notice; 6) written terms. Each has an owner and a date.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a neighbour says registration can wait until the first R$ 45.000 of sales. Question: what do you do? Model answer: do not delay; write the compliance table first, confirm the register, tax and sector licence with the official office, and start only where the table shows no open blocker.

Activity. Build your own six-line compliance table for your first location. Mark what is done, what is pending and who confirms it.

Standard to meet. Every line cites an official source; nothing is assumed; pending items have dates. Local law always wins over this course.

Module 3 Design the offer 4 hCreate

An offer states what the customer gets, how well, by when and at what price. For a measured plan to cut waste and emissions, the offer must make the quality visible and the next step easy.

  • In sustainability management: Offer a baseline, a target, a plan and a yearly report.
  • Describe the core offer in three lines: what is delivered (a measured plan to cut waste and emissions), the standard it meets, and the time it takes.
  • Add one proof for each promise: a sample, a test result, a guarantee or a customer record.
  • Price from cost upward and from customer value downward, then pick a price inside both.
  • Write a simple one-page terms sheet: what is included, what is extra, how to cancel.
  • Create two options at most, so choice stays easy.
Worked example. Offer: 'A measured plan to cut waste and emissions, delivered within agreed time, checked against a written standard, price R$ 9.000 per assessment. Proof: a dated record the customer can check and sign. Changes or cancellation by written agreement before work begins.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a customer asks for a discount from R$ 9.000 to R$ 8.100 per assessment. Question: decide with numbers. Model answer: contribution falls from R$ 5.500 to R$ 4.600 per assessment; offer an extra of value (for example faster service) before cutting price, and accept only if the lower contribution still clears break-even.

Longer notes. Begin with a baseline for a small bakery, clinic or workshop: copy twelve months of electricity, water and waste bills into one sheet and read the meters on a normal day. Find the three biggest costs and the hours they run. Pick two or three actions with a short payback, such as efficient lamps, door seals on cold rooms or a sorting corner for recyclables. Compare each by cost, installation time and expected saving in kilowatt-hours. Report results in reais and in units, never in vague green words. Check the current official text of environmental and labeling rules before you publish a statement about a product.

Extra worked example. A small bakery pays R$ 1.800 per month for electricity. The audit finds that efficient lighting and fridge door seals can cut use by 15 percent. Monthly saving is R$ 1.800 x 0,15 = R$ 270. The upgrade costs R$ 2.700. Payback is R$ 2.700 / R$ 270 = 10 months. After that, the saving of R$ 3.240 per year stays with the bakery.

Activity. Write the offer card, the proof list and the terms sheet. Test them with three customers and note every point where they hesitated.

Standard to meet. Promise, proof and price are consistent; terms fit one page; three customer reactions are recorded.

Module 4 Run the work step by step 5 hApply

Good operations make results repeatable. For sustainability management, write each step so a new person could follow it on their first day, and measure each step.

  • In sustainability management: Measure energy, water, waste and emissions monthly; use the same method each time.
  • Map the process from first contact to paid and closed. Typical stages: enquiry, quote, preparation, delivery of the assessment, check, payment, follow-up.
  • Name the key resource at each stage: a measured baseline. Note what happens if it is missing.
  • Write a one-page standard procedure per stage: who, what, tool, time, record.
  • Choose three measures: speed, quality (errors per 100) and customer rating. Review them every week.
  • Fix the cause of a defect, not the symptom. Ask 'why' five times and record the answer.
Worked example. Stage record: 'Prepare. Owner: lead. Time: 30 min. Tool: checklist. Record: signed sheet. Defect found on 1 of 2 assessments last week; cause: missing check on a measured baseline; fix: add check at start, review next Friday.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: of 6 assessments last month, 1 had a defect traced to a measured baseline. Question: what is the defect rate and what is your first action? Model answer: about 17 defects per 100; stop the cause (add a check at the start), correct affected customers, log the cause, and re-measure next week.

Activity. Draw the process map and write procedures for the two riskiest stages. Run each once and time it.

Standard to meet. Procedures are followable by a stranger; measures have weekly numbers; one defect is traced to a root cause.

Module 5 Safety, quality and risk 4 hEvaluate

The most serious risk in this field is claims that cannot be proven. A calm, written risk routine protects people first and the business second.

  • In sustainability management: Greenwashing is the key risk; every claim needs data and a source.
  • List ten things that could go wrong. Score each for how likely (1 to 5) and how serious (1 to 5). Multiply and sort.
  • For the top five, choose a control: remove the hazard, replace it, guard it, train people, or use protective gear, in that order of preference.
  • Write who does what in an emergency, and rehearse it once a quarter.
  • Keep an incident and near-miss log. Near-misses are free lessons.
  • Check that insurance and legal duties match the risk you actually carry.
Worked example. Risk row: 'Hazard: claims that cannot be proven. Likelihood 3, severity 5, score 15. Control: written check before each assessment, trained staff, log sheet. Owner: site lead. Review monthly.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a near-miss occurs: claims that cannot be proven nearly happened but no one was hurt. Question: what do you do in the next 24 hours? Model answer: make safe, record the event, ask the five whys, add or tighten one control, brief the team, and set a review date; near-misses count as lessons, not blame.

Activity. Build a ten-row risk register and a one-page emergency plan. Run a ten-minute drill with a peer and note two improvements.

Standard to meet. Top risks have controls ranked by preference; drill done; log started. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.

Module 6 People, skills and service 4 hApply

People deliver the promise. Define roles, train to a standard and give feedback in the open.

  • In sustainability management: Train site champions to read meters and log data.
  • Write a role card for each person: purpose, five tasks, the standard for each, and who to ask for help.
  • Train by showing, doing with support, then doing alone with a check. Record sign-off per task.
  • Set service rules for answering customers: reply times, tone, how to apologise and fix.
  • Hold a ten-minute team check each day: yesterday's problem, today's plan, one risk.
  • Build a growth path: what a new person can do in week 1, month 3 and month 12.
Worked example. Training record: 'Task: handle a complaint. Shown 2 May; done with support 4 May; done alone 8 May, checked: apology, fix, follow-up call. Signed by lead.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a new hire completes training but a customer complaint shows a skipped step. Question: whose gap is it? Model answer: first the system: check the role card, the training sign-off and the checklist; then coach the person and fix the document so the step cannot be skipped.

Longer notes. Name one champion in each team: a cook, a driver, a cleaner, a clerk. Give each a single weekly task, such as reading the water meter on Monday or emptying and weighing the sorting bins. Walk the premises together with the team and ask them to point at every tap left running, light left on and bin mixed up. Count rather than guess: kilograms of waste, liters of water, kilowatt-hours. Thank the team in the Friday meeting when a number improves. Tailor the message, since drivers control fuel and cleaners control detergent. Draft a one-page guide in plain words and revise it after feedback.

Extra worked example. Four team champions each spend 1 hour per week on checks, at R$ 30 per hour, so weekly cost is 4 x R$ 30 = R$ 120. Over 4 weeks that is R$ 480. Their checks find water leaks and idle machines that save R$ 650 per month. Net benefit is R$ 650 - R$ 480 = R$ 170 per month, with better awareness as an extra gain.

Activity. Write two role cards and a training plan for one new hire. Role-play a hard customer conversation twice and review.

Standard to meet. Role cards have measurable standards; training shows sign-off steps; service rules are written in plain words.

Module 7 Money: cost, price and break-even 5 hAnalyse

A business lives or dies on a few numbers. Know your contribution per unit, your fixed costs and the volume you need.

  • In sustainability management: Show payback of each measure in months.
  • Contribution per assessment = price minus variable cost. Fixed costs are paid whether you sell or not.
  • Break-even units = fixed cost divided by contribution. Plan to pass it by a safe margin.
  • Make a 12-month cash plan. Cash timing matters as much as profit: customers may pay late, suppliers may want money first.
  • Keep money for business and money for the owner apart.
  • Test the plan: what if sales fall by a quarter, or cost rises by a tenth?
Worked example. Worked example, all figures in reais: price R$ 9.000, variable cost R$ 3.500, contribution R$ 5.500 per assessment. Fixed cost R$ 15.000 a month. Break-even = R$ 15.000 / R$ 5.500 = 3 assessments a month. At 6 assessments a month, profit = R$ 5.500 x 6 - R$ 15.000 = R$ 18.000. These are teaching figures that belong to this example only; later examples and the case study use their own figures. Use your own.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: sales fall to 4 assessments a month. Question: what is the monthly result? Model answer: R$ 5.500 x 4 - R$ 15.000 = R$ 7.000; compare with break-even of 3 assessments and decide whether to cut fixed cost or raise volume first. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build your own table: price, variable cost, contribution, fixed cost, break-even and a downside case at 75% of sales. Show it to a peer or mentor.

Standard to meet. Arithmetic is correct; every figure has a source or is marked as an assumption; a downside case is shown.

Module 8 Grow, measure and renew 4 hEvaluate

Growth should follow proof. Measure, learn, then add; and keep returning to the customer.

  • In sustainability management: Grow by publishing verified results and repeating the cycle.
  • Pick one growth lever at a time: new customers, a higher repeat rate, a better price, or a new offer.
  • Set one measurable target for 90 days, such as 'raise repeat customers from 20% to 30%'.
  • Ask each customer how they found you, and record it. Spend more where the record shows results.
  • Review monthly: numbers, defects, complaints, cash. Write three decisions each time.
  • Plan the renewal of licences, equipment and skills a year ahead.
Worked example. 90-day plan: 'Lever: repeat customers. Action: follow-up call after every assessment. Target: repeat rate from 20% to 30%. Check: weekly count. Review date: day 45 and day 90.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: you can add 2 assessments a month by one of two levers: a follow-up call to past customers or a price rise of R$ 900 to R$ 9.900. Question: how do you choose? Model answer: test the cheaper reversible lever first (the call), measure for 30 days, and only then try the price change, so you can tell which one worked.

Longer notes. Record monthly use of energy, water and materials, plus the weight of waste sent to landfill. Divide each by output, for example liters per unit produced, so a busy month does not hide real progress. Compare against the baseline year and set the next target only after the last one is met or explained. Put sites side by side and copy the habit of the best one. Review the plan yearly and retire actions that stalled. Say openly which targets you missed. Avoid claims you cannot prove, and check the current official text of reporting and claims rules.

Extra worked example. A workshop used 4.000 liters of water per month for 800 units, so 5 liters per unit. After new taps and a reuse tank, use fell to 3.200 liters for 800 units, or 4 liters per unit. That is a 20 percent cut, saving 800 liters. At R$ 12 per 1.000 liters, the saving is 0,80 x R$ 12 = R$ 9,60 per month, small in reais but a clear start.

Activity. Write a 90-day plan with one target, three actions and a review date. Present it in five minutes and take questions.

Standard to meet. Target is measurable and dated; actions are small enough to start this week; review date is in the calendar.

case study

A case to work through

Situation. A bakery with 14 employees in Pinheiros, São Paulo, spends R$ 3.800 a month on electricity and throws away about 60 kilograms of unsold bread each week. The owner wants to be more sustainable, but he sees only costs. A friend suggests new ovens at R$ 28.000, which he cannot afford. He has never measured how much energy each oven uses or what food is wasted by product. A nearby shelter might accept some unsold bread if it is handled safely, and a recycling cooperative collects cardboard. He needs a plan that saves money first, uses simple measurement, and avoids empty promises to customers about being green. He has two weeks to start.

Task. Say what you would do first, next and last, and what record you would keep.

Model answer. First, measure for four weeks: weigh the unsold bread each day by product, and read the electricity meter at opening and closing. Next, rank the waste and the energy use to find the biggest items. Then act on the cheapest first: bake smaller batches of the products with the most waste, switch off ovens during the idle hours, and send cardboard to the cooperative. After that, ask the shelter about safe handling rules and check the current official text on food donation. Pilot for another four weeks and compare. Last, publish only the numbers you can prove. Records: the daily waste sheet, the meter readings, the donation receipts, and the electricity bills. Re-compute: 60 kilograms a week is 240 kilograms a month; if batches shrink and waste falls by a quarter, 60 kilograms a month are saved. Standard: every claim made to customers is supported by a record.

Q3. Which Brazilian law governs customer personal data?

Model answer: LGPD, the general data protection law. (Covered in module 2: Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil. To pass that module: Every line cites an official source; nothing is assumed; pending items have dates. Local law always wins over this course.)

Q4. What does an offer state, and what backs it up?

Q6. What is the main risk named for sustainability management in this course, and what is the first control to try?

(Covered in module 5: Safety, quality and risk. To pass that module: Top risks have controls ranked by preference; drill done; log started. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.)

Q7. Order the controls from best to last.

Model answer: Remove, replace, guard, train, protect with gear. (Covered in module 5: Safety, quality and risk. To pass that module: Top risks have controls ranked by preference; drill done; log started. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.)

Q8. Define contribution per unit.

Model answer: Price minus variable cost per unit. (Covered in module 7: Money: cost, price and break-even. To pass that module: Arithmetic is correct; every figure has a source or is marked as an assumption; a downside case is shown.)

Q9. Using the worked example, what is break-even in assessments?

Model answer: 3 assessments a month (fixed R$ 15.000 / contribution R$ 5.500, rounded up). (Covered in module 7: Money: cost, price and break-even. To pass that module: Arithmetic is correct; every figure has a source or is marked as an assumption; a downside case is shown.)

levels L1 to L7

Where this sits on the ladder

Seven levels, one slide each. Read the slide, then read the speaker notes under it. Credits stack and you can step in and out; this course is L4.

Slide 1 of 7: L1 Basic: Sustainability Management

  • Name main sustainability topics such as energy, water and waste
  • Identify everyday actions that reduce impact
  • Recall why records matter
Notes for slide 1

This level suits anyone in Sao Paulo who is new to Sustainability Management, including school leavers and career changers. Plan about 8 to 10 hours, with a short quiz or notebook page as evidence. The standard is to use the basic words correctly and name safe first steps. It prepares the learner for L2, where they see how the subject works in real Brazilian settings.

Slide 2 of 7: L2 Awareness: Sustainability Management

  • Describe the link between business and environment
  • Explain carbon and recycling simply
  • Recognise Sao Paulo city challenges
Notes for slide 2

This level suits curious learners, managers and helpers who need to talk with people working in Sustainability Management. Plan about 15 to 20 hours, with a short summary or talk as evidence. The standard is to explain the main ideas and limits in plain words. It leads to L3, where the learner starts doing small tasks with their own hands.

Slide 3 of 7: L3 Foundation: Sustainability Management

  • Measure energy and waste in a small site
  • Compare options to reduce use
  • Report findings in plain language
Notes for slide 3

This level suits learners ready to do real beginner tasks in Sustainability Management, under guidance. Plan about 40 to 60 hours, with a small practical portfolio as evidence. The standard is accurate, safe and tidy work that another person can check. It leads to L4, the main course, where these tasks become a full working service.

Slide 4 of 7: L4 Practitioner: Sustainability Management

  • Plan a sustainability service in Sao Paulo across 8 modules
  • Start with a measured baseline, tools and first clients
  • Run improvement plans and yearly reports; check the current official text
Notes for slide 4

This is the main course for Sustainability Management, built for people who want to work or trade in Sao Paulo. It has 8 modules and about 120 to 160 hours. Evidence is a full plan, a started service and a run record. The standard is a service that could serve real clients legally and safely. It leads to L5, where one area is studied in depth.

Slide 5 of 7: L5 Specialist: Sustainability Management

  • Analyse supply chain impacts
  • Design a waste and energy plan
  • Evaluate results against goals
Notes for slide 5

This level suits practitioners who have run real work in Sustainability Management and want depth in one area. Plan about 100 hours, with a case study or project report as evidence. The standard is sound analysis with clear reasons for every choice. It leads to L6, where the learner guides other people and larger projects.

Slide 6 of 7: L6 Advanced: Sustainability Management

  • Lead a reporting cycle
  • Develop staff and supplier programmes
  • Negotiate targets with leaders
Notes for slide 6

This level suits experienced people who lead teams or large tasks in Sustainability Management. Plan about 150 hours, with evidence from a led project, results data and peer review. The standard is steady, measured results and clear communication with owners and partners. It leads to L7, where the learner shapes how the whole field works.

Slide 7 of 7: L7 Super expert: Sustainability Management

  • Create a long-term sustainability strategy
  • Mentor teams and review claims
  • Critique methods and guide good practice
Notes for slide 7

This level suits recognised leaders in Sustainability Management with several years of proven results. Expect more than 200 hours of project, teaching and review work, with evidence from published work or independent expert review. The standard is original, trusted judgement that others copy. It closes the ladder, and the learner now helps raise others from L1.

Your place

Local rules change licences, taxes, data duties and customer rights. This course is written for São Paulo. Check the current official text for your own place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registration: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registration: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registration: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registration: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registration: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registration: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registration: registration with ACRA. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registration: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registration: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registration: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registration: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registration: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation
