Module 6: Rooted cosmopolitanism: adapt, aggregate, arbitrage

A 2.5 ECTS executive module, 75 hours.

what you take

Module 6

  • Size

    2.5 ECTS, 75 hours of study.

  • Credit

    Counts in 16 countries. See the table.

how it is marked

Assessment

  • Written memo 30%
  • Live interactive simulation 35%
  • Boardroom presentation 35%

Marking bands: A, B or fail. Presentations use at most 10 slides, with a short live defence.

Weights shown follow the shared pattern for modules 3 to 14, and will be confirmed.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task

Choose, for each product or market, how far to adapt to local needs, how much to pool across countries, and where to profit from differences, while keeping the firm's core intact.

Condition

Given a product line with cost, price and volume figures in three countries and a statement of the firm's core values and offer.

Standard

Each choice is backed by a correct payback, unit-cost or margin figure, and the final strategy keeps the stated core unchanged.

Who it is for: Leaders of firms that sell in many countries and must balance local fit with global scale.

eight parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 Rooted cosmopolitanism: core and flex 9 hUnderstand: define and sort

A firm can be global and still be itself. It keeps a fixed core and lets everything else flex to place.

  • Core: what must not change
  • Flex: what can change by market
  • Test: would a loyal customer still know us?

Worked example: A bakery chain lists 100 features of its offer. Experts sort them: 70 are core (recipes, hygiene rules, price band, staff respect), 30 are flexible (menu extras, opening hours, shop size). Core share = 70%. In one country, the team asks to change 5 core items. That would drop core to 65 of 100. The rule says no: move those items to flex only if customers across markets accept them.

Activity: List 30 features of a business, sort them into core and flex, and justify five hard cases.

Standard: All 30 sorted, core share calculated, and five reasons stated.

Part 2 Adaptation: when to change the offer 10 hApply: calculate

Adapting costs money. It pays when the extra sales exceed the cost within a time limit.

  • Cost of change, one-off
  • Extra yearly profit
  • Payback = cost / extra profit

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Sales in the market are 20m a year at a 25% margin = 5m profit. Adapting the pack size and taste costs 2m once. Sales are expected to rise 15% to 23m. Extra profit = 3m x 25% = 0.75m a year. Payback = 2 / 0.75 = 2.7 years. Time limit is 3 years, so adapt. If sales rose only 10% (2m x 25% = 0.5m), payback = 4 years and the plan fails.

Activity: Evaluate three adaptation options against a 3-year limit and rank them.

Standard: Three paybacks correct and the ranking agrees with the limit.

Part 3 Aggregation: pooling to gain scale 9 hApply: calculate

Selling one standard product, or sharing a plant, spreads fixed cost across more units. The cost per unit falls.

  • Unit cost = fixed cost / units + variable cost
  • More units, lower unit cost
  • Check where extra volume stops helping

Worked example: Fixed cost 6m, variable cost 4 per unit. At 1m units: 6 + 4 = 10 per unit. At 3m units: 2 + 4 = 6 per unit. Saving 4 per unit, which is 40%. At 6m units: 1 + 4 = 5. Going from 3m to 6m saves only 1 more. The first extra volume matters most. Pooling three countries to 3m units makes sense; pooling to 6m yields little.

Activity: Calculate unit cost at four volumes and find the point where further saving is under 10%.

Standard: Four unit costs correct and the stopping point identified with working.

Part 4 Arbitrage: profiting from differences 9 hApply: calculate

Prices, wages and rules differ by country. A firm can earn from the gap if costs of moving goods or work do not eat it.

  • Margin = sell price - buy cost - transport - duty
  • Check the gap is stable
  • Beware of rules that close the gap

Worked example: Buy at 8 in country A and sell at 12 in country B. Transport 1.5, duty 10% of 12 = 1.2. Margin = 12 - 8 - 1.5 - 1.2 = 1.3 per unit, which is 16% on cost of 8. On 500,000 units that is 650,000. If duty rises to 20%, duty = 2.4 and margin = 0.1, almost nothing. The plan depends on a rule staying fixed.

Activity: Find the margin of three arbitrage routes and test each with a doubled duty.

Standard: Six margins correct and the weakest route named.

Part 5 Choosing the mix of the three moves 9 hAnalyse: score

For each product we decide how much to adapt, aggregate or arbitrage. A simple score helps.

  • Score each move 1 to 5 for each product
  • Weight by importance
  • Pick the highest weighted score

Worked example: Product X: adapt 4, aggregate 2, arbitrage 3. Weights from firm aims: adapt 40%, aggregate 40%, arbitrage 20%. Adapt = 0.4 x 4 = 1.6, aggregate = 0.4 x 2 = 0.8, arbitrage = 0.2 x 3 = 0.6. Total = 3.0 out of 5. Highest part: adapt. Product Y: 2, 5, 2 gives 0.8 + 2.0 + 0.4 = 3.2, where aggregate leads.

Activity: Score three products on the three moves and state the lead move for each.

Standard: All weighted scores correct and the lead move named for each.

Part 6 Rooted in place: people and partners 9 hAnalyse: assess

Local staff and partners carry local knowledge. A firm that is rooted hires, buys and partners locally where it matters.

  • Share of local managers
  • Share of local suppliers
  • Link to a local result

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Country B office: 40 managers, 28 local. Local share = 28 / 40 = 70%. Suppliers: 50 firms, 30 local = 60%. Local spend 12m of 20m = 60%. A target of 75% local managers means 30 managers, which is 2 more. Hiring cost 2 x 60,000 = 120,000. The 2 hires are justified if local insight adds more than 120,000 of profit. Suppose the office also has sales of 20m at a 25% margin: a 5% sales rise adds 0.05 x 20m x 0.25 = 250,000, which clears it.

Activity: Set a local-share target for managers and suppliers in one country and cost it.

Standard: Shares correct, a target set, cost computed and one benefit estimated.

Part 7 Trade-offs: pulling the three moves together 10 hEvaluate: judge

The three moves pull in different directions. Adapting raises cost, aggregating reduces fit, arbitrage may harm trust. We judge the net result.

  • Add up profit from each move
  • Subtract the side costs
  • Check against the core

Worked example: Plan A (adapt): profit +0.75m, cost of change 0.5m a year spread, net 0.25m. Aggregate: saves 4 per unit on 3m = 12m, but lowers local fit, estimated sales loss 2m at 25% margin = 0.5m, net 11.5m. Arbitrage: 0.65m profit, but a customer trust risk of 0.3m, net 0.35m. Best: aggregate. Check: aggregation touches 3 of 70 core items, which breaks the core, so it is limited to flex items.

Activity: Evaluate three plans with profit, side cost and core check, and recommend one.

Standard: Net values correct, a core check done and a clear recommendation.

Part 8 Writing the global strategy statement 10 hCreate: write and defend

The statement brings together core, flex, scale and arbitrage in one page. A board should be able to see what stays and what moves.

  • State the core in one paragraph
  • List three adaptations, three pooled items, one arbitrage
  • Give numbers and a review date

Worked example: Statement: 'Our core is quality, fair price and fast service. We adapt flavour in 3 markets (payback 2.7 years), pool packaging in 3 countries (unit cost from 10 to 6), and buy in one low-cost country (margin 1.3 a unit).' Net values from module 7 sum: 0.25m + 11.5m + 0.35m = 12.1m. Review every 12 months, with a stop rule if duty exceeds an agreed limit (for example 15%).

Activity: Write and present the one-page strategy statement for a firm in 5 minutes with a short question session.

Standard: Core stated, all three moves quantified, a review date, and the total benefit correct.

ten checks

Check yourself

  1. A firm sorts 100 features into 70 core and 30 flex. What is the core share, and what happens if 5 core items move to flex?
    70%; core falls to 65% and the rule says do not do it unless customers across markets accept the change. (Covered in part 1: Rooted cosmopolitanism: core and flex. To pass that part: All 30 sorted, core share calculated, and five reasons stated.)
  2. Adapting costs 2m and adds 0.75m profit a year. What is the payback?
    2 / 0.75 = about 2.7 years. (Covered in part 2: Adaptation: when to change the offer. To pass that part: Three paybacks correct and the ranking agrees with the limit.)
  3. If sales rise only 10%, extra profit is 0.5m. Payback on a 2m cost?
    4 years. (Covered in part 2: Adaptation: when to change the offer. To pass that part: Three paybacks correct and the ranking agrees with the limit.)
  4. Fixed cost 6m and variable cost 4. What is the unit cost at 3m units?
    6/3 + 4 = 6. (Covered in part 3: Aggregation: pooling to gain scale. To pass that part: Four unit costs correct and the stopping point identified with working.)
  5. Why does going from 3m to 6m units save less than going from 1m to 3m?
    Fixed cost per unit shrinks by smaller amounts each time; 3m to 6m saves only 1 per unit. (Covered in part 3: Aggregation: pooling to gain scale. To pass that part: Four unit costs correct and the stopping point identified with working.)
  6. Buy at 8, sell at 12, transport 1.5, duty 10% of 12. What is the margin per unit?
    12 - 8 - 1.5 - 1.2 = 1.3. (Covered in part 4: Arbitrage: profiting from differences. To pass that part: Six margins correct and the weakest route named.)
  7. Why is the arbitrage margin fragile?
    If duty doubles to 2.4 the margin falls to 0.1, so it depends on rules staying fixed. (Covered in part 4: Arbitrage: profiting from differences. To pass that part: Six margins correct and the weakest route named.)
  8. Scores adapt 4, aggregate 2, arbitrage 3 with weights 40, 40, 20. What is the total?
    1.6 + 0.8 + 0.6 = 3.0. (Covered in part 5: Choosing the mix of the three moves. To pass that part: All weighted scores correct and the lead move named for each.)
  9. 28 of 40 managers are local. What share is that and how many more are needed for 75%?
    70%; 75% of 40 is 30, so 2 more. (Covered in part 6: Rooted in place: people and partners. To pass that part: Shares correct, a target set, cost computed and one benefit estimated.)
  10. Pooling saves 12m but loses 0.5m of profit from fit. What is the net and what must be checked?
    11.5m net; check it does not break the core. (Covered in part 7: Trade-offs: pulling the three moves together. To pass that part: Net values correct, a core check done and a clear recommendation.)

slides L1 to L7

The seven-level ladder, slide by slide

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: Core and flex. A global firm keeps a fixed core and flexes everything else by place. Speaker note: Say: ask learners to name one thing their firm would never change. Check: sort five items. Mistake: calling everything core.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Adapting with a payback test. Adaptation costs money now and earns later. Payback equals cost divided by extra yearly profit. Speaker note: Say: do the 2m example. Check: payback against limit. Mistake: using revenue instead of profit.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Pooling for scale. Fixed cost spread over more units cuts unit cost, with smaller gains each time. Speaker note: Say: show three volumes. Check: compute unit costs. Mistake: ignoring the fit lost.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Arbitrage margins. Gap between buy and sell price, minus transport and duty, is the margin. Rules can close it. Speaker note: Say: change duty and watch the margin fall. Check: compute margin. Mistake: forgetting duty on selling price.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Scoring the mix. Score each move for each product, weight by the firm's aims and pick the leader. Speaker note: Say: keep weights visible. Check: totals. Mistake: copying the same scores for every product.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Rooted in people. Local managers and suppliers bring local insight. Set a target and cost it. Speaker note: Say: compute a share. Check: cost of 2 hires. Mistake: targets with no benefit estimate.
  7. Slide 7: L7 The strategy statement. Core, three moves with numbers, review date and a stop rule. Speaker note: Say: show a completed page. Check: do the numbers sum? Mistake: leaving the core out.

jobs this leads to

Where this takes you

  • Chief strategy officer. Sets how the firm balances global scale and local fit.
  • Country manager. Adapts the offer to the local market within the firm's core.
  • Global product manager. Decides which product features are shared and which vary.
  • Sourcing and trade manager. Finds and tests price, wage and cost gaps between countries.
