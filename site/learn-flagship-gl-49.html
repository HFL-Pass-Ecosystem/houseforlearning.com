Cryobank Operations (Awareness Level)

Understand how biological sample banks are run: consent, labelling, cold storage, records, security and ethics. Awareness only.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2 Awareness16:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain what a cryobank stores, why consent matters and how a sample is traced.
  • Apply Complete a sample record and label with a two-person check.
  • Analyse Examine the cold chain and identify risks with controls.
  • Evaluate Judge a bank's security, privacy and ethics policies for gaps.
  • Create Draft a daily operations checklist and run a mock audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Create a daily operations checklist and a sample record template for a practice cryobank, and run a mock audit.

Condition

A computer, practice forms and a mentor. No real samples, personal data or hazardous materials are used.

Standard

Checklist covers temperature, alarms, labelling, access and records; mock audit finds at least 3 planted errors; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Lab trainees, hospital store staff, health administrators. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What a cryobank holds 2 hUnderstand

A cryobank is a managed store where biological samples are kept at about minus 80 to minus 196 degrees Celsius so they stay stable until an agreed use. It is a records business as much as a freezer business: a sample is only valuable if it can be found, identified and released to the right person.

  • Common holdings are blood cells, tissue pieces, cord blood, reproductive cells and research samples, each with storage rules and legal limits that differ by place.
  • Liquid nitrogen tanks hold near minus 196 degrees Celsius and mechanical freezers near minus 80, and the bank chooses by sample type and time kept.
  • Each sample has a custodian, a stated purpose, a storage period and written conditions for release or disposal.
  • Traceability means any sample can be located in under 10 minutes and its identity proved from the record.
Worked example. A family stores cord blood for 20 years. The contract lists the storage period (20 years), who may ask for release (the guardian, then the child at 18), the renewal steps and where samples go if the bank closes. The record shows tank 2, rack B, box 4, slot 7.

Activity. Write a one-paragraph summary of what a cryobank stores, why consent matters and how one sample is found again, using the five contract items above.

Standard to meet. Name consent, storage conditions and traceability in the summary, and use at least four of the contract items.

Module 2 Consent and ownership 2 hUnderstand

Consent means a person understands what will happen to a sample and agrees freely. Without valid consent a bank cannot properly store, use or release a sample, so consent is checked before anything enters the freezer.

  • A plain-language form covers purpose, storage length, research use, risks, costs, disposal and the right to withdraw, written so the person can follow it.
  • A child's sample needs guardian consent and a dated plan to ask the person again at adulthood, often at 18, depending on local rules.
  • Each choice is a separate tick box and research use defaults to No, so silence never counts as agreement.
  • The signed form is scanned, given the sample ID and linked to the record; a withdrawal is logged with its date and acted on within the stated time.
Worked example. A donor receives a form with 6 boxes. Research use defaults to No; she ticks Yes, anonymised only, signs and dates it. The reviewer matches form ID C-2031 to the label, finds all 6 boxes complete and files a scan the same day. Questions about local law go to a qualified legal adviser.

Activity. Review a practice consent form and list four gaps, each with a suggested fix and the reason it matters.

Standard to meet. Find four real gaps, each with a fix; invent no gap and give no legal advice.

Module 3 Labelling and records 2 hApply

A sample without a correct label is a lost sample. Labels and records are the bank's memory, so every entry follows one fixed pattern and a second person checks it.

  • Every record holds a unique ID, date, sample type, volume, consent reference and full location in the order tank, rack, box, slot.
  • Two people check entry and removal: one reads the label aloud, the other reads the record, and both initial and time-stamp it.
  • Use a barcode plus a handwritten backup, because frost and wear can make a barcode unreadable.
  • Never reuse, overwrite or delete an ID; a mistake is crossed with one line, initialled and explained.
Worked example. Operator A logs sample C-2031 as 2.0 ml cord blood, consent ref F-118, tank 2, rack B, box 4, slot 7. Checker B scans the barcode, reads the handwritten backup and both agree. Both sets of initials and the time 09:40 go on the record before the sample moves.

Activity. Fill five practice records with the full pattern, then swap with a peer who checks each one against its label.

Standard to meet. Record zero errors across five records, and give every record two sets of initials and a time.

Module 4 Cold chain and alarms 2 hAnalyse

The cold chain is the unbroken run of safe temperatures from collection to release. One failure can damage a whole tank within hours, so risks are found early and each gets a control and an owner.

  • Read and log temperature and nitrogen level at a set time each day, and compare today's figure with the last 7 days to spot drift.
  • Alarms need a call-out list of at least 3 names, a rule that someone answers within 15 minutes, and backup power for sensors.
  • Transport uses validated containers with a temperature logger, and the printout is attached to the record on arrival.
  • Test every alarm monthly and note the date, the result and who tested it.
Worked example. A tank level falls from 60 percent to 52 percent in 7 days, faster than the usual 2 points a week. The log shows the trend, the owner orders a lid check and a refill, and the level returns to 60 percent. A night alarm at 02:10 reaches the second name when the first does not answer.

Activity. Mark five cold-chain risks on a practice floor plan and write one control and one named owner for each.

Standard to meet. Give every risk a control, an owner and a test date; leave none as 'be careful'.

Module 5 Safety for staff 2 hApply

Extreme cold and nitrogen are real hazards: cold burns, and oxygen loss in closed rooms. This module gives awareness only; real handling needs formal training from a qualified safety professional.

  • Wear insulated cryogenic gloves, a face shield and an apron when opening a tank, and keep sleeves and trousers covering skin.
  • An oxygen sensor in each nitrogen room sounds an alarm when oxygen falls below its set level, set a little below normal air level, and the room is ventilated.
  • Never work alone in the store; use a buddy rule and a sign-in sheet, and learn two evacuation routes.
  • For a cold burn, staff follow the site first-aid plan and call the trained first aider or emergency services rather than improvise treatment.
Worked example. The oxygen alarm sounds at 10:15. Both staff leave at once and close the door behind them, and the supervisor switches on ventilation from outside. They return only when a trained person confirms the portable monitor shows a safe reading. Time out of the room is 12 minutes and is logged.

Activity. Write five specific safety rules and one emergency drill that names who does what in the first 5 minutes.

Standard to meet. Write specific and measurable rules, and name a person for every step of the drill; advise no medical treatment.

Module 6 Security and privacy 2 hEvaluate

Samples and the data linked to them are sensitive, so access is limited, logged and reviewed. A policy is judged by whether it can be checked, not by how strong its wording sounds.

  • Badge access is given by role; the list of badge holders is reviewed every 3 months and leavers are removed within 1 day.
  • Cameras cover doors and storage rooms but never private areas; visitors sign a log and are escorted at all times.
  • Identity data is kept apart from sample codes, only authorised staff can join the two, and every view is logged.
  • A breach, such as a lost badge or a wrongly shared record, is reported the same day to a named person and written up.
Worked example. A reviewer scores a practice policy. Strengths (3): badge by role, escorted visitors, logged record views. Gaps (3): no leaver deadline, no breach contact, no log review. She suggests removal in 1 day, a named contact and a monthly log check.

Activity. Review a practice security policy and list three strengths and three gaps, each with a suggested fix.

Standard to meet. Quote the policy line that each strength and gap refers to, and write fixes that are specific and testable.

Module 7 Ethics, fairness and cost 2 hEvaluate

A bank asks people to trust it for years, often at a hopeful or worrying time. Ethics means honest claims, clear costs and fair treatment, so a person can decide without pressure.

  • Fees, renewal terms and extra charges are shown in writing before signing, with a cooling-off time to think.
  • No promise of future cures is made; claims about benefit use careful words and point people to qualified health professionals.
  • An exit plan explains where samples go if the bank closes, who pays, and how owners are told, with plenty of notice.
  • Staff respect different cultural, faith and family views about tissue and explain choices in plain language, in the person's language where possible.
Worked example. A leaflet says 'may help treat many diseases'. The reviewer flags it as a misleading claim and rewrites it: 'Stored samples have limited, specific uses. Ask your doctor what applies to you.' The leaflet also has no exit plan, so the reviewer adds a question about it.

Activity. Write five ethics questions you would ask a bank, each tied to fees, claims, exit plans, consent or fair treatment.

Standard to meet. Write five fair and specific questions that cover at least four of the five themes.

Module 8 Checklist and mock audit 2 hCreate

Routine keeps samples safe, and an audit proves the routine works. You will build a checklist and then test it by finding errors planted by a peer.

  • Split tasks by rhythm: daily (temperature, level, alarm lights, door log), weekly (alarm test, label spot-check of 10 samples), monthly (record review, backup power test).
  • Each line has a tick, initials, a time and a space for a problem; a blank line means not done.
  • An audit trail follows 3 samples from consent to location and checks every signature on the way.
  • Each fault gets a corrective action with an owner and a due date, and the checklist is reviewed monthly.
Worked example. A peer plants 3 errors: a missing second-person check, a temperature entry made 4 hours late, and an unlabelled box. The auditor finds all 3 in 25 minutes and writes actions: retrain on the two-person check by Friday, set a 09:00 reminder, relabel and verify with a second person today.

Activity. Draft the checklist, plant three errors in a practice record set for a peer, then run the audit and write the report.

Standard to meet. Cover temperature, alarms, labelling, access and records in the checklist; find all three planted errors, each with an action, owner and date.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What does a cryobank store?

Model answer: It stores biological samples such as cells, tissue, cord blood, reproductive cells and research samples at very low temperatures, with records showing who owns each sample, why it is kept and how it can be found again. (Covered in module 1: What a cryobank holds. To pass that module: Name consent, storage conditions and traceability in the summary, and use at least four of the contract items.)

Q2. Why are two-person checks used?

Model answer: A second person reads the label against the record, which catches a wrong ID or slot before a sample moves, and two sets of initials show who was responsible. (Covered in module 3: Labelling and records. To pass that module: Record zero errors across five records, and give every record two sets of initials and a time.)

Model answer: Never work alone in a nitrogen store and leave at once if the oxygen alarm sounds, because nitrogen can push out air in a closed room. (Covered in module 5: Safety for staff. To pass that module: Write specific and measurable rules, and name a person for every step of the drill; advise no medical treatment.)

Q5. Who may access identity data?

Model answer: Only authorised staff whose role needs it, with every view logged, and with identity kept apart from sample codes. (Covered in module 6: Security and privacy. To pass that module: Quote the policy line that each strength and gap refers to, and write fixes that are specific and testable.)

Q6. What does valid consent include?

Model answer: Purpose, how long the sample is kept, any research use, risks, costs, disposal and the right to withdraw, in plain language and signed and dated. (Covered in module 2: Consent and ownership. To pass that module: Find four real gaps, each with a fix; invent no gap and give no legal advice.)

Q7. Why keep backup labels?

Model answer: Frost, cold and wear can make a barcode unreadable, so a handwritten label and paper record still let staff prove identity. (Covered in module 3: Labelling and records. To pass that module: Record zero errors across five records, and give every record two sets of initials and a time.)

Q8. What is an exit plan?

Model answer: A written plan for moving samples to another licensed bank if the bank closes, saying who pays and how owners are told. (Covered in module 7: Ethics, fairness and cost. To pass that module: Write five fair and specific questions that cover at least four of the five themes.)

Q9. What is an audit trail?

Model answer: A dated record of who did what and when, so a reviewer can follow a sample from consent to storage and release. (Covered in module 8: Checklist and mock audit. To pass that module: Cover temperature, alarms, labelling, access and records in the checklist; find all three planted errors, each with an action, owner and date.)

Q10. Why keep logs?

Model answer: Logs show that checks happened, reveal trends such as falling nitrogen, and let auditors find faults and prove they were fixed. (Covered in module 8: Checklist and mock audit. To pass that module: Cover temperature, alarms, labelling, access and records in the checklist; find all three planted errors, each with an action, owner and date.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Laws for storing human tissue and data differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
