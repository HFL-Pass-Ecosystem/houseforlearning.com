Cryobank Operations (Awareness Level)

Understand how biological sample banks are run: consent, labelling, cold storage, records, security and ethics. Awareness only.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2 Awareness16:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain what a cryobank stores and why consent matters.
  • Apply Fill a sample record and label correctly.
  • Analyse Find risks in the cold chain.
  • Evaluate Judge a bank's policies for security and fairness.
  • Create Draft a daily operations checklist.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Create a daily operations checklist and a sample record template for a practice cryobank, and run a mock audit.

Condition

A computer, practice forms and a mentor. No real samples, personal data or hazardous materials are used.

Standard

Checklist covers temperature, alarms, labelling, access and records; mock audit finds at least 3 planted errors; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Lab trainees, hospital store staff, health administrators. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What a cryobank holds 2 hUnderstand

Banks store cells, tissue or cord blood at very low temperatures.

  • Used for later treatment or research under consent.
  • Cold storage pauses biological activity.
  • Each sample has an owner and conditions of use.
Worked example. A family stores cord blood; a contract states how long and who can request it.

Activity. Write a one-paragraph summary.

Standard to meet. Summary names consent, storage and use.

Module 2 Consent and ownership 2 hUnderstand

People must know what they agree to.

  • Consent in plain language; the right to withdraw.
  • Rules for research use and disposal.
  • Children's samples need guardian consent and review at adulthood in many places.
Worked example. A form has a tick box for research use that defaults to No.

Activity. Review a sample consent form.

Standard to meet. Four gaps identified.

Module 3 Labelling and records 2 hApply

A sample without a correct label is lost.

  • Unique ID, date, type, volume, location.
  • Two-person check at entry and removal.
  • Barcode and a paper backup.
Worked example. ID C-2031 is logged in tank 2, rack B, box 4, slot 7.

Activity. Fill 5 practice records.

Standard to meet. No errors.

Module 4 Cold chain and alarms 2 hAnalyse

Temperature is the main risk.

  • Daily log of temperature and nitrogen or power levels.
  • Alarms with call-out lists; backup power.
  • Transport in validated containers.
Worked example. A night alarm leads to a refill within 20 minutes.

Activity. Mark risks on a floor plan.

Standard to meet. Five risks with controls.

Module 5 Safety for staff 2 hApply

Extreme cold and gases are hazards.

  • Gloves, face shield, ventilation.
  • Oxygen sensors in rooms with nitrogen.
  • Never work alone.
Worked example. Staff leave the room immediately when the oxygen alarm sounds.

Activity. Write five safety rules.

Standard to meet. Rules are specific.

Module 6 Security and privacy 2 hEvaluate

Access is limited and logged.

  • Badge access, camera at doors, visitor log.
  • Only authorised staff see identity data.
  • Data is stored securely and deleted per rule.
Worked example. Staff must show a badge and sign the log for each entry.

Activity. Review a policy.

Standard to meet. Strengths and gaps listed.

Module 7 Ethics, fairness and cost 2 hEvaluate

Commercial banks must be honest.

  • Clear fees; no pressure.
  • No misleading claims about future cures.
  • Exit plan if a bank closes.
Worked example. A bank has a written plan to transfer samples if it closes.

Activity. List five ethics questions.

Standard to meet. Questions are fair.

Module 8 Checklist and mock audit 2 hCreate

Practise audit.

  • Daily, weekly, monthly tasks.
  • Audit trail and corrective action.
  • Review each month.
Worked example. The audit finds a missing second-person check and a late log.

Activity. Run a mock audit.

Standard to meet. Findings logged.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What does a cryobank store?

Model answer: Cells, tissue or cord blood at low temperatures. (Covered in module 1: What a cryobank holds. To pass that module: Summary names consent, storage and use.)

Q2. Why two-person checks?

Model answer: They catch labelling mistakes. (Covered in module 2: Consent and ownership. To pass that module: Four gaps identified.)

Q3. Name a cold-chain risk.

Model answer: Power failure or low nitrogen. (Covered in module 3: Labelling and records. To pass that module: No errors.)

Q4. Name a safety rule.

Model answer: Never work alone; use ventilation. (Covered in module 4: Cold chain and alarms. To pass that module: Five risks with controls.)

Q5. Who may access data?

Model answer: Authorised staff only. (Covered in module 5: Safety for staff. To pass that module: Rules are specific.)

Q6. What does consent include?

Model answer: Use, duration, research, right to withdraw. (Covered in module 6: Security and privacy. To pass that module: Strengths and gaps listed.)

Q7. Why backup labels?

Model answer: Barcodes can fail. (Covered in module 7: Ethics, fairness and cost. To pass that module: Questions are fair.)

Q8. What is an exit plan?

Model answer: Steps to move samples if the bank closes. (Covered in module 8: Checklist and mock audit. To pass that module: Findings logged.)

Q9. What is an audit trail?

Model answer: Records showing who did what and when. (Covered in module 8: Checklist and mock audit. To pass that module: Findings logged.)

Q10. Why logs?

Model answer: To show control and find faults. (Covered in module 8: Checklist and mock audit. To pass that module: Findings logged.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Laws for storing human tissue and data differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
