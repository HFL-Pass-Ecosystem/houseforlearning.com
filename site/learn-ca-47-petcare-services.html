Petcare Services

Run a real petcare Services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small petcare Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: health, care, wellness and education. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know pets and petcare work 6 hUnderstand

Petcare services help owners look after animals. Learn what pets need and what each service does.

  • Name common services: walking, sitting, grooming, training, day care.
  • Describe five basic needs of a pet: food, water, rest, exercise, health care.
  • Explain the signs of a happy pet and a stressed pet.
  • List words: leash, vaccine record, crate, allergy, temperament.
  • Identify which of the five needs a client's pet meets poorly and which service helps.
  • Compare dog, cat and small-animal care by time per visit and special needs.
  • Ask an owner what a good day for their pet looks like and write it down.
Worked example. A dog needs a walk twice a day. A walker meets the owner, learns the dog's habits and writes them on a care card. Two walks of 30 minutes make 1 hour a day, or 7 hours a week.

Activity. Visit a local pet shop or shelter and interview one worker about the daily tasks.

Standard to meet. Task: list services and pet needs. Condition: after one visit. Standard: five services and five needs correctly named.

Module 2 Read animal behaviour and stay safe 6 hApply

Safe work starts with reading the animal. Many bites happen when signs are missed.

  • Describe warning signs: stiff body, pinned ears, growl, hiding, tail tucked.
  • Apply calm handling: slow moves, no cornering, let the animal sniff first.
  • Check the first-aid basics for pets and know the nearest emergency vet.
  • Explain why you never handle an animal you are not trained for.
  • Describe the signs of heat stress in a dog and the steps to cool it.
  • Apply the rule that a dog stays on a leash and a cat travels in a closed carrier.
  • Use voice and distance first if two dogs meet badly; never put a hand between them.
Worked example. A dog backs away and licks its lips when a stranger leans over it. This is stress. The walker steps back, speaks softly and lets the dog come closer. In ten minutes at the park, 2 of 5 dogs showed stress signs, which is 40%.

Activity. Watch ten minutes of dogs in a park. Write down five body signs and what each may mean.

Standard to meet. Task: read animal signals. Condition: ten minutes of observation. Standard: five signs matched to likely feelings.

Module 3 Follow the rules in Canada 10 hApply

Animal and business rules are set by cities, provinces and the federal level. Always check with the local authority.

  • Check local rules on dog licences, leash laws, noise and the number of animals at a home.
  • Explain that a home business may need a city business licence and zoning approval; check with your city.
  • Describe animal welfare laws: neglect and cruelty must be reported as the law asks.
  • Check the federal and destination rules for health papers before travelling with a pet across a border.
  • Ask your city whether a home-based pet service needs a business licence and write the answer.
  • Check whether day care or boarding needs a vaccine record under provincial or local rules.
  • Find the off-leash and park rules for your city.
Worked example. Rules table row: Dog licence | city | owner shows tag number | before the first walk | copy in client file. If the licence takes 10 business days and the first walk was planned for day 7, move the walk to day 10 or later.

Activity. Make a rules table for your city, with the official page and date for each row.

Standard to meet. Task: list the rules for a petcare business. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.

Module 4 Do the core care task well 8 hEvaluate

Pick one service and do it to a written method. Small, careful tasks build trust.

  • Build a care card: feeding, medicine, habits, fears, vet contact, owner contact.
  • Apply a walk method: leash check, route, water, waste pickup, return and report.
  • Compare ways to do a task such as bathing or brushing and choose the safest.
  • Record each visit with time, what happened, and anything unusual.
  • Practice giving medicine exactly as the owner wrote it, only with written consent.
  • Measure walk time with a watch and compare it with the time on the visit note.
  • Check the house before leaving: door locked, gates closed, water bowl full.
Worked example. Visit note: arrived 4:00, walked 30 minutes, dog ate half its dinner, no limp, left water. The owner sees it by message that evening. Five practice visits of 30 minutes each take 5 x 30 = 150 minutes, or 2.5 hours.

Activity. Do five practice visits with a friend's pet and write a visit note for each.

Standard to meet. Task: complete a care visit. Condition: with a care card and permission. Standard: all card items done and a note written the same day.

Module 5 Price your service and plan your week 8 hApply

A fair price covers your time, travel and costs. Many small sitters forget insurance and unpaid time.

  • Compute cost per visit: time, travel, supplies, insurance, tax set-aside.
  • Compare local prices for ten similar services before you choose yours.
  • Build a weekly plan with travel time between visits.
  • Check what happens when you are sick: have a back-up person.
  • Calculate pay per hour by dividing margin by the hours of the visit plus travel.
  • Compare a fixed price with a price by visit length.
  • Set aside a share of margin for tax as a stated assumption and check it with the tax agency.
Worked example. Visit price 30 CAD, travel and supplies 5. Margin 25 per visit. With fixed costs of 400 a month, break-even is 400 / 25 = 16 visits. At 15 visits a week (60 a month), margin is 60 x 25 = 1,500, minus 400 leaves 1,100 before tax. Teaching numbers only.

Activity. Build a cost sheet and a weekly timetable for 15 visits.

Standard to meet. Task: price a service. Condition: all costs counted. Standard: break-even visits are correct and shown with units.

Module 6 Find clients and build trust 6 hCreate

Pet owners trust people who are clear, kind and reliable. Referrals matter most.

  • Build a one-page offer: service, area, price, hours, what you do in an emergency.
  • Create a meet-and-greet routine: meet owner and pet before the first job.
  • Write a simple service agreement with cancellation, payment and emergency vet permission.
  • Track the funnel: enquiries, meetings, bookings, repeat clients.
  • Ask three pet owners what made them trust their last sitter.
  • Write what you say when a request is outside your skill, such as a sick or aggressive animal.
  • Collect a short written review from happy clients, with permission.
Worked example. Four weeks: 20 enquiries, 10 meetings (50%), 6 bookings (60%), 4 repeat (67%). The weakest step is enquiry to meeting; reply faster and test a short phone call.

Activity. Contact 20 people or local groups and book 5 meet-and-greets.

Standard to meet. Task: win first clients. Condition: four weeks. Standard: funnel numbers recorded and a signed agreement for each booking.

Module 7 Handle emergencies and protect everyone 6 hEvaluate

Plan for the bad day before it comes. A calm plan saves animals and trust.

  • Write an emergency plan: who to call, nearest vet, how to get the pet there.
  • Check insurance options for business and animal care; ask a licensed adviser.
  • Apply keys and data safety: label keys by code, not address, and store them safely.
  • Explain how to handle a lost pet: search steps, notices, owner contact.
  • Identify the nearest 24-hour emergency vet and write the phone and route on the emergency sheet.
  • Check that the client signed permission for vet care before you take a pet.
  • Practice a calm report to the owner: what happened, what you did, what is next.
Worked example. Dog slips its collar at 4:10. Walker stays calm, calls the owner at 4:12, searches the area, posts a notice at 4:45, and finds the dog at 6:30, 2 hours 20 minutes later. An incident note is written the same day.

Activity. Write an emergency sheet and a one-page privacy note, and practice the lost-pet steps with a partner.

Standard to meet. Task: respond to an incident. Condition: practice drill. Standard: owner called within minutes and a full incident note is written.

Module 8 Run the service for real 8 hCreate

Now run the service for real with clients, records and a plan.

  • Run a 60-day pilot with a stop rule agreed in advance.
  • Review weekly: visits, pay, complaints, pet health notes.
  • Create a 12-month plan with licences, insurance and growth steps.
  • Lead others: train a helper with your care card and visit note method.
  • Calculate visits per week and income per hour at the end of the pilot.
  • Compare results with the stop rule on the planned date and record the decision.
  • Identify one risk to fix before adding clients, such as travel time or one demanding pet.
Worked example. Stop rule: if fewer than 40 visits in 60 days, stop and write lessons. Result: 70 visits, which is 70 x 25 = 1,750 in margin, minus 800 fixed costs for two months = 950. Two repeat clients; add a helper in steps.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and present your plan to three people.

Standard to meet. Task: run a petcare pilot. Condition: 60 days, real clients. Standard: results compared with the stop rule and rules re-checked with the city.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
