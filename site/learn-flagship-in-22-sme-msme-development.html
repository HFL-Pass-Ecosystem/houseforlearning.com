flagship course

SME and MSME Development

Start, formalise and grow a small enterprise: choosing a legal form, registration, records, cash flow, markets, compliance and government support schemes.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 45 h lessons plus practice

Start, formalise and grow a small enterprise: choosing a legal form, registration, records, cash flow, markets, compliance and government support schemes.

  • Define the enterprise type, size class and legal form.
  • Apply registration, licences and daily records.
  • Analyse cost, margin, break-even and cash flow.
  • Evaluate funding options and support schemes.
  • Create a growth plan with dated actions.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare a registration-ready, finance-ready growth plan for a real or practice small enterprise.

Condition

About 10 weeks part-time. A real or practice enterprise, access to government portals, a spreadsheet, a mentor.

Standard

Documents are complete, cash flow is forecast for 12 months with every figure traced to a source, compliance dates are on a calendar, and the plan has dated actions.

Who it is for: Founders, artisans, shop owners, farm-based producers, development workers. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. An enterprise idea or a running small business. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Enterprise types and size classes 4 hDefine
  • Know the size classes: micro, small and medium, set by investment and turnover limits in current rules.
  • Compare sole proprietor, partnership, limited liability partnership and company.
  • Match the form to liability, tax, number of owners and funding needs.
  • Check the latest limits on the official portal before deciding.
Worked example. A tailoring unit with one owner, 6 workers and yearly sales of 18 lakh fits a sole proprietorship in the micro class. Two partners adding machines may prefer a partnership.

Activity. Choose a legal form and a size class for your enterprise and justify them.

Standard to meet. Cite the current limits in your choice and name one risk of the form chosen.

Module 2 Registration and licences 6 hApply
  • Register on the government portal for small enterprises with an identity number and business activity codes.
  • Check if GST registration is needed by turnover and type of sale.
  • List local licences: shop and establishment, food safety, trade or pollution consent as relevant.
  • Keep scanned and printed copies and a renewal date for each.
Worked example. A food unit needs registration as a micro enterprise, food safety licence, local trade licence and GST once turnover passes the state threshold. Each gets a renewal date.

Activity. Build a registration and licence checklist for your enterprise.

Standard to meet. List every document with the issuing office, documents needed and renewal date.

Module 3 Records and bookkeeping 6 hApply
  • Record every sale and expense on the day in a cash book.
  • Separate personal money from business money in a bank account.
  • Keep bills, invoices and receipts in dated folders.
  • Close the books monthly and compare with the bank statement.
Worked example. Opening cash Rs 20,000; sales Rs 90,000; expenses Rs 62,000; closing cash should be Rs 48,000. If the bank shows Rs 45,000, find the Rs 3,000 gap.

Activity. Keep a cash book for 30 days and reconcile it to bank.

Standard to meet. Match the closing balance to the bank, or explain each difference.

Module 4 Costing, pricing and break-even 6 hAnalyse
  • Split costs into fixed (rent, salary) and variable (material, packing).
  • Work out cost per unit and margin.
  • Break-even units equal fixed costs divided by selling price minus variable cost.
  • Test price changes of 5 to 10 percent against sales volume.
Worked example. Fixed cost Rs 30,000 a month; price Rs 150 a unit; variable cost Rs 90 a unit. Margin Rs 60 a unit. Break-even is 500 units a month.

Activity. Calculate cost per unit, margin and break-even for one product.

Standard to meet. Calculate correctly and take each input from a bill or quote.

Module 5 Cash flow and working capital 6 hAnalyse
  • List expected cash in and cash out for each of 12 months.
  • Include delays: customers pay in 30 to 60 days, suppliers want advance.
  • Find the lowest closing balance and the month it occurs.
  • Plan to cover the gap: faster collection, smaller stock or a working capital loan.
Worked example. Month 4 shows cash in 1.2 lakh and out 1.6 lakh, with opening 0.3 lakh: closing minus 0.1 lakh. A gap of Rs 0.1 lakh (Rs 10,000) must be funded.

Activity. Build a 12-month cash flow forecast with the funding gap highlighted.

Standard to meet. Balance every month and identify the lowest balance with a plan.

Module 6 Funding and support schemes 6 hEvaluate
  • Compare own savings, bank loans, government-backed loan schemes and investor money.
  • Check eligibility, collateral rules and credit guarantee support on official sites.
  • Work out the monthly instalment and total interest.
  • Ask what a lender needs: registration, bank statements, project report.
Worked example. A loan of 5 lakh at 12% for 3 years has an instalment near Rs 16,600 a month, total repaid near Rs 5.98 lakh. The unit must earn that surplus.

Activity. Compare two funding options for your plan and choose one.

Standard to meet. Compare the options, showing the monthly instalment and whether cash flow can bear it.

Module 7 Markets, customers and compliance calendar 5 hApply
  • Find three customer groups and the channels to reach them: local retail, wholesale, online marketplace, government purchase.
  • Register on a government procurement portal if selling to public buyers.
  • File returns and pay dues on time: GST, income tax, provident fund and state insurance if applicable.
  • Put every due date on a shared calendar with a named owner.
Worked example. A craft unit sells 60% locally, 25% via an online marketplace and 15% to a wholesale buyer. Suppose a GST return is due on the 20th (check the current due date with the tax authority); the owner then books the 15th for preparation.

Activity. Produce a market list and a 12-month compliance calendar.

Standard to meet. Build a calendar that covers every filing with a date and owner, and name three groups in the market list.

Module 8 Capstone: growth plan 6 hCreate
  • Combine registration, records, costing, cash flow, funding and markets into one plan.
  • Set three growth goals with numbers and dates.
  • List risks and a response for each.
  • Present to a mentor or lender and record feedback.
Worked example. Plan to raise sales from 6 lakh to 9 lakh in 12 months by adding a second market, with a 2 lakh working capital need and a risk plan for late payments.

Activity. Present the growth plan with all supporting sheets.

Standard to meet. Plan is consistent across sheets, goals are dated and risks have responses.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why does the legal form matter?

Model answer: It sets liability, tax, ownership and how you can raise funds. (Covered in module 1: Enterprise types and size classes. To pass that module: Cite the current limits in your choice and name one risk of the form chosen.)

Q2. Why keep renewal dates?

Model answer: Lapsed licences can stop trade and attract penalties. (Covered in module 2: Registration and licences. To pass that module: List every document with the issuing office, documents needed and renewal date.)

Q3. Why separate personal and business money?

Model answer: Without it, profit and loss cannot be known. (Covered in module 3: Records and bookkeeping. To pass that module: Match the closing balance to the bank, or explain each difference.)

Q4. How is break-even found?

Model answer: Fixed costs divided by selling price minus variable cost. (Covered in module 4: Costing, pricing and break-even. To pass that module: Calculate correctly and take each input from a bill or quote.)

Q5. Why can a profitable business run out of cash?

Model answer: Customers pay late while costs must be paid now. (Covered in module 5: Cash flow and working capital. To pass that module: Balance every month and identify the lowest balance with a plan.)

Q6. What must you check before taking a loan?

Model answer: Whether monthly cash surplus can pay the instalment. (Covered in module 6: Funding and support schemes. To pass that module: Compare the options, showing the monthly instalment and whether cash flow can bear it.)

Q7. Why use a compliance calendar?

Model answer: It prevents missed dates and penalties. (Covered in module 7: Markets, customers and compliance calendar. To pass that module: Build a calendar that covers every filing with a date and owner, and name three groups in the market list.)

Q8. What makes a growth plan credible?

Model answer: Numbers from real records, consistent sheets and dated actions. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: growth plan. To pass that module: Plan is consistent across sheets, goals are dated and risks have responses.)

Q9. What is working capital?

Model answer: Money needed to run daily operations between paying costs and receiving sales. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why check scheme rules on the official site?

Model answer: Limits and conditions change, and old information can be wrong. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know what an enterprise is.
  2. L2 Awareness. Keep simple daily records.
  3. L3 Foundation. Register and file for a micro enterprise.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Cost, price and forecast cash flow.
  5. L5 Specialist. Prepare funding applications and lead growth.
  6. L6 Advanced. Advise many enterprises or run a support programme.
  7. L7 Super expert. Shape enterprise policy and support across a region.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Enterprise basics

Identify the type and size class of 5 sample enterprises from their investment and turnover, and state which one is a micro unit.

Speaker notes. Teacher gives 5 short profiles and checks each is placed in the right class. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Daily records

Keep a daily cash book for 14 days with every sale and expense entered and a closing balance that matches the cash in hand.

Speaker notes. Teacher counts the cash in a tin against the book balance. Covered in module 3, Records and bookkeeping.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Registration and filing

Prepare a registration file for a micro enterprise with 6 required documents and a filing calendar of the next 12 months. Check local rules with an expert.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks every document is listed with a due date, using the official site. Covered in module 2, Registration and licences.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Costing and cash flow

Cost one product, set a price with at least a 20 percent margin, and forecast cash flow for 6 months showing any month with a shortfall.

Speaker notes. Teacher recomputes the break-even point and checks the forecast adds up. Covered in modules 4 and 5.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Funding and growth lead

Prepare a funding file with a 12-month forecast and 3 scheme options compared, and lead a growth plan with 3 dated targets.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks each scheme rule was read from the official source.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Multi-enterprise advising

Advise 5 enterprises over a quarter, using one checklist to score records, compliance and cash, and show a score gain in at least 3.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the score sheets for the first and last visit.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Regional enterprise policy

Draft a regional support proposal with 3 measured aims, a budget table and a yearly review plan, using data from at least 20 enterprises.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks every aim has a number, a date and a data source.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
