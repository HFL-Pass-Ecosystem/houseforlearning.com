Veterinary and Pet Care Business

Veterinary and Pet Care Business: Plan clinic or service operations with welfare standards.

about this course

Veterinary and Pet Care Business: Plan clinic or service operations with welfare standards.

  • Understand Explain who needs Veterinary and Pet Care Business and name one real first user, with evidence from your own market scan.
  • Apply Build and run the practice task: Plan clinic or service operations with welfare standards.
  • Analyse Record every step, cost and problem, then compare the plan with what really happened.
  • Evaluate Judge your result against your goal using numbers, and write three lessons learned.
  • Create Present your evidence to a review room and give two peers written feedback.
  • Create Plan your next 90 days and contribute one template or worked example back to learners.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan clinic or service operations with welfare standards.

Condition

About 60 hours over 1-3 months. Own tools, free spreadsheet and form tools, and a real practice partner. Mentor and peer circle available. Currency written on every number.

Standard

Evidence reviewed by a mentor; at least 70% on the 10-question check; two written peer reviews given; every number carries its currency and unit.

Who it is for, requirements and materials

  • Who it is for: learners aged 16 and over, small-business starters, workers moving to a new field, and teams (see B2B, B2C, D2C).
  • Requirements: finish Online Therapy Support (Awareness Level) first, or show equivalent work.
  • Materials included: lesson text, worked examples, case exercise, 10-question check, templates in My page.
  • Teaching team: a trainer from our network, a mentor who reviews your evidence, and a peer circle. Names are given at enrolment.

lesson by lesson

9 modules, in the order you do them

Module 1 Who keeps pets and what services are missing IdeaStudy · 2 hUnderstand

Goal: choose one pet-care service and find its first customers.

  1. List pet services near you: clinic, grooming, boarding, walking, training, supplies. Note price per visit (with currency).
  2. Find the gap: opening hours, travel to the home, price, a species not served, or care for older animals.
  3. Talk to 5 pet keepers: what service they use, how often, what they pay, what worries them.
  4. Write a one-page service idea: service, customer, price, hours, who is qualified to do each task.

Evidence you keep: the service table and five keeper notes.

Module 2 Kick-off circle IdeaShare and mentoring · 3 hUnderstand

Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.

  1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
  2. Peers ask three questions: who is qualified to do this, how do animals stay safe, what happens in an emergency.
  3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: an operations plan, a welfare standard sheet, a record template, a cost sheet, and a licence check list.

Evidence you keep: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

Module 3 Write the operations plan ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Goal: describe how one day runs, who does what and how animals are kept safe.

  1. Draw the floor plan or round: arrival, waiting, service area, clean area, exit. Separate sick or nervous animals from others.
  2. Write a daily timetable and staff roles. Mark which tasks need a licensed professional and which do not.
  3. Write the welfare standard sheet: food, water, space, rest, exercise, temperature, handling, no force. Base it on the widely shared 'five freedoms' or 'five domains' ideas of animal welfare.
  4. Write an emergency plan: nearest 24-hour vet, fire exit, escape of an animal, injury to a person.

Evidence you keep: floor plan, timetable, welfare sheet, emergency plan.

Module 4 Animal welfare, records and licensing ActionStudy · 2 hApply

Short modules:

  • Welfare: hunger and thirst, comfort, pain and injury, normal behaviour, fear and distress. Check each for every animal, every day.
  • Records: one card per animal with owner contact kept private, vaccines as told by the owner or vet, allergies, behaviour notes, visit dates. Keep it accurate and dated.
  • Licensing: most places require a licence or registration for boarding, breeding and clinics. Write down what applies in your area and who confirmed it.
  • Safety for people: bites, scratches, allergies, infections passed from animals. Wash hands, use proper handling, keep first-aid ready.

Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

Module 5 Build the records and booking flow ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Goal: make a record card and booking process that works on paper or a free form tool.

  1. Create the animal record card and test it with five made-up animals.
  2. Build a booking form: owner contact, animal, service, date, special needs, consent for emergency care.
  3. Write the intake script: five questions to ask when an animal arrives (health, food, behaviour, medicine from the owner, emergency contact).
  4. Run a pretend day with classmates as owners and note every place the process slowed down.

Evidence you keep: the record card, booking form, intake script and day notes.

Module 6 Practice-manager panel ValueShare and mentoring · 6 hEvaluate

Goal: get an experienced manager to test your plan.

  1. Present the plan in 8 minutes to a panel of practice managers or experienced pet-care operators.
  2. They question licensing, staff cover, emergencies and complaints.
  3. Record each challenge and your answer.
  4. Agree three changes to make.

Evidence you keep: panel notes and the three changes.

Module 7 Cost the service and finish the plan ValuePractice · 14 hEvaluate

Goal: work out whether the service can pay for itself.

  1. Cost per visit: staff time, supplies, cleaning, insurance, rent, utilities, waste. Add the currency to each line.
  2. Break-even visits per month = fixed costs divided by (price minus variable cost per visit).
  3. Test one price change and show how it moves break-even.
  4. Write three lessons learned and one decision: start small, change or stop.

Evidence you keep: cost sheet, break-even calculation and decision note.

Module 8 Review room EmpowermentShare and mentoring · 3 hCreate

Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.

  1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the service, the welfare sheet, the cost sheet and the decision.
  2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
  3. Get a written note from a practice manager or a pet keeper.

Evidence you keep: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

Module 9 Check and next step RegenerationStudy · 2 hCreate

Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.

  1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
  2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Alternative Protein Food Products, Fitness Technology, Skincare Spa and Clinic Management.
  3. Contribute back: share your record card or intake script so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).

Evidence you keep: check result and the 90-day plan.

practice material and assessment

Worked examples, case, check and local law pointers

Worked examples
  1. Service: dog daycare. Price 22 per day (currency shown). Variable cost 6 per dog-day (food, cleaning, laundry). Contribution = 16.
  2. Fixed costs per month: rent 1,400, staff 1,000, insurance 200, utilities 200, records and software 200 = 2,800.
  3. Break-even = 2,800 / 16 = 175 dog-days a month (rounded up 175). With 22 working days that is 8.0 dogs a day.
  4. At 12 dogs a day: 12 x 22 = 264 dog-days, income 5,808, profit = 264 x 16 - 2,800 = 1,424.
Case exercise

A boarding service has 10 kennels and runs at 50 percent. Costs rise by 300 a month. Questions: New break-even number of nights? Which welfare item must not be cut to save cost? What does the emergency plan say if a dog escapes? Write a one-page plan for the owner.

Check questions (draft set, 10)
  1. Name three signs an animal is in distress. 2. Why separate sick animals? 3. What goes on a record card? 4. Which tasks need a licensed professional? 5. What is break-even? 6. Why wash hands after handling? 7. What do you do if an animal escapes? 8. Why must you never force an animal? 9. What licence applies to your service? 10. What is your decision and why?

Rules for this course

No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. No treatment, dosage or diagnosis advice. Work with animals only under proper supervision. Owner details stay private. Confirm local licensing before any real service.
