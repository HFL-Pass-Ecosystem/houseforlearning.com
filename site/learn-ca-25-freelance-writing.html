Freelance Writing

Run a real freelance Writing pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small freelance Writing offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: content, creative and marketing work. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the words and the kinds of writing work 6 hUnderstand

Freelance writing means writing for pay for different clients. Start with the basic words and the work.

  • Learn the terms: pitch, brief, draft, edit, byline, deadline, rate, kill fee, invoice.
  • Kinds of work: articles, web pages, newsletters, reports, product text.
  • Name one kind of client and one topic you know well.
  • Read five paid pieces and note the reader, length and style.
  • Compare an article, a newsletter and a web page by length, voice and pay.
  • List three subjects where you have real knowledge or experience.
  • Find where clients post requests for writers and note what they ask for.
Worked example. Topic: home energy savings. Clients: a local energy firm's blog, a community newsletter, a magazine. If you read five pieces of 600, 800, 700, 900 and 500 words, the average is (600 + 800 + 700 + 900 + 500) / 5 = 700 words.

Activity. Read five paid pieces and fill a table of reader, length and style. Write your niche in one line.

Standard to meet. Task: study five pieces. Condition: public pages. Standard: table complete and niche fits one line.

Module 2 Explain how a writing job runs 6 hApply

A job has steps. If you can name them, you can plan your time and avoid surprises.

  • The steps: pitch, brief, research, outline, draft, edit, submit, revise, invoice.
  • A brief says reader, goal, length, tone, deadline and sources.
  • Ask questions before you write.
  • Agree how many rounds of changes are included.
  • Write your questions for the brief as a checklist.
  • Plan your time backward from the deadline.
  • Agree the payment date before you start.
Worked example. An editor asks for 800 words on heat pumps for homeowners. You ask: reader, tone, sources, deadline, pay. If the job needs 5 hours and the deadline is Friday, you plan 1 hour a day from Monday: 5 x 1 = 5 hours.

Activity. Write a one-page brief form with ten questions.

Standard to meet. Task: complete a brief for a pretend client. Condition: in one 30-minute conversation. Standard: brief states reader, goal, length, tone, deadline and rounds.

Module 3 Write a clear piece to a standard 10 hApply

Now you apply the basics. Write a piece that is clear, true and easy to read.

  • Put the main point first and use short words.
  • Write three title options and pick the clearest.
  • Use facts you can check and name your sources.
  • Read aloud and cut every useless word.
  • Use active verbs and cut hedging words.
  • Check each number against its source and write the source down.
  • Ask a friend to read the draft and say its main point.
Worked example. Before: 'Heat pumps are a very interesting option.' After: 'A heat pump can cut your heating bill. Here is how it works.' Cutting a 900-word draft by 10 percent removes 90 words and leaves 810.

Activity. Write a 600-word article and edit it twice.

Standard to meet. Task: write one article. Condition: from a brief, in two hours. Standard: one point, checkable facts, no spelling errors; second draft shorter and clearer.

Module 4 Follow Canadian rules for writers 8 hEvaluate

Copyright, honest claims and privacy rules apply. Learn which ones touch your work.

  • You own what you write unless a contract says otherwise; do not copy others' text.
  • Quote briefly and name the source; do not present others' ideas as yours.
  • Advertising and sponsored posts must be truthful and clearly marked.
  • Personal information in your story falls under privacy law; ask consent. Check the current rules.
  • Ask in writing whether the client wants exclusive rights or first rights.
  • Keep interview notes and ask permission before you quote anyone by name.
  • Label opinion as opinion and support facts with sources.
Worked example. A client asks you to write 'Best in Canada'. You ask for proof; there is none, so you write a claim you can verify. You keep the exchange with the client in your files.

Activity. Make a table of rule, what you must do and where you checked, for ten situations.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: from official public pages. Standard: each row has a dated source; unclear cases say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.

Module 5 Price your work and check it pays 8 hApply

You are paid for results and time. Work out your cost per hour and what a project must earn.

  • Time includes research, calls, drafting, edits and admin.
  • Hourly cost = yearly costs and needed pay, divided by hours you can really bill.
  • Compare per-word, per-hour and per-project pricing.
  • Collect sales tax once registered; check the small-supplier limit with the tax agency.
  • Include unpaid time for pitches and invoicing in your hourly cost.
  • Set a minimum fee per project and state it in your quote.
  • Ask for a deposit on large projects.
Worked example. An article takes 5 hours at 45 dollars an hour: 5 x 45 = 225 dollars. At 800 words that is 225 / 800 = about 0.28 per word. If a client offers 0.20 per word, 800 x 0.20 = 160, which is 160 / 5 = 32 per hour. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Time three pieces from start to finish and set a price for each.

Standard to meet. Task: price three projects. Condition: from your time log. Standard: hours, rate and total shown; a mentor can follow.

Module 6 Find clients and show proof 6 hCreate

Clients hire writers they trust. Build a small portfolio and a steady way to be found.

  • Make three samples that match the clients you want.
  • Get written permission before showing client work.
  • Send ten specific pitches, each with one idea for that client.
  • Keep a list of contact, date, reply and next step.
  • Create one page that lists your topics, samples and contact details.
  • Write an introduction email under 100 words.
  • Follow up once after a week.
Worked example. You pitch a local newsletter: 'Three ways renters can cut winter bills. 500 words.' You attach a short outline. If you send 10 pitches and 2 get a reply, 2 / 10 = 20 percent.

Activity. Send ten pitches in two weeks and log replies.

Standard to meet. Task: run a two-week outreach. Condition: with consent rules met. Standard: ten pitches sent, replies logged, one lesson written.

Module 7 Protect ownership, privacy and quality 6 hEvaluate

A professional writer is careful with rights, secrets and facts.

  • Write down who owns the text and what the client may do with it. Ask a legal adviser for large deals.
  • Do not copy; use your own words or licensed text.
  • Keep client files safe with strong passwords and backups.
  • Fact-check names, prices, dates and quotes.
  • Keep drafts and signed agreements in two places.
  • Tell the client if a source needs a licence.
  • Keep a client's private information out of your portfolio.
Worked example. Your contract says: 'The client owns the final text once paid in full. The writer may show it in a portfolio.' If you cannot show a quote's source, you remove the quote before you deliver.

Activity. Write a one-page agreement and a delivery checklist.

Standard to meet. Task: produce both. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: scope, fees, rounds, rights and payment date are clear.

Module 8 Run a real pilot and decide 8 hCreate

Now you run it for real. Take jobs, measure and decide how to grow.

  • Set a 60-day goal, such as three paid pieces and one repeat client.
  • Every week record hours, income, late payments and feedback.
  • Ask each client for a short review.
  • At the end choose: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Track time per job to find which jobs pay best.
  • Ask the repeat client what they will need next month.
  • Set a new rate and plan when you will tell clients.
Worked example. After 60 days: 4 pieces, 24 hours, 900 dollars billed, one repeat client. 900 / 24 = 37.50 per hour, below the 45 you planned, so you raise your rate.

Activity. Run the pilot, keep the log and present a 12-month plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a 60-day pilot. Condition: real clients, paid or agreed work. Standard: log complete, decision uses numbers, a panel can repeat the plan.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
