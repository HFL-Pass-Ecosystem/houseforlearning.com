flagship course

Biotechnology Foundations

Learn cell biology, safe lab practice, fermentation and basic data work, and judge ethics and safety rules, using small experiments and real calculations.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 43 h lessons plus practice

Learn cell biology, safe lab practice, fermentation and basic data work, and judge ethics and safety rules, using small experiments and real calculations.

  • Describe cell structures and the flow from gene to protein.
  • Apply lab safety, measurement and sterile technique.
  • Analyse growth and fermentation data.
  • Evaluate a biotech use against ethics and biosafety rules.
  • Create a small experiment report with a method, data and conclusion.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run one small fermentation or growth experiment, record data, analyse it, and write a short report with a safety and ethics note.

Condition

About 12 weeks part-time. A school or college lab or a safe kitchen lab, a microscope, a balance, a thermometer, a mentor.

Standard

Measurements are within 5 percent of target, controls are used, data is graphed, and the report states the result, the limits and the safety steps followed.

Who it is for: Science graduates, lab assistants, agriculture and food students, teachers, early researchers. Requirements: School-level biology and chemistry, and basic arithmetic. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Cells and microscopes 5 hDescribe
  • Name the parts: wall, membrane, nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplast.
  • Tell plant, animal and bacterial cells apart.
  • Focus a microscope from low to high power and never use the coarse knob at high power.
  • Draw what you see with a scale.
Worked example. Under 400x magnification an onion cell appears 4 mm long, so its real length is 4 mm divided by 400, which is 0.01 mm.

Activity. Prepare and draw two slides with scale.

Standard to meet. Drawings are labelled and sizes are calculated correctly.

Module 2 Lab safety and biosafety 5 hApply
  • Wear coat, gloves and eye cover, and tie back hair.
  • Treat all cultures as harmful: no eating, no mouth pipetting, wash hands after work.
  • Disinfect benches before and after, and kill waste cultures by autoclave or a 1 percent bleach soak for 30 minutes.
  • Know your institution's rules and keep records of each biological material used.
Worked example. A 1 percent bleach solution is 10 ml of household bleach made up to 1 litre with water.

Activity. Complete a safety checklist and a spill drill.

Standard to meet. Checklist is complete and the spill steps are done in order.

Module 3 Measurement, solutions and dilution 6 hApply
  • Weigh with a balance and record to two decimals.
  • Make a percent solution: grams of solute per 100 ml.
  • Use C1 V1 equals C2 V2 for dilution.
  • Label every tube with contents, date and your initials.
Worked example. To make 200 ml of 0.5 percent salt solution, weigh 1.0 g of salt and add water to 200 ml.

Activity. Make three solutions and one dilution series.

Standard to meet. Concentrations are within 5 percent of target.

Module 4 DNA, genes and proteins 5 hDescribe
  • Explain DNA as a code of four bases that is copied and read.
  • Follow the path from gene to messenger RNA to protein.
  • Read a simple codon table.
  • Explain how a change in one base can change a protein.
Worked example. The codons AUG, GGC, UUU read as methionine, glycine, phenylalanine; three bases make one amino acid.

Activity. Translate a 12 base sequence to amino acids.

Standard to meet. The four amino acids are correct.

Module 5 Sterile technique and culture 6 hApply
  • Work near a flame or in a clean cabinet and sterilise tools.
  • Prepare growth medium and sterilise it at 121 degrees Celsius for 15 minutes in a pressure steriliser or pressure cooker under supervision.
  • Streak a plate to get single colonies.
  • Incubate at the right temperature and seal plates before opening for counts.
Worked example. A plate streaked with yeast at 30 degrees Celsius shows 40 separate colonies after 48 hours.

Activity. Streak and incubate three plates and one blank control.

Standard to meet. Control stays clean and colonies are separated.

Module 6 Fermentation and bioprocess 6 hAnalyse
  • Set up yeast in a sugar solution with a balloon or gas trap.
  • Change one factor: temperature, sugar or time.
  • Measure gas volume or sugar left at fixed times.
  • Plot the curve and find the fastest period.
Worked example. Three flasks at 25, 30 and 37 degrees Celsius make 30, 52 and 38 ml of gas in an hour; 30 degrees Celsius works best.

Activity. Run the experiment and graph results.

Standard to meet. Graph is correct and best condition is justified with numbers.

Module 7 Bioinformatics and data basics 5 hAnalyse
  • Store data in a table with units.
  • Compute mean and spread from three repeats.
  • Compare two DNA sequences by counting matching bases.
  • Use public sequence databases and keep a record of what was searched.
Worked example. Two 20 base sequences match in 17 places, so identity is 17 divided by 20, which is 85 percent.

Activity. Compute identity for three sequence pairs.

Standard to meet. All percentages are correct.

Module 8 Ethics, rules and the final report 5 hCreate
  • Check national biosafety and animal ethics rules before any work with gene change, animals or human samples.
  • Ask consent and protect privacy for any human data.
  • Write a report: aim, method, data, graph, result, limits, safety.
  • Share results honestly, including failed runs.
Worked example. A report states: best temperature 30 degrees Celsius, mean gas 52 ml over three repeats, limit of one yeast strain only.

Activity. Present the report with an ethics note.

Standard to meet. Report includes a graph, limits and ethical steps.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
MethodMissing or unclearMeets the stated checkControlled, repeated and clearly written
Data analysisGuessingEvidence keptCorrect calculations and graphs with limits stated
RecordsGapsCompleteComplete and reviewed by a peer
Safety and ethicsOverlookedFollowedFollowed and taught to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. How do you find real size from a drawing?

Model answer: Divide the drawn size by the magnification. (Covered in module 1: Cells and microscopes. To pass that module: Drawings are labelled and sizes are calculated correctly.)

Q2. How long does waste soak in 1 percent bleach?

Model answer: At least 30 minutes. (Covered in module 2: Lab safety and biosafety. To pass that module: Checklist is complete and the spill steps are done in order.)

Q3. How much salt is in 500 ml of 2 percent solution?

Model answer: 10 g. (Covered in module 3: Measurement, solutions and dilution. To pass that module: Concentrations are within 5 percent of target.)

Q4. How many bases code for one amino acid?

Model answer: Three. (Covered in module 4: DNA, genes and proteins. To pass that module: The four amino acids are correct.)

Q5. Why keep a blank control plate?

Model answer: To show that contamination did not come from the method. (Covered in module 5: Sterile technique and culture. To pass that module: Control stays clean and colonies are separated.)

Q6. Why change only one factor at a time?

Model answer: So the cause of any difference is clear. (Covered in module 6: Fermentation and bioprocess. To pass that module: Graph is correct and best condition is justified with numbers.)

Q7. What does 85 percent identity mean?

Model answer: 85 of every 100 bases match. (Covered in module 7: Bioinformatics and data basics. To pass that module: All percentages are correct.)

Q8. Why report failed runs?

Model answer: They show the true method limits and prevent repeated errors. (Covered in module 8: Ethics, rules and the final report. To pass that module: Report includes a graph, limits and ethical steps.)

Q9. Why use repeats in an experiment?

Model answer: To check the result is consistent and not chance. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why get consent for human samples?

Model answer: It respects the person and is a legal and ethical duty. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know basic cell and gene ideas.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain how cultures grow.
  3. L3 Foundation. Follow a protocol under guidance.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Design and run a small experiment.
  5. L5 Specialist. Lead a lab team and train others.
  6. L6 Advanced. Manage a lab quality and biosafety system.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set standards or lead research programmes.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Cells and genes basics

Describe the parts of a cell and the roles of DNA and genes, and focus a microscope to draw 3 labelled cells.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the drawing has at least 5 correct labels. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: How cultures grow

Explain the 4 phases of culture growth and plot a growth curve from at least 6 timed readings.

Speaker notes. Teacher gives a table of readings and checks the learner marks the fast-growth phase. Covered in module 5.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Protocol under guidance

Follow a written protocol to make a 1 in 10 dilution series of 4 tubes, with biosafety steps ticked, and get the volumes right within 5 percent.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the volumes with a pipette check and the safety ticks. Covered in modules 2 and 3.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Own small experiment

Design and run an experiment with a control and 3 repeats, then analyse the results with averages and one chart and state a limit.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the control exists and the repeat values are all recorded. Covered in module 8, Ethics, rules and the final report.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Lab team training

Lead a lab team of 3 for a month and train each one to pass a 10-step practical check on sterile technique.

Speaker notes. Teacher observes one trainee and checks the 10 steps against the checklist.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Quality and biosafety system

Set up a lab quality and biosafety system with waste, spill and record rules, and pass an internal audit of at least 90 percent. Check local rules with an expert.

Speaker notes. Teacher audits the logbook and the waste labels.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Research programme standards

Set standards for a research programme with ethics checks, consent for human samples and data storage, and review 3 projects against them.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks each project has a signed ethics note. Check local rules with an expert.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
