flagship course

Vertical Farming

Plan and run a small indoor or stacked farm: crops, light, water, nutrients, climate, food safety and business case.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner30:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain how stacked growing works and what suits it.
  • Apply Set light, water and nutrient schedules.
  • Analyse Compute yield, energy and water per kilogram.
  • Evaluate Judge cost and risk.
  • Create Write a 90-day pilot plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and run a small stacked growing pilot with schedules, logs, hygiene plan, harvest records and unit economics.

Condition

A small space or shelving kit, lights, seeds, a spreadsheet, two peers and a grower mentor.

Standard

Schedules are followed and logged; yield per tray recorded; hygiene plan applied; energy and water per kg calculated; cost per kg shown; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Growers, hospitality staff, school teachers, urban entrepreneurs. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Choosing crops that pay 3 hUnderstand

Start with the crop and the buyer, not with the equipment.

  • Fast, light, high-value crops suit stacked growing: microgreens, lettuce, herbs, edible flowers and some strawberries.
  • Cycle times: microgreens 8 to 12 days, lettuce 30 to 40 days, herbs 4 to 6 weeks.
  • Ask three local buyers what they buy weekly, at what price and in what pack.
  • Rank crops by income per tray per week.
Worked example. Three buyers want 20 kg of basil per week at 300 INR per kg (an estimate; check your market). A basil tray gives 1.2 kg every 5 weeks, so about 85 trays in rotation are needed.

Activity. Build a demand table with three buyers, quantities and prices and choose three crops.

Standard to meet. Support the choice with buyer numbers and cycle times.

Module 2 Growing systems and fail-safes 4 hUnderstand

Choose a system you can run reliably.

  • Deep-water culture floats plants on aerated nutrient solution; nutrient film technique sends a thin stream over roots; ebb and flow floods and drains trays.
  • Aeroponics saves the most water but fails fastest if a pump stops.
  • Plan for failure: a battery or generator for pumps, alarms, and a manual watering routine.
  • Keep a spare pump and tubing.
Worked example. A 4-level ebb-and-flow rack uses a 40 L reservoir and a timer. When the pump fails at night, a battery alarm wakes the grower within 10 minutes and the crop survives.

Activity. Draw your layout and write a fail-safe plan.

Standard to meet. Label the parts in the layout, and cover power and pump failure in the plan.

Module 3 Light schedules and cost 4 hApply

Light is usually the largest running cost.

  • Light strength is measured as photosynthetic photon flux density (PPFD, the usable light reaching the plants). Many leafy greens do well at 150 to 250 micromoles per square metre per second.
  • White full-spectrum LEDs work for most beginners.
  • Hang lights 20 to 40 cm above the canopy and raise as plants grow.
  • Use timers; 12 to 16 hours of light a day for most leafy crops.
Worked example. A 4-tray rack with 100 W of LEDs for 14 hours uses 1.4 kWh a day. At 8 INR per kWh, that is 11.2 INR per day for the rack.

Activity. Set a light schedule and calculate daily light energy cost.

Standard to meet. Schedule and cost are correct and recorded.

Module 4 Water and nutrients 4 hApply

Plants take nutrients dissolved in water, so water quality is crop quality.

  • Measure pH (about 5.8 to 6.2 for lettuce) and conductivity (about 1.2 to 1.8 mS per cm) every day.
  • Add nutrients slowly and test again after 30 minutes.
  • Change the solution on a schedule and keep it covered from light to prevent algae.
  • Never mix concentrated nutrients together before dilution.
Worked example. A reading of pH 7.2 is corrected with a few millilitres of acid solution, then re-tested after 30 minutes and logged.

Activity. Keep a 14-day reading sheet.

Standard to meet. Log has daily pH, conductivity, temperature and actions.

Module 5 Climate, hygiene and food safety 4 hEvaluate

Indoor air is warm and humid, which is ideal for disease.

  • Keep humidity near 50 to 70 per cent and airflow gentle but constant.
  • Wash hands, clean tools, use a footbath or shoe change and quarantine new plants.
  • Remove diseased plants at once; sanitise trays between cycles.
  • Wash and pack produce in a clean area; keep a harvest record.
Worked example. Grey mould appears at 85 per cent humidity. A small fan and a dehumidifier bring humidity to 65 per cent within a day and the outbreak stops.

Activity. Write a hygiene and cleaning schedule.

Standard to meet. Schedule has daily, weekly and between-crop tasks.

Module 6 Yield, energy and water 4 hAnalyse

Measure efficiency to compare crops and decide what to scale.

  • Yield per square metre of floor area counts all levels.
  • Water use is often much lower than field growing; energy per kg is the weak point.
  • Track weight per tray, losses and sales.
  • Compare cycles to find improvements.
Worked example. A 6 m2 floor with 4 levels gives 24 m2 of growing area and produces 40 kg of lettuce every 5 weeks, about 8 kg per week at 9 kWh per kg.

Activity. Calculate yield, energy and water per kg for your pilot.

Standard to meet. Calculate correctly, with units.

Module 7 Unit economics and break-even 4 hEvaluate

A viable unit needs steady sales.

  • Cost lines: racks, lights, pumps, space rent, power, water, seeds, nutrients, labour, packing and transport.
  • Sell direct to restaurants and shops, and consider weekly subscription boxes.
  • Break-even kilograms per week = weekly cost / margin per kg.
  • Test price and yield changes.
Worked example. Weekly costs of 9,500 INR and a margin of 110 INR per kg give break-even of about 86 kg a week. The unit grows about 8 kg a week (40 kg every 5 weeks), so it needs either more levels, a higher price or lower costs.

Activity. Build a weekly break-even sheet.

Standard to meet. Calculate break-even and compare it with output.

Module 8 Ninety-day pilot and decision 3 hCreate

Run long enough to see repeats.

  • Set weekly targets for yield, losses and energy.
  • Log every problem and fix.
  • Hold a review at day 30, 60 and 90.
  • At day 90 decide: scale, change crop or stop.
Worked example. By week 12 the pilot sells 62 kg of herbs, loses 6 per cent to disease and cuts energy per kg by 12 per cent after lights are moved closer.

Activity. Write the day-90 decision memo.

Standard to meet. Write a memo that uses real numbers and a clear decision.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: plan, daily log, harvest record, cost sheet. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Growing controlIrregularScheduledLogged and corrected
HygieneGapsPlanPlan applied
EconomicsNo costsCostsBreak-even
Grow logYields claimedDaily light, water and temperature recordedLog used to explain one crop loss or gain
Food safety and power rulesFood rules not checkedFood-safety, water and electrical rules for the place are written downRules checked with the authority and a hazard plan tested

Model answer: Leafy greens, herbs, microgreens. (Covered in module 1: Choosing crops that pay. To pass that module: Support the choice with buyer numbers and cycle times.)

Q2. Why LEDs?

Model answer: Efficient, less heat. (Covered in module 3: Light schedules and cost. To pass that module: Schedule and cost are correct and recorded.)

Q3. What pH range?

Model answer: About 5.8 to 6.2 for many crops. (Covered in module 4: Water and nutrients. To pass that module: Log has daily pH, conductivity, temperature and actions.)

Q4. Why hygiene?

Model answer: Prevent disease. (Covered in module 5: Climate, hygiene and food safety. To pass that module: Schedule has daily, weekly and between-crop tasks.)

Q5. Yield measure?

Model answer: Per m2 per cycle. (Covered in module 6: Yield, energy and water. To pass that module: Calculate correctly, with units.)

Q6. Energy per kg unit?

Model answer: kWh per kg. (Covered in module 6: Yield, energy and water. To pass that module: Calculate correctly, with units.)

Q7. Biggest risk?

Model answer: Pump or power failure. (Covered in module 2: Growing systems and fail-safes. To pass that module: Label the parts in the layout, and cover power and pump failure in the plan.)

Q8. Why compare to field?

Model answer: See whether it pays. (Covered in module 6: Yield, energy and water. To pass that module: Calculate correctly, with units.)

Q9. What is break-even?

Model answer: Volume covering costs. (Covered in module 7: Unit economics and break-even. To pass that module: Calculate break-even and compare it with output.)

Q10. Why log readings?

Model answer: Spot trends. (Covered in module 4: Water and nutrients. To pass that module: Log has daily pH, conductivity, temperature and actions.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Food safety and water rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.
