Professional Services

Run a real professional Services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small professional Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: professional, scientific and community services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know what professional services are 6 hUnderstand

Professional services sell expert advice and work to clients, such as accounting, consulting, legal support, design and planning.

  • Terms: client, scope, engagement, deliverable, fee, retainer, conflict of interest, confidentiality.
  • Some professions are regulated by a provincial body; others are open to anyone. Check which applies to you.
  • Clients buy trust, so reputation matters.
  • Interview one professional about a normal week.
  • Compare three service types, such as accounting, planning and design, by what the client receives.
  • Learn how professionals are paid: hourly, fixed fee, retainer.
  • Write what you can offer today with your skills and what you would need to learn first.
Worked example. A deliverable is the thing the client receives, such as a report, a filing or a plan. If an engagement has 3 deliverables and 2 are handed over, 1 remains. The final fee is due only when the last one is accepted.

Activity. Interview one professional and write the services they sell, who buys, and how they are paid.

Standard to meet. Task: write an interview summary. Condition: one 20-minute interview. Standard: services, clients and fee method clearly stated with correct terms.

Module 2 Explain how an engagement runs 6 hApply

Good work follows steps, from first call to a closed file.

  • Steps: first call, check fit and conflicts, proposal, agreement, work, review, delivery, follow-up, close.
  • A proposal states the problem, scope, steps, fees, timing and what you need from the client.
  • Use an agreement before work starts.
  • Keep a file of notes, versions and decisions.
  • Ask at the first call what the client wants done, by when, and what they have already tried.
  • Check for conflicts before you send a proposal: do you serve a rival or know private facts?
  • Agree in writing how many rounds of changes are included.
Worked example. First call notes: client wants a staffing plan. Fit check: no conflict. Proposal: three steps, two weeks, fixed fee of 2,400 CAD. If the work takes 16 hours, the fee works out to 2,400 / 16 = 150 CAD per hour.

Activity. Write a one-page proposal template and test it on a pretend client.

Standard to meet. Task: write a proposal. Condition: from a short pretend brief. Standard: problem, scope, steps, fee, timing and client duties all stated.

Module 3 Do a core analysis or task to a standard 10 hApply

Apply a method and show your working so another person can check it.

  • State the question first, in one sentence.
  • Gather facts from the client and public sources; note where each fact came from.
  • Show steps, assumptions and limits in your work.
  • Have a second person check your work before it goes out.
  • List the assumptions behind any number and mark the ones that are guesses.
  • Offer the client more than one option and show the cost and risk of each.
  • Write the conclusion in the first paragraph, then support it.
Worked example. Question: 'Can the shop afford one more worker?' Facts: monthly sales 18,000, costs 14,500, new worker 3,200. Profit now is 18,000 - 14,500 = 3,500; after the hire it is 3,500 - 3,200 = 300. Answer: yes, but only if sales stay the same, so show a case where sales fall 10 percent.

Activity. Complete one small analysis or plan for a real or pretend client with a checker.

Standard to meet. Task: produce a short report. Condition: within eight hours. Standard: question, facts with sources, steps, assumptions, recommendation, and a checker's initials.

Module 4 Know regulation, insurance and tax basics 8 hEvaluate

Rules depend on your profession and province. Find them before you take clients.

  • Regulated titles and work may require membership of a provincial body, exams and ongoing training. Do not use a protected title unless you hold it.
  • Professional liability insurance covers claims about advice; ask an insurance adviser what suits you.
  • Register for sales tax once taxable sales pass the small-supplier limit. Check the current rules.
  • Keep records of income and expenses and set aside money for tax.
  • Ask the provincial body which titles are protected and what work is reserved for members.
  • Keep a list of your required courses and renewal dates if you hold a licence.
  • Open a separate bank account for business income and expenses.
Worked example. Rules table row: 'Use of a protected title | provincial professional body | use only after registration | before marketing | registration number shown'. If registration takes 3 months and you want to start advertising in month 2, advertise only the services open to non-members until month 3.

Activity. Build a rules table for your field and province, with sources and dates.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: from official pages and body websites. Standard: each row has enforcer, action and date checked; unclear cases say to check with the body.

Module 5 Price, bill and manage cash 8 hApply

Pricing is not only time. It reflects value, risk and your costs.

  • Methods: hourly, fixed fee, retainer, value-based. Pick one that fits the work and the client.
  • Estimate hours with a buffer for review and client delays.
  • Invoice on time, state payment terms and follow up on late payments.
  • Billable hours are less than total hours; plan for it.
  • Work out your cost per hour from yearly costs divided by billable hours.
  • Ask for a deposit on larger jobs and write the payment dates in the agreement.
  • Compare your price with similar services in your area and explain the difference.
Worked example. At 150 CAD an hour and 20 hours: 20 x 150 = 3,000 CAD. Costs: 20 x 35 = 700. Margin: 3,000 - 700 = 2,300 per job, about 77 percent. If you can bill only 25 hours of a 40-hour week, billable use is 25 / 40 = 62.5 percent. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build a fee sheet for three services with hours, rate and costs, and compute break-even.

Standard to meet. Task: build a fee and cost sheet. Condition: spreadsheet with visible formulas. Standard: units shown, three what-if cases, mentor checks the arithmetic.

Module 6 Manage client work, files and quality 6 hCreate

Order in your files and process gives better results and fewer errors.

  • Use one folder per client with fixed sub-folders: agreement, notes, drafts, final.
  • Track time per task so you learn how long work really takes.
  • Use a checklist before delivery: facts, numbers, names, format.
  • Ask for client feedback at the end of each job.
  • Date every file and add a version number, such as v1, v2, final.
  • Record the time spent on each client every day, not at the end of the month.
  • Back up client files to a second safe place each week.
Worked example. Time log: estimate 20 hours, actual 26. Overrun is 6 hours, or 30 percent (6 / 20). Cause: three extra rounds of client changes. At 150 CAD an hour, 6 hours is 900 CAD of lost fee. Fix: write the change limit in the agreement.

Activity. Keep a four-week time log and file system for two pretend or real clients.

Standard to meet. Task: manage files and time for four weeks. Condition: real or practice clients. Standard: log complete, files named by rule, one improvement made.

Module 7 Protect confidentiality, ethics and fairness 6 hEvaluate

Clients trust you with sensitive facts. Handle them with care.

  • Keep client information private. Share only with permission or as the law requires.
  • Tell the client about any conflict of interest and decline when you cannot serve fairly.
  • Do not promise results you cannot control.
  • Follow privacy law: collect only what you need and delete when no longer needed.
  • Lock paper files and use passwords on files that hold client data.
  • Tell the client what you do with their data when the job ends.
  • Check any gift or favour policy so it never affects your advice.
Worked example. You are asked to advise two competing shops. You decline one and tell both why in general terms, without sharing facts. If you took both, you would hold private facts that each could use against the other.

Activity. Write a confidentiality and conflict policy of one page and a risk register of eight rows.

Standard to meet. Task: write a policy and risk register. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: each risk has a control, owner and review date; the policy is in plain words.

Module 8 Find clients, run a pilot and grow 8 hCreate

Build trust, run a pilot and decide how to grow.

  • Pick one client type and one clear offer; ask for referrals.
  • Offer a limited pilot, such as a one-month plan or a fixed-scope review.
  • Track leads, proposals, wins, hours and income each week.
  • Review at 60 days and write a 12-month plan.
  • Write a one-sentence offer: who you help, with what, and the result.
  • Ask each finished client for one written comment and one name of someone who may need help.
  • Set a monthly income goal and work back to how many clients and hours it needs.
Worked example. Funnel for 8 weeks: 30 contacts, 10 calls, 4 proposals, 2 wins. Calls are 10 / 30, about 33 percent of contacts; proposals are 4 / 10 = 40 percent of calls; wins are 2 / 4 = 50 percent of proposals. Weak step is contact to call; add a clearer offer.

Activity. Run the pilot with at least one client and present the review and plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a client pilot. Condition: legal and ethical practice under your rules table. Standard: measures tracked, client feedback gathered, decision supported by numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
