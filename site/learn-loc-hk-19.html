Drones: Hong Kong edition

The global course "Drones" with local rules, jobs and examples for Hong Kong.

about this course

Drones: Hong Kong edition

L1 to L4 location edition8 modules

Drones from the global list, applied to Hong Kong: local rules, jobs and examples in HKD.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan a small Drones business for Hong Kong from fit check to launch plan.

Condition

About six weeks part-time; public sources, two local readers and a mentor.

Standard

All module standards met; every claim has a public source and date; mentor and peer mark 'Meets' or better.

Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real. Draft; not legal, tax, medical or financial advice. All amounts are estimates for planning, not quotes.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read your local market 4 hUnderstand

Every plan starts with the place. Read Hong Kong as your customer does before you choose a sector.

  • A dense, trade-led city. Space is costly, so online and shared-space models fit well.
  • Money moves fast by bank transfer and fast-payment apps; customers expect instant confirmation.
  • Three languages in daily use: your page in two (Traditional Chinese and English) is the minimum.
  • Close to the wider region: many small firms serve customers across the border, so shipping and data rules matter early.
Worked example. Market page for Hong Kong: size in one line, three languages or fewer, main payment methods, three public holidays that affect delivery, and one risk. Each line has a source you can open and a date.

Activity. Write a one-page market page with sources and dates. Ask two local people to correct it.

Standard to meet. Cite a source and a date for every line, have two local readers confirm the facts, and name one risk with a response.

Module 2 Check the fit of this sector 4 hAnalyse

Drones sits in the cluster "Mobility, aerial and space (regulated)". Judge its fit for Hong Kong before spending money.

  • Local angle: Regulated and space-limited. Plan around airspace and road rules; start with charging points or shared mobility on paper.
  • Score demand, skills you hold, start-up cost, rules and partner access from 1 to 5.
  • A rules score below 3 makes this a later project.
  • Regulated: planning only. Partner with a licensed firm.
Worked example. Fit table for Drones in Hong Kong: list three local customer groups, one local competitor type (no names), one rule that could stop you and one partner type. Score each criterion with a reason.

Activity. Write a fit table for Drones with scores and reasons. Ask one local reader to challenge two scores.

Standard to meet. Score five criteria with reasons, name one rule risk, and state the regulated status.

Module 3 Register and license 4 hApply

Rules differ by place. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real.

  • Register the business with the tax authority (business registration) before you trade; companies are also listed with the companies registry.
  • Choose sole trader, partnership or company: a company separates personal and business risk, but has yearly filing duties.
  • Some activities need an extra licence (food, health, money, education centres). List yours before you take a first order.
  • Keep a registered address and a named contact; a shared-office address is common for a first year.
Worked example. Licence list for a small Drones business: registration type, tax registration, address, any activity licence, insurance. Next to each, write who issues it, what it costs in HKD (from their public page), how long it takes, and the date you checked.

Activity. Build a licence table for your chosen sector with issuer, cost, time and the date checked. Do not apply; this is a plan.

Standard to meet. Complete the table with a public source for every row, put the steps in a logical order, and write one question for an adviser.

Module 4 Protect data and customers 3 hApply

Trust is the real product. Protect customer data and keep your promises in writing.

  • The personal data ordinance sets six principles, from collection and use to security and access.
  • Say in plain words what you collect and why; give a way to see and correct it.
  • Direct marketing needs a clear choice for the customer to say no; keep that record.
  • Sending data outside the city may need a safeguard; check with a local expert and list every tool that stores customer data.
Worked example. Privacy notice in five lines: who we are; what we collect; why; who sees it; how to ask for a copy, correction or removal. Plus a data map listing each tool and where it stores data.

Activity. Write the five-line notice and a data map for your plan. Ask a peer to find any gap.

Standard to meet. Write the notice in plain words, list every tool in the data map, and write and date the refund and complaint steps.

Module 5 Find and serve customers 4 hCreate

Go where your customers already are, in the language they use. Language: Cantonese, Mandarin and English; write the page in Traditional Chinese and English.

  • Channels: messaging apps, social media and marketplaces are where customers already are.
  • Language: show both languages side by side; keep prices in HKD only.
  • Payments: bank transfer and fast-payment apps first; give an instant receipt.
  • Trust: put the business registration number, a real address and a refund note on every page.
Worked example. Test plan: show the offer to 20 real people through two channels over two weeks; count enquiries, replies and first orders. Example result: 20 shown, 6 enquiries, 2 orders: a 10% order rate. Change one thing (price or wording) and test again.

Activity. Test two channels with 20 people. Record results in a table and write the next change.

Standard to meet. Test has a start and end date; results are counted, not guessed; one change is chosen and justified.

Module 6 Price, tax and money 4 hEvaluate

Know your numbers in HKD. Tax rule in one line: No sales tax or VAT; profits tax has a lower first-band rate, so read the current table.

  • Write HKD next to every number.
  • Contribution per unit = price minus the cost of each unit.
  • Break-even units = fixed monthly cost divided by contribution per unit; round up.
  • Add tax and duty explicitly; never bury them in a price.
  • Check against a worse case: if sales are 25% lower, do you still cover costs?
Worked example. For a small Drones business (all amounts are estimates): price HKD 240 per unit, cost HKD 60 per unit, fixed cost HKD 14,400 per month. Contribution = 240 - 60 = 180. Break-even = 14,400 / 180 = 80.0, so 80 sessions per month. If sales fall 25% from 160, you sell 120 and the margin is 7,200.

Activity. Build your own sheet in HKD: price, unit cost, fixed cost, contribution, break-even and a 25% worse case. Put the tax and duty rules in a separate line with a source.

Standard to meet. Complete all six lines of the sheet in HKD, have a peer check the arithmetic, and show the worse case.

Module 7 People and partners 3 hApply

Plans become real through people. Hire, partner and write every promise down.

  • Employers usually pay a compulsory retirement-fund contribution for staff; budget it from the first hire.
  • Written terms, rest days and paid leave follow the employment law; keep signed copies.
  • Freelancers are common; write scope, price and date in one page before work starts.
  • Partner with a college, training body or business chamber to find helpers and to practise.
Worked example. Role card for a first helper: tasks, hours, pay in HKD, trial period, review date, and one measure of success. Signed by both sides.

Activity. Write one role card and one partner letter (what each side gives). Role-play a first meeting for 10 minutes.

Standard to meet. Write a role card with pay in local currency, hours and a review date, balance the partner letter, and keep signed copies of both.

Module 8 Launch plan and review 4 hCreate

Finish with a one-page launch plan and a plan to review it every month.

  • Dates: first test, first order, first review.
  • One measure per week: enquiries, orders or revenue; pick one.
  • A monthly review: what we planned, what happened, what we change.
  • Keep a risk list with a response for each; include a rules change.
  • Local caution: A city with high costs: test one small offer before you sign a lease.
Worked example. Launch page for Hong Kong: Week 1 licence table done; Week 2 test with 20 people; Week 3 numbers sheet checked; Week 4 launch decision with a go or no-go rule written in advance.

Activity. Write the launch page and present it to two people for 10 minutes. Record their questions and your answers.

Standard to meet. Plan has dates, one weekly measure, a go or no-go rule and a risk list; presenter answers three questions with evidence.

jobs

Where this takes you

  • Drones assistant. supports a small Drones business in Hong Kong.
  • Online shop operator. runs product pages, orders and delivery for a small seller.
  • Digital marketing assistant. plans posts, reports results and tests offers.
  • IT support technician. fixes and monitors small-office systems.
  • Coworking community coordinator. runs events and member support.
  • Business coach assistant. prepares plans, trackers and follow-ups for clients.

Six steps from entry to senior

  1. Drones assistant (L2, Entry). helps with set tasks, keeps records and follows the checklist.
  2. Junior Drones coordinator (L3, Junior). runs small jobs end to end and reports each week.
  3. Drones practitioner (L4, Practitioner). plans and delivers the work alone, with a mentor check.
  4. Drones specialist (L5, Specialist). goes deep in one area, such as rules, numbers or customers.
  5. Drones team lead or manager (L6, Advanced). leads people and partners, owns the plan and the data.
  6. Senior Drones adviser or mentor (L7, Senior). designs the whole system, reviews others and publishes cases.

See how these steps connect to study and work in Career pathways and Pathways.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Plan one job

Write a job page for one task, such as a survey of 20 farm plots in 4 flights, with one report per plot. Planning only.

Speaker notes. The teacher checks the page has numbers for plots, flights and reports.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Know the three models

Describe 3 models (partner operator, own drone with pilots, data resale) and compare margin per plot in HKD.

Speaker notes. Use HKD 40, HKD 25 plus pilot and HKD 15 and ask which carries the most risk.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Map aviation rules

List 5 rules (pilot licence, registration, flight permit, insurance, privacy notice), each with a checked date. Check local rules with an expert; use a licensed firm for all flying.

Speaker notes. The mentor asks where flight is banned near the farm and how the learner knows.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Handle image data

Blur faces, store images safely and delete raw images after 90 days, tested on 3 sample sets.

Speaker notes. Open a sample image and see faces are blurred.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Analyse survey output

Analyse a sample survey of 20 plots and report the share with a problem, with 2 actions for the client.

Speaker notes. Give 20 plot results and check the share within 1 point.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Design a flight plan

Design a flight plan with a route, a weather limit, a battery reserve of 25% and a stop rule, checked by a licensed pilot.

Speaker notes. Change the wind and see the plan says no-fly.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Lead a client programme

Lead a programme of 100 plots with a calendar, a licensed partner, a risk log of at least 6 items and a monthly client report.

Speaker notes. Ask what happens after a near miss; the plan must show a review.
