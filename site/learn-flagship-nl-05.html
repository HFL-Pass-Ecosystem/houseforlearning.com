NL-05 · full course

IT Business and Managed Services

Set up a small IT service for a local client, deliver it for 30 days and report what you did and found.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 150 h guidedPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Set up a small IT service for a local client, deliver it for 30 days and report what you did and found.

  • Understand Explain managed service against fix-when-broken.
  • Apply Onboard a client and run daily service.
  • Analyse Report tickets, risks and cost.
  • Create Design a service catalogue.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Deliver a managed IT service to one small client for 30 days and report results.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3 to 6 months. Computer, free tools for tickets and monitoring, a friendly client, mentor and peer circle.

Standard

Service catalogue; onboarding checklist; 30-day ticket log; monthly report; security checks; at least 70% on the check.

Learning only. Follow your local law and get written permission before you work with real customers, premises, data or money.

Requirements: none. Who it is for: learners 16 and over, workers changing field, small-business starters, teams. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (150 h guided)

Module 1 What managed means 8 hUnderstand

You look after IT every month for a fixed fee.

  • Prevent rather than react
  • Promise response times
  • Clients value steady cost
Worked example. 10-person office pays EUR 450 a month for help desk, updates and backup checks.

Activity. Describe your service in plain words. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Prevent rather than react; Promise response times; Clients value steady cost), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. A non-technical reader understands it. Meets the standard when your record shows: prevent rather than react; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 2 Catalogue and service levels 14 hCreate

Say what, how fast, how much.

  • Two or three levels
  • Response targets: urgent 1 hour, normal 1 day
  • State exclusions
Worked example. Basic EUR 25 per user, Standard 40 with backup checks.

Activity. Write the catalogue. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Two or three levels; Response targets: urgent 1 hour, normal 1 day; State exclusions), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Inclusions, exclusions, targets and prices stated.

Module 3 Price and break-even 16 hEvaluate

Price covers time, tools, risk and profit.

  • Fixed costs / margin per client = break-even clients
  • Include onboarding
  • Test price with two prospects
Worked example. Fixed EUR 900, margin 260: 3.5, so 4 clients.

Activity. Build a break-even sheet. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Fixed costs / margin per client = break-even clients; Include onboarding; Test price with two prospects), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Currency on every line. Meets the standard when your record shows: fixed costs / margin per client = break-even clients; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 4 Agreement and permission 14 hApply

Written permission to touch systems is essential.

  • Agreement: scope, price, term, exit, data
  • Adviser reviews any template
  • Keep change records
Worked example. One-page agreement with 30-day notice.

Activity. Draft and get adviser comment. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Agreement: scope, price, term, exit, data; Adviser reviews any template; Keep change records), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Comments recorded. Meets the standard when your record shows: agreement: scope, price, term, exit, data; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 5 Onboard the client 30 hApply

First impression and first safety net.

  • Inventory devices and accounts
  • Fix updates, backup, passwords
  • Give a one-page help guide
Worked example. 12 devices, 3 old laptops, no backup: backup first.

Activity. Onboard one client with a checklist. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Inventory devices and accounts; Fix updates, backup, passwords; Give a one-page help guide), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Checklist complete. Meets the standard when your record shows: inventory devices and accounts; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 6 Run 30 days 40 hApply

Keep promises daily.

  • Log each ticket with times
  • Test one backup restore
  • Apply updates in a planned window
Worked example. 14 tickets in week 2; 12 within target.

Activity. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Log each ticket with times; Test one backup restore; Apply updates in a planned window), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Ticket log complete; restore tested. Meets the standard when your record shows: log each ticket with times; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 7 Report 20 hAnalyse

Show value with numbers.

  • Tickets, response, outages
  • Top three repeat problems
  • Next month plan
Worked example. 22 tickets, 91% on target; replace printer EUR 180.

Activity. Write and present report. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Tickets, response, outages; Top three repeat problems; Next month plan), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Report ends with actions. Meets the standard when your record shows: tickets, response, outages; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 8 Grow 8 hEvaluate

Grow without losing quality.

  • Capacity per person
  • One tool that saves time
  • Decide grow, change, stop
Worked example. 8 clients at 6 hours each a week.

Activity. Present plan. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Capacity per person; One tool that saves time; Decide grow, change, stop), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Plan has numbers and dates. Meets the standard when your record shows: capacity per person; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: your records, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Evidence qualityClaims without recordsRecords for most claimsEvery claim linked to a dated record

Rules and safetyRules ignoredRules named and followedRules checked with the local authority, note kept

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Managed against break-fix?

Model answer: Managed prevents and has a fixed fee; break-fix reacts and bills by hour.

Q2. Why list exclusions?

Model answer: To avoid disputes about scope.

Q3. Break-even clients for 900 and 260?

Model answer: 4.

Q4. Why written permission?

Model answer: It protects client and you legally.

Q5. What comes first in onboarding?

Model answer: Inventory, then basics like backup and updates.

Q6. Why test a restore?

Model answer: A backup that cannot restore is useless.

Q7. What does a monthly report show?

Model answer: Numbers, risks and next actions.

Q8. How find capacity?

Model answer: Hours per client against hours available.

Q9. What is the task of this course, in one sentence?

Model answer: Deliver a managed IT service to one small client for 30 days and report results.

Q10. What standard must your final evidence meet?

Model answer: Service catalogue; onboarding checklist; 30-day ticket log; monthly report; security checks; at least 70% on the check.

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, with notes

  1. L1 Basic: First contact: words, tools and safety. Short units, about 2 to 6 hours, no record needed.
  2. L2 Awareness: Explain what the field is and who it serves. Shared with schools and community groups.
  3. L3 Foundation: Do guided tasks with a checklist. Evidence is a small set of records.
  4. L4 Practitioner: Run the full task alone with a mentor. This course sits here.
  5. L5 Specialist: Take one narrow area to depth and teach a peer circle.
  6. L6 Advanced: Lead a team, design a service or a study, and review other people's evidence.
  7. L7 Super expert: Set the standard: write the method, audit others, publish worked examples back to learners.

Notes: credits stack and you can step in and out. This course is L4. After it, L5 takes one narrow area of IT Business and Managed Services to depth; L6 leads a team; L7 sets and audits the standard. Aligned with, not accredited by, national credit frameworks.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local custom changes rules, language, currency and who you practise with. Check each rule with your local authority before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
