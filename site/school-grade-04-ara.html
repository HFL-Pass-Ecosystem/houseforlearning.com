school course

Grade 04: Arabic

4 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2Primary4 units36 teaching weeks36-week school yearBloom levels: Remember, Understand
  • Understand Describe the weather and seasons.
  • Understand Describe a garden and its animals.
  • Remember Spell common words.
  • Understand Ask and answer questions.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 04 Arabic, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 4 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

unit by unit

4 units with worked examples

Unit 1 Seasons and weather Weeks 1 to 9Understand

In this unit, 'Seasons and weather', you learn to describe the weather and seasons. Lessons are short and playful, with objects, pictures and talk. You see it, say it, then do it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Seasons and weather' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: describe the weather and seasons, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: في الصيف الجو حار وفي الشتاء الجو بارد. أذهب إلى الشاطئ في الصيف. (In summer it is hot, in winter cold. I go to the beach in summer.) Method: Model run for 'Seasons and weather'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Name the four seasons. الرَّبِيعُ [spring], الصَّيْفُ [summer], الْخَرِيفُ [autumn], الشِّتَاءُ [winter]. Say them in order and point to a calendar. Notice that الرَّبِيعُ, الصَّيْفُ and الشِّتَاءُ start with a sun letter, so the l of الْ is silent.
  2. Step 2, Learn weather words. الْجَوُّ حَارٌّ [The weather is hot]. الْجَوُّ بَارِدٌ [cold]. الْجَوُّ مُعْتَدِلٌ [mild]. الْجَوُّ مُمْطِرٌ [rainy]. الْجَوُّ مُشْمِسٌ [sunny]. Ask: كَيْفَ الْجَوُّ الْيَوْمَ؟ [How is the weather today?]
  3. Step 3, Describe each season. فِي الصَّيْفِ يَكُونُ الْجَوُّ حَارًّا. [In summer the weather is hot.] فِي الشِّتَاءِ تَهْطِلُ الْأَمْطَارُ. [In winter rain falls.] فِي الرَّبِيعِ تُزْهِرُ الْأَزْهَارُ. [In spring flowers bloom.] فِي الْخَرِيفِ تَسْقُطُ أَوْرَاقُ الْأَشْجَارِ. [In autumn the leaves fall.]
  4. Step 4, Short dialogue. الْبِنْتُ: كَيْفَ الْجَوُّ الْيَوْمَ؟ [How is the weather today?] الْوَلَدُ: الْجَوُّ بَارِدٌ وَمُمْطِرٌ. [The weather is cold and rainy.] الْبِنْتُ: أَيُّ فَصْلٍ تُحِبُّ؟ [Which season do you like?] الْوَلَدُ: أُحِبُّ الرَّبِيعَ. [I like spring.]
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. A child says الْجَوُّ حَارَّةٌ. Correction: الْجَوُّ is masculine, so say الْجَوُّ حَارٌّ. But for a feminine noun use the feminine form: الشَّمْسُ حَارَّةٌ [The sun is hot]. Match the adjective to its noun.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: write two sentences about your favourite season and its weather. Model answer: أُحِبُّ الشِّتَاءَ لِأَنَّ الْأَمْطَارَ تَهْطِلُ. الْجَوُّ بَارِدٌ وَأَلْبَسُ مِعْطَفًا. [I like winter because rain falls. The weather is cold and I wear a coat.]

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Describe the weather and seasons: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 2 Gardens and animals Weeks 10 to 18Understand

In this unit, 'Gardens and animals', you learn to describe a garden and its animals. Lessons are short and playful, with objects, pictures and talk. You see it, say it, then do it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Gardens and animals' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: describe a garden and its animals, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: في الحديقة زهور وشجرة وعصفور. العصفور يطير. (In the garden are flowers, a tree and a bird. The bird flies.) Method: Model run for 'Gardens and animals'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Name things in a garden. حَدِيقَةٌ [garden], شَجَرَةٌ [tree], وَرْدَةٌ [rose], عُشْبٌ [grass], فَرَاشَةٌ [butterfly], نَحْلَةٌ [bee], عُصْفُورٌ [sparrow], أَرْنَبٌ [rabbit]. Label a picture with these words.
  2. Step 2, Describe with adjectives that agree. شَجَرَةٌ كَبِيرَةٌ [a big tree], وَرْدَةٌ جَمِيلَةٌ [a beautiful rose], أَرْنَبٌ صَغِيرٌ [a small rabbit]. The adjective copies the gender of the noun and adds ـَةٌ for a feminine noun.
  3. Step 3, Say what is in the garden. فِي الْحَدِيقَةِ شَجَرَةٌ كَبِيرَةٌ. [In the garden there is a big tree.] تَقِفُ عَلَيْهَا عَصَافِيرُ. [Sparrows stand on it.] الْفَرَاشَةُ تَطِيرُ بَيْنَ الْأَزْهَارِ. [The butterfly flies among the flowers.]
  4. Step 4, Say what animals do. الْأَرْنَبُ يَأْكُلُ الْجَزَرَ. [The rabbit eats carrots.] النَّحْلَةُ تَجْمَعُ الرَّحِيقَ. [The bee collects nectar.] Ask: مَاذَا تَفْعَلُ النَّحْلَةُ؟ [What does the bee do?] تَجْمَعُ الرَّحِيقَ. [It collects nectar.]
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. A child says وَرْدَةٌ جَمِيلٌ. Correction: وَرْدَةٌ جَمِيلَةٌ, because وَرْدَةٌ is feminine. Another says أَرْنَبٌ صَغِيرَةٌ. Correction: أَرْنَبٌ صَغِيرٌ, because أَرْنَبٌ is masculine.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: describe a garden with a white rabbit and a red rose. Model answer: فِي حَدِيقَتِي أَرْنَبٌ أَبْيَضُ وَوَرْدَةٌ حَمْرَاءُ. الْأَرْنَبُ يَأْكُلُ الْعُشْبَ. [In my garden there is a white rabbit and a red rose. The rabbit eats grass.]

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Describe a garden and its animals: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 3 Dictation and spelling Weeks 19 to 27Remember

In this unit, 'Dictation and spelling', you learn to spell common words. Lessons are short and playful, with objects, pictures and talk. You see it, say it, then do it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Dictation and spelling' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: spell common words, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Sound out each part: s-t-ay stays; r-ai-n rain; b-oa-t boat. Vowel pairs ai, ay, oa, ee make one sound. Write three more words that share one pair. Method: Model run for 'Dictation and spelling'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Listen, say and split the word. The helper says كِتَاب [book]: ki-taa-b. Write it from right to left as four letters, ك ت ا ب. Notice the alif (ا) joins to the letter before it but never to the letter after it, so the last ب is written on its own, in its separate shape.
  2. Step 2, Mark the long sounds. قَلَم [pen] has only short sounds, so no long letter is written. بَاب [door] has a long aa, written with alif. نُور [light] has a long oo, written with waw (و). فِيل [elephant] has a long ee, written with ya (ي). Listen for how long each sound lasts before you write.
  3. Step 3, Spell the round ta. مَدْرَسَة [school] ends in ة, the round ta with two dots, which sounds like a short a at the end of the word. Compare تَمْر [dates], which ends in the ordinary ت and is pronounced t. Do not write the round ta as ه; it needs its two dots.
  4. Step 4, Write words with sun and moon letters. الشَّمْس [the sun] is said ash-shams, because the L sound disappears and ش is doubled with a shadda (ّ). الْقَمَر [the moon] is said al-qamar, and the L is heard. Both are written with ال; say the word aloud before writing the first letter.
  5. Step 5, Take a short dictation. The helper reads ذَهَبَ الْوَلَدُ إِلَى الْمَدْرَسَةِ [The boy went to the school] three times: whole, then phrase by phrase, then whole again. Write it, then check three things: every dot, the round ta in المدرسة, and إِلَى, which ends in the dotless ى that sounds like aa.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: write these words from the English meaning, with ال in front: the pen, the school, the sun. Say each aloud first. Answer: الْقَلَم، الْمَدْرَسَة، الشَّمْس. The first uses a moon letter, the third a sun letter, and the second ends with the round ta.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Spell common words: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 4 Conversation practice Weeks 28 to 36Understand

In this unit, 'Conversation practice', you learn to ask and answer questions. Lessons are short and playful, with objects, pictures and talk. You see it, say it, then do it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Conversation practice' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: ask and answer questions, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: — السلام عليكم، هل أستطيع التحدث إلى المعلم؟ — وعليكم السلام، لحظة من فضلك. (Hello, may I speak to the teacher? One moment please.) Method: Model run for 'Conversation practice'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Learn question words. مَا [what], مَنْ [who], أَيْنَ [where], مَتَى [when], كَمْ [how many], لِمَاذَا [why], كَيْفَ [how]. Say each with a pointing hand. Remember مَنْ asks about people and مَا about things.
  2. Step 2, Ask personal questions to a boy. مَا اسْمُكَ؟ [What is your name?] كَمْ عُمْرُكَ؟ [How old are you?] أَيْنَ تَسْكُنُ؟ [Where do you live?] مَاذَا تُحِبُّ؟ [What do you like?] To a girl the endings change: مَا اسْمُكِ؟ كَمْ عُمْرُكِ؟
  3. Step 3, Answer in a full sentence. اسْمِي ... [My name is ...] عُمْرِي تِسْعُ سَنَوَاتٍ. [I am nine years old.] أَسْكُنُ فِي مَدِينَةٍ كَبِيرَةٍ. [I live in a big city.] أُحِبُّ الْقِرَاءَةَ. [I like reading.]
  4. Step 4, Practise a conversation. الْوَلَدُ: مَرْحَبًا، مَا اسْمُكِ؟ [Hello, what is your name?] الْبِنْتُ: اسْمِي ...، وَمَا اسْمُكَ؟ [My name is ..., and yours?] الْوَلَدُ: اسْمِي .... أَيْنَ تَسْكُنِينَ؟ [My name is .... Where do you live?] الْبِنْتُ: أَسْكُنُ فِي الْحَيِّ الْقَدِيمِ. [I live in the old quarter.]
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. A child asks مَنْ اسْمُكَ؟ Correction: مَا اسْمُكَ؟ because a name is a thing, not a person. Another answers the question كَمْ عُمْرُكَ؟ with أَنَا مِنْ مِصْرَ; reply with the age, عُمْرِي عَشْرُ سَنَوَاتٍ [I am ten years old].
  6. Step 6, Your turn: ask a new classmate two questions and answer two yourself. Model answer: مَا اسْمُكَ؟ وَأَيْنَ تَسْكُنُ؟ اسْمِي ...، وَأَسْكُنُ بِجَانِبِ الْمَدْرَسَةِ. [What is your name? And where do you live? My name is ..., and I live next to the school.]

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Ask and answer questions: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: First sounds and greetings

On the slide: L1 Basic · First sounds and greetings

Notes: Say hello, give your name and name three everyday objects in Arabic. Notes: teach مرحبا (marhaba, hello) and كتاب (kitab, book) aloud before any writing; learners greet a partner and point to a book, a pen and a door while saying each word.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Letters and the right-to-left page (this course)

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Letters and the right-to-left page

Notes: Recognise that Arabic is written right to left and copy the shapes of the first eight letters. Notes: show that letters change form at the start, middle and end of a word; learners trace the letter for b, then find it in three printed words.

This course: Grade 04 Arabic is at this level. Its units include Seasons and weather; Gardens and animals; Dictation and spelling; Conversation practice.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Simple sentences and a first reading

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Simple sentences and a first reading

Notes: Read and write short present-tense sentences about yourself, family and daily life with short vowels marked. Notes: use هذا كتاب (hadha kitab, this is a book) as the model; learners build five new sentences, swapping the noun each time, and read them aloud.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Everyday conversation and roots

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Everyday conversation and roots

Notes: Hold a two-minute conversation on school, food and travel and use the three-letter root idea to guess new words. Notes: show k-t-b giving kitab (book) and katib (writer); learners find the root in four given words, then order a meal in a role-play.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Formal Arabic and dialect awareness

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Formal Arabic and dialect awareness

Notes: Compare Modern Standard Arabic with one spoken dialect and write a formal letter of about 150 words. Notes: take one phrase, such as how to say 'how are you', in both registers; learners list three differences and choose the right register for a news item and a chat.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Literary analysis and linguistics

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Literary analysis and linguistics

Notes: Analyse a classical or modern text for grammar, rhetoric and register, and write a reasoned essay in Arabic. Notes: choose a short poem of eight lines; learners mark case endings, name one image used, and explain in a 400-word essay how the form supports the meaning.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Translation, research and teaching

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Translation, research and teaching

Notes: Design and defend a translation or research project that handles dialect variation, with a documented method. Notes: pick a 500-word article and translate it for two audiences; justify every choice about register, note untranslatable terms, and have a native reader check the result.

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

India (Bengaluru)

Use INR in money problems; deliver in English, Hindi, Kannada; personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Use AED in money problems; deliver in Arabic, English; personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Use BRL in money problems; deliver in Portuguese (Spanish and English support); personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Canada (Toronto)

Use CAD in money problems; deliver in English, French; personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

United States

Use USD in money problems; deliver in English, Spanish; personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Singapore

Use SGD in money problems; deliver in English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Australia

Use AUD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Use HKD in money problems; deliver in Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Use GEL or ILS in money problems; deliver in Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Use KYD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Data Protection Act; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Mexico

Use MXN in money problems; deliver in Spanish, English; personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; guardian contact for every learner under 18.
