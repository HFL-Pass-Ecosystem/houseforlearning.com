flagship course

Online Medical Supply and Equipment

Run an honest online shop for medical supplies and equipment: sourcing, rules, product information, storage and safe delivery.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner38:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain which supplies are general goods and which are regulated devices.
  • Apply Write accurate product pages with warnings, storage and returns.
  • Analyse Check suppliers for licence, batch records and cold or dry storage needs.
  • Evaluate Judge risks in a product line and decide what you will not sell.
  • Create Launch a small catalogue with order-to-delivery steps.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and open a small online catalogue of 10 low-risk supplies with verified suppliers, product pages, storage rules and a recall plan.

Condition

A spreadsheet, a basic shop tool, supplier documents (real or practice), two peers and a mentor with trade knowledge. Rules are checked with an adviser.

Standard

Every product has a licence or rule check; no medical claims beyond the label; storage and expiry rules are written; recall plan runs in a mock test in under 24 hours; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Small traders, pharmacy and clinic staff, logistics staff and entrepreneurs entering health retail. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What can you sell, and who may 4 hUnderstand

Health products sit on a risk ladder. Selling the wrong item can hurt people.

  • General items: gloves, masks, thermometers (check rules), bandages. Regulated: prescription medicines, many devices; these need a licence and sometimes a pharmacist.
  • Check the rule in your place before listing: licence, registration, age limits, prescription need.
  • Start with low-risk items and grow only with a licensed partner.
Worked example. A trader lists blood pressure monitors only after learning that her country needs a device registration; she waits for a licensed distributor.

Activity. List 20 items and sort them into low risk, needs licence, do not sell.

Standard to meet. Each item has a rule note with a source.

Module 2 Supplier checks 5 hAnalyse

A shop is only as safe as its supplier.

  • Ask for licence, batch number, expiry date, storage needs and a product data sheet.
  • Test with a small order; inspect packs, labels and seals.
  • Keep records for each batch so you can trace and recall.
Worked example. A glove supplier cannot give batch numbers; the trader drops the offer and picks one with labelled cartons.

Activity. Complete a supplier check form for two suppliers.

Standard to meet. Forms are filled with real or practice evidence; one supplier is rejected with a reason.

Module 3 Product pages that tell the truth 5 hCreate

Pages must be clear, accurate and not claim to cure.

  • State what it is, size, quantity, intended use, warnings, storage and expiry.
  • Use only the maker's approved claims; never promise cures or diagnosis.
  • Show real photos and a clear return rule.
Worked example. Weak: "Best protection against disease." Strong: "Single-use nitrile gloves, size M, box of 100, not for sterile surgery."

Activity. Write 10 product pages.

Standard to meet. Pages have all required facts; no unapproved claim.

Module 4 Storage, cold chain and expiry 5 hApply

Heat, damp and age spoil supplies.

  • Store dry, clean, off the floor, away from sunlight and pests; track temperature if needed.
  • First-expiry-first-out: sell earliest expiry first; remove expired stock to a labelled bin.
  • Record temperature or humidity checks on a daily log.
Worked example. A box of test kits needs storage at 2 to 8 degrees Celsius; a fridge log is kept twice a day and a spoiled batch is separated.

Activity. Draw a storage plan and daily log sheet.

Standard to meet. Plan covers heat, damp, expiry and pest control.

Module 5 Orders, packing and delivery 5 hApply

Right item, right condition, right person.

  • Check each order against the pick list; seal and label; protect from shock and heat.
  • Choose a courier able to handle the product; give tracking.
  • Verify age or prescription where the rule needs it.
Worked example. An order of ear drops is held until the prescription is checked by the partner pharmacist.

Activity. Write the order-to-delivery steps and run a mock order.

Standard to meet. Mock order passes with no mistakes.

Module 6 Returns, complaints and recall 5 hEvaluate

Plan for failure before it happens.

  • Medical items usually cannot be resold after opening; write a clear returns rule.
  • A recall plan: find batch, stop sales, tell buyers, collect stock, report to the authority, record.
  • Log every complaint and look for patterns.
Worked example. A batch of masks has a seal fault. The shop finds 40 buyers by batch number within 6 hours.

Activity. Run a mock recall within 24 hours.

Standard to meet. Every step is recorded with time.

Module 7 Privacy and payment safety 4 hEvaluate

Order data can reveal health, which is sensitive.

  • Collect only what is needed; store securely; delete when no longer needed.
  • Use payment methods approved in your place; never ask for card numbers by message.
  • Pack discreetly where the product is private.
Worked example. The shop removes the product name from the delivery label and shows only the order number.

Activity. Write a one-page privacy and payment note.

Standard to meet. Note lists data kept, why, and for how long.

Module 8 Launch and improve 5 hCreate

Open small, learn, and grow only what is safe.

  • Launch with 10 items; track orders, complaints, returns and stock-outs.
  • Review monthly with a licensed adviser; add items only after the rule check.
  • Plan responsible disposal and give customer safety tips.
Worked example. After one month: 62 orders, 2 complaints, 0 recalls; one item removed for slow sales and a supplier changed.

Activity. Present your launch review.

Standard to meet. Numbers are true and a clear next step is dated.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: product register, supplier checks, 10 product pages, storage plan, recall test record. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name one low-risk and one regulated item.

Model answer: Bandages low risk; prescription medicine or a regulated device regulated. (Covered in module 1: What can you sell, and who may. To pass that module: Each item has a rule note with a source.)

Q2. What must a supplier provide?

Model answer: Licence, batch number, expiry date, storage needs, data sheet. (Covered in module 2: Supplier checks. To pass that module: Forms are filled with real or practice evidence; one supplier is rejected with a reason.)

Q3. Why avoid cure claims?

Model answer: They mislead buyers and may be illegal where you sell; check the local rule. (Covered in module 3: Product pages that tell the truth. To pass that module: Pages have all required facts; no unapproved claim.)

Q4. What does first-expiry-first-out mean?

Model answer: Sell the earliest-expiry stock first. (Covered in module 4: Storage, cold chain and expiry. To pass that module: Plan covers heat, damp, expiry and pest control.)

Q5. Why keep batch records?

Model answer: To trace and recall products fast. (Covered in module 2: Supplier checks. To pass that module: Forms are filled with real or practice evidence; one supplier is rejected with a reason.)

Q6. What is a returns rule for opened medical items?

Model answer: Usually not resellable; state it clearly. (Covered in module 6: Returns, complaints and recall. To pass that module: Every step is recorded with time.)

Q7. Name two storage dangers.

Model answer: Heat and damp (also pests, sunlight). (Covered in module 4: Storage, cold chain and expiry. To pass that module: Plan covers heat, damp, expiry and pest control.)

Q8. What should delivery labels avoid?

Model answer: Showing private product names. (Covered in module 7: Privacy and payment safety. To pass that module: Note lists data kept, why, and for how long.)

Q9. When do you add a new product?

Model answer: After the rule check with a licensed adviser. (Covered in module 8: Launch and improve. To pass that module: Numbers are true and a clear next step is dated.)

Q10. How long should a mock recall take here?

Model answer: Under 24 hours. (Covered in module 6: Returns, complaints and recall. To pass that module: Every step is recorded with time.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Medical product rules differ by place. Confirm each rule and licence with an adviser before selling.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
