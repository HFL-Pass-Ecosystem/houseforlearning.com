Task, condition and standard

Task

Map one real or simulated process; find three waste points and propose fixes with numbers.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3-6 months. Own tools, free spreadsheet and form tools, and a real practice partner. Mentor and peer circle available. Currency written on every number.

Standard

Evidence reviewed by a mentor; at least 70% on the 10-question check; two written peer reviews given; every number carries its currency and unit.

Who it is for, requirements and materials

  • Who it is for: learners aged 16 and over, small-business starters, workers moving to a new field, and teams (see B2B, B2C, D2C).
  • Requirements: finish Food Industry and Food Technology first, or show equivalent work.
  • Materials included: lesson text, worked examples, case exercise, 10-question check, templates in My page.
  • Teaching team: a trainer from our network, a mentor who reviews your evidence, and a peer circle. Names are given at enrolment.

lesson by lesson

9 modules, in the order you do them

Module 1 Who needs this, and who is your first user IdeaStudy · 5 hUnderstand

Goal: name one real first user: a plant or workshop manager who wants less waste, fewer stoppages and safer work.

  1. Market scan: Visit or interview 3 workshops or plants (or use a public process description). Note the product, the main steps, the time per unit, the scrap rate and the stoppages people complain about.
  2. Talk to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what do you pay, what would you change?
  3. Write a one-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.

Evidence you keep: the scan notes and the one-page idea.

Module 2 Kick-off circle IdeaShare and mentoring · 7 hUnderstand
  1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
  2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
  3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce: task record, cost or result sheet, peer feedback, check result.

Evidence you keep: revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

Module 3 Start the practice task ActionPractice · 35 hApply
  1. Pick one real or simulated process of 6 to 10 steps, for example cutting, assembling and packing a simple product.
  2. Map it: each step, who does it, minutes per unit, waiting time between steps, scrap at each step.
  3. Find the three biggest waste points: waiting, rework, motion, over-production, scrap, stoppages.
  4. Propose one fix for each with an expected saving in minutes or currency.

Evidence you keep: Process map, three waste points and fixes with numbers.

Module 4 Short modules ActionStudy · 5 hApply
  • Industrial safety: guards on moving parts, lock-out before repair, noise and dust control, safe lifting, clear walkways, trained operators. Stop the machine if a guard is missing.
  • Quality tools: a check sheet to count defects, a bar chart ordered by size to show the biggest cause, and the five whys to find the root of a problem.
  • Preventive maintenance: small regular jobs prevent large failures. Record every job and every breakdown, then look for repeating causes.

Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

Module 5 Continue and log ActionPractice · 35 hApply

Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.

  1. Test one fix for two weeks or in a simulation. Record before and after times and scrap.
  2. Draw a simple chart of stoppages by cause and find the largest.
  3. Write the machine's weekly preventive-maintenance checklist: look, listen, clean, lubricate, tighten, record.

Evidence you keep: Before and after data and maintenance checklist.

Module 6 Review circle ValueShare and mentoring · 15 hEvaluate
  1. Bring your process map to the debrief circle after a visit.
  2. An operations engineer asks where you measured and where you guessed.
  3. Agree which data you must still collect.
  4. Swap records with a peer and write what you could not understand without asking.

Evidence you keep: the circle notes and the agreed change.

Module 7 Finish and measure ValuePractice · 35 hEvaluate
  1. Apply the agreed change and keep recording.
  2. Measure the result against your goal: minutes per unit, scrap percent, stoppage hours per week, output per shift.
  3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.

Evidence you keep: before and after table and the decision note.

Module 8 Review room EmpowermentShare and mentoring · 8 hCreate
  1. Present to a review room: idea, record, numbers, risks, decision.
  2. Give two peers written feedback: one strong point, one thing to fix.
  3. Get a written note from one real user or expert.

Evidence you keep: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

Module 9 Check and next step RegenerationStudy · 5 hCreate
  1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
  2. Write a 90-day plan and pick a follow-on course.
  3. Contribute back: share a template or worked example. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).

Evidence you keep: check result and the 90-day plan.

practice material and assessment

Worked examples, case, check and local law pointers

Worked examples
  1. Process: cut 4 min, drill 3 min, assemble 6 min, pack 2 min = 15 min of work per unit, but units wait 20 min before assembly. Waiting is the largest waste.
  2. Scrap: 500 units made, 40 scrapped = 8%. If a unit costs 50 in material and labour, scrap loses 2,000 (same currency).
  3. Check sheet: of 40 defects, 22 are drill misalignment, 10 burrs, 8 other. Fix the largest first: a simple jig.
  4. Five whys: misaligned > fixture loose > bolt worn > no inspection > no checklist. Root cause: no weekly check.
Case exercise

A line makes 500 units a day with 8% scrap and 3 hours of stoppages weekly. A jig costs 6,000 and is expected to cut drill defects by half. Questions: How many units and how much money does it save per week (5 days)? In how many weeks does it pay back? What safety check must go with the jig? Write a one-page proposal for the manager.

Check questions (draft set, 10)
  1. Name three kinds of waste. 2. What is lock-out before repair? 3. What does a bar chart ordered by size show? 4. Calculate scrap percent: 40 of 500. 5. What are the five whys? 6. Why record breakdowns? 7. What goes on a weekly checklist? 8. How do you measure payback? 9. What must be fitted to any moving part? 10. Your decision: scale, change or stop, and why?

Rules for this course

Use simulated or supervised processes. Never bypass guards. Follow local safety rules. No company names or logos on any file. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files.
