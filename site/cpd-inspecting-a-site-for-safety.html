CPD · Building, construction and property

Inspecting a site for safety

A short walk-through that finds hazards before work starts.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 4 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playBuilding, construction and property

A short walk-through that finds hazards before work starts.

  • Identify hazards, given a site photo set, listing at least 8 with a control for each.
  • Rank risks, given a matrix, by likelihood and severity.
  • Record an inspection, given a template, with date, place, finding and owner.
  • Escalate an urgent hazard, given a scenario, within the agreed time.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Walk a site, identify at least eight hazards, rank the risks with a reason, write an owned and dated action list, and escalate any imminent danger at once

Condition

Given a set of ten invented site photos described in text, a risk matrix and an inspection log template, in a 4-hour practical session

Standard

List at least eight hazards with a control for each, rank every risk correctly on the matrix, give every action an owner and due date, and stop work and tell the lead at once for imminent danger

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Hazard, risk, control 30 min
  • A hazard is something that can cause harm.
  • Risk is how likely and how serious the harm is.
  • Controls go from most to least effective.
Worked example. Hazard: noisy saw. Controls in order: 1 eliminate (use a cut-to-size supply), 2 substitute (quieter tool), 3 engineer (enclosure), 4 admin (time limits), 5 PPE (ear defenders).

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "hazard, risk, control": identify hazards, given a site photo set, listing at least 8 with a control for each.

Standard to meet. Define hazard, risk and control in plain words, and order controls from most to least effective, giving an example of each for a site hazard.

Module 2 Spotting hazards in photos 45 min
  • Look from the ground up, then edges, then overhead.
  • Name the hazard and one control for each.
  • Aim for at least eight from ten photos.
Worked example. Photo 3: ladder not tied and nobody footing it. Hazard: fall. Control: tie the ladder or use a platform.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "spotting hazards in photos": rank risks, given a matrix, by likelihood and severity.

Standard to meet. Identify hazards in a set of site photos, given as text, listing at least eight and naming a control for each one.

Module 3 Ranking by risk with a reason 35 min
  • Score likelihood 1 to 5 and severity 1 to 5.
  • Multiply: on this sample matrix 15 or more is High, 8 to 14 Medium, under 8 Low.
  • Always give the reason for the rank.
Worked example. Unprotected edge: likelihood 4 x severity 5 = 20, High. Stacked bricks near a path: 3 x 3 = 9, Medium. Trailing cable: 4 x 2 = 8, Medium. Reason for the edge: a fall from first floor can be fatal.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "ranking by risk with a reason": record an inspection, given a template, with date, place, finding and owner.

Standard to meet. Rank the identified hazards, given a risk matrix, by likelihood and severity, and give a clear reason for each rank such as an unprotected edge being High.

Module 4 Action list: owner and date 30 min
  • Every action has one owner and one date.
  • Higher risk means an earlier date.
  • Set a re-inspection date for each item.
Worked example. Edge: edge protection today, site manager, re-inspect tomorrow. Bricks: restack away from the path, labourer lead, tomorrow. Cable: reroute, electrician, within 2 days.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "action list: owner and date": escalate an urgent hazard, given a scenario, within the agreed time.

Standard to meet. Write an action list, given the ranked hazards, with a named owner and a due date for every action, and state when each item is re-inspected.

Module 5 Walk-through practice 50 min
  • Walk a set route so nothing is missed.
  • Log date, place, finding, photo and owner.
  • Imminent danger: stop work and tell the lead now.
Worked example. Log line: 2026-03-10 | first floor east | open edge, no guard | photo 4 | site manager | High | stop work in the area. One walk-through, ten photos, every finding logged.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "walk-through practice": escalate an urgent hazard, given a scenario, within the agreed time. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Apply the inspection method to a described site: record each finding in the inspection log with photo evidence, and stop work and tell the lead at once for any imminent danger.

Module 6 Assessment 20 min
  • Use the template from start to finish.
  • Check every hazard has a rank, owner and date.
  • Hand in a log another person can follow.
Worked example. New scenario: 9 hazards found, 2 High, 4 Medium, 3 Low. Pass needs at least 8 hazards, 9 of 9 ranked, 9 of 9 actions with owner and date, and one stop-work call.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "assessment": escalate an urgent hazard, given a scenario, within the agreed time. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Complete a short inspection of a new scenario, with hazards found, risks ranked, and actions owned and dated, using the template correctly.

Module 7 Second worked case: open edge, cables and bricks 15 min
  • Rank all three before writing any action.
  • Give the reason in one short sentence.
  • Match the due date to the rank.
Worked example. Case card 2 ranked: open edge 20 High, same day; stacked bricks 9 Medium, next day; loose cables 8 Medium, within 2 days. Order of work: edge first, then bricks, then cables.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second worked case: open edge, cables and bricks": escalate an urgent hazard, given a scenario, within the agreed time. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Rank three hazards from a case card on the matrix, give a reason for each rank, and order the actions by risk with an owner and date.

Module 8 Teach-back and peer check 15 min
  • Swap logs with a partner.
  • Check every line for rank, owner and date.
  • Name one strength and one improvement.
Worked example. Pairs of 2: partner lists 8 hazards from the 10 photos and checks them against the key. Target: no missed High risk, 8 of 8 actions with owner and date.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "teach-back and peer check": escalate an urgent hazard, given a scenario, within the agreed time. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate a partner's inspection log against the key, find any missed hazard or missing owner or date, and give one strength and one improvement.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Order of control (highest first)?

Model answer: Eliminate, substitute, engineer controls, admin, PPE. (Covered in module 1: Hazard, risk, control. To pass that module: Define hazard, risk and control in plain words, and order controls from most to least effective, giving an example of each for a site hazard.)

What does a good action list have?

Model answer: Owner and date. (Covered in module 4: Action list: owner and date. To pass that module: Write an action list, given the ranked hazards, with a named owner and a due date for every action, and state when each item is re-inspected.)

Who stops work for imminent danger?

Model answer: Anyone; follow stop-work rule. (Covered in module 5: Walk-through practice. To pass that module: Apply the inspection method to a described site: record each finding in the inspection log with photo evidence, and stop work and tell the lead at once for any imminent danger.)

Record where?

Model answer: The inspection log. (Covered in module 5: Walk-through practice. To pass that module: Apply the inspection method to a described site: record each finding in the inspection log with photo evidence, and stop work and tell the lead at once for any imminent danger.)

What is the best control?

Model answer: Remove the hazard first; protective equipment is the last resort. (Covered in module 1: Hazard, risk, control. To pass that module: Define hazard, risk and control in plain words, and order controls from most to least effective, giving an example of each for a site hazard.)

Who gets the action list?

Model answer: The site lead and each owner. (Covered in module 4: Action list: owner and date. To pass that module: Write an action list, given the ranked hazards, with a named owner and a due date for every action, and state when each item is re-inspected.)

When re-inspect?

Model answer: At the date set or after any change. (Covered in module 4: Action list: owner and date. To pass that module: Write an action list, given the ranked hazards, with a named owner and a due date for every action, and state when each item is re-inspected.)

What do you do for imminent danger?

Model answer: Stop work and tell the lead now. (Covered in module 5: Walk-through practice. To pass that module: Apply the inspection method to a described site: record each finding in the inspection log with photo evidence, and stop work and tell the lead at once for any imminent danger.)

Why photograph?

Model answer: Evidence of what was found. (Covered in module 5: Walk-through practice. To pass that module: Apply the inspection method to a described site: record each finding in the inspection log with photo evidence, and stop work and tell the lead at once for any imminent danger.)

A hazard scores likelihood 4 and severity 5 on the sample matrix. What is its score, rank and timing?

Model answer: 4 x 5 = 20, High; act the same day, for example with edge protection. (Covered in module 3: Ranking by risk with a reason. To pass that module: Rank the identified hazards, given a risk matrix, by likelihood and severity, and give a clear reason for each rank such as an unprotected edge being High.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What a hazard is. Speaker note: A hazard is anything on a site that can hurt someone. At this first step the learner can name common site hazards, such as open edges, loose cables and stacked materials, and say why a quick look matters.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Hazard, risk and control. Speaker note: The learner can tell hazard from risk and name the five levels of control from most to least effective. They know that anyone may stop work for imminent danger under the site rule.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Spot and score. Speaker note: The learner can spot hazards in a photo with prompts and score likelihood and severity on a simple matrix. They follow the worked example of an open edge ranked High and a trailing cable ranked Medium.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Inspect, rank and act. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner walks a described site, lists at least eight hazards with controls, ranks them with reasons, writes owned and dated actions, and escalates imminent danger at once.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Busy, changing sites. Speaker note: The specialist inspects sites where several trades work at once and conditions change daily. They re-inspect after changes, choose better controls than PPE where they can, and follow up late actions.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Audit and coach. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner audits inspection logs, finds patterns in repeated hazards, coaches others to rank risks with clear reasons, and checks that actions were closed and not only written.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Lead the safety routine. Speaker note: The super expert designs the inspection routine, matrix and templates for a company. They study near misses, set escalation times, and train site leads to run walk-throughs without blame.

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Credits stack; aligned with, not accredited by, any framework.
