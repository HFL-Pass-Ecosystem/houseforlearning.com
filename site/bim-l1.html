BIM academy · L1 Basic · 6 hours

Buildings and models: the basics

What a building is made of, why we draw it before we build it, and what a digital model adds to a drawing.

BIM academyThe BIM and LOD rules

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

  • Task. Name the main parts of a simple building and say what a digital model of it can answer.
  • Condition. Using the campus module as the example, with a picture and a short list of parts.
  • Standard. Name at least eight parts correctly and give three questions a model can answer.

four modules

Modules, with worked examples

Module 1: What a building is made of (1.5 h; Identify)
  • A building has a base, a frame, walls, a roof, openings and services.
  • Each part has a job: carry load, keep weather out, let people and light in.
  • Services are power, water, air, data and safety systems.

Worked example. Look at the campus module: four posts, two frames, a floor, walls, a door and a window. That is eight parts.

Activity. List the parts of a room you know and say the job of each.

Standard. Eight parts named with a job each.

Module 2: From a drawing to a model (1.5 h; Describe)
  • A drawing is a picture. A model is a set of objects that know their size and type.
  • When one object changes, the plan, the list and the section change together.
  • A model is only as good as the facts stored in it.

Worked example. Move a wall in a model and the room area and the paint quantity update at once. In a drawing you must redo both.

Activity. Draw a room on paper, then list what would have to be redone if the wall moved.

Standard. Two things that would need redoing named.

Module 3: Why we model before we build (1.5 h; Explain)
  • Mistakes cost far less on screen than on site.
  • A model lets many people see the same thing: owner, designer, builder and operator.
  • A model keeps the story of the building for its whole life.

Worked example. A stair that does not fit is found on screen in minutes. On site it costs days.

Activity. Write one sentence on how each of the four groups uses the same model.

Standard. Four uses written, one per group.

Module 4: Safe sites and honest records (1.5 h; Recall)
  • Hard hats, safe edges and clear routes protect people on site.
  • A record is only useful if it is true, dated and signed by the person who made it.
  • Never put a brand name or private detail into a shared model.

Worked example. A photo with a date and a name is a record. A photo with no date is just a picture.

Activity. Name three safety rules from your own site or school.

Standard. Three rules named.

five checks

Check yourself

Think of your answer first, then open the question.

Q1. What are the main parts of a simple building?

Model answer. A base, a frame, walls, a roof, openings and services.

Q2. How does a model differ from a drawing?

Model answer. A model holds objects that know their size and type, so plans, lists and sections change together.

Q3. Name one reason to model before building.

Model answer. Mistakes cost far less on screen than on site.

Q4. What makes a record useful?

Model answer. It is true, dated and signed by the person who made it.

Q5. What must never be put into a shared model?

Model answer. A brand name or a private detail.

where this takes you

Jobs this supports

  • Site helper or trainee. Supports a build team and learns the vocabulary.
  • Junior drafter assistant. Helps prepare simple drawings and checks.

ladder L1 to L7

The seven levels

Step in at your level and move up. Credits stack.

  1. L1 Basic: Buildings and models: the basics (you are here)
  2. L2 Awareness: BIM awareness: read a model and a standard
  3. L3 Foundation: Model a campus module to LOD 200
  4. L4 Practitioner: Coordinate trades and take off quantities (LOD 300)
  5. L5 Specialist: Fabrication models, programme and cost (LOD 350 to 400)
  6. L6 Advanced: Lead a BIM project: plans, rules and audits
  7. L7 Super expert: Campus network standards and digital twins

Learning material, not a design or safety instruction. Real buildings are designed and signed off by licensed professionals under local rules.
