Hostel or Hotel Business

Eight modules take you from the first customer to a running, measured service in hostel or hotel business.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, start and run hostel or hotel business in São Paulo: serve a traveller or group, deliver beds, rooms and services, keep records, and show a working month of numbers.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. Own phone or computer, real customers or a real practice site, a peer and a mentor for the final review. Local rules and licences checked against official sources.

Standard

Every module standard is met; all claims are true and backed by records; peers and a mentor mark the evidence 'Meets' or better.

Who it is for: students, graduates, career changers, owners, workers and returners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, tracker, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know your customer and market 4 hAnalyse

Every sound business in hostel or hotel business begins with one clear customer and one clear problem. In São Paulo and the wider state, demand is real but crowded, so a small, well-described customer group wins before a broad one.

  • In hostel or hotel business: Define guests: backpackers, business, groups; each wants different services.
  • Write the customer in one sentence: a traveller or group. Add the place, the moment of need and what they use today.
  • List five rivals or substitutes within reach. For each, record price, strength, weakness and how people reach it.
  • Interview at least eight real customers with open questions. Record exact words, not your summary.
  • Count the market in units: how many room-nights could one area buy in a month? Use two public sources and your own count, then take the lower figure.
  • Choose one first segment and say why the others wait.
Worked example. Weak: 'Everyone needs beds, rooms and services.' Strong: 'Our first customer is a traveller or group within 8 km of the centre, who today pays R$ 374 per room-night and complains about delay. We target 360 room-nights a month by month three.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: ten customers are interviewed for hostel or hotel business. Seven say price, two say speed, one says trust. Question: which first message do you write, and which interview record backs it? Model answer: lead with the room-night price and speed promise, keep trust proof as the second line, and cite the seven price answers by date.

Activity. Write the one-sentence customer, a five-row rival table and eight interview records. Share them with a peer for a challenge round.

Standard to meet. Define a specific customer, build a rival table with prices, and trace every claim to an interview or a source.

Module 2 Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil 4 hUnderstand

Brazil asks every trader to be visible and accountable. Know which register, tax regime and sector rules apply before the first sale, and check each against the current official text.

  • In hostel or hotel business: Hotels need licences, fire and safety approvals, and guest registration.
  • Choose a legal form: micro-entrepreneur (MEI) only if your activity appears on the official MEI list, or a company registered with a CNPJ. Regulated activities need their sector authorisation first. Check the current limits and lists on the official portal.
  • Learn the tax regime that fits your size and write down which taxes you will collect and pay, and when.
  • Find the sector rules for hostel or hotel business: licences, inspections, professional registers. List each with the office that issues it and the renewal date.
  • Personal data of customers falls under Brazil's data protection law (LGPD). Collect only what you need, say why, keep it safe, and name who answers questions.
  • Consumer law gives buyers clear rights on information, returns and complaints. Put your terms in plain Portuguese.
Worked example. Checklist for a new hostel or hotel business trader: 1) legal form (CNPJ, or MEI only if your activity is on the official list); 2) city operating licence; 3) sector licence or register; 4) tax set-up; 5) privacy notice; 6) written terms. Each has an owner and a date.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a neighbour says registration can wait until the first R$ 1.700 of sales. Question: what do you do? Model answer: do not delay; write the compliance table first, confirm the register, tax and sector licence with the official office, and start only where the table shows no open blocker.

Activity. Build your own six-line compliance table for your first location. Mark what is done, what is pending and who confirms it.

Standard to meet. Cite an official source for every line, assume nothing, and date every pending item. Local law always wins over this course.

Module 3 Design the offer 4 hCreate

An offer states what the customer gets, how well, by when and at what price. For beds, rooms and services, the offer must make the quality visible and the next step easy.

  • In hostel or hotel business: Package room types, meals and extras; keep rates consistent across channels.
  • Describe the core offer in three lines: what is delivered (beds, rooms and services), the standard it meets, and the time it takes.
  • Add one proof for each promise: a sample, a test result, a guarantee or a customer record.
  • Price from cost upward and from customer value downward, then pick a price inside both.
  • Write a simple one-page terms sheet: what is included, what is extra, how to cancel.
  • Create two options at most, so choice stays easy.
Worked example. Offer: 'Beds, rooms and services, delivered within agreed time, checked against a written standard, price R$ 340 per room-night. Proof: a dated record the customer can check and sign. Changes or cancellation by written agreement before work begins.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a customer asks for a discount from R$ 340 to R$ 306 per room-night. Question: decide with numbers. Model answer: contribution falls from R$ 210 to R$ 176 per room-night; offer an extra of value (for example faster service) before cutting price, and accept only if the lower contribution still clears break-even.

Longer notes. Choose the room mix you can fill, such as private rooms, four-bed dorms or family rooms, and set one standard room-night rate for each. Cost a night from housekeeping, laundry, breakfast, utilities and amenities. Look at what similar hostels and hotels in your district charge on weekdays and weekends. Plan a clear cancellation rule and a check-in routine. Confirm your licence, fire-safety plan and guest-registration routine before you open the first room. Take photos of each room type in daylight and update them yearly, and write an honest description of noise, stairs and shared bathrooms. Accurate listings reduce complaints and keep review scores high.

Extra worked example. A 30-room property has 900 room-nights a month, sold at R$ 340. Variable cost is R$ 60 for housekeeping and laundry, R$ 30 for breakfast, R$ 25 for utilities and R$ 15 for amenities, so R$ 60 + R$ 30 + R$ 25 + R$ 15 = R$ 130. Margin is R$ 210, or 61,8 percent. Fixed cost is R$ 45.000, so break-even is 45.000 / 210 = 214,29, rounded up to 215. Those items are illustrative, not real market prices. Filling 600 of 900 nights leaves R$ 81.000 after staff and rent.

Activity. Write the offer card, the proof list and the terms sheet. Test them with three customers and note every point where they hesitated.

Standard to meet. Check that promise, proof and price are consistent, fit the terms on one page, and record three customer reactions.

Module 4 Run the work step by step 5 hApply

Good operations make results repeatable. For hostel or hotel business, write each step so a new person could follow it on their first day, and measure each step.

  • In hostel or hotel business: Front desk, housekeeping, maintenance and breakfast run on written checklists.
  • Map the process from first contact to paid and closed. Typical stages: enquiry, quote, preparation, delivery of the room-night, check, payment, follow-up.
  • Name the key resource at each stage: a booking and housekeeping routine. Note what happens if it is missing.
  • Write a one-page standard procedure per stage: who, what, tool, time, record.
  • Choose three measures: speed, quality (errors per 100) and customer rating. Review them every week.
  • Fix the cause of a defect, not the symptom. Ask 'why' five times and record the answer.
Worked example. Stage record: 'Prepare. Owner: lead. Time: 30 min. Tool: checklist. Record: signed sheet. Defect found on 1 of 150 room-nights last week; cause: missing check on a booking and housekeeping routine; fix: add check at start, review next Friday.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: of 600 room-nights last month, 36 had a defect traced to a booking and housekeeping routine. Question: what is the defect rate and what is your first action? Model answer: about 6 defects per 100; stop the cause (add a check at the start), correct affected customers, log the cause, and re-measure next week.

Activity. Draw the process map and write procedures for the two riskiest stages. Run each once and time it.

Standard to meet. Write procedures a stranger can follow, record weekly numbers for each measure, and trace one defect to a root cause.

Module 5 Safety, quality and risk 4 hEvaluate

The most serious risk in this field is empty rooms and slow cleaning. A calm, written risk routine protects people first and the business second.

  • In hostel or hotel business: Fire, security and food safety are priorities; drill evacuation.
  • List ten things that could go wrong. Score each for how likely (1 to 5) and how serious (1 to 5). Multiply and sort.
  • For the top five, choose a control: remove the hazard, replace it, guard it, train people, or use protective gear, in that order of preference.
  • Write who does what in an emergency, and rehearse it once a quarter.
  • Keep an incident and near-miss log. Near-misses are free lessons.
  • Check that insurance and legal duties match the risk you actually carry.
Worked example. Risk row: 'Hazard: empty rooms and slow cleaning. Likelihood 3, severity 5, score 15. Control: written check before each room-night, trained staff, log sheet. Owner: site lead. Review monthly.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a near-miss occurs: empty rooms and slow cleaning nearly happened but no one was hurt. Question: what do you do in the next 24 hours? Model answer: make safe, record the event, ask the five whys, add or tighten one control, brief the team, and set a review date; near-misses count as lessons, not blame.

Activity. Build a ten-row risk register and a one-page emergency plan. Run a ten-minute drill with a peer and note two improvements.

Standard to meet. Rank controls by preference for the top risks, run the drill, and start the log. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.

Module 6 People, skills and service 4 hApply

People deliver the promise. Define roles, train to a standard and give feedback in the open.

  • In hostel or hotel business: Train for service and shifts; cross-train staff.
  • Write a role card for each person: purpose, five tasks, the standard for each, and who to ask for help.
  • Train by showing, doing with support, then doing alone with a check. Record sign-off per task.
  • Set service rules for answering customers: reply times, tone, how to apologise and fix.
  • Hold a ten-minute team check each day: yesterday's problem, today's plan, one risk.
  • Build a growth path: what a new person can do in week 1, month 3 and month 12.
Worked example. Training record: 'Task: process a complaint. Shown 2 May; done with support 4 May; done alone 8 May, checked: apology, fix, follow-up call. Signed by lead.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a new hire completes training but a customer complaint shows a skipped step. Question: whose gap is it? Model answer: first the system: check the role card, the training sign-off and the checklist; then coach the person and fix the document so the step cannot be skipped.

Longer notes. Write a one-page standard for each department: front desk, housekeeping and maintenance. Run a daily ten-minute briefing on arrivals, departures and rooms out of order. Rehearse a check-in, a noise complaint and a lost key until staff reply the same way. Give each attendant a floor and inspect two rooms a day. Keep a roster with weekend cover, and confirm the working-hours rules for hospitality staff before you fix shifts. Give each department a short written handover at shift change, listing rooms out of order, late arrivals and special requests. Missed handovers cause most repeat complaints and are cheap to prevent.

Extra worked example. There are about 450 turnovers a month. Attendants trained to a set routine clean a room 8 minutes faster, which is 450 x 8 = 3.600 minutes, or 60 hours. At R$ 22 per hour that saves R$ 1.320 a month. Training 6 attendants for 4 hours costs 6 x 4 x R$ 22 = R$ 528. Those training hours and costs are illustrative worked-example numbers. The net gain in month one is R$ 1.320 - R$ 528 = R$ 792.

Activity. Write two role cards and a training plan for one new hire. Role-play a hard customer conversation twice and review.

Standard to meet. Write role cards with measurable standards, show the sign-off steps in training, and write the service rules in plain words.

Module 7 Money: cost, price and break-even 5 hAnalyse

A business lives or dies on a few numbers. Know your contribution per unit, your fixed costs and the volume you need.

  • In hostel or hotel business: Revenue per available room guides price; track occupancy daily.
  • Contribution per room-night = price minus variable cost. Fixed costs are paid whether you sell or not.
  • Break-even units = fixed cost divided by contribution. Plan to pass it by a safe margin.
  • Make a 12-month cash plan. Cash timing matters as much as profit: customers may pay late, suppliers may want money first.
  • Keep money for business and money for the owner apart.
  • Test the plan: what if sales fall by a quarter, or cost rises by a tenth?
Worked example. Worked example, all figures in reais: price R$ 340, variable cost R$ 130, contribution R$ 210 per room-night. Fixed cost R$ 45.000 a month. Break-even = R$ 45.000 / R$ 210 = 214,29, so 215 room-nights a month. At 600 room-nights a month, profit = R$ 210 x 600 - R$ 45.000 = R$ 81.000. These are teaching figures that belong to this example only; later examples and the case study use their own figures. Use your own.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: sales fall to 450 room-nights a month. Question: what is the monthly result? Model answer: R$ 210 x 450 - R$ 45.000 = R$ 49.500; compare with break-even of 215 room-nights and decide whether to cut fixed cost or raise volume first. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build your own table: price, variable cost, contribution, fixed cost, break-even and a downside case at 75% of sales. Show it to a peer or mentor.

Standard to meet. Calculate correctly, give every figure a source or mark it as an assumption, and show a downside case.

Module 8 Grow, measure and renew 4 hEvaluate

Growth should follow proof. Measure, learn, then add; and keep returning to the customer.

  • In hostel or hotel business: Grow by direct booking and corporate contracts.
  • Pick one growth lever at a time: new customers, a higher repeat rate, a better price, or a new offer.
  • Set one measurable target for 90 days, such as 'raise repeat customers from 20% to 30%'.
  • Ask each customer how they found you, and record it. Spend more where the record shows results.
  • Review monthly: numbers, defects, complaints, cash. Write three decisions each time.
  • Plan the renewal of licences, equipment and skills a year ahead.
Worked example. 90-day plan: 'Lever: repeat customers. Action: follow-up call after every room-night. Target: repeat rate from 20% to 30%. Check: weekly count. Review date: day 45 and day 90.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: you can add 120 room-nights a month by one of two levers: a follow-up call to past customers or a price rise of R$ 34 to R$ 374. Question: how do you choose? Model answer: test the cheaper reversible lever first (the call), measure for 30 days, and only then try the price change, so you can tell which one worked.

Longer notes. Record daily room-nights sold, average rate, complaints and review scores. Compare occupancy by weekday and find the weak nights. Build a corporate or group offer for those nights, with a clear price and cancellation rule. Track repeat guests and ask them for referrals. Review maintenance requests monthly, because a room out of order is a lost night. Adjust rates each season against costs and local events. Compare your rates and reviews with three nearby properties every month and note events that fill the city. Planning rates a month ahead for big weekends beats reacting after the rooms are gone.

Extra worked example. You sold 600 room-nights out of 900, an occupancy of 66,7 percent. Revenue is 600 x R$ 340 = R$ 204.000 and variable cost is 600 x R$ 130 = R$ 78.000. After R$ 45.000 fixed cost, profit is R$ 81.000. The pipeline and conversion numbers are illustrative worked-example figures. A group offer on quiet nights adds 60 room-nights, which is 60 x R$ 210 = R$ 12.600, so profit becomes R$ 93.600.

Activity. Write a 90-day plan with one target, three actions and a review date. Present it in five minutes and take questions.

Standard to meet. Set a measurable, dated target, break the actions down small enough to start this week, and put the review date in the calendar.

case study

A case to work through

Situation. the owner manages a small hotel with 30 rooms in Sao Paulo, which offers 900 room-nights in a month. The average price is R$ 340 per room-night, and housekeeping, breakfast, utilities and amenities cost about R$ 130. Fixed costs are R$ 45.000 a month for staff, rent, maintenance and booking software. Last month the hotel sold 600 room-nights, but weekdays were quiet. A company offers 60 extra room-nights a month on weekdays if the price falls to R$ 300. The owner does not know the break-even occupancy or whether the company deal is worth accepting. Weekend rooms sell out early, but the booking system shows no report by weekday, and the team makes price changes by feel.

Task. Say what you would do first, next and last, and what record you would keep.

Model answer. First, margin per room-night is R$ 340 - R$ 130 = R$ 210, or 61,8 percent. Break-even is 45.000 / 210 = 214,29, so 215 room-nights, or 23,9 percent occupancy. At 600, profit is 600 x R$ 210 - R$ 45.000 = R$ 81.000, with 66,7 percent occupancy. Next, the company deal gives R$ 300 - R$ 130 = R$ 170 per night. For 60 nights that is R$ 10.200, so profit becomes R$ 91.200, provided the nights would otherwise be empty and regular guests do not ask for the lower price. The R$ 300 rate and 60 nights are a worked-example scenario. Sign it only with a written weekday-only clause. Also, export occupancy by weekday every month, mark the quiet nights, and set one rate for each type of night. Review the housekeeping and maintenance logs weekly so rooms out of order do not silently reduce sellable nights. the owner's first report should split the 600 nights by weekday, so the 60 company nights fill quiet gaps, not sold-out Saturdays.

levels L1 to L7

Where this sits on the ladder

Seven levels, one slide each. Read the slide, then read the speaker notes under it. Credits stack and you can step in and out; this course is L4.

Slide 1 of 7: L1 Basic

  • Name the main departments in a hostel or hotel
  • Describe a guest journey from booking to departure
  • Identify basic safety rules
Notes for slide 1

Spend a shift at a front desk or on a housekeeping cart and name reception, housekeeping, maintenance, kitchen and security. Trace a guest from booking to checkout on a one-page chart, and learn fire exits, key control and spill handling. Hand in the chart and a glossary of ten hotel words. About six hours.

Slide 2 of 7: L2 Awareness

  • Explain occupancy, room rate and revenue per room
  • Recognize the difference between hostel and hotel models
  • Compare guest types in Sao Paulo
Notes for slide 2

Learn occupancy, average room rate and revenue per available room by working a week of mock figures for a 40-bed hostel and a 60-room hotel. Compare dorm and private-room models and the guest types in Sao Paulo: backpackers, business travellers, event visitors. Evidence is a calculation sheet and a one-page model comparison.

Slide 3 of 7: L3 Foundation

  • Handle a booking, check-in and complaint
  • Prepare a daily housekeeping plan
  • Read a simple daily sales report
Notes for slide 3

Run a front desk role play: take a walk-in, check in against a reservation, move a guest after a noise complaint, and close the shift with a cash and key count. Draw up a daily housekeeping plan by floor. Evidence is a supervisor's checklist and your reading of a daily sales report.

Slide 4 of 7: L4 Practitioner

  • Plan a hostel or hotel in Sao Paulo
  • Start it with licences, safety checks, staff and booking systems
  • Run front desk, housekeeping, pricing and monthly accounts
Notes for slide 4

Prepare an opening file for a 20-room property: licences and safety checks to confirm in the current official text, staff rota, booking system set-up, room rate sheet and a monthly accounts template. Operate a trial week of mock bookings. Evidence is the file plus a month of occupancy, housekeeping and income figures.

Slide 5 of 7: L5 Specialist

  • Specialize in one area such as pricing, events or guest experience
  • Use occupancy data to set rates
  • Design shared-space and social offers
Notes for slide 5

Choose pricing, events or guest experience and use occupancy by weekday to set rates for low, normal and peak nights. Design a common-room programme such as a walking tour or a communal dinner and measure attendance. Evidence is a rate calendar, a shared-space plan and the revenue effect.

Slide 6 of 7: L6 Advanced

  • Lead departments and set service standards
  • Manage budgets, maintenance and suppliers
  • Raise ratings and repeat bookings
Notes for slide 6

Lead housekeeping, front desk and maintenance heads with daily briefings and written standards. Manage a budget with lines for linen, energy and repairs, and a maintenance log for lifts and boilers. Evidence is a rating trend, repeat-booking share and cost per occupied room across two quarters.

Slide 7 of 7: L7 Super expert

  • Create a brand and operating system for several properties
  • Mentor managers and department heads
  • Advise on investment, expansion and risk
Notes for slide 7

Build a brand and operating manual that a second and third property can adopt, covering standards, systems and training. Mentor general managers and department heads. Review an investment or expansion case with occupancy, margin and risk. Evidence is the manual, mentee records and a decision paper.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, taxes, data duties and customer rights. This course is written for São Paulo. Check the current official text for your own place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registration: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registration: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registration: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registration: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registration: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registration: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore
