flagship course · GL-16

Mobile Application Testing Services

Test a mobile app methodically: plan tests, run them on real devices, report bugs so developers can fix them, and check speed, safety and access.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Test a mobile app methodically: plan tests, run them on real devices, report bugs so developers can fix them, and check speed, safety and access.

  • Understand Explain test types, risk and what makes a good bug report.
  • Apply Write test cases from requirements and run them on real or emulated devices.
  • Analyse Analyse bug patterns and prioritise by user harm.
  • Create Create a test plan, a bug report set and a release recommendation.
  • Evaluate Evaluate app quality with measures and user feedback.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, run and report a test cycle for one real or sample mobile app, with at least 30 test cases and 10 well-written bug reports.

Condition

An app you may test (open sample app or a practice build), two or more devices or an emulator, a free bug tracker or spreadsheet, a mentor with testing experience and a peer circle. About 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

Test plan approved; 30 cases run with pass or fail recorded; every bug has steps, expected and actual result, device and a picture; top five bugs ranked by user harm; release recommendation backed by numbers.

Who it is for: Testers, support staff, junior developers, college learners, career changers. Requirements: Basic computer and phone skills. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Test only apps you may test. Never use real personal or payment data; use practice accounts. Respect the owner's rules on publishing bugs.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Why test, and what 4 hUnderstand

Testing finds problems before users do.

  • Test levels: single parts, parts together, whole app, real users.
  • Types: function, usability, speed, safety, access, compatibility.
  • Testing shows bugs exist; it cannot show none exist.
  • Focus on risk: money, privacy and sign-in first.
  • Learn the app's purpose in one sentence before writing any case.
  • Check store listing claims against the real features.
Worked example. A food app: ordering and payment first, colour theme last.

Activity. List ten risks for a sample app and rank them.

Standard to meet. Ranking has reasons.

Common mistake. Testing everything equally.

Second worked example. Payment gets 15 cases, colour theme 2, because failure there harms users most.

Check yourself. What are your top three risks?

Module 2 Understand the app 6 hUnderstand

You test against what the app should do.

  • Read the requirements or user stories.
  • Draw the main user paths.
  • List devices, screen sizes and system versions to cover.
  • Note limits: network loss, low battery, interruptions.
  • Interview a user to learn how they actually use the app.
  • List permissions the app asks for and whether each is needed.
Worked example. Path: open, sign in, search, add to cart, pay, receive receipt. Seven steps, seven things to test.

Activity. Draw the paths and a device table.

Standard to meet. Paths complete; devices justified.

Common mistake. Testing without knowing the intended behaviour.

Second worked example. You ask the owner and learn that discounts apply after tax.

Check yourself. Where did you check the requirement?

Module 3 Write test cases 8 hApply

A good case is clear, small and repeatable.

  • Fields: id, goal, steps, data, expected result.
  • Include normal, edge and wrong inputs.
  • One idea per case.
  • Link each case to a requirement.
  • Add a precondition and a data set to each case.
  • Mark cases that must be re-run on every release.
Worked example. Case 12: enter a 3-digit PIN; expected: message "PIN needs 4 digits".

Activity. Write 30 test cases.

Standard to meet. Cases cover every path and at least 5 edge cases.

Common mistake. Cases that combine several ideas.

Second worked example. Split "sign up and pay" into two cases so a failure points to one cause.

Check yourself. Is each case one idea?

Module 4 Run on devices 8 hApply

Real devices show real faults.

  • Use at least two device sizes and two system versions.
  • Record pass, fail, blocked.
  • Capture screenshots and screen recordings.
  • Repeat a failure before reporting.
  • Test interruptions: calls, alarms, low battery and switching apps.
  • Test at least one old system version still in use.
Worked example. Case 7 fails on the small screen: button hidden under the keyboard; passes on the large screen.

Activity. Run all cases and record results.

Standard to meet. All 30 results recorded with evidence for fails.

Common mistake. Testing on one phone.

Second worked example. A button hidden on a small screen is found on device two.

Check yourself. Which devices did you use?

Module 5 Bug reports 8 hAnalyse

A report must let a developer see the bug in one try.

  • Title with place and symptom.
  • Steps to reproduce, expected, actual, device and version.
  • Picture or recording; how often it happens.
  • Severity (harm) and priority (urgency) are different.
  • Attach the log file if the tool offers one.
  • Mark if the bug is new or came back.
Worked example. "Cart total ignores discount code on small screen. Steps 1-5. Expected 90.00, actual 100.00. Always."

Activity. Write 10 bug reports.

Standard to meet. Each report is reproducible by a peer.

Common mistake. Vague bug titles.

Second worked example. "Doesn't work" becomes "Cart total ignores discount code on small screen".

Check yourself. Can a peer reproduce your bug?

Module 6 Speed, safety and access 10 hCreate

Non-functional faults hurt users too.

  • Time start-up and key screens; compare with a target.
  • Check basic safety: sign-out, data shown on screen, permissions asked.
  • Access: text size, contrast, screen reader labels.
  • Weak network and offline behaviour.
  • Time sign-in and checkout on slow data.
  • Check text size and contrast at the largest setting.
Worked example. Start-up 4.2 seconds against a 3-second target: raise as a speed bug.

Activity. Run five non-functional checks.

Standard to meet. Each check has a target and a result.

Common mistake. Ignoring weak networks.

Second worked example. The app hangs after 10 seconds without a message; raise it.

Check yourself. What did you test offline?

Module 7 Analyse and prioritise 6 hEvaluate

Not every bug matters equally.

  • Group bugs by area and cause.
  • Rank by user harm and how many are affected.
  • Find patterns: one cause may explain five bugs.
  • Recommend release, delay or release with limits.
  • Group bugs by screen to spot weak areas.
  • Keep a list of known issues the owner accepted.
Worked example. 12 bugs: 2 block payment (high), 5 layout (medium), 5 text (low). Recommendation: delay until payment fixed.

Activity. Build a bug summary table and rank the top five.

Standard to meet. Ranking uses harm and reach.

Common mistake. Ranking by how annoying it feels.

Second worked example. A typo is annoying; a double charge is harmful. Rank by harm.

Check yourself. What is your top bug by harm?

Module 8 Report and retest 4 hCreate

Close the loop.

  • Send a one-page summary: what was tested, found, risk, advice.
  • Retest fixes and a few related areas.
  • Update the case list for next time.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Add new cases for every fixed bug.
  • Share a short lesson for the developers.
Worked example. Retest shows payment fixed but a new bug in receipts: caught before release.

Activity. Write the summary and retest report.

Standard to meet. Summary leads with the recommendation.

Common mistake. Not retesting.

Second worked example. The fix to payment breaks receipts; retest found it.

Check yourself. What did retest find?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. The crash on rotate

The app crashes when rotating on one device.

Question: How do you report it?

Model answer: Give device, version, steps, expected, actual, frequency and a recording.

Case 2. The tight release

Two high bugs remain with launch tomorrow.

Question: What do you advise?

Model answer: Delay or release with the feature off, based on harm and numbers.

Glossary

Test case
A written check with steps and expected result.
Bug
A fault in behaviour.
Severity
How harmful a bug is.
Regression
A new fault after a change.
Emulator
A software copy of a device.
Edge case
A limit or unusual input.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: test plan, 30 cases with results, 10 bug reports, five non-functional checks, summary and retest. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why can testing not prove no bugs?

Model answer: It only shows that bugs found exist; untested paths remain.

Q2. Name three test types.

Model answer: Function, usability, speed, safety, access or compatibility.

Q3. What goes in a test case?

Model answer: Id, goal, steps, data, expected result.

Q4. Why repeat a failure before reporting?

Model answer: To confirm it is real and find the steps.

Q5. Severity versus priority?

Model answer: Severity is harm; priority is how soon to fix.

Q6. Why more than one device?

Model answer: Screens and versions change behaviour.

Q7. What is an edge case?

Model answer: A value at the limit or an unusual input.

Q8. What leads a test summary?

Model answer: The recommendation.

Q9. Why retest?

Model answer: To confirm fixes work and nothing else broke.

Q10. What data to use?

Model answer: Practice accounts and fake data only.

ten locations and global

Your place

App stores, accessibility and consumer rules differ by place: record which version and region you tested. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a Brainport-area small firm.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Partner to practise with: SEBRAE small-business support, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or a SkillsFuture-linked training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a TAFE or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico (Central America)

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
