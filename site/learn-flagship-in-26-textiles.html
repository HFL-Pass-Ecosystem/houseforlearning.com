flagship course

Textiles and Sustainable Manufacturing

Follow fibre to yarn to fabric to garment, test quality with simple numbers, cut waste and water use, and run a small unit safely and legally.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 44 h lessons plus practice

Follow fibre to yarn to fabric to garment, test quality with simple numbers, cut waste and water use, and run a small unit safely and legally.

  • Identify fibres and fabric structures by simple tests.
  • Apply yarn count, fabric weight and cost calculations.
  • Analyse defects and dye results to find the cause.
  • Evaluate a unit for water, energy and worker safety.
  • Create a costed production plan for one garment lot.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and track a small lot of 100 garments from fabric choice to packed goods, with a quality report and a sustainability record.

Condition

About 12 weeks part-time. Fabric swatches, a hand lens, a weighing scale, a tape, access to a small stitching or weaving unit, a mentor.

Standard

Fibres are identified correctly in 8 of 10 swatches; costs add up to within 2 percent; defects are logged per piece; water and waste per garment are measured and a reduction target is set.

Who it is for: Artisans, tailors, unit supervisors, design and merchandising students, co-operative members. Requirements: Basic arithmetic and ability to handle fabric and simple tools. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Fibres and their properties 5 hIdentify
  • Sort fibres into natural plant (cotton, jute), natural animal (wool, silk) and man-made (polyester, viscose).
  • Use the burn test with tongs in a safe tray: cotton burns fast with paper smell, wool curls with hair smell, polyester melts into a hard bead.
  • Check feel, strength and moisture take-up under a hand lens.
  • Match fibre to use: cotton for comfort, polyester for strength and quick drying.
Worked example. Ten swatches are numbered; a learner burns a 2 cm thread of each and names 8 correctly, noting that swatch 4 melted and so is polyester.

Activity. Identify ten fibre swatches and record the evidence.

Standard to meet. At least 8 of 10 are named correctly with the test result written.

Module 2 Yarn making and yarn count 5 hApply
  • Follow the steps: cleaning, carding, drawing, spinning, winding.
  • Weigh a 1 metre length of yarn to find its thickness.
  • Use the direct system: tex equals grams per 1000 metres.
  • Check twist, because too little twist weakens yarn and too much makes it kinky.
Worked example. A 10 metre piece of yarn weighs 0.6 g, so 1000 metres weigh 60 g and the yarn is 60 tex.

Activity. Weigh three yarns and calculate tex.

Standard to meet. All three tex values are correct and the thickest yarn is named.

Module 3 Weaving, knitting and fabric structure 6 hAnalyse
  • Tell woven fabric (two thread sets crossing) from knitted fabric (loops).
  • Name the plain, twill and satin weaves by their float pattern.
  • Count threads per inch in warp and weft with a pick glass.
  • Choose structure for use: knits stretch, woven fabrics hold shape.
Worked example. A shirting fabric has 60 warp and 52 weft threads per inch, giving 112 threads per square inch, which is a medium-dense plain weave.

Activity. Analyse five fabrics for structure and thread count.

Standard to meet. Structure and count are recorded and a use is justified for each.

Module 4 Dyeing, printing and finishing 6 hApply
  • Prepare fabric by scouring so dye takes evenly.
  • Follow a recipe by liquor ratio, for example 1 kg fabric in 20 litres of water.
  • Wear gloves, apron and mask, and keep dyes away from food areas.
  • Fix colour, rinse until water runs clear, and test for rubbing and wash fastness.
Worked example. A 2 kg lot at a 1 to 20 ratio needs 40 litres; the dye is 2 percent of fabric weight, which is 40 g.

Activity. Dye a 200 g swatch lot and run a wash test.

Standard to meet. Colour is even, quantities are calculated correctly and fastness is graded.

Module 5 Pattern, cutting and stitching 6 hCreate
  • Take body measures in centimetres and record them.
  • Draft a basic pattern with seam allowance of 1.5 cm.
  • Plan the cutting layout to waste less fabric.
  • Set stitch length and tension on a test piece before the lot.
Worked example. A kurta needs 2.4 metres in a good layout; a tighter layout brings it to 2.2 metres, saving 0.2 metres on each of 100 pieces, which is 20 metres.

Activity. Draft, cut and stitch one garment.

Standard to meet. Seams are straight and measures match within 1 cm.

Module 6 Quality control and defects 5 hAnalyse
  • Inspect each piece for holes, stains, loose threads and uneven seams.
  • Count defects per 100 pieces and chart them.
  • Trace the cause: needle damage, wrong tension, dirty machine.
  • Fix the cause and re-check the next batch.
Worked example. In 100 shirts, 12 had skipped stitches; changing a blunt needle cut the next lot to 3, a drop of 75 percent.

Activity. Inspect a lot of 20 pieces and log defects.

Standard to meet. Every defect is logged with a cause and a fix.

Module 7 Sustainability, safety and law 5 hEvaluate
  • Measure water used per kilogram of fabric and set a cut target.
  • Treat dye water and reuse rinse water where possible; do not dump effluent in drains or fields.
  • Keep fire exits clear, wire safe, and lint cleaned; provide guards and seating for workers.
  • Follow the labour codes on wages and hours and the law on child and adolescent labour, register the unit (for example on Udyam), and check State Pollution Control Board consent where dyeing is done.
Worked example. A unit using 120 litres per kilogram reuses the final rinse and falls to 90 litres, saving 30 litres per kilogram or 25 percent.

Activity. Audit a unit with a checklist.

Standard to meet. The audit lists five risks, each with an action and an owner.

Module 8 Capstone: a costed lot of 100 garments 6 hCreate
  • Choose fabric, count yardage and add 5 percent for waste.
  • Cost fabric, trims, labour, power and packing per piece.
  • Run the lot with a quality log and a water record.
  • Set a price with a margin and a plan to reduce waste.
Worked example. Cost per piece: fabric 180, trims 20, labour 90, power 10, packing 10, total 310 rupees; with a 20 percent margin the price is 372 rupees.

Activity. Present the plan, log and price.

Standard to meet. Costs reconcile within 2 percent and the quality and water records are complete.

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why is the burn test done in a tray with tongs?

Model answer: To avoid burns and fire and to catch melted drips. (Covered in module 1: Fibres and their properties. To pass that module: At least 8 of 10 are named correctly with the test result written.)

Q2. What does tex measure?

Model answer: Grams of yarn per 1000 metres, so higher tex is thicker. (Covered in module 2: Yarn making and yarn count. To pass that module: All three tex values are correct and the thickest yarn is named.)

Q3. Why do knitted fabrics stretch more than woven ones?

Model answer: Loops can open and change shape, while crossed threads lock. (Covered in module 3: Weaving, knitting and fabric structure. To pass that module: Structure and count are recorded and a use is justified for each.)

Q4. How much water does 3 kg of fabric need at a 1 to 20 ratio?

Model answer: 60 litres. (Covered in module 4: Dyeing, printing and finishing. To pass that module: Colour is even, quantities are calculated correctly and fastness is graded.)

Q5. Why test stitching on scrap first?

Model answer: To set tension and length without ruining the garment. (Covered in module 5: Pattern, cutting and stitching. To pass that module: Seams are straight and measures match within 1 cm.)

Q6. Why trace the cause rather than just repair the piece?

Model answer: So the defect does not repeat in the next lot. (Covered in module 6: Quality control and defects. To pass that module: Every defect is logged with a cause and a fix.)

Q7. Why is untreated dye water a problem?

Model answer: It pollutes water and soil and harms health and farming. (Covered in module 7: Sustainability, safety and law. To pass that module: The audit lists five risks, each with an action and an owner.)

Q8. What proves the lot was well planned?

Model answer: Costs, quality log and waste data that match the plan. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: a costed lot of 100 garments. To pass that module: Costs reconcile within 2 percent and the quality and water records are complete.)

Q9. Why add an allowance to fabric yardage?

Model answer: Cutting and faults waste some cloth. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why keep a defect log?

Model answer: To find causes and prevent repeats. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know common fibres and fabrics.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain the fibre to garment chain.
  3. L3 Foundation. Do tests and costing under guidance.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Run a small lot alone.
  5. L5 Specialist. Supervise a line and manage quality.
  6. L6 Advanced. Manage a unit with sustainability targets.
  7. L7 Super expert. Lead standards and training across a cluster or sector.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Fibres and fabrics

Identify 8 common fibres and fabrics by feel and a burn or water test, and state one property of each correctly.

Speaker notes. Teacher hands over 8 labelled swatches and checks the identification. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Fibre to garment chain

Explain each of the 6 steps from fibre to garment in order, naming the input and the output of each step.

Speaker notes. Teacher asks where a shirt cloth comes from and checks the order of steps. Covered in modules 2 to 5.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Tests and costing

Run 3 fabric tests under guidance and cost one garment with fabric, labour and a 5 percent wastage allowance on a costing sheet.

Speaker notes. Teacher recomputes the garment cost and checks the allowance was added. Covered in module 8, Capstone: a costed lot of 100 garments.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Small lot alone

Run a lot of 50 garments alone with a cutting plan, a defect log and fewer than 4 percent rejects at final check.

Speaker notes. Teacher counts rejects in the log against the lot size. Covered in module 6, Quality control and defects.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Line supervision

Supervise a line of 8 workers for a week, reaching the daily target on 4 of 5 days and keeping a defect chart updated each hour.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the chart for entries and the cause noted for each defect.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Sustainability unit management

Manage a unit with 3 measured targets, such as water per metre down 10 percent, and report the monthly figures with actions. Check local law with an expert.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the meter readings back the reported figures.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Sector standards and training

Write cluster standards for quality, safety and sustainability, and train 10 supervisors until 8 pass a graded audit of their units.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the standard and two audit sheets.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Risk lesson (estimates, not quotes). In India a rough sheet figure is Rs 480 per metre of fabric sold against a unit cost of Rs 340 and a fixed cost of Rs 120,000 a month: a margin of about 29% and a break-even of about 858 units a month. If the price falls 25% to about Rs 360, break-even rises from 858 to 6,000 units a month. Plan a price floor or a lower cost before you launch, and test the plan at the lower price. These sheet figures are separate from the sample numbers in the modules. Check them with a local price expert.
