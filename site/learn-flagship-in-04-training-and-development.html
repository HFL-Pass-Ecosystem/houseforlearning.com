flagship course

Training and Development Practice

Plan, deliver and measure workplace training that changes what people do on the job, from the first needs check to a 30-day follow-up with numbers.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 40 h lessons plus practice

Plan, deliver and measure workplace training that changes what people do on the job, from the first needs check to a 30-day follow-up with numbers.

  • Define a training need as task, condition and standard, backed by records.
  • Apply objectives, a timed plan and active practice to a real session.
  • Analyse assessment and job data to see what changed.
  • Evaluate a session using four levels of evidence.
  • Create a two-hour course with materials, a checklist and a follow-up plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design, deliver and evaluate a two-hour training session for a real work need, and report results 30 days later.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. A group of 8 to 15 learners, a room or online space, a projector or board, printed checklists, a mentor.

Standard

Every objective has an action verb and a measure; every activity links to an objective; at least 80% of learners meet the checklist standard; a 30-day follow-up shows a measured change in a work number.

Who it is for: Trainers, team leaders, HR staff, supervisors, teachers moving into industry. Requirements: None. Experience of any workplace helps. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Find the training need 5 hAnalyse
  • Separate a knowledge gap (they do not know how) from a motivation gap (they know but do not) and a system gap (tools or rules block them); training fixes only the first.
  • Gather evidence from three sources: records, direct observation of five tasks, and short talks with five workers and their supervisor.
  • Write the need as task, condition and standard, for example: record a stock-out within 10 minutes using the register.
  • Estimate the cost of the gap in rupees or hours so the sponsor can see why it matters.
Worked example. A shop chain finds 18 of 60 billing errors a week come from wrong discount entry. Observation shows 4 of 5 cashiers never learned the discount screen. Gap cost: 18 errors x Rs 150 average loss = Rs 2,700 a week. Need: enter a discount correctly in under 30 seconds.

Activity. Write a needs statement for a real group, with three pieces of evidence and a weekly cost of the gap.

Standard to meet. Statement has task, condition and standard, three evidence sources, and a cost figure.

Module 2 Write objectives 4 hApply
  • Use one observable verb per objective: list, demonstrate, calculate, compare, evaluate, design.
  • Add a measure: number correct, time limit or accuracy, for example 5 of 6 steps in order.
  • Avoid verbs you cannot see, such as understand, know or appreciate.
  • Match each objective to one assessment task so nothing is taught that is not checked.
Worked example. Weak: understand fire safety. Strong: list the 5 steps to use an extinguisher in order without notes, and demonstrate pulling the pin and aiming at the base of the fire in under 20 seconds.

Activity. Write five objectives for your session and pair each with its assessment task.

Standard to meet. All five have an action verb, a measure and a matching assessment.

Module 3 Plan the session 6 hCreate
  • Use the order open, teach, practise, check, close, with practice taking at least half of the time.
  • Time every step and keep 5 to 10 minutes of slack; prepare one spare activity and one cut-down option.
  • List materials, room layout and printouts per learner, and test every item the day before.
  • Use two worked examples: one shown by you, one done by learners with you watching.
Worked example. A 120-minute plan: 10 open with a real problem, 20 teach with a worked example, 60 practise in pairs, 15 check with a checklist, 10 close with action plans, 5 slack. Materials: 15 checklists, 15 practice sheets, 1 spare case study.

Activity. Build a timed plan with a materials list for your two-hour session.

Standard to meet. Times add to 120 minutes, each step links to an objective, and materials are listed.

Module 4 Deliver with confidence 5 hApply
  • Stand where all can see you, speak at a steady pace, pause after each question for five seconds.
  • Use names and ask open questions; invite quiet learners by asking for an example from their own work.
  • Handle a dominant speaker by thanking them and asking someone else to add a view.
  • Use a story from the learner's own work, not a distant example, to link new skills to daily tasks.
Worked example. A trainer asks a quiet cashier, Tell me about the last time a customer asked for a discount, then writes the steps she describes on the board. Learner talk time rises from 20% to about 60%.

Activity. Deliver a 20-minute segment to peers and collect notes from two observers.

Standard to meet. Peer notes name two strengths and one change; the change is tried in a second delivery.

Module 5 Online and blended delivery 4 hApply
  • Cut content into chunks of 10 to 15 minutes and add a poll, a question or a task between chunks.
  • Give instructions in text and voice, and say what the output must look like and by when.
  • Test the link, sound, screen sharing and a low-bandwidth plan on a mobile network before the day.
  • Keep a helper to answer chat and admit people, so you can focus on teaching.
Worked example. A 60-minute online session: 12 minutes teach, 3 minutes poll, 12 minutes breakout task, 10 minutes share, 12 minutes teach, 11 minutes practice and close. Audio fails for 2 learners, so the helper shares the slides as a downloaded file.

Activity. Convert a 30-minute segment to an online format and run a test with two people.

Standard to meet. Chunks are no longer than 15 minutes and the test run works on a mobile connection.

Module 6 Assess learning 5 hEvaluate
  • Use an observation checklist of 4 to 6 observable steps and mark each step done or not done.
  • Check during the session with quick questions, and after it with a short practical task.
  • Give feedback that names one thing done well, one step missed and one way to fix it.
  • Set a pass line before the session, for example 5 of 6 steps, and record results by learner.
Worked example. Checklist for entering a discount: opens screen, selects item, enters percent, checks total, prints bill, files copy. 12 of 15 learners complete 5 or more steps (80%). Three missed the total check; they get a 10-minute re-practice.

Activity. Build a checklist and mark two learners, giving feedback to each.

Standard to meet. Checklist items are observable and feedback names a specific step.

Module 7 Measure results 5 hEvaluate
  • Use four levels: reaction (did they like it), learning (can they do it), behaviour (do they do it at work), results (did a business number move).
  • Collect reaction in the last 5 minutes with a 3-question form; collect behaviour 30 days later by observing five tasks.
  • Compare a before and an after number, for example errors per week, and note other changes that could explain it.
  • Be honest in reports: say what is measured and what is a guess.
Worked example. Before: 18 discount errors a week. After 30 days: 5 errors. Reduction: 13 errors x Rs 150 = Rs 1,950 saved a week. Session cost Rs 6,000, so payback in about 3 weeks. Cashier turnover also fell, so the report notes this as a possible second cause.

Activity. Write a measurement plan with a tool, date and owner for each of the four levels.

Standard to meet. Plan has a named measure and date for each level, plus one caution about other causes.

Module 8 Capstone: run and report 6 hCreate
  • Deliver your two-hour session to at least 8 learners using your plan.
  • Collect reaction forms, checklist marks and, after 30 days, one work number.
  • Write a one-page report with objectives, data, three improvements and next steps.
  • Present the report to peers and a mentor and take questions.
Worked example. Report: 12 learners, 10 met the standard (83%), average reaction 4.4 out of 5, errors fell from 18 to 5 a week. Improvements: more practice time, a pocket guide, a refresher at week 6.

Activity. Deliver, measure and present the report.

Standard to meet. Report has all four levels of evidence and three improvements; at least 80% met the standard.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. How do you know a problem is not a training problem?

Model answer: If workers know how but tools, rules or rewards stop them, it is a system or motivation gap. (Covered in module 1: Find the training need. To pass that module: Statement has task, condition and standard, three evidence sources, and a cost figure.)

Q2. Why is the word understand a poor objective verb?

Model answer: It cannot be observed or measured, so you cannot tell if the objective was met. (Covered in module 2: Write objectives. To pass that module: All five have an action verb, a measure and a matching assessment.)

Q3. Why keep slack time in a plan?

Model answer: Learners and equipment are unpredictable, and slack prevents rushing the practice. (Covered in module 3: Plan the session. To pass that module: Times add to 120 minutes, each step links to an objective, and materials are listed.)

Q4. What can you do when one learner talks too much?

Model answer: Thank them, then ask others for a view or give each person a turn. (Covered in module 4: Deliver with confidence. To pass that module: Peer notes name two strengths and one change; the change is tried in a second delivery.)

Q5. How long should an online teaching chunk be before an activity?

Model answer: About ten to fifteen minutes. (Covered in module 5: Online and blended delivery. To pass that module: Chunks are no longer than 15 minutes and the test run works on a mobile connection.)

Q6. What three things should feedback include?

Model answer: What was done well, which step was missed and how to improve it. (Covered in module 6: Assess learning. To pass that module: Checklist items are observable and feedback names a specific step.)

Q7. Name the four levels of evaluation.

Model answer: Reaction, learning, behaviour and results. (Covered in module 7: Measure results. To pass that module: Plan has a named measure and date for each level, plus one caution about other causes.)

Q8. What makes a training report credible?

Model answer: It shows measured data at several levels and admits what is not known. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: run and report. To pass that module: Report has all four levels of evidence and three improvements; at least 80% met the standard.)

Q9. Why plan spare activities?

Model answer: Time and learner pace vary, and spare tasks prevent gaps or rushing. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. When do you follow up on the job?

Model answer: About thirty days later, once learners have had time to use the skill. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know what a training session is.
  2. L2 Awareness. Describe how adults learn.
  3. L3 Foundation. Deliver a short session using a plan.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Design and run a full session alone.
  5. L5 Specialist. Design programmes, coach trainers and measure results.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead a training function with budgets and standards.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set learning strategy and standards for an organisation.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: What a session is

Describe the parts of a training session, such as aim, activity and review, naming 5 with their purpose.

Speaker notes. Teacher asks the learner to label a sample session plan. Covered in module 3, Plan the session.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: How adults learn

Explain 4 ways adults learn best, and give one classroom example of each.

Speaker notes. Learner links each idea to a short activity. Covered in module 3, Plan the session.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Deliver a planned session

Deliver a 20-minute session from a given plan with 2 clear objectives and finish within 2 minutes of time.

Speaker notes. Teacher times the talk and ticks objectives met. Covered in module 3.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Design and run a session

Design and run a 60-minute session alone, with 3 objectives, 2 activities and a 5-question check of learning.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the plan and the quiz scores. Covered in module 7, Measure results.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Programmes and coaching

Design a 4-session programme, coach one trainer using a 6-point observation form and compare before and after scores.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the form and the score comparison.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Lead a training function

Lead a training team with a yearly budget, 5 quality standards and a report on attendance and results.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the budget sheet and one results report.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Set learning strategy

Set an organisation-wide learning strategy with 4 priorities, 3 measures and a review cycle.

Speaker notes. Teacher tests whether each priority has a measure and an owner.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
