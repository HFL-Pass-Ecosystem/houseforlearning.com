Reference Intelligence

Every book, bibliography, public library and open course behind HFL pathways, gathered into one register and wired into a single loop where authoring, training, learning and earning keep improving each other.

The continuous improvement loop

Learn, earn, train, author, and round again

Each HFL unit runs this loop. A learner who finishes a unit can sell what they made, then teach it, then improve it. Every turn leaves the unit better for the next cohort and pays the people who improved it.

Learn Education

The learner works through the unit with its reading shelf: an open textbook chapter, a national open-course lesson, a library e-book in their language, one live data set and two classic readings. The unit ends in a real output, such as a scenario map or a cash plan.

  • Passes to Author: questions asked, items most often failed, readings people abandon.
  • Award: HFL Module Certificate, with a Verified Progress Statement on request.

Earn Enterprise

The learner's output becomes a paid microservice for a local business or employer. A coach reviews the first three jobs. Logged hours count toward practicum credit.

  • Passes to Author: client ratings, rework requests, and jobs the unit did not prepare them for.
  • Gate to Train: Module Certificate plus three jobs rated 4 out of 5 or better.

Train Engagement

Proven earners become peer trainers for the same unit. Each trainer supports at least ten people and is paid per facilitated hour. Trainers keep a short log of what confused learners.

  • Passes to Author: facilitator notes and local examples.
  • Gate to Author: one rung above the unit, 50 facilitated hours, and learner outcomes at or above the unit average.

Author All three

Experienced trainers turn real jobs into case notes, write new questions, translate lessons and annotate readings. Each author also adds verified references to this register. Two assessors, one external, review every change before release.

  • Passes to Learn: a new unit version for the next cohort, with a change note.
  • Credit: named in the unit's version history and paid a share of unit usage.

Rules that hold the loop together

  • People before technology: the digital tutor drafts, people decide grades, sign-offs and anything ethical.
  • Learning before automation: nobody automates a task they have not first learned to do by hand.
  • Knowledge belongs to HFL; authors and trainers are its named custodians.
  • No unit depends on one person: at least one author with a backup and two trainers per unit.
  • Fair sign-off: two assessors, escalation after 15 working days, one free change of assessor.
  • Nobody is paid for recruiting. People are paid for work done: jobs, hours taught, content used.

How each role is paid (proposal for pricing review)

  • Earner: client fee for the microservice, less a platform fee.
  • Trainer: an hourly rate per facilitated session, plus a bonus when the group's results beat the unit average.
  • Author: a share of the unit's net revenue, split by how many learner-minutes each author's material carries.
  • Reviewer: a flat fee per reviewed change.
  • All rates are set in the pricing review; none are fixed on this page.

Signals the loop watches

  • Loop time: days from a gap signal to a released fix (target under 90).
  • Freshness: share of readings from the last five years, per unit.
  • Earners per 100 learners, and median earnings per earner.
  • Trainers per unit (at least 2) and authors per unit (at least 1 plus a backup).
  • Register growth: new verified references per quarter.

Loop maturity, unit by unit

  1. Aware: unit published with its reading shelf.
  2. Competent: first earners paid for microservices.
  3. Collaborative: two peer trainers running cohorts.
  4. Intelligent: signals drive a quarterly new version.
  5. Perpetual: graduates author most of the changes.
