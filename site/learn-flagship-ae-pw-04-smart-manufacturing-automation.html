Smart Manufacturing and Automation

Learn smart manufacturing and automation for the UAE: rules, workflow, costing in AED and a recorded 30-day practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 177 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Understand Explain the UAE market, rules and hazards for smart manufacturing and automation.
  • Apply Run the workflow: map the line, add sensors, automate one step, monitor and improve.
  • Analyse Measure overall equipment effectiveness in percent and find the biggest cause of loss.
  • Evaluate Cost and price in AED and judge scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a record pack a supervisor can audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a safety-first process brief, a costed throughput model and a 30-day observation log for automated production with sensors and data, in a simulated or supervised setting.

Condition

About 177 hours over 3 to 6 months. A supervised site visit or virtual plant walk-through, a free spreadsheet, a mentor and a peer circle. You do not operate live plant alone.

Standard

Hazards named with controls; every number has a unit and AED; records complete; rules confirmed with the local authority; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better.

Process learning only. Main risks: moving machinery, stored energy and robot zones. You work only under a competent supervisor and follow the site permit and safety rules.

Requirements: none. Currency: AED on every number.

lesson by lesson

11 modules with worked examples (177 h guided)

Module 1 Map the market and the people 5 hUnderstand

Start with who needs automated production with sensors and data and why. Around factories and contract makers drive demand in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other emirates.

  • List 10 offers or suppliers in the UAE with price per line audit in AED and who buys.
  • Interview 5 people from factories and contract makers: what do they buy now, how often, what do they pay, what annoys them?
  • Learn three terms: OEE (a score combining availability, speed and quality); PLC (a rugged computer that controls machines); Lockout (locking a power source off before any work).
  • Write a one-page brief: user, need, hoped-for price, and the rule that matters most.
Worked example. Five interviews all say delay is the main pain. You write: 'Reliable automated production with sensors and data, answer in one day, price near AED 8,000 per line audit.'

Activity. Complete the market table, five interview notes and the brief.

Standard to meet. Show ten offers priced; five users heard; one need stated in one sentence.

Module 2 Rules, hazards and licences 6 hApply

Know the rule before the work. Main hazards here: moving machinery, stored energy and robot zones. Hazard thinking means list each step, ask what could go wrong, and decide the control.

  • Ask your local authority or free zone which licence, permit or inspection applies. Write the answer and the date you asked.
  • For each step (map the line; add sensors; automate one step; monitor and improve) write the hazard and the control.
  • Use a hierarchy: remove the hazard, replace it, separate people from it, change the way of work, then protective equipment last.
  • Keep a one-page incident rule: who you call, what you stop, what you record.
Worked example. Step 'map the line': hazard = moving machinery, stored energy; control = written check before start and a named supervisor; record = signed log line.

Activity. Draw the flow and mark hazard and control at each step.

Standard to meet. Show that every step has a hazard and a control; licence question answered in writing.

Module 3 Core workflow, step by step 12 hApply

The work is a short chain: map the line, add sensors, automate one step, monitor and improve. Learn each link, then link them with records.

  • Write a checklist for each of the 4 steps: input, action, check, output.
  • Time each step three times and note the slowest.
  • Record a defect or delay each time it happens: what, where, cause, fix.
  • Measure overall equipment effectiveness in percent; this is your main number.
Worked example. Three timed runs of 'add sensors' take 42, 38 and 51 minutes. Average 43.7; the 51-minute run had a missing tool. Fix: a tool tray checked at start.

Activity. Run the chain three times (simulated or real) and fill the log.

Standard to meet. Show that three complete logs; the slowest step has a cause and a fix.

Module 4 Quality and measurement 10 hAnalyse

What you do not measure you cannot improve. Your main measure is overall equipment effectiveness in percent.

  • Define overall equipment effectiveness in percent: what counts, what does not, and over what period.
  • Draw a simple chart of the last 10 results and mark the average.
  • Find the top two causes of loss with a tally sheet, then fix the biggest one first.
  • Check measuring tools: are they accurate, clean and in date?
Worked example. Tally of 40 faults: 22 from late inputs, 9 from wrong settings, 9 other. Fix late inputs first: that is 55% of faults.

Activity. Collect a tally for one week and make a Pareto chart.

Standard to meet. Show that tally complete; biggest cause named; one fix planned with a date.

Module 5 Costing and pricing in AED 12 hEvaluate

Know the cost of one line audit before you set the price. Include materials, labour, energy, transport, licence share, waste and a safety margin.

  • Cost per line audit = total cost divided by line audits sold (not made).
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs divided by gain per unit.
  • Test the price with two users and write down the answer.
Worked example. At AED 8,000 per line audit and cost AED 3,500, gain is AED 4,500 and margin 56.3%. At 6 line audits a month, gross gain is AED 27,000. If fixed costs are AED 4,200, break-even is 1 line audit.

Activity. Build the cost sheet with AED on every line and have your mentor check it.

Standard to meet. Show no missing cost found; margin calculated twice by different routes.

Module 6 Serve and sell responsibly 12 hApply

Customers return when you keep promises. Promise only what your workflow can deliver.

  • Write a one-page offer: what is included, what is not, price, time, how you fix mistakes.
  • Write a short script for the first call or visit.
  • Agree dates in writing; confirm each change.
  • Handle a complaint in four steps: listen, apologise for the impact, fix, record what changed.
Worked example. Offer: 'Automated production with sensors and data delivered within one working day; price AED 8,000 per line audit; faults corrected free within 3 days.'

Activity. Present the offer to two users and note changes.

Standard to meet. Show that offer has scope, price, time and fix rule; two users commented.

Module 7 Run the 30-day observation 55 hCreate

Small, real and recorded. Change one thing at a time and learn quickly.

  • Keep a daily log: date, units, cost, delays, defects, safety notes.
  • Hold a 15-minute review each week with your mentor.
  • Write down every complaint and what you changed.
  • Take photos only where allowed and never of people without consent.
Worked example. Week 2 shows overall equipment effectiveness in percent at 71%; week 4 at 84% after the tool tray and an earlier input check.

Activity. Complete 30 days of the log and four weekly reviews.

Standard to meet. Show 30 days logged; each defect has a cause; weekly notes kept.

Module 8 Review, decide and plan 38 hEvaluate

A decision beats a feeling. Compare plan with result and choose to scale, change or stop.

  • Make a before and after table: cost per unit, defects, delays, repeat users.
  • Write three lessons.
  • Decide scale, change or stop, with the numbers behind it.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
Worked example. Result: cost per line audit fell from AED 3,500 to AED 3,290; repeat users 6 of 9. Decision: scale to a second site; keep price.

Activity. Present to a review room and collect one written comment from a user.

Standard to meet. Show decision backed by numbers; two peer reviews given.

Module 9 Sensors, PLCs and the data trail 8 hUnderstand

A smart line senses what is happening, decides with a controller and records the result. Knowing the chain helps you trace a fault.

  • A sensor measures something such as position, temperature or count and sends a signal.
  • A PLC (programmable logic controller) reads inputs, runs a stored program in a repeating scan and switches outputs such as motors and valves.
  • Data is time-stamped and stored so trends can be studied later.
  • Draw the chain from sensor to PLC to actuator to screen for one machine.
Worked example. A bottle filler counts 120 bottles per minute. The sensor sends 2 pulses per bottle, so the PLC sees 240 pulses per minute. If it reads only 200 pulses per minute, either the line slowed or a sensor is missing pulses, so check the sensor first.

Activity. Draw the sensor-to-screen chain for one machine and list what would be logged each minute.

Standard to meet. Show every stage named; signal types labelled; log fields have units.

Module 10 Machine guarding and lockout 10 hApply

Automated machines can start without warning. Guards and lockout are the main protection.

  • Never remove or bypass a guard, light curtain or emergency stop.
  • Lockout every energy source: electrical, air, hydraulic, stored spring or gravity.
  • Test that the machine will not start after locking, then work.
  • Robot zones have marked limits; enter only with the zone switched to a safe mode.
Worked example. To clear a jam on a packing machine, the operator presses stop, isolates power and air, fits a personal lock, releases trapped air pressure of 6 bar, presses start to prove nothing moves, then clears the jam.

Activity. Write a lockout sequence for one machine listing every energy source and the proving step.

Standard to meet. Show all energy sources listed; proving step present; no guard bypassed.

Module 11 OEE and deciding what to improve 9 hEvaluate

Overall equipment effectiveness shows where time and output are lost, so effort goes where it pays.

  • OEE equals availability times performance times quality.
  • Availability is run time divided by planned time.
  • Performance is actual speed divided by ideal speed.
  • Quality is good units divided by total units.
Worked example. Planned 480 minutes, run 408 minutes: availability 85%. Ideal 100 units per minute, actual 90: performance 90%. 36,720 made, 35,620 good: quality 97%. OEE = 0.85 x 0.90 x 0.97 = 74%. Biggest loss is availability, so look at stops first.

Activity. Compute OEE for a sample shift and rank the three losses.

Standard to meet. Show each factor shown as a percent; product correct; biggest loss named with a suggested check.

levels L1 to L7

Slide by slide, with notes

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Sensor, controller and machine

On the slide: L1 Basic · Sensor, controller and machine

Notes: Name the parts of a smart line (sensor, PLC, actuator, screen) and say what each one does. Notes: use one machine the learner can see; draw the chain from sensor to screen with arrows.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Guards and lockout rules

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Guards and lockout rules

Notes: Describe why a guard must never be bypassed and why every energy source is locked out before work, under a competent supervisor. Notes: list electrical, air, hydraulic, spring and gravity energy; a trapped pressure such as 6 bar must be released and then proved safe.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Read the pulses from a sensor

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Read the pulses from a sensor

Notes: Apply a simple count to check a sensor, working only on sample data. Notes: 120 bottles a minute at 2 pulses per bottle should show 240 pulses a minute; if the PLC shows 200, check the sensor first.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Measure OEE for one shift

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Measure OEE for one shift

Notes: Compute availability, performance, quality and OEE for a sample shift and name the biggest loss. Notes: check 408 of 480 minutes is 85%, 90 of 100 units a minute is 90% and 35,620 of 36,720 good is 97%, giving OEE of 74%.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Pick the loss that pays most

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Pick the loss that pays most

Notes: Compare line audits before and after a change and decide to scale, change or stop. Notes: OEE rising from 71% in week 2 to 84% in week 4 is the practice case; price check is AED 8,000 per line audit against AED 3,500 cost, a margin of 56.3%.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Coach a team on lockout and stops

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Coach a team on lockout and stops

Notes: Set a lockout sequence with a proving step, a stop-reason code list and a weekly OEE review for a small team. Notes: sample five lockouts a week for every energy source listed and no guard bypassed; rank the top three losses each week.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Design the whole smart line system

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Design the whole smart line system

Notes: Design the sensor, PLC, data and safety controls for several lines, audit guard and lockout risks and write a 12-month plan. Notes: plan for 6 line audits a month; present one risk, one saving and an OEE target for each quarter.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.
