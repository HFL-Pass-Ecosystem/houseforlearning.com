Sell Products Online

Run a pilot of sell Products Online in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small sell Products Online offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: commerce, retail and online selling. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the words and the ways to sell online 6 hUnderstand

Selling online means a buyer finds, pays and receives a product without meeting you. Start with the basics.

  • Learn the terms: listing, SKU, stock, shipping, returns, fees, checkout, marketplace, own store.
  • An online marketplace lends you its buyers; an own store gives you control but you bring the buyers.
  • Name one buyer in one line: who, what they need, what they worry about.
  • Search ten listings of a product and note price, photos and delivery time.
  • Compare marketplace and own-store fees for the same item.
  • Describe the buyer's path: search, compare, trust, pay, wait, receive.
  • List what a buyer cannot check online, such as size and feel, and how your listing answers it.
Worked example. Product: handmade candles. Buyer: a gift shopper who wants a clear scent, fast delivery and easy returns. Of ten listings you read, 5 promise delivery in 3 days or less, so 5 / 10 = 50 percent, and you decide to promise it too.

Activity. Compare ten listings in a table and write a buyer profile.

Standard to meet. Task: study ten listings. Condition: public pages. Standard: table complete and buyer fits one line.

Module 2 Explain how an order moves from click to delivery 6 hApply

An order has steps. If you can name them, you avoid late or lost parcels.

  • The steps: order, payment, pick, pack, ship, track, deliver, support, return.
  • Know your handling time and tell buyers a delivery window.
  • Keep a stock count that updates after each sale.
  • Write what you do when an item is lost or damaged.
  • Set a cut-off time for same-day packing and state it in the listing.
  • Choose tracked shipping for parcels over a set value and write the value.
  • Keep spare packing material so a rush of orders does not delay shipping.
Worked example. A buyer orders at 2 p.m. You pack by 5 p.m., ship the next day and send the tracking number. If handling takes 1 day and the carrier takes 3 to 5 days, you tell the buyer 4 to 6 days (1 + 3 to 1 + 5).

Activity. Draw an order flow for your product with a time and an owner for each step.

Standard to meet. Task: map the order flow. Condition: one page. Standard: every step has an owner, a time and a record.

Module 3 Create a clear listing and photos 10 hApply

Now you apply the basics. Make a listing that is honest and easy to buy from.

  • Title: say what it is, size, colour and material in plain words.
  • Take photos in good light from several sides, and show the real item.
  • Describe condition, size, weight and care. State delivery cost and time.
  • Do not use claims you cannot prove.
  • Add a scale photo of the item beside a common object.
  • Include the weight and box size so shipping is priced right.
  • Check that your first photo is clear on a small phone screen.
Worked example. Before: 'Amazing candle!!' After: 'Soy wax candle, 200 g, cedar scent, burns about 40 hours.' If you burn it 5 hours an evening, 40 / 5 = 8 evenings, which you can state once you have tested it.

Activity. Create three listings with five photos each and edit each once.

Standard to meet. Task: write three listings. Condition: from real products. Standard: all facts true and checkable, price and delivery stated.

Module 4 Follow Canadian selling rules 8 hEvaluate

Consumer, tax and product rules apply when you sell online in Canada. Learn which ones touch you.

  • Consumer protection is mostly provincial: refunds, warranties and price display differ by province.
  • Collect and remit sales tax once you pass the small-supplier limit; check the current limit with the tax agency.
  • Some goods have label, bilingual and safety rules, especially for children and food; check the current rules.
  • Sell only goods you have the right to sell. Check the marketplace's own seller rules.
  • Write a shipping and return page in plain words.
  • Check whether your products need ingredient, material or origin labels; check the current rule.
  • Keep sales records and receipts for the period the tax agency requires.
Worked example. A buyer asks for a refund on a faulty item. You check your stated return rule and your province's rule, then respond in writing within a day. You keep the reply and the order number in one folder.

Activity. Build a table of rule, who sets it, what you must do and where you checked, for ten items.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: from official public pages. Standard: each row has a dated source; unknowns say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.

Module 5 Price products and check it pays 8 hApply

Price covers the product, fees, shipping and your time. Work the numbers for each item.

  • Landed cost = item cost + shipping in + packaging.
  • Fees: marketplace fee, payment fee, ads. Subtract them from the sale price.
  • Margin = (price - all costs) / price.
  • Test a price rise of 10 percent and see if sales hold.
  • Add every cost to one sheet, one row per product.
  • Find the lowest price that still gives the margin you want.
  • Decide whether to show shipping separately or build it into the price.
Worked example. Item costs 12, packaging 1.50, fees 5 and shipping paid by you 8, so total cost = 12 + 1.50 + 5 + 8 = 26.50. At a sale price of 48, profit = 48 - 26.50 = 21.50, and margin = 21.50 / 48 = about 45 percent. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build a price sheet for ten items with all costs and margin.

Standard to meet. Task: compute profit per order. Condition: with a spreadsheet. Standard: every cost shown and a mentor can follow the numbers.

Module 6 Get found and win trust 6 hCreate

Buyers buy from sellers they trust. Build a steady way to be found.

  • Use the words buyers type in your titles and descriptions.
  • Answer questions within one working day.
  • Ask satisfied buyers for honest reviews; never buy or write fake reviews.
  • Use consent when you collect emails; follow Canada's anti-spam rules.
  • Write answers to the five most common questions and place them in the listing.
  • Keep a list of the words buyers use in messages and reuse them in titles.
  • Track the share of visitors who buy and write the number.
Worked example. A buyer asks about size. You reply with measurements and a photo beside a coin. They order and leave a review. If 200 visitors give 6 orders, 6 / 200 = 3 percent buy.

Activity. Run a four-week test: list, answer, ship and ask for reviews. Log sales and replies.

Standard to meet. Task: run a four-week test. Condition: real listings. Standard: log complete with sales, response time and one lesson.

Module 7 Protect buyers, data and money 6 hEvaluate

Care for buyer data, payments and stock builds a lasting shop.

  • Use the platform's secure checkout; never ask for card details by message.
  • Keep buyer addresses private and delete what you no longer need.
  • Watch for fake orders, chargebacks and overpayment scams.
  • Check recalls and safety for goods, especially used or imported items.
  • Check the platform's payment rules before you ship a high-value order.
  • Photograph each parcel before it ships to answer damage claims.
  • Limit who can sign in to your seller account and review the list monthly.
Worked example. A buyer offers to overpay and asks you to return the difference. You refuse and report it to the platform. Refunding an extra 30 from a payment that later fails would cost you 30 dollars.

Activity. Write a returns policy, a privacy note and a scam checklist (one page each).

Standard to meet. Task: write three documents. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: each is in plain words and dated.

Module 8 Run a real pilot and decide 8 hCreate

Now you run it for real. Sell, measure and decide how to grow.

  • Set a 60-day goal, such as 30 orders and a 30 percent margin.
  • Every week record orders, profit, returns, late parcels and questions.
  • Ask five buyers what to improve.
  • At the end choose: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Pick the one number you will improve most: orders, margin or returns.
  • Photograph your packing station and note what slows you down.
  • Hold a short review each week and write one decision.
Worked example. After 60 days: 34 orders, profit 920 dollars, 2 returns. 920 / 34 = about 27 dollars per order, and 2 / 34 is about 6 percent returns. You decide to drop the slowest product and raise one price.

Activity. Run the pilot, keep the weekly log and present a 12-month plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a 60-day pilot. Condition: real products and buyers. Standard: log complete, decision uses numbers, a panel can repeat the plan.

Model answer: Access to its buyers, for a fee. (Covered in module 1: Know the words and the ways to sell online. To pass that module: Task: study ten listings. Condition: public pages. Standard: table complete and buyer fits one line.)

Q5. How do you compute margin?

Model answer: Price minus all costs, divided by price. (Covered in module 5: Price products and check it pays. To pass that module: Task: compute profit per order. Condition: with a spreadsheet. Standard: every cost shown and a mentor can follow the numbers.)

Model answer: Once you pass the small-supplier limit or choose to register; check the current rule. (Covered in module 4: Follow Canadian selling rules. Q8. How do you earn good reviews?

Model answer: Ship as promised, answer fast and ask honestly; never fake them. (Covered in module 6: Get found and win trust. To pass that module: Task: run a four-week test. Condition: real listings. Standard: log complete with sales, response time and one lesson.)

Model answer: Check recalls and safety rules before selling. (Covered in module 7: Protect buyers, data and money. To pass that module: Task: write three documents. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: each is in plain words and dated.)

Q10. What do you do when unsure about a rule?

Model answer: Check the current rule with the local authority and keep the answer in writing. (Covered in module 4: Follow Canadian selling rules. To pass that module: Task: build a rules table. Condition: from official public pages. Standard: each row has a dated source; unknowns say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.)

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
