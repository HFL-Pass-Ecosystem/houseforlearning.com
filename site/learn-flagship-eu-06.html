Childcare Products and Services

Plan, pilot and review one safe, play-based childcare product or session with a partner centre, with a trained adult always present.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 142 hLearning Course, 3-6 months
  • Understand Explain what makes a play session safe for an age range and why a trained adult is always present.
  • Analyse Break one session into steps, hazards and cost per child.
  • Apply Run the pilot session with the partner centre and keep the session log.
  • Evaluate Judge the session against the safety checklist and the parents' feedback.
  • Create Produce a session pack and a 90-day plan for repeat sessions.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, pilot and review one safe, play-based childcare product or session with a partner centre, with a trained adult always present.

Condition

A partner centre with a trained adult present at all times, a real or model group of children, free tools, currency written on every number. This course teaches process and safety awareness only; the child-care authority sets the rules.

Standard

The centre lead and a mentor review the pack; the safety checklist has no gaps for the age range; at least 70% on the check; two peer reviews given.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Find a real need 6 hUnderstand

Talk to five parents or carers and one centre. Ask what is hardest in the day: safe play, meals, learning aids, after-school care. Write down what they do now, how often and what they pay.

  • Pick ONE age group (for example 3 to 5 years). Needs change fast with age.
  • Separate what adults say they want from what children can safely do.
  • Check the price range: note three similar offers and their prices in your currency.
Worked example. Need found: parents of 3-4 year olds want a 45-minute weekend play session for 8 children that is calm, safe and teaches colours and counting. Three local sessions charge EUR 12 to 20 per child.

Activity. Interview five adults and write a one-page idea: age group, offer, place, price you hope for.

Standard to meet. Record five interviews, choose one age group and compare three price points.

Module 2 Safety standards and age-appropriate design 6 hAnalyse

Safe design starts with the age range. Small parts, sharp edges, long cords, unsafe materials and unsupervised water are the main hazards.

  • Choking: anything that fits through a tube the size of a toilet-roll core is unsafe for under-threes.
  • Materials: non-toxic, washable, no loose paint or small magnets. Check your country's product-safety rules.
  • Design for what the child can do: 2-3 year olds grasp and stack; 4-5 year olds follow two-step games.
  • Write the supervision ratio your local authority expects and plan to exceed it in the pilot.
Worked example. A wooden counting set for 3-year-olds: pieces larger than the choking size, water-based paint, rounded corners, tested by pulling and dropping. Label: age 3+.

Activity. Write a one-page safety checklist for your product or session and mark each line pass or fix.

Standard to meet. Give every hazard a control, and have the trained adult in charge sign the checklist.

Module 3 Build the first version 35 hApply

Design a 45-minute play-based session or a first product. Learning through play means the activity is the lesson.

  • Structure: welcome (5 min), main play (20), quiet activity (10), tidy and goodbye (10).
  • Write the learning aim as one verb and one measure: "Count to 5 with objects" and note how many reach it.
  • Plan B: what you do if the group is tired, loud or one child is upset.
  • Safeguarding: never be alone with a child; always a second adult; know whom to tell and how to record a concern.
Worked example. Session plan "Colour hunt": hide 10 coloured cards, children find and sort by colour. Aim: name 4 colours. Plan B: seated sorting game. Ratio planned: 1 adult for 4 children.

Activity. Write the full session plan or product spec with checklist, plan B and materials list with costs.

Standard to meet. Plan fits 45 minutes; safety checklist attached; costs listed with currency.

Module 4 Safeguarding and communication 10 hApply

Safeguarding means protecting children from harm. You teach the awareness here; local law and your partner centre set the rules.

  • Know the reporting route: who to tell, in what time, and how to record facts only.
  • Never promise to keep a secret; never be alone with a child; never take photos without written permission.
  • Talk to parents in plain words: what happened, what we did, one next step.
  • Keep personal data minimal and locked.
Worked example. A parent note after the pilot: "Today the group practised counting to 5 with blocks. One child counted to 4 and asked for more. At home you could count stairs together."

Activity. Write your parent note and a one-page safeguarding rule card for helpers.

Standard to meet. Write a note under 80 words that is factual, and name the reporting person on the card.

Module 5 Run the supervised pilot 35 hAnalyse

Run the session or product trial with the partner centre. A trained adult is always present and in charge.

  • Before: check the room, the checklist and the first-aid kit location.
  • During: observe more than you talk; write quick notes, not names in public files.
  • After: clean up, record any near miss, and thank the staff.
  • Collect parent feedback with three simple questions.
Worked example. Pilot result: 8 children, 7 stayed for 45 minutes, 6 counted to 4 or more. One near miss: a chair too close to the door. Fixed by moving it.

Activity. Run the pilot; keep the checklist, observation notes and feedback forms.

Standard to meet. Pilot happened with a trained adult; all notes dated; one improvement made.

Module 6 Feedback, costing and price 14 hEvaluate

Value comes when parents see learning and the price covers cost.

  • Cost per child = (materials + helper time + room + insurance share) ÷ children.
  • Margin = (price − cost) ÷ price.
  • Read feedback for patterns: three parents say the same thing, change it.
  • Compare your price to the three you found in module 1.
Worked example. Cost: materials EUR 40, helper time EUR 60, room EUR 30 (total EUR 130) for 8 children = EUR 16.25 per child. Price EUR 20 gives a margin of 18.75%. Parents said the session was a little long: shorten the quiet time.

Activity. Build the cost sheet and compute cost per child and margin; decide: keep, change or stop.

Standard to meet. Give every cost line a currency and write the decision with a reason.

Module 7 Improve and prepare to offer it 28 hCreate

Turn the pilot into a repeatable offer.

  • Write the final session plan, safety checklist and a one-page parent leaflet.
  • Set a schedule, number of places and cancellation rule.
  • Check legal needs with the local authority: registration, insurance, background checks.
  • Plan how you will find the next ten families.
Worked example. Offer card: Saturday 10:00, 8 places, EUR 20 per child, ages 3 to 5, refund if cancelled 24 hours before.

Activity. Produce the final offer pack and a 90-day plan.

Standard to meet. Pack is complete; the 90-day plan names who, what, when.

Module 8 Review room and next step 8 hCreate

Present your evidence and give peer feedback.

  • Show: need, safety checklist, plan, pilot notes, feedback, costing, decision.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
  • Take the 10-question check.
  • Choose a follow-on: Online Sales or IT Business.
Worked example. Score sheet, each line marked 0 to 5 (3 or more meets the standard): child safety 5, learning aim 3, parent communication 4, safeguarding 4, costing 4. Total 20 of 25 = 80%, above the 70% line. Learning aim at 3 is the weakest line, so it gets the first dated fix.

Activity. Present for 8 minutes, then answer three questions: what you changed after the pilot, what you would stop, and how many children one adult can supervise.

Standard to meet. Score all rubric lines at 3 or more and give the weakest line a dated fix.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use local units, rules and authorities.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Business set-up: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Product and food rules: FSSAI.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Business set-up: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Product and food rules: NVWA.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Business set-up: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Product and food rules: the municipality food-control team (for example Dubai Municipality).

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Business set-up: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Product and food rules: ANVISA and MAPA.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Business set-up: federal or Ontario business registration. Personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Product and food rules: the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA).

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Business set-up: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Product and food rules: the FDA and the state health department.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Business set-up: registration with ACRA. Personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Product and food rules: the Singapore Food Agency (SFA).

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Business set-up: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Product and food rules: Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) and the state health authority.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Business set-up: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Product and food rules: the Centre for Food Safety.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Business set-up: local business registration in the country where you work. Personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Product and food rules: the national food-safety authority.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Business set-up: a trade and business licence or company registration. Personal data: the Data Protection Act. Product and food rules: the public health authority.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Business set-up: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Product and food rules: the federal health-risk authority.
