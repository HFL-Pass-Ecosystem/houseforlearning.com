CPD · Teaching

Adapting a lesson for mixed ability

Planning one lesson that works for learners at different levels.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 3 h 20 min of CPDSelf-study plus role-playTeaching

Planning one lesson that works for learners at different levels.

  • Identify three learner needs, given a class profile, with one support for each.
  • Modify a task, given a standard task, into easier, same and harder versions with the same outcome.
  • Group learners, given a list, with a reason for each grouping.
  • Evaluate the adapted lesson, given a checklist, with two changes for next time.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Adapt one lesson so that learners at different levels reach the same outcome by different routes, with scaffolds, extensions and recorded reasons

Condition

Given a class profile, a standard task, a ten-item worksheet and a checklist, in a 3-hour planning workshop

Standard

Write three levels of one task leading to the same outcome, choose a scaffold for each learner need, and evaluate the lesson against a checklist with two changes

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Same outcome, different routes 30 min
  • The outcome stays the same; the route changes.
  • Adapting does not lower the standard.
  • Write the outcome first.
Worked example. Outcome: "Compare two fractions." Level 1: pictures with halves and quarters. Level 2: common denominators with prompts. Level 3: unlike denominators and explain why. All 3 lead to one outcome.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "same outcome, different routes": identify three learner needs, given a class profile, with one support for each.

Standard to meet. Show that the outcome stays the same while the route changes, by writing three levels of one task that all lead to that outcome.

Module 2 Scaffolds: prompts, word banks, pictures 40 min
  • Choose prompts, word banks or pictures from learner needs.
  • Scaffolds are temporary.
  • Remove them as skill grows.
Worked example. Class profile: 2 learners new to the language (word bank), 3 learners who need steps (prompt card). 5 scaffolds for 5 learners, 2 types.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "scaffolds: prompts, word banks, pictures": modify a task, given a standard task, into easier, same and harder versions with the same outcome.

Standard to meet. Identify learner needs from a class profile and choose a scaffold for each, such as prompts, a word bank or pictures.

Module 3 Extension for fast learners 25 min
  • Extend with an open question or a create-a-problem task.
  • Do not raise the standard for others.
  • Keep extension optional.
Worked example. Extension: "Write a fraction pair where the larger fraction has the smaller denominator and explain why." Offered after 4 of 5 items are correct.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "extension for fast learners": group learners, given a list, with a reason for each grouping.

Standard to meet. Modify a task with an extension for fast learners, such as an open question or asking them to create a problem, without raising the standard for others.

Module 4 Time and environment 25 min
  • Extra time keeps the standard the same.
  • Adjust seating, noise and room.
  • Record the reason for each change.
Worked example. Learner A gets 15 minutes instead of 10 (5 extra) and a seat near the board. Standard: 4 of 5 stays the same for all.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "time and environment": evaluate the adapted lesson, given a checklist, with two changes for next time.

Standard to meet. Adjust time and the room for a learner who needs it, such as extra time or seating, while keeping the standard unchanged.

Module 5 Practice: adapt your worksheet 40 min
  • Turn a ten-item worksheet into three levels.
  • Write a five-line note on the changes.
  • Link each change to a learner need.
Worked example. 10 items become Level 1: 6 items with pictures, Level 2: 8 items with prompts, Level 3: 10 items. Note: 5 lines, 3 levels, 3 needs.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "adapting a lesson for mixed ability" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (identify three learner needs; modify a task; group learners; evaluate the adapted lesson).

Standard to meet. Adapt a ten-item worksheet into three levels and write a five-line note explaining each change and the learner need behind it.

Module 6 Assessment 20 min
  • Score the lesson against a checklist.
  • Give two changes for next time.
  • Give a reason for each grouping.
Worked example. Checklist of 8 items: 6 ticked. Two changes: shorten Level 3 instructions, add a word bank to Level 2. Groups of 4 chosen from last check results.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "assessment": evaluate the adapted lesson, given a checklist, with two changes for next time. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate your adapted lesson against a checklist, giving two changes for next time and one reason for each grouping.

Module 7 Second worked case: the reading task 10 min
  • Use the second case card.
  • Create three versions.
  • Keep one outcome.
Worked example. Reading task, 3 versions: Level 1 with a 10-word bank and pictures, Level 2 with a 5-word bank, Level 3 with an inference question. One outcome, 3 versions.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second worked case: the reading task": evaluate the adapted lesson, given a checklist, with two changes for next time. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Create three versions of the reading task that all lead to one outcome and justify the scaffold chosen for each.

Module 8 Teach-back and portfolio 10 min
  • Explain your changes to a partner.
  • Keep your adapted worksheet and note.
  • Plan one next step.
Worked example. Teach-back: 2 minutes to explain 3 changes. Partner repeats 3 of 3 back. Portfolio: 1 worksheet, 1 five-line note, 1 checklist.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "teach-back and portfolio": evaluate the adapted lesson, given a checklist, with two changes for next time. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Explain your adaptations to a peer, check the peer can repeat each reason, and file your worksheet and note in a portfolio.

practice

Worked example and case cards

Worked example

same outcome "Compare two fractions". Level 1: pictures and halves/quarters; Level 2: common denominators with prompts; Level 3: unlike denominators and explain why. Time extension for a learner who needs more time; standard unchanged.

Case card

a worksheet with 10 items; learners create three levels and a 5-line note explaining the changes.

Case card 2

A reading task; one learner is new to the language, another reads fluently. Create three versions.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Does adapting lower the standard?

Model answer: No; the outcome stays the same, the route changes. (Covered in module 1: Same outcome, different routes. To pass that module: Show that the outcome stays the same while the route changes, by writing three levels of one task that all lead to that outcome.)

Name two adaptations.

Model answer: More time, a simpler first step, pictures, a word bank. (Covered in module 2: Scaffolds: prompts, word banks, pictures. To pass that module: Identify learner needs from a class profile and choose a scaffold for each, such as prompts, a word bank or pictures.)

How do you decide who gets which?

Model answer: From recent check results and learner needs. (Covered in module 1: Same outcome, different routes. To pass that module: Show that the outcome stays the same while the route changes, by writing three levels of one task that all lead to that outcome.)

How do you record it?

Model answer: A short note linking the change to the learner need. (Covered in: background fact, not taught in one module.)

What is scaffolding?

Model answer: Temporary support that is removed as skill grows. (Covered in module 2: Scaffolds: prompts, word banks, pictures. To pass that module: Identify learner needs from a class profile and choose a scaffold for each, such as prompts, a word bank or pictures.)

Name two ways to extend.

Model answer: Open question, ask them to teach or create a problem. (Covered in module 3: Extension for fast learners. To pass that module: Modify a task with an extension for fast learners, such as an open question or asking them to create a problem, without raising the standard for others.)

Does extra time change the standard?

Model answer: No. (Covered in module 4: Time and environment. To pass that module: Adjust time and the room for a learner who needs it, such as extra time or seating, while keeping the standard unchanged.)

How do you record the plan?

Model answer: A short note linked to the learner need. (Covered in: background fact, not taught in one module.)

Who agrees adaptations?

Model answer: Teacher with the support lead, per policy. (Covered in: background fact, not taught in one module.)

How do you decide which learner gets which version of a task?

Model answer: Use recent check results and the learner's needs, offer choice where you can, and review the grouping after the next check. (Covered in module 1: Same outcome, different routes. To pass that module: Show that the outcome stays the same while the route changes, by writing three levels of one task that all lead to that outcome.)

marking

How the practice and assessment are marked

LevelMeaningMarks of 4
SecureMeets the standard in the outcome every time, no prompts4
MeetingMeets the standard with one small slip, corrected when prompted3
DevelopingMeets part of the standard; needs a re-attempt2
Not yetStandard not met1

Pass = average 3 or more, and no outcome at 1. You may re-attempt after feedback. The subject expert sets any stricter pass mark.

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: Learners differ. Speaker note: Learners in one class know different amounts and need different help. The learner can say why one task does not suit everyone and that adapting is a normal part of teaching.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Same outcome, different route. Speaker note: The learner understands that adapting changes the route, not the standard. They can name two supports, such as a word bank and extra time. They see that a word bank or extra time helps one learner reach the same 4 of 5 pass line as everyone else.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Simple supports. Speaker note: The learner can add a prompt card, pictures or a word bank to a ready-made task, with help from a checklist, and note why it was added.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Adapt and evaluate. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner identifies learner needs, writes three levels of one task for the same outcome, adds extensions and scaffolds, and evaluates the lesson against a checklist.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Complex needs. Speaker note: The specialist plans for several needs at once, such as a new language learner, a fluent reader and a learner who needs more time, and agrees plans with the support lead.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Coach and share. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner shares adapted resources, coaches colleagues on scaffolds that fade, and checks that adaptations still lead to the same standard. They also keep a shared bank of word lists, picture cards and extension tasks so colleagues save planning time.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Lead inclusive teaching. Speaker note: The super expert builds a department approach to adapting lessons, trains teachers, audits support notes and measures whether all learners reach the outcome. They also check results by group over time to be sure support is removed as learners grow in skill.

assessment and certificate

How you are checked
