EU-08 · flagship course

Industrial Activity: Machinery, High-Tech and Process

Map one real or simulated process, find three waste points and propose fixes with numbers and a safety check.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 142 hLearning Course, 3-6 months
  • Understand Explain the process, its steps and the hazards at each step.
  • Analyse Break the process into times, waits and waste points.
  • Apply Measure the process on site or on a model and map it with data.
  • Evaluate Judge each fix by its saving, cost and safety effect.
  • Create Produce a fix pack with a payback figure and a 90-day plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Map one real or simulated process, find three waste points and propose fixes with numbers and a safety check.

Condition

A real or simulated process, a mentor who knows the site, free tools, currency written on every number. This course teaches process and safety awareness only; the site's safety rules and the local authority come first.

Standard

A mentor reviews the pack; three waste points are shown with measured data; every fix has a safety check; at least 70% on the check; two peer reviews given.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Who needs this and how a factory runs 6 hUnderstand

A factory turns inputs into outputs through steps. Each step has people, machines, time and quality checks.

  • Identify a process of 6 to 10 steps.
  • Inputs, steps, outputs, scrap, rework, waiting.
  • A manager cares about cost, time, quality, safety.
  • Use a public description or a simulation if you cannot visit.
Worked example. Packing line: cut → assemble → test → pack. 40 units an hour at best; 31 on average.

Activity. Interview three people and write the problems they complain about.

Standard to meet. Conduct three interviews and choose one process.

Module 2 Industrial safety first 6 hAnalyse

Safety comes before output. Stop the machine if a guard is missing.

  • Guards on moving parts; lock-out before repair; trained operators only.
  • Noise, dust, heat, lifting, walkways.
  • You do not operate machinery in this course; you observe and analyse.
  • Follow the site rules and wear required protection when visiting.
Worked example. Hazard list for a cutting step: blade, noise, dust, manual lifting. Controls: fixed guard, ear protection, extraction, lifting aid.

Activity. Write a hazard and control table for each step.

Standard to meet. Give every step hazards and controls.

Module 3 Map the process 35 hApply

Write each step, who does it, minutes per unit, waits and scrap.

  • Use a simple flow chart.
  • Time each step at least 10 times and take the average.
  • Waiting between steps is often the biggest waste.
  • Count scrap at each step.
Worked example. Step times (min): cut 2.0, assemble 3.5, test 1.5, pack 1.0; waiting before assemble 4.0; scrap 6% at assemble.

Activity. Map your process with measured times.

Standard to meet. Map has times, waits and scrap for every step.

Module 4 Quality tools 10 hApply

Count problems, rank them, find the cause.

  • Check sheet: tally defects by type.
  • Pareto chart: bar chart ordered by size; 80% of trouble often comes from 20% of causes.
  • Five whys: ask why five times to reach a root cause.
  • Measure before and after any change.
Worked example. Defects in a week: wrong size 42, scratches 18, missing part 9, other 6. Wrong size is 56% of defects. Five whys: wrong size ← gauge worn ← no check schedule ← no owner.

Activity. Collect defect data for 50 units; draw the chart; do five whys on the top cause.

Standard to meet. Chart correct; root cause found.

Module 5 Find the three waste points 35 hAnalyse

Waste types: waiting, rework, motion, over-production, scrap, stoppages, extra steps.

  • Rank by cost.
  • Use measured data not opinion.
  • Write a problem statement with numbers.
  • Check that the waste is real by observing again.
Worked example. Waste: waiting 4.0 min per unit (33% of the 12.0 min total), scrap 6% at assemble, stoppages 35 min a day.

Activity. Write three problem statements with numbers.

Standard to meet. Give each a measurement and a cost.

Module 6 Maintenance basics 14 hEvaluate

Small regular jobs prevent large failures.

  • Preventive maintenance: cleaning, lubricating, checking, replacing parts on a schedule.
  • Record every job and every breakdown.
  • Look for repeating causes.
  • Lock-out and permission before any repair; only trained staff repair.
Worked example. Gauge check each shift cuts wrong-size defects from 42 to 12 in a week.

Activity. Write a one-page maintenance check list for a machine.

Standard to meet. List is safe, specific and has owners.

Module 7 Propose fixes with numbers 28 hCreate

Fixes need costs, benefits and safety checks.

  • Fix, cost, time to do, benefit per month, payback = cost ÷ benefit.
  • Test small first.
  • Consider safety effects of the change.
  • Get agreement from the process owner.
Worked example. Fix: move parts closer, cost EUR 150; saves 0.8 min per unit; at 1,000 units a month = 800 minutes ≈ 13.3 hours; at labour cost EUR 6 per hour = EUR 80 a month; payback 1.9 months.

Activity. Write three fix proposals with payback.

Standard to meet. Give each cost, benefit, payback and a safety note.

Module 8 Review room and next step 8 hCreate

Present and check.

  • Show map, waste, fixes, safety.
  • Peer feedback.
  • Take the check.
  • Next: Construction or Trading.
Worked example. Score sheet, each line marked 0 to 5 (3 or more meets the standard): process map 5, waste analysis 4, safety 5, fixes 3, evidence 4. Total 21 of 25 = 84%, above the 70% line. Fixes at 3 is the weakest line, so it gets a costed payback figure next.

Activity. Present for 8 minutes, then answer: which waste was largest in minutes, which fix you would do first, and what could go wrong with it.

Standard to meet. Score all rubric lines at 3 or more and give the weakest line a dated fix.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name four wastes.

Model answer: Waiting, rework, motion, over-production, scrap or stoppages. (Covered in module 5: Find the three waste points. To pass that module: Give each a measurement and a cost.)

Q2. What is a Pareto chart?

Model answer: A bar chart ordered by size to show the biggest causes. (Covered in module 4: Quality tools. To pass that module: Chart correct; root cause found.)

Q3. What are the five whys?

Model answer: Asking why five times to reach a root cause. (Covered in module 4: Quality tools. To pass that module: Chart correct; root cause found.)

Q4. Payback if cost is EUR 150 and benefit EUR 80 a month?

Model answer: About 1.9 months. (Covered in module 7: Propose fixes with numbers. To pass that module: Give each cost, benefit, payback and a safety note.)

Q5. What must happen before repairing a machine?

Model answer: Lock-out and permission; only trained staff repair. (Covered in module 6: Maintenance basics. To pass that module: List is safe, specific and has owners.)

Q6. Why time each step many times?

Model answer: To get a reliable average. (Covered in module 3: Map the process. To pass that module: Map has times, waits and scrap for every step.)

Q7. What do you do if a guard is missing?

Model answer: Stop the machine and report it. (Covered in module 2: Industrial safety first. To pass that module: Give every step hazards and controls.)

Q8. Name one preventive maintenance job.

Model answer: Clean, lubricate, check or replace a part on a schedule. (Covered in module 6: Maintenance basics. To pass that module: List is safe, specific and has owners.)

Q9. Why measure before and after?

Model answer: To show the fix worked. (Covered in module 4: Quality tools. To pass that module: Chart correct; root cause found.)

Q10. Do you operate machines in this course?

Model answer: No: you observe and analyse only. (Covered in module 2: Industrial safety first. To pass that module: Give every step hazards and controls.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Use local units, rules and authorities.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Business set-up: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Product and food rules: FSSAI.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Business set-up: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Product and food rules: NVWA.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Business set-up: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Product and food rules: the municipality food-control team (for example Dubai Municipality).

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Business set-up: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Product and food rules: ANVISA and MAPA.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Business set-up: federal or Ontario business registration. Personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Product and food rules: the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA).

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Business set-up: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Product and food rules: the FDA and the state health department.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Business set-up: registration with ACRA. Personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Product and food rules: the Singapore Food Agency (SFA).

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Business set-up: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Product and food rules: Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) and the state health authority.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Business set-up: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Product and food rules: the Centre for Food Safety.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Business set-up: local business registration in the country where you work. Personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Product and food rules: the national food-safety authority.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Business set-up: a trade and business licence or company registration. Personal data: the Data Protection Act. Product and food rules: the public health authority.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Business set-up: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Product and food rules: the federal health-risk authority.
