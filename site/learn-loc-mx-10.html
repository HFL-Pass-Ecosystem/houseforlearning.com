The global course "Fitness Tech" with local rules, jobs and examples for Mexico.

about this course

Fitness Tech: Mexico edition

L1 to L4 location edition8 modules

Fitness Tech from the global list, applied to Mexico: local rules, jobs and examples in MXN.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan a small Fitness Tech business for Mexico from fit check to launch plan.

Condition

About six weeks part-time; public sources, two local readers and a mentor.

Standard

All module standards met; every claim has a public source and date; mentor and peer mark 'Meets' or better.

Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real. Draft; not legal, tax, medical or financial advice. All amounts are estimates for planning, not quotes.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read your local market 4 hUnderstand

Every plan starts with the place. Read Mexico as your customer does before you choose a sector.

  • A large market with strong regional differences. Start in one city and one niche.
  • Many customers use bank transfers, cash-payment points and cards; a lot of buying is on messaging apps.
  • Electronic invoices are part of daily trade: learn them in week one.
  • Close to the northern trading partners: near-shoring creates demand for services, testing and training.
Worked example. Market page for Mexico: size in one line, three languages or fewer, main payment methods, three public holidays that affect delivery, and one risk. Each line has a source you can open and a date.

Activity. Write a one-page market page with sources and dates. Ask two local people to correct it.

Standard to meet. Every line has a source and a date; two local readers confirm the facts; one risk is named with a response.

Module 2 Check the fit of this sector 4 hAnalyse

Fitness Tech sits in the cluster "Health, care and wellbeing (regulated)". Judge its fit for Mexico before spending money.

  • Local angle: Regulated. Practise only with the right licence and registration. This course teaches planning, not practice.
  • Score demand, skills you hold, start-up cost, rules and partner access from 1 to 5.
  • A rules score below 3 makes this a later project.
  • Regulated: planning only. Partner with a licensed firm.
Worked example. Fit table for Fitness Tech in Mexico: list three local customer groups, one local competitor type (no names), one rule that could stop you and one partner type. Score each criterion with a reason.

Activity. Write a fit table for Fitness Tech with scores and reasons. Ask one local reader to challenge two scores.

Standard to meet. Five criteria scored with reasons; one rule risk named; regulated status stated.

Module 3 Register and license 4 hApply

Rules differ by place. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real.

  • Register with the tax authority (a tax ID) before you invoice; choose sole trader or company.
  • A simplified small-taxpayer regime may fit a first year; read the current limits.
  • Issue electronic invoices as the tax rules require; learn the tool before the first customer.
  • Some activities need extra licences (food, health, tourism, money); list yours before the first order.
Worked example. Licence list for a small Fitness Tech business: registration type, tax registration, address, any activity licence, insurance. Next to each, write who issues it, what it costs in MXN (from their public page), how long it takes, and the date you checked.

Activity. Build a licence table for your chosen sector with issuer, cost, time and the date checked. Do not apply; this is a plan.

Standard to meet. Table is complete; every row has a public source; the order of steps is logical; one adviser question is written.

Module 4 Protect data and customers 3 hApply

Trust is the real product. Protect customer data and keep your promises in writing.

  • The private-sector data law needs a privacy notice (check the current text): who you are, what you collect, why and how to correct it.
  • Customers may ask to see, correct, remove or object to use of their data; set a reply time.
  • Keep marketing consent records; offer a clear way to stop messages.
  • Keep a list of every tool that stores customer data, including messaging apps.
Worked example. Privacy notice in five lines: who we are; what we collect; why; who sees it; how to ask for a copy, correction or removal. Plus a data map listing each tool and where it stores data.

Activity. Write the five-line notice and a data map for your plan. Ask a peer to find any gap.

Standard to meet. Notice is plain words; data map lists every tool; refund and complaint steps are written and dated.

Module 5 Find and serve customers 4 hCreate

Go where your customers already are, in the language they use. Language: Spanish first; add English for tourism and trade customers.

  • Channels: messaging apps, social media and marketplaces; local fairs and markets for a first test.
  • Language: Spanish first, in plain words; add English only if customers ask.
  • Payments: bank transfer, cards and cash-payment points; show prices with the sales tax clearly.
  • Trust: tax ID, address, delivery days and a refund note on every page; consumer rules apply.
Worked example. Test plan: show the offer to 20 real people through two channels over two weeks; count enquiries, replies and first orders. Example result: 20 shown, 6 enquiries, 2 orders: a 10% order rate. Change one thing (price or wording) and test again.

Activity. Test two channels with 20 people. Record results in a table and write the next change.

Standard to meet. Test has a start and end date; results are counted, not guessed; one change is chosen and justified.

Module 6 Price, tax and money 4 hEvaluate

Know your numbers in MXN. Tax rule in one line: Income tax plus a sales tax (IVA); a simple regime may fit small firms, and most sales need an electronic invoice.

  • Write MXN next to every number.
  • Contribution per unit = price minus the cost of each unit.
  • Break-even units = fixed monthly cost divided by contribution per unit; round up.
  • Add tax and duty explicitly; never bury them in a price.
  • Check against a worse case: if sales are 25% lower, do you still cover costs?
Worked example. For a small Fitness Tech business (all amounts are estimates): price MXN 450 per unit, cost MXN 270 per unit, fixed cost MXN 37,800 per month. Contribution = 450 - 270 = 180. Break-even = 37,800 / 180 = 210.0, so 210 boxes per month. If sales fall 25% from 420, you sell 315 and the margin is 18,900.

Activity. Build your own sheet in MXN: price, unit cost, fixed cost, contribution, break-even and a 25% worse case. Put the tax and duty rules in a separate line with a source.

Standard to meet. Sheet has all six lines in MXN; arithmetic is checked by a peer; the worse case is shown.

Module 7 People and partners 3 hApply

Plans become real through people. Hire, partner and write every promise down.

  • Employers register staff for social security and pay the year-end bonus; budget both.
  • Staff may share in profits; small and new firms can differ, so read the rule before you hire.
  • Written contracts and signed copies for every helper, even part-time.
  • Partner with a technical college or business chamber to find helpers and to practise.
Worked example. Role card for a first helper: tasks, hours, pay in MXN, trial period, review date, and one measure of success. Signed by both sides.

Activity. Write one role card and one partner letter (what each side gives). Role-play a first meeting for 10 minutes.

Standard to meet. Role card has pay in local currency, hours and a review date; partner letter is balanced; both are signed copies.

Module 8 Launch plan and review 4 hCreate

Finish with a one-page launch plan and a plan to review it every month.

  • Dates: first test, first order, first review.
  • One measure per week: enquiries, orders or revenue; pick one.
  • A monthly review: what we planned, what happened, what we change.
  • Keep a risk list with a response for each; include a rules change.
  • Local caution: A large country: start in one city, test one offer, then grow.
Worked example. Launch page for Mexico: Week 1 licence table done; Week 2 test with 20 people; Week 3 numbers sheet checked; Week 4 launch decision with a go or no-go rule written in advance.

Activity. Write the launch page and present it to two people for 10 minutes. Record their questions and your answers.

Standard to meet. Plan has dates, one weekly measure, a go or no-go rule and a risk list; presenter answers three questions with evidence.

jobs

Where this takes you

  • Fitness Tech assistant. supports a small Fitness Tech business in Mexico.
  • Online seller. runs product pages, orders, invoices and delivery.
  • Digital marketing assistant. plans posts and reports results in Spanish.
  • IT and testing technician. supports near-shoring firms with testing and support.
  • Solar and water assistant. prepares surveys and quotes.
  • Food business assistant. plans menus, safety logs and orders.

Six steps from entry to senior

  1. Fitness Tech assistant (L2, Entry). helps with set tasks, keeps records and follows the checklist.
  2. Junior Fitness Tech coordinator (L3, Junior). runs small jobs end to end and reports each week.
  3. Fitness Tech practitioner (L4, Practitioner). plans and delivers the work alone, with a mentor check.
  4. Fitness Tech specialist (L5, Specialist). goes deep in one area, such as rules, numbers or customers.
  5. Fitness Tech team lead or manager (L6, Advanced). leads people and partners, owns the plan and the data.
  6. Senior Fitness Tech adviser or mentor (L7, Senior). designs the whole system, reviews others and publishes cases.

See how these steps connect to study and work in Career pathways and Pathways.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Name fitness tech parts

Name 6 parts of a fitness product (sensor, app, plan, coach, data, reward) and say what each does.

Speaker notes. Show a wearable and an app screen and ask for labels. Check that data is named as a part.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Health data and safe advice

Describe 5 safety limits for exercise advice and say how health data must be protected.

Speaker notes. Ask what the app does if a user reports chest pain. Check local rules with an expert for health data.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Scope a 20-minute plan app

Plan an app scope for busy parents with 3 workouts, a 20-minute limit and a weekly goal of 3 sessions.

Speaker notes. Review a one-page scope. Check that anything beyond three workouts is cut.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Run a user test

Run a test with 10 users for 2 weeks and record sessions completed and drop-offs.

Speaker notes. Hand over a log. Check the completion rate is worked out as a percentage.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Design habit and reward loop

Design a loop with a streak, a reminder and a reward and set a target of 40 percent weekly return.

Speaker notes. Ask what would stop the loop working. Check it avoids unsafe pushing.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Analyse retention and pricing

Analyse 30-day retention and a price test between two plans in MXN, and choose one with numbers.

Speaker notes. Provide two tables. Check the choice uses revenue per 100 users.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Lead a product roadmap

Lead a 6-month roadmap with 3 releases, a safety review, a data audit and a coach partner agreement.

Speaker notes. A peer plays a coach asking for data access. Check the answer sets limits.
