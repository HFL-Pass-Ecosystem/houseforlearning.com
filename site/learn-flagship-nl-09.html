Construction Industry Skills

Plan a small, safe building task on paper and on site: measure, estimate, schedule, check quality and record.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 150 h guidedPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Plan a small, safe building task on paper and on site: measure, estimate, schedule, check quality and record.

  • Understand Explain site roles and safety duties.
  • Apply Measure and set out a small task.
  • Analyse Analyse cost and delay.
  • Create Create a schedule and quality plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and complete one small supervised construction task with a safety plan, material list, schedule and quality record.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3 to 6 months. Supervised site or workshop, tape and level, free spreadsheet, qualified supervisor, mentor and peer circle.

Standard

Safety plan signed; measurements correct; material list and cost; schedule; quality record; at least 70% on the check.

Learning only. Work only under a qualified supervisor with the required site safety training and protection.

Requirements: none. Who it is for: learners 16 and over, workers changing field, small-business starters, teams. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (150 h guided)

Module 1 Site roles and safety 12 hUnderstand

A building site works safely only when everyone knows who decides what. Safety duties apply to every person, from the client to the newest helper. In this module you learn the roles and the habits that prevent accidents.

  • Name the main roles: client, designer, main contractor, supervisor and each trade, and write what each one decides.
  • Hold a short safety talk of about 10 minutes at the start of each day to cover the task, hazards and emergency points.
  • Check with your local authority which permits, training and protection are required for your task, for example for work at height or in a trench.
  • Report hazards at once, and know that anyone on site, and always the supervisor, can stop unsafe work.
  • Wear the required protection, usually a helmet, boots, gloves and eye protection, and keep the site tidy.
Worked example. A trench needs support before anyone goes in, and the supervisor stops work until it is in place. The delay is 2 hours, which costs 2 hours x EUR 15 x 3 workers = EUR 90 in wages but avoids the risk of a collapse.

Activity. Write a one-page roles and safety sheet with each role, daily safety talk topics, protection needed and emergency points.

Standard to meet. All roles are named with decisions, hazards have controls, and a supervisor has checked the sheet.

Module 2 Read plans 14 hUnderstand

Plans show where things go and how big they are. You must read scale, symbols and dimensions before you measure or cut anything. In this module you practise reading a simple plan and converting scale to real size.

  • Find the scale in the title block, for example 1:50, meaning 1 unit on paper equals 50 units on site.
  • To find real length, multiply the measured length on paper by the scale number, then convert units.
  • Learn the common symbols for walls, doors, windows and levels, and check the legend on the drawing.
  • Compare the plan with the section and detail drawings so that heights and depths match.
  • Check each dimension twice and write down any that do not add up, then ask the designer.
Worked example. At 1:50, a line that measures 40 mm on paper is 40 x 50 = 2000 mm, which is 2 m. A wall of 6.5 m would be 6500 / 50 = 130 mm long on the drawing.

Activity. Read a simple plan and list ten dimensions with their real size, scale used and any mismatch you found.

Standard to meet. All ten real sizes are correct, units are shown and any mismatch is recorded.

Module 3 Measure and set out 20 hApply

Accurate setting out saves rework and money. You mark lines on the ground with a tape and check them for square and level. Small errors at the start grow into big errors later.

  • Use a steel tape pulled straight and read it from the same end each time, and write the number straight away.
  • Check a rectangle is square by measuring both diagonals, which are equal if the corners are right angles.
  • Use the 3-4-5 rule for a quick right angle: sides of 3 m and 4 m give a diagonal of 5 m.
  • Check levels with a spirit level or water level and mark the level on pegs.
  • Record every reading, and re-measure if two readings differ by more than 5 mm.
Worked example. For a slab of 4 m by 3 m, both diagonals should measure 5 m, because 3 squared plus 4 squared is 25 and the square root is 5. If one diagonal reads 5.02 m and the other 4.98 m, the difference is 4 cm, which means the frame is not square and needs adjusting.

Activity. Set out a small rectangle, check the diagonals and level, and record readings in a table.

Standard to meet. The sizes are within 5 mm, diagonals agree and every reading is recorded.

Module 4 Quantities and cost 20 hEvaluate

Counting before buying prevents shortages and waste. You work out volumes and areas, add waste and price each item. A clear bill helps the client understand the cost.

  • Volume equals length times width times depth, all in metres, and the result is in cubic metres.
  • Area equals length times width in square metres, used for items such as boards or paint.
  • Add 5 to 10 percent for waste depending on the material, and round up to the unit you buy.
  • Multiply quantity by price per unit and add the lines to get a total, with the currency on every line.
  • Ask a supplier for a written price and note how long it is valid.
Worked example. A slab of 4 x 3 x 0.1 m has a volume of 1.2 m3. Adding 7% waste gives 1.2 x 1.07 = 1.284 m3, rounded up to 1.3 m3 to order, and at EUR 110 per m3 the cost is 1.3 x 110 = EUR 143.00.

Activity. Make a bill of quantities for your task with units, waste, price and total cost.

Standard to meet. Volumes and areas are correct, waste is shown, currency is on every line and the total adds up.

Module 5 Schedule 16 hCreate

A schedule puts the tasks in order and shows how long each one takes. Some tasks must wait for others, such as concrete that needs time to cure. A buffer protects the plan from weather and late deliveries.

  • List every task with its length in days, such as dig, formwork, reinforcement, pour and cure.
  • Mark which tasks depend on others, and which can run at the same time.
  • Include waiting periods, for example concrete usually needs several days before it can carry load, and follow the supplier's instructions.
  • Add a buffer of about 15 to 20 percent for weather and delivery delays.
  • Draw a simple bar chart with a day number on each bar and review it with your supervisor.
Worked example. Dig 1 day, formwork 1, reinforcement 1, pour 1 and cure 7 days make 11 days. A 20% buffer is 11 x 0.20 = 2.2, rounded to 2 days, so the plan runs for 13 days.

Activity. Make a bar chart schedule with task durations, dependencies and a buffer.

Standard to meet. The order is logical, waiting periods are shown and the total includes a buffer.

Module 6 Do the task 40 hApply

Doing the task safely and recording each stage proves the quality of your work. Photos and a daily log protect you and the client. Problems fixed at once cost less than problems found later.

  • Hold the daily safety talk, check protection and tools, and walk the area for hazards before work starts.
  • Follow the plan and the schedule, and write in the log what was done, by whom and how long it took.
  • Take photos of each stage, especially work that will be covered such as reinforcement and pipes.
  • Measure and check each stage against the plan before you move to the next.
  • Fix errors straight away and record what was wrong and what you did.
Worked example. Before the pour you take 4 photos of the reinforcement with a tape in view, and you measure the cover as 40 mm against a plan value of 40 mm. The photos prove the steel position after it is covered by concrete.

Activity. Complete the task under supervision with a daily log, stage photos and measured checks.

Standard to meet. The log has no missing days, each hidden stage has photos, and checks are written with measured numbers.

Module 7 Quality and defects 16 hAnalyse

Quality checks find faults early, when they are cheap to fix. You use a checklist for each stage and measure against tolerance. A snag list collects faults to fix before handover.

  • Write a short checklist for each stage, with the item, the target and the tolerance.
  • Measure with the right tool, for example a straightedge and ruler for surface level over 3 m, and record the number.
  • Compare each reading to the tolerance and mark pass or fail.
  • List each fault on a snag list with its place, size, cause and who will fix it.
  • Re-check each fix and close the item only when it passes.
Worked example. The tolerance for surface flatness is 5 mm under a 3 m straightedge, and you measure a gap of 8 mm. That fails by 3 mm, so you grind the high spot and re-test, and the second reading is 3 mm, which passes.

Activity. Run a quality check on your task with a checklist, measurements and a snag list with closed items.

Standard to meet. Each item shows target, reading and pass or fail, and all snags are fixed and re-tested.

Module 8 Handover and review 12 hEvaluate

A clean handover with full records builds trust. You give the client the documents, photos and care notes. A short review captures lessons for the next task.

  • Clear and clean the site, remove waste properly and make sure no hazards are left.
  • Prepare a handover pack with drawings, photos, material records, test results and care instructions.
  • Walk through the work with the client or supervisor and show the snag list as closed.
  • Compare planned and actual time and cost, and write the difference in days and currency.
  • Write three lessons and one change you will make next time.
Worked example. Planned time was 13 days and actual time was 14 days, so you were 1 day late, which is 1 / 13 = 8% over. Planned cost was EUR 143 for concrete and actual EUR 150, so the difference is EUR 7.

Activity. Prepare a handover pack and present a review that compares plan and actual with three lessons.

Standard to meet. The pack contains drawings, photos, test results and care notes, and the review shows differences in days and currency.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: your records, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Who can stop unsafe work on a site?

Model answer: Anyone on site can raise a stop, and the supervisor has the duty to stop work until it is safe. (Covered in module 1: Site roles and safety. To pass that module: All roles are named with decisions, hazards have controls, and a supervisor has checked the sheet.)

Q2. At a scale of 1:50, how long is 40 mm on paper?

Model answer: 40 x 50 = 2000 mm, which is 2 m. (Covered in module 2: Read plans. To pass that module: All ten real sizes are correct, units are shown and any mismatch is recorded.)

Q3. How do equal diagonals show a rectangle is square?

Model answer: In a rectangle with right-angle corners both diagonals have the same length, so unequal diagonals show the frame is out of square. (Covered in module 3: Measure and set out. To pass that module: The sizes are within 5 mm, diagonals agree and every reading is recorded.)

Q4. What is the volume of a slab 4 m by 3 m by 0.1 m?

Model answer: 4 x 3 x 0.1 = 1.2 cubic metres, before any waste allowance. (Covered in module 4: Quantities and cost. To pass that module: Volumes and areas are correct, waste is shown, currency is on every line and the total adds up.)

Q5. Why add a buffer to the schedule?

Model answer: Weather, late deliveries and rework often cause delays, and a buffer keeps the end date realistic. (Covered in module 5: Schedule. To pass that module: The order is logical, waiting periods are shown and the total includes a buffer.)

Q6. Why photograph each stage of the work?

Model answer: Photos prove what is hidden later and help settle questions about quality. (Covered in module 6: Do the task. To pass that module: The log has no missing days, each hidden stage has photos, and checks are written with measured numbers.)

Q7. What is a snag list?

Model answer: It is a list of faults that must be fixed before handover, with place, cause and who fixes each one. (Covered in module 7: Quality and defects. To pass that module: Each item shows target, reading and pass or fail, and all snags are fixed and re-tested.)

Q8. What goes in a handover pack?

Model answer: Drawings, photos of each stage, material and test records, and care instructions. (Covered in module 8: Handover and review. To pass that module: The pack contains drawings, photos, test results and care notes, and the review shows differences in days and currency.)

Q9. What is the task of this course, in one sentence?

Model answer: Plan and complete one small supervised construction task with a safety plan, material list, schedule and quality record. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. What standard must your final evidence meet?

Model answer: Safety plan signed; measurements correct; material list and cost; schedule; quality record; at least 70% on the check. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic, First words, tools and safety. Speaker note: Learner can say what Construction Industry Skills is about and name the safety rule. Use module 1, Site roles and safety, as the first short unit (about 2 to 6 hours). Say this rule aloud: Learning only. Work only under a qualified supervisor with the required site safety training and protection. No record is needed at this level.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness, What the field is and who it serves. Speaker note: Learner explains the idea in plain words: Explain site roles and safety duties. Shared with schools and community groups. Note for the guide: ask one learner to explain it to a neighbour and check the words are right.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation, Guided tasks with a checklist. Speaker note: Learner does guided tasks from the first three modules (Site roles and safety; Read plans; Measure and set out) with a checklist. Evidence is a small set of dated records. Note for the guide: sit beside the learner for the first record, then step back.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner, Run the full task alone (this course). Speaker note: Task: Plan and complete one small supervised construction task with a safety plan, material list, schedule and quality record. Standard: Safety plan signed; measurements correct; material list and cost; schedule; quality record; at least 70% on the check. Note for the guide: a mentor reviews the records; two peers mark with the rubric above.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist, One narrow area to depth. Speaker note: Learner picks one area, for example Do the task (the longest module at 40 h), and goes deeper than this course asks. Learner then teaches a peer circle of three or four. Note for the guide: check the teaching, not only the work.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced, Lead a team and review evidence. Speaker note: Learner leads a small team or designs a service in this field and marks other people's evidence against the rubric. Roles this points to: Site assistant; Estimator assistant; Trade apprentice. Note for the guide: sample three records from the team and compare marks.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert, Set and audit the standard. Speaker note: Learner writes the method for Construction Industry Skills as a short guide, audits other learners' evidence, and publishes worked examples back to learners. Note for the guide: worked examples keep currency and units on every number and are checked by a second expert.

Notes: credits stack and you can step in and out. This course is L4. Aligned with, not accredited by, national credit frameworks.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local custom changes rules, language, currency and who you practise with. Check each rule with your local authority before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
