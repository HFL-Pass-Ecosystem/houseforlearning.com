Managing a file to the audit standard

Keeping files that stand up to an audit.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 3 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playLegal

Keeping files that stand up to an audit.

  • Organise a file, given a sample, with index, dates and version.
  • Record decisions, given a scenario, with who, what, when and why.
  • Close a file, given a checklist, with every item complete.
  • Retain records, given a policy, for the stated period and then dispose safely.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Organise a case file in the standard order, record each decision as it is made, complete the closing checklist, and apply the retention period and safe disposal.

Condition

Given a messy sample file, a file index template, a closing checklist and a records policy, working in a three-hour supervised session with no legal advice given.

Standard

Produce a file in the standard order, every note dated with who, what, when and why, a fully completed closing checklist, and the correct retention date stated.

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Standard file order 20 min
  • Index, instructions, correspondence, notes, documents, billing, checklist
  • The order lets an auditor find items quickly
  • Add an index and a version
Worked example. Messy file of 25 papers sorted into 7 sections: index 1, instructions 2, correspondence 9, dated notes 5, documents 4, billing 3, closing checklist 1. Check: 1 + 2 + 9 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 1 = 25, so none is lost.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "standard file order": organise a file, given a sample, with index, dates and version.

Standard to meet. Arrange the papers in a messy sample file into the standard order: index, instructions, correspondence, dated notes, documents, billing and closing checklist.

Module 2 Recording time and decisions 25 min
  • Record who, what, when and why
  • Date every note at the time
  • Keep a time record
Worked example. Note: "12 Mar 10:15. Client agreed option B. Who: the client and a colleague. Why: lower risk. Recorded at the time." Time record: three calls of 10, 15 and 20 minutes = 45 minutes, entered the same day.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "recording time and decisions": record decisions, given a scenario, with who, what, when and why.

Standard to meet. Record each decision in a scenario at the time it is made, with who, what, when and why, and date every note.

Module 3 Closing checklist 20 min
  • Outcome, documents returned, retention date
  • Every item complete before closing
  • Sign and date the checklist
Worked example. Closing checklist: outcome "matter settled 30 Apr 2026"; documents returned 4 of 4; retention date set. Example policy of 6 years (your own policy and regulator set the real period), so retention ends 30 Apr 2032 (2026 + 6). All 3 items complete, then it is signed.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "closing checklist": close a file, given a checklist, with every item complete.

Standard to meet. Complete the closing of a file by working through every item on the closing checklist: the outcome, the documents returned and the retention date.

Module 4 Retention and privacy 15 min
  • Only authorised people may see a file
  • Retention follows your regulator and policy
  • Dispose of records safely and log it
Worked example. File access list: 3 authorised people (lead, assigned worker, auditor) of 7 staff, so 4 may not open it. At 30 Apr 2032 the file is disposed of safely: paper shredded, electronic copies deleted, and the disposal logged.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "retention and privacy": retain records, given a policy, for the stated period and then dispose safely.

Standard to meet. State who may see a file and how long it is kept under a given policy, and name how to dispose of records safely afterwards.

Module 5 Practice 15 min
  • Reorder first, then fill the gaps
  • Never invent a date; note "date from email"
  • Complete the checklist
Worked example. Case card 2: loose notes, none dated, no time record. Learner dates 5 notes from the covering emails, adds the three calls (10 + 15 + 20 = 45 minutes), adds an index and marks the file "version 2".

Practise: Run one full practice round for "managing a file to the audit standard" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (organise a file; record decisions; close a file; retain records).

Standard to meet. Reorder a file with loose notes and no time record, fill the gaps with an index, dates and version, and complete its checklist.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Show standard order and dated decisions
  • Show a completed closing checklist
  • State the correct retention period
Worked example. Check result: 7 of 7 sections in order, 5 of 5 notes dated, 3 of 3 checklist items done, retention date 30 Apr 2032 correct against the example 6-year policy.

Practise: Take the end check for "managing a file to the audit standard": show that you can retain records, given a policy, for the stated period and then dispose safely, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Demonstrate the filing method by showing a sample file in standard order with dated decisions, a completed closing checklist and the correct retention period.

Module 7 Second case: closing with gaps 45 min
  • Find what is missing at closing
  • Fix the file before it is closed
  • Recalculate the retention date
Worked example. A file closed 15 Jan 2026 under an example 5-year policy keeps until 15 Jan 2031 (2026 + 5). Gaps found: no index, no version, 1 of 4 documents not returned. Learner fixes the 3 gaps, then signs the checklist.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second case: closing with gaps": retain records, given a policy, for the stated period and then dispose safely. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate a file at closing, find missing items, correct them and calculate the retention date from the closing date and policy.

Module 8 Audit trail test and reflection 35 min
  • Auditor asks for 3 items
  • Find each in under 2 minutes
  • Reflect on weak points
Worked example. An auditor asks for the instruction letter, the 12 Mar decision note and the billing summary. The learner finds all 3 in order in under 2 minutes each, 3 of 3. Reflection: one place the file order slowed a search and its fix.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "audit trail test and reflection": retain records, given a policy, for the stated period and then dispose safely. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Demonstrate that an auditor can trace three requested items in your file within two minutes, then reflect on one change to your filing habits.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Why date every note?

Model answer: Shows the order and timing of decisions. (Covered in module 2: Recording time and decisions. To pass that module: Record each decision in a scenario at the time it is made, with who, what, when and why, and date every note.)

What is on the closing checklist?

Model answer: Outcome, documents returned, retention date. (Covered in module 3: Closing checklist. To pass that module: Complete the closing of a file by working through every item on the closing checklist: the outcome, the documents returned and the retention date.)

Who may see it?

Model answer: Only authorised people. (Covered in module 4: Retention and privacy. To pass that module: State who may see a file and how long it is kept under a given policy, and name how to dispose of records safely afterwards.)

Retention?

Model answer: Per your regulator and policy. (Covered in module 4: Retention and privacy. To pass that module: State who may see a file and how long it is kept under a given policy, and name how to dispose of records safely afterwards.)

When record a decision?

Model answer: At the time it is made. (Covered in module 2: Recording time and decisions. To pass that module: Record each decision in a scenario at the time it is made, with who, what, when and why, and date every note.)

What closes a file?

Model answer: The closing checklist signed. (Covered in module 3: Closing checklist. To pass that module: Complete the closing of a file by working through every item on the closing checklist: the outcome, the documents returned and the retention date.)

Who may open a closed file?

Model answer: Authorised staff only. (Covered in module 4: Retention and privacy. To pass that module: State who may see a file and how long it is kept under a given policy, and name how to dispose of records safely afterwards.)

How long kept?

Model answer: Per local rule. (Covered in module 4: Retention and privacy. To pass that module: State who may see a file and how long it is kept under a given policy, and name how to dispose of records safely afterwards.)

Why order matters?

Model answer: Auditors find items quickly. (Covered in module 1: Standard file order. To pass that module: Arrange the papers in a messy sample file into the standard order: index, instructions, correspondence, dated notes, documents, billing and closing checklist.)

A file is closed on 30 Apr 2026 and the policy keeps files for 6 years. On what date does retention end?

Model answer: 30 Apr 2032. (Covered in module 3: Closing checklist. To pass that module: Complete the closing of a file by working through every item on the closing checklist: the outcome, the documents returned and the retention date.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What is a case file. Speaker note: A case file is the single place that shows what was asked, decided and done. Say it in one sentence. At this level the learner knows that a tidy file protects the client and the firm.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: The standard order. Speaker note: Show the seven sections: index, instructions, correspondence, notes, documents, billing, checklist. The learner at this level can name each part and say why dated notes matter when someone checks the file later.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Filing with a template. Speaker note: Learners sort a messy sample into order using a template and write dated notes with who, what, when and why. They are supervised and follow the closing checklist one tick at a time.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Keep and close to the audit standard. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner organises a file alone, records decisions as they are made, completes the closing checklist, applies the retention period, and disposes of records safely.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Fixing damaged files. Speaker note: The specialist rebuilds a file with loose notes and gaps, decides what can be filled from emails, and notes what cannot. They coach colleagues and judge when a gap must be reported to a lead.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: File standards and sampling. Speaker note: The advanced worker sets file standards, samples files for missing dates and checklists, and reports trends. Talk about why the same gap, such as no time record, repeats and how to fix its cause.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: A records system for the firm. Speaker note: The super expert designs the file and retention system across the whole firm: templates, access rules, retention schedules, disposal logs and audit evidence. They prove it works with measures such as time to find an item.

assessment and certificate

How you are checked

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own body's forms, units and currency. Local professional bodies set the rules for CPD hours; check them first.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Credit and levels: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Credit and levels: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Credit and levels: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Credit and levels: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Credit and levels: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Credit and levels: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Credit and levels: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Credit and levels: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Credit and levels: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Credit and levels: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Personal data: the Data Protection Act. Credit and levels: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Credit and levels: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
