flagship course

Tourism and Hospitality Operations

Plan and run tours, stays and food service that guests trust, with safety, local culture and fair pricing.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 36 h lessons plus practice

Plan and run tours, stays and food service that guests trust, with safety, local culture and fair pricing.

  • Define guest needs and local assets.
  • Apply service routines and safety checks.
  • Analyse bookings and costs.
  • Evaluate risk and reviews.
  • Create a tour or stay product.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design and run a small tour or stay product for a group, with a costed plan and guest feedback.

Condition

About 10 weeks part-time. A local site, a small guest group, a mentor.

Standard

The plan is safe, priced above cost, respectful of local people and supported by guest feedback.

Who it is for: Hotel and tourism students, guides, homestay owners. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Guests and local assets 4 hDefine
  • Types of guests and what they want.
  • Map heritage, nature, food and craft.
  • Respect local people and rules.
Worked example. A craft-village walk with a weaver and a meal at a home kitchen.

Activity. Map ten local assets.

Standard to meet. Each asset has a guest benefit and a local permission.

Module 2 Design the experience 5 hCreate
  • Story, route, time and rest points.
  • Group size and pace.
  • Accessible options.
Worked example. Half-day heritage walk with three stops and a tea break.

Activity. Design a tour.

Standard to meet. Timed plan with accessibility notes.

Module 3 Safety and health 5 hApply
  • Risk assessment.
  • First aid and emergency numbers.
  • Food hygiene and water.
Worked example. Heat plan: water every 30 minutes and shade stops.

Activity. Write a risk assessment.

Standard to meet. Risks have controls and owners.

Module 4 Service standards 5 hApply
  • Greet, ask, serve, check, thank.
  • Handle complaints calmly.
  • Language and manners.
Worked example. Greeting at the door with a name card.

Activity. Role-play service.

Standard to meet. Standards followed.

Module 5 Pricing and costs 5 hAnalyse
  • Direct and shared costs.
  • Margin and group size.
  • Seasonal prices.
Worked example. Cost Rs 900 per guest, price Rs 1300 before GST.

Activity. Cost a tour.

Standard to meet. Numbers correct.

Module 6 Booking and records 4 hApply
  • Booking sheet and confirmations.
  • Deposit and cancellation rules.
  • Guest data privacy under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023.
Worked example. Confirmation sent with meeting point.

Activity. Run bookings.

Standard to meet. Records complete.

Module 7 Sustainable and fair tourism 4 hEvaluate
  • Waste, water and crowding.
  • Fair pay for local partners.
  • Cultural respect.
Worked example. Reusable bottles and a local-supplier rule.

Activity. Audit a tour.

Standard to meet. Findings and actions listed.

Module 8 Capstone: run a tour or stay 4 hCreate
  • Run the product.
  • Collect feedback.
  • Report.
  • Improve.
Worked example. A tour for 12 guests; feedback 4.6 out of 5.

Activity. Present.

Standard to meet. Plan, records and feedback are complete.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why involve local people?

Model answer: Fairness and authentic experience. (Covered in module 1: Guests and local assets. To pass that module: Each asset has a guest benefit and a local permission.)

Q2. Why include rest points?

Model answer: Comfort and safety. (Covered in module 2: Design the experience. To pass that module: Timed plan with accessibility notes.)

Q3. What is in a risk assessment?

Model answer: Hazard, who is at risk, control, owner. (Covered in module 3: Safety and health. To pass that module: Risks have controls and owners.)

Q4. Name the five service beats.

Model answer: Greet, ask, serve, check, thank. (Covered in module 4: Service standards. To pass that module: Standards followed.)

Q5. What is break-even group size?

Model answer: Guests needed to cover fixed costs. (Covered in module 5: Pricing and costs. To pass that module: Numbers correct.)

Q6. Why a cancellation rule?

Model answer: Fair for both sides. (Covered in module 6: Booking and records. To pass that module: Records complete.)

Q7. Name one fair-tourism rule.

Model answer: Pay local partners fairly. (Covered in module 7: Sustainable and fair tourism. To pass that module: Findings and actions listed.)

Q8. What proves quality?

Model answer: Safe delivery and guest feedback. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: run a tour or stay. To pass that module: Plan, records and feedback are complete.)

Q9. Why get consent from local people?

Model answer: Respect and trust. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why record guest feedback?

Model answer: To improve. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know guest needs.
  2. L2 Awareness. Describe local assets.
  3. L3 Foundation. Assist on tours.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Run tours alone.
  5. L5 Specialist. Design products.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead operations.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set destination strategy.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Guest needs

List 8 common guest needs, such as food, safety and information, and match each to one service action.

Speaker notes. Teacher gives 4 guest requests and checks the match. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Local assets

Describe 5 local assets (places, crafts, food, stories, events) with one fact and one visitor tip for each, with local consent for stories.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks permission was asked before a story was used. Covered in module 1, Guests and local assets.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Assist on tours

Assist on 2 tours by counting guests at 3 checkpoints, handling one first-aid or safety step and recording guest feedback.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the head count at each checkpoint. Covered in modules 3 and 4.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Run tours alone

Run a tour of at least 10 guests alone, on time within 10 minutes, with a safety briefing and a costed booking record. Check local licence rules with an expert.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the briefing was given and the booking sheet matches the headcount. Covered in module 8, Capstone: run a tour or stay.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Product design

Design a new tour or stay product with a price, a cost sheet showing at least 25 percent margin and a plan for fair pay to local partners.

Speaker notes. Teacher recomputes the margin from the cost sheet.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Operations leadership

Lead operations for a season with a team of 5, a rota, a guest feedback score above 4 out of 5 and a monthly review.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the feedback data and rota.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Destination strategy

Set a destination strategy with 4 measured aims for visitors, income and nature care, and consult at least 10 local people before finalising it.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the consultation list and the aims have numbers.

All rupee amounts in the worked examples are rounded estimates for practice, not quotes or advice. Prices, tariffs and taxes change by place and over time: check current local figures before you use them.

Risk lesson (estimates, not quotes). In India a rough sheet figure is Rs 1,300 per guest served against a unit cost of Rs 900 and a fixed cost of Rs 40,000 a month: a margin of about 31% and a break-even of about 100 units a month. If the price falls 25% to about Rs 975, break-even rises from 100 to 534 units a month. Plan a price floor or a lower cost before you launch, and test the plan at the lower price. These sheet figures are separate from the sample numbers in the modules. Check them with a local price expert.
