executive MBA subject 12

IT Management

Year 1, term E. 5.0 ECTS, about 125 hours, 6 parts. Part 1 is free.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task

Write a business case for a digital project including sourcing, integration, security and AI rules.

Condition

Given the current systems map, a budget and a list of business needs.

Standard

The case links each need to a solution, compares costs over five years, and covers security and data rules.

Who it is for: Managers who must decide on digital projects, systems and data without being IT specialists.

how this subject works

Free entry, voice and your learning style

6 parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 Digital context and strategy 21 hUnderstand: explain

Digital strategy is business strategy using digital tools. Start with the business goal, then ask what technology helps reach it.

  • Goal first, tool second
  • Digital can cut cost, improve service or create new products
  • Measure the business result, not the go-live

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Online ordering lifts orders from 1,000 to 1,150 a month (+15%) and cuts order handling from 6 to 2 minutes, saving 4,600 minutes a month, about 77 hours.

Activity: Write one business goal and two digital ways to reach it.

Standard: Goal is measurable and both ideas are linked to it.

Part 2 Business and IT alignment 21 hAnalyse: align

Alignment means IT spends on what the business needs most. A simple portfolio view helps choose projects.

  • Score projects on value and effort
  • Do high value, low effort first
  • Review the portfolio each quarter

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Project A: value 8, effort 3. Project B: value 6, effort 7. A gives 2.7 value per effort point and B 0.9. Do A first.

Activity: Score five IT projects on value and effort and rank them.

Standard: All five scored and ranked with reasons.

Part 3 Digitisation and platforms 21 hAnalyse: compare models

Platforms connect two or more groups, such as buyers and sellers. They grow as each side attracts the other.

  • Network effects: more users make it more useful
  • Start with the hardest side
  • Charge the side that gains most

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A marketplace takes 10% of each sale. 500 sellers selling 2,000 a month each = 1m in sales and 100,000 in fees a month.

Activity: Sketch a platform for your industry and say who pays.

Standard: Both sides named, the fee model explained and one number worked out.

Part 4 Systems integration and data 21 hApply: connect

Systems that do not talk create double typing and errors. Integration and a single source for key data save time and improve decisions.

  • One owner and one source for each key data item
  • Connect systems through standard interfaces
  • Check data quality regularly

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Orders are typed twice, 400 a day at 1 minute each: 400 minutes a day. With a 2% error rate, 8 orders a day go wrong. Linking the systems removes both.

Activity: Map where one key data item is created and used in your firm.

Standard: All systems shown, the source named and duplicate entry identified.

Part 5 Sourcing and cloud 21 hEvaluate: compare costs

Build, buy or rent. Cloud services turn large up-front costs into monthly fees, but costs must be compared over several years.

  • Total cost over five years, not year one
  • Include staff, support and exit costs
  • Check where data is kept and who can access it

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Own servers: 200,000 up front plus 30,000 a year: 350,000 over 5 years. Cloud: 70,000 a year: 350,000 over 5 years. Equal cost, so decide on flexibility and skills.

Activity: Compare five-year costs of two sourcing options.

Standard: Totals correct and the non-cost factor stated.

Part 6 Managing IT, security and AI governance 21 hEvaluate: set rules

Security and AI need rules: who can access what, how data is protected, and how AI outputs are checked before use.

  • Least access: people see only what they need
  • Backups and a tested recovery plan
  • AI: human check on decisions about people and money

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Phishing tests: 18 of 100 staff clicked in January. After training, 6 of 100 clicked in March: a fall from 18% to 6%.

Activity: Write five rules for safe use of AI tools in your team.

Standard: Five clear rules covering data, checking and accountability.

ten checks

Check yourself

Say or write your answer first, then read the model answer.

  1. Q1. What comes first, the goal or the tool?
    Model answer: The goal. (Covered in part 1: Digital context and strategy.)
  2. Q2. Orders rise from 1,000 to 1,150. Percentage rise?
    Model answer: 15%. (Covered in part 1: Digital context and strategy.)
  3. Q3. Value 8, effort 3 versus value 6, effort 7. Which first?
    Model answer: The first: value 8, effort 3. (Covered in part 2: Business and IT alignment.)
  4. Q4. What is a network effect?
    Model answer: More users make the service more useful to each user. (Covered in part 3: Digitisation and platforms.)
  5. Q5. 500 sellers x 2,000 x 10%. Monthly fees?
    Model answer: 100,000. (Covered in part 3: Digitisation and platforms.)
  6. Q6. Why have one source for key data?
    Model answer: To avoid double entry and conflicting numbers. (Covered in part 4: Systems integration and data.)
  7. Q7. 400 orders a day with a 2% error rate. Errors a day?
    Model answer: 8. (Covered in part 4: Systems integration and data.)
  8. Q8. Why compare costs over five years?
    Model answer: Up-front and monthly costs only compare fairly over time. (Covered in part 5: Sourcing and cloud.)
  9. Q9. What is least access?
    Model answer: People can see only what they need for their work. (Covered in part 6: Managing IT, security and AI governance.)
  10. Q10. Clicks fall from 18 to 6 per 100. New rate?
    Model answer: 6%. (Covered in part 6: Managing IT, security and AI governance.)
