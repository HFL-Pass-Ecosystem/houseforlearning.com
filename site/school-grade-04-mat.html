Grade 04: Mathematics

11 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2Primary11 units36 teaching weeks36-week school yearBloom levels: Remember, Understand, Apply
  • Apply Read, write, round and compare numbers to 10,000.
  • Apply Add and subtract four-digit numbers and solve multi-step problems.
  • Apply Multiply by one and two digits and use estimation.
  • Apply Divide up to four digits by one digit.
  • Apply Find factors, multiples and common multiples.
  • Apply Compare, add and subtract fractions; link tenths and hundredths to decimals.
  • Apply Find perimeter and area of rectangles and convert units.
  • Apply Solve problems with time, timetables and the calendar.
  • Understand Classify angles, triangles and quadrilaterals and find lines of symmetry.
  • Remember Read bar graphs and line graphs and find the mode.
  • Apply Continue number patterns and solve money problems.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 04 Mathematics, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 11 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

Unit 1 Large numbers Weeks 1 to 3Apply

In this unit you work on 'Large numbers', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: read, write, round and compare numbers to 10,000. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Large numbers' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: read, write, round and compare numbers to 10,000. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: 5,38,246 has 5 lakh, 38 thousand, 2 hundreds, 4 tens and 6 ones. The 8 stands for 8,000. Rounded to the nearest thousand it is 5,38,000. Method: Model run for 'Large numbers'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Read 7,308: seven thousand, three hundred and eight. Place values: 7 thousands, 3 hundreds, 0 tens, 8 ones. The zero holds the tens place. Expanded: 7000 + 300 + 0 + 8.
  2. Step 2, Write from words: four thousand, six hundred and fifty is 4,650. Put 4 in thousands, 6 in hundreds, 5 in tens and 0 in ones. Check by reading 4,650 back in words.
  3. Step 3, Compare 5,962 and 5,926: thousands equal (5), hundreds equal (9), tens 6 is greater than 2. So 5,962 > 5,926. Compare place by place from the left.
  4. Step 4, Round 6,478 to the nearest hundred: look at the tens digit 7. It is 5 or more, so round up to 6,500. To the nearest thousand look at hundreds digit 4: round down to 6,000.
  5. Step 5, Order 3,405, 3,045, 3,450, 3,504: hundreds are 4, 0, 4, 5. 3,045 is smallest. Of 3,405 and 3,450, tens 0 < 5. Order: 3,045, 3,405, 3,450, 3,504.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: round 8,251 to the nearest hundred and nearest thousand, and write 9,070 in words. Answer: 8,300 and 8,000; nine thousand and seventy.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Read, write, round and compare numbers to 10,000: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

  • Use it: add and subtract four-digit numbers and solve multi-step problems. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.

Worked example. Solved: 47 + 28. Tens: 40 + 20 = 60. Ones: 7 + 8 = 15. Add: 60 + 15 = 75. Check by taking away: 75 - 28 = 47. Method: Model run for 'Addition and subtraction'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Add 4,256 + 3,789 in columns. Ones: 6 + 9 = 15, write 5 carry 1. Tens: 5 + 8 + 1 = 14, write 4 carry 1. Hundreds: 2 + 7 + 1 = 10, write 0 carry 1. Thousands: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. Sum 8,045.
  2. Step 2, Estimate: 4,300 + 3,800 = 8,100, near 8,045. Reasonable. Subtract 7,003 - 2,846: borrow through the zeros. Ones 13 - 6 = 7, tens 9 - 4 = 5, hundreds 9 - 8 = 1, thousands 6 - 2 = 4. Answer 4,157.
  3. Step 3, Check subtraction by adding: 4,157 + 2,846 = 7,003 (7 + 6 = 13, 5 + 4 + 1 = 10, 1 + 8 + 1 = 10, 4 + 2 + 1 = 7). The sum matches, so 4,157 is correct.
  4. Step 4, Multi-step problem: a shop has 5,200 items. It sells 1,845 on Monday and receives 2,300 on Tuesday. After Monday: 5,200 - 1,845 = 3,355. After Tuesday: 3,355 + 2,300 = 5,655 items.
  5. Step 5, Check with estimation: 5,200 - 1,800 = 3,400, then + 2,300 = 5,700. This is close to 5,655, so the working is sensible. Write each step as its own line.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: a school had 6,400 books, gave away 2,175 and bought 1,850. How many now? Answer: 6,400 - 2,175 = 4,225; then 4,225 + 1,850 = 6,075 books.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Add and subtract four-digit numbers and solve multi-step problems: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 4 Division Weeks 12 to 15Apply

In this unit you work on 'Division', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: divide up to four digits by one digit. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Division' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: divide up to four digits by one digit. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: 36 sweets shared equally among 4 children: 36 / 4 = 9 each. Check: 4 x 9 = 36. Method: Model run for 'Division'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Divide 756 by 3 using short division. 7 hundreds / 3 = 2 with 1 left. That 1 hundred joins 5 tens to make 15 tens; 15 / 3 = 5. Then 6 / 3 = 2. Answer 252.
  2. Step 2, Check by multiplying: 252 x 3 = 756 (2 x 3 = 6, 5 x 3 = 15 write 5 carry 1, 2 x 3 + 1 = 7). The product equals the dividend, so 252 is right.
  3. Step 3, Division with remainder: 1,475 / 6. 14 / 6 = 2 rem 2; 27 / 6 = 4 rem 3; 35 / 6 = 5 rem 5. So 245 remainder 5. Check: 245 x 6 = 1,470, plus 5 gives 1,475.
  4. Step 4, Four digits: 3,648 / 4. The 3 thousands cannot be shared among 4, so join them with the hundreds: 36 hundreds / 4 = 9 hundreds. Then 4 tens / 4 = 1 ten and 8 ones / 4 = 2 ones. Result 912. Check: 912 x 4 = 3,648.
  5. Step 5, Word problem: 1,284 oranges are packed in boxes of 6. 12 / 6 = 2; 8 / 6 = 1 rem 2; 24 / 6 = 4. So 214 boxes with nothing left. Check: 214 x 6 = 1,284.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: divide 2,596 by 4 and 905 by 5. Answer: 2,596 / 4 = 649 since 649 x 4 = 2,596; and 905 / 5 = 181 since 181 x 5 = 905.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

In this unit you work on 'Factors and multiples', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: find factors, multiples and common multiples. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Use it: find factors, multiples and common multiples. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: HCF and LCM of 12 and 18. 12 = 2 x 2 x 3 and 18 = 2 x 3 x 3. HCF = 2 x 3 = 6. LCM = 2 x 2 x 3 x 3 = 36. Check: 6 x 36 = 216 = 12 x 18. Method: Model run for 'Factors and multiples'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Factors of 24: test numbers in pairs. 1 x 24, 2 x 12, 3 x 8, 4 x 6. Factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24. A factor divides the number exactly.
  2. Step 2, Multiples: the multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36. Multiples of 8 are 8, 16, 24, 32, 40. Multiples come from counting by the number, while factors go into it.
  3. Step 3, Common multiples of 6 and 8: both lists contain 24, and the next common multiple is 48. The lowest common multiple (LCM) is 24. Write the first match you find.
  4. Step 4, Common factors of 12 and 18: factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12; factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6. Greatest is 6.
  5. Step 5, Word problem: two buses leave a stop, one every 6 minutes and one every 8 minutes. They leave together at 9:00. They next leave together after 24 minutes, at 9:24.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: list the common factors of 20 and 30, and the LCM of 4 and 10. Answer: common factors are 1, 2, 5, 10; LCM of 4 and 10 is 20.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Unit 6 Fractions and decimals Weeks 20 to 23Apply

In this unit you work on 'Fractions and decimals', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: compare, add and subtract fractions; link tenths and hundredths to decimals. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Fractions and decimals' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: compare, add and subtract fractions; link tenths and hundredths to decimals. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: 3/4 + 1/6. Common denominator 12. 9/12 + 2/12 = 11/12. Half of 12 is 6 and a quarter of 12 is 3. Method: Model run for 'Fractions and decimals'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Compare 3/5 and 4/5: same denominator, so compare numerators. 4 > 3, so 4/5 > 3/5. For unlike denominators, 1/2 is greater than 1/4 because halves are bigger parts.
  2. Step 2, Add: 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8 since the denominators match. Subtract: 7/10 - 3/10 = 4/10, which simplifies to 2/5 (divide top and bottom by 2).
  3. Step 3, Tenths to decimals: 3/10 = 0.3 because one tenth is 0.1. So 7/10 = 0.7. Hundredths: 45/100 = 0.45, with 4 tenths and 5 hundredths.
  4. Step 4, Compare decimals: 0.6 and 0.45. Write 0.6 as 0.60. Now compare hundredths: 60 > 45, so 0.6 > 0.45. A longer decimal is not always bigger.
  5. Step 5, Money link: 0.75 dirham is 75 fils, which is 75/100 = 3/4 of a dirham. Check: 3/4 of 100 fils = 75 fils. Fractions, decimals and money agree.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: find 5/9 - 2/9 and write 3/100 and 6/10 as decimals. Answer: 3/9 = 1/3; and 0.03 and 0.6.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Compare, add and subtract fractions; link tenths and hundredths to decimals: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 7 Measurement and perimeter Weeks 24 to 26Apply

In this unit you work on 'Measurement and perimeter', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: find perimeter and area of rectangles and convert units. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Measurement and perimeter' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: find perimeter and area of rectangles and convert units. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: Rectangle 12 cm by 5 cm. Perimeter = 2 x (12 + 5) = 34 cm. Area = 12 x 5 = 60 square cm. A cuboid 12 x 5 x 3 has volume 180 cubic cm. Method: Model run for 'Measurement and perimeter'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Perimeter of a rectangle 9 cm by 5 cm: add all four sides, 9 + 5 + 9 + 5 = 28 cm. Or use 2 x (9 + 5) = 2 x 14 = 28 cm. Perimeter is the distance around.
  2. Step 2, Area of the same rectangle: length x width = 9 x 5 = 45 square cm. Area counts squares inside, so the unit is square cm (cm2), not cm.
  3. Step 3, Find a missing side: a rectangle has perimeter 30 cm and length 10 cm. Half the perimeter is 15, and 15 - 10 = 5. So the width is 5 cm and area is 10 x 5 = 50 cm2.
  4. Step 4, Convert units: 2.5 m = 250 cm since 1 m = 100 cm. A ribbon 3 m 40 cm long is 340 cm. For mass, 3 kg 200 g = 3,200 g, and 4,500 ml = 4 l 500 ml.
  5. Step 5, Word problem: a garden is 12 m by 8 m. Fencing is its perimeter: 2 x (12 + 8) = 40 m. Grass area is 12 x 8 = 96 m2. Fence length and grass area use different units.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: find the perimeter and area of a 15 m by 6 m field. Answer: perimeter 2 x 21 = 42 m; area 15 x 6 = 90 m2.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Find perimeter and area of rectangles and convert units: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Step by step.

  2. Step 2, 24-hour time: 3:15 p.m. is 15:15, found by adding 12 to 3. And 18:40 is 6:40 p.m. by subtracting 12. Midnight is 00:00, noon is 12:00.

  6. Step 6, Your turn: a train leaves at 10:50 and arrives at 13:05. How long is the journey? Answer: 10 minutes to 11:00, then 2 hours to 13:00, plus 5 minutes; total 2 hours 15 minutes.

Unit 10 Data handling Weeks 33 to 35Remember

In this unit you work on 'Data handling', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: read bar graphs and line graphs and find the mode. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Data handling' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: read bar graphs and line graphs and find the mode. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: Data 4, 7, 7, 9, 13. Mean = 40 / 5 = 8. Median (middle value) = 7. Mode (most common) = 7. Method: Model run for 'Data handling'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Read a bar graph: bars show pupils per sport. Football 12, cricket 9, tennis 5, swimming 9. The scale is 2 per square, so read the top of each bar against the axis.
  2. Step 2, Find the mode: the value that occurs most often. In 12, 9, 5, 9 the number 9 appears twice, so the mode is 9. In the graph, cricket and swimming share it.
  3. Step 3, Line graph: temperatures at 6, 8, 10, 12 o'clock were 18, 21, 26, 29 degrees. The line rises each time. From 8 to 10 the rise is 26 - 21 = 5 degrees.
  4. Step 4, Find the greatest rise: between 6 and 8 the rise is 3, 8 to 10 is 5, 10 to 12 is 3. The steepest part of the line is 8 to 10, with a rise of 5.
  5. Step 5, Total and difference from bars: total pupils = 12 + 9 + 5 + 9 = 35. Football minus tennis is 12 - 5 = 7. State what the numbers mean in a sentence.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: find the mode of 4, 7, 7, 3, 7, 4 and the rise from 21 to 29 degrees. Answer: mode is 7, which appears three times; rise is 8 degrees.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Read bar graphs and line graphs and find the mode: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 11 Patterns and money Weeks 36 to 36Apply

In this unit you work on 'Patterns and money', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: continue number patterns and solve money problems. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Show it with objects or a picture: build or draw the idea in 'Patterns and money' before using symbols.
  • Say the rule in your own words, then write one worked line, naming each step.
  • Use it: continue number patterns and solve money problems. Start with one easy question, then one with a real-life story.
  • Check it: use a second method, estimate, or work backwards to test your answer.
Worked example. Solved: 3 books at 45 each: 3 x 45 = 135. Paid 200, so change = 200 - 135 = 65. Method: Model run for 'Patterns and money'. Read the question and underline what is asked. Draw or write what is known. Choose the method named in the unit. Work it line by line, one idea per line, with units where they apply. Estimate first so the answer can be tested for sense. Check by working backwards. What a good answer looks like: correct method, every step visible, units stated, and a one-line check.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Number pattern: 7, 14, 21, 28, __. The rule is add 7, so the next terms are 35 and 42. These are the multiples of 7. Check: 6 x 7 = 42.
  2. Step 2, Pattern with a changing step: 2, 3, 5, 8, 12. Differences are 1, 2, 3, 4, so the next difference is 5 and the next term is 17. Always check the gaps.
  3. Step 3, Money problem: three books cost 12.50, 8.75 and 15.25 dirhams. Total: 12.50 + 8.75 = 21.25, plus 15.25 = 36.50 dirhams. Align the decimal points in columns.
  4. Step 4, Change: pay with 50 dirhams. 50.00 - 36.50 = 13.50. Check by counting up: 36.50 to 37 is 0.50, to 50 is 13, so 13.50. Change is 13.50 dirhams.
  5. Step 5, Unit price: 4 pens cost 14 dirhams, so one pen costs 14 / 4 = 3.50 dirhams. Then 9 pens cost 9 x 3.50 = 31.50 dirhams. Check: 9 x 3 = 27 and 9 x 0.50 = 4.50, total 31.50.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: continue 5, 12, 19, 26 and find the cost of 6 notebooks at 2.75 each. Answer: next 33, 40; and 6 x 2.75 = 16.50 dirhams.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Continue number patterns and solve money problems: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Count and name shapes

On the slide: L1 Basic · Count and name shapes

Notes: Count objects up to 20 and name a circle, square and triangle. Notes: give ten buttons and two trays; learners count aloud, move three across and say how many are left (7), then hunt the room for one shape of each kind.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Add, subtract and measure (this course)

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Add, subtract and measure

Notes: Add and subtract within 100 and measure a length with a ruler. Notes: work 46 + 27 = 73 on a number line, jumping 20 then 7; learners measure a pencil in centimetres and explain how they checked their answer a second way.

This course: Grade 04 Mathematics is at this level. Its units include Large numbers; Addition and subtraction; Multiplication; Division and more.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Fractions and decimals

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Fractions and decimals

Notes: Compare fractions and decimals and use the four operations with whole numbers. Notes: shade 3/4 and 6/8 on equal strips to show they match, then add 0.5 + 0.25 = 0.75 and write it as 75 per cent of one whole.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Equations and percentages

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Equations and percentages

Notes: Solve one-step and two-step equations and percentage problems, showing each step. Notes: practice case is 3x + 5 = 20, so x = 5, checked by substituting back; then a 15 per cent discount on 400 leaves 340, because 400 x 0.85 = 340.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Geometry, data and graphs

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Geometry, data and graphs

Notes: Use the right-angled triangle rule, the mean and a straight-line graph to solve a problem. Notes: legs of 6 and 8 give a longest side of 10; the data 4, 6, 6, 8, 11 have mean 7; plot y = 2x + 1 and read y = 7 at x = 3.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Proof, calculus and algebra

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Proof, calculus and algebra

Notes: Prove results and use calculus and matrices to model change. Notes: differentiate f(x) = x^3 - 3x to get 3x^2 - 3, so turning points sit at x = 1 and x = -1; the second derivative 6x shows a minimum then a maximum. Learners prove the square root of 2 is irrational.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Research-level modelling

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Research-level modelling

Notes: Formulate and defend a proof or statistical model, stating assumptions and limits. Notes: for 25 values with mean 50 and standard deviation 10, the standard error is 2 and a 95 per cent interval is about 45.9 to 54.1 (t = 2.064). Learners check residuals and write a referee-style critique.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.
