Task, condition and standard

Task

Use free satellite data to answer one local question.

Condition

About 60 hours over 1-3 months. Own tools, free spreadsheet and form tools, and a real practice partner. Mentor and peer circle available. Currency written on every number.

Standard

Evidence reviewed by a mentor; at least 70% on the 10-question check; two written peer reviews given; every number carries its currency and unit.

Who it is for, requirements and materials

  • Who it is for: learners aged 16 and over, small-business starters, workers moving to a new field, and teams (see B2B, B2C, D2C).
  • Requirements: finish Lead Generation for Small Businesses first, or show equivalent work.
  • Materials included: lesson text, worked examples, case exercise, 10-question check, templates in My page.
  • Teaching team: a trainer from our network, a mentor who reviews your evidence, and a peer circle. Names are given at enrolment.

lesson by lesson

9 modules, in the order you do them

Module 1 What small satellites do, and what data they give IdeaStudy · 2 hUnderstand

Goal: turn a local question into a question that satellite data can answer.

  1. Explain in plain words: a nanosatellite is a very small spacecraft (often a few kilograms) that orbits Earth, usually in low orbit, carrying cameras or sensors.
  2. List 8 things satellite data can show: vegetation, water, heat, night lights, flood extent, coastline change, air quality, cloud cover.
  3. Choose a local question you care about (is the lake shrinking, are fields greener this year than last?).
  4. Write the question, the place on a map, the time period and what answer would change a decision.

Evidence you keep: the question, the map area and the decision it informs.

Module 2 Kick-off circle IdeaShare and mentoring · 3 hUnderstand
  1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
  2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
  3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this module: a data-source note, one map or chart, a method record and a plain-language answer.

Evidence you keep: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

Module 3 Find and download the data ActionPractice · 14 hApply
  1. Find two free public sources that cover your place and time. Write the source, the pixel size (what one square covers on the ground), how often it is updated and the usage terms.
  2. Download a small area and a few dates only. Keep file sizes small.
  3. Open the data in a free viewer. Find your place. Note clouds, gaps and odd values.
  4. Write down what each band or layer means in your own words.

Evidence you keep: source note with terms, a screenshot of your area, layer notes.

Module 4 Orbits, data sources and mission basics ActionStudy · 2 hApply

Short modules:

  • Orbits: low orbit versus higher orbits, why small satellites pass over a place at set times, why revisit time matters for change detection.
  • Data sources: visible images versus other bands, resolution, cloud cover, why the same place looks different in different seasons.
  • Mission basics: what a payload is, power, communication windows, why small teams now build satellites, and why we still check data against the ground.

Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

Module 5 Answer the question ActionPractice · 14 hApply
  1. Pick two dates a season apart or a year apart. Make comparable maps or charts.
  2. Compute a simple measure (for example the share of pixels that are green or watery) with a spreadsheet or small program. Record every step.
  3. Check against the ground: a photo you took, a local record or a person who knows the place.
  4. Write what the data can and cannot say. Name the limits (clouds, resolution, dates).

Evidence you keep: two comparable maps, calculation record, ground check, limits note.

Module 6 Data-review circle ValueShare and mentoring · 6 hEvaluate
  1. Present map and numbers. Peers challenge whether the two dates are fair to compare.
  2. Check for mistakes in the method: wrong area, cloud pixels, different seasons.
  3. A mentor with geography or remote-sensing experience reviews your claim for honesty.

Evidence you keep: peer challenge notes, corrected method.

Module 7 Finish and measure ValuePractice · 14 hEvaluate
  1. Redo the comparison with the fixes.
  2. Write a one-page plain-language answer for a non-expert with one map and one number.
  3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: share the answer, collect more dates, or drop the question.

Evidence you keep: one-page answer and decision note.

Module 8 Review room EmpowermentShare and mentoring · 3 hCreate
  1. Present to a review room: the question, the data, the method, the map, the limits and your decision.
  2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
  3. Ask one real user, customer or reviewer for a short written note.

Evidence you keep: slides or notes, two peer reviews, the written note.

Module 9 Check and next step RegenerationStudy · 2 hCreate
  1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
  2. Write your 90-day plan: what you will do next, how you will measure it, what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Drones: Operations and Applications; Water Management and Conservation; Greenhouse Gas Auditing.
  3. Contribute back: share your data-source notes, method template or one-page answer so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).

Evidence you keep: check result and the 90-day plan.

practice material and assessment

Worked examples, case, check and local law pointers

Worked examples
  1. Question: is the lake smaller than last year? Pixel size 10 m, so one pixel covers 10 x 10 = 100 square metres.
  2. Water pixels last year (same season): 41,000; this year: 36,900. Area last year = 41,000 x 100 = 4,100,000 square metres = 4.1 sq km. This year = 3.69 sq km.
  3. Change = (3.69 - 4.1) / 4.1 = -10%. Clouds covered 3% of the area on the second date; the result is a range of about -10% to -7%.
  4. Ground check: a local record shows water level down 0.6 m, consistent with a smaller area.
Case exercise

The two images are from different seasons, and one has 25% cloud. Questions: Can you compare them? What would you do? What would you write as the answer for a village meeting? Write one page.

Check questions (draft set, 10)
  1. What is a nanosatellite? 2. What does pixel size mean? 3. Why must the two dates be comparable? 4. Why do clouds matter? 5. What is revisit time? 6. Why check data against the ground? 7. Where do you record the usage terms? 8. What is a payload? 9. What are the limits of your answer? 10. What was your decision and why?

Rules for this course

No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, slide or file. No personal data of real people in shared files. Use only data you may use. Credit the data source by agency or programme only if the terms ask for it; no company logos.
