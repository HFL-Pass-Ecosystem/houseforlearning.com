Reporting findings for peer review

Clear, honest reports that reviewers can check.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5 SpecialistAbout 3 hours of CPDSelf-study plus role-playScientists

Clear, honest reports that reviewers can check.

  • Structure a report, given a template, with question, method, results, limits and conclusion.
  • Cite sources, given a reference style, with every claim sourced.
  • State limits, given your results, in at least three honest sentences.
  • Respond to a reviewer comment, given a sample, politely, with evidence or a change.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write a short, honest report from a result table in the standard sections, cite every source in one style, state limits, and respond politely to a reviewer comment.

Condition

Given a result table, a report template, an agreed reference style and a sample reviewer comment, working in a three-hour supervised session.

Standard

Finish with a report in the standard order, one citation style used throughout, at least three honest sentences on limits, no claim beyond the data, and a polite reply with evidence or a change.

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Standard sections 20 min
  • Title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, limits, references
  • Results and discussion stay apart
  • Each section has one job
Worked example. Result table: control mean 11.0 cm, fertiliser mean 14.0 cm, 10 plants each, one site. Placed in 8 sections: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results (the table), discussion, limits (one site only), references. Results hold the numbers; discussion holds the meaning.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: structure a report, given a template, with question, method, results, limits and conclusion.

Standard to meet. Arrange a result table into a short report with the standard sections: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, limits and references.

Module 2 Citing sources, one style 25 min
  • Use one agreed style
  • Every claim has a source
  • Every citation appears in the list
Worked example. The text cites 3 sources in the form (name, year) and the reference list has 3 entries. 3 = 3, so each citation matches a listed source. One style, kept the same from first page to last.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: cite sources, given a reference style, with every claim sourced.

Standard to meet. Cite every source in a report using one agreed style, kept the same throughout, so each claim can be traced.

Module 3 Limits and what was not tested 20 min
  • State what was not tested
  • State possible errors
  • Do not hide a weakness
Worked example. Limits: "Only one site was tested. Only day 14 was measured. Heights were read by hand to the nearest 0.5 cm." 3 honest sentences covering what was not tested and one possible error.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: state limits, given your results, in at least three honest sentences.

Standard to meet. Write at least three honest sentences on limits, covering what was not tested and the possible errors, for a small one-site study.

Module 4 Clear language 15 min
  • Use short sentences and plain words
  • Claim no more than the data show
  • Cut filler words
Worked example. A sentence of 34 words is split into two of 17 words each. "It could perhaps be suggested that results may show" becomes "Results show". Gap 3 cm is stated as seen, not as proven cause.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: respond to a reviewer comment, given a sample, politely, with evidence or a change.

Standard to meet. Edit a draft paragraph into clear, short sentences with plain words and no claim beyond the data.

Module 5 Practice 15 min
  • Fill every section in order
  • Keep results in results
  • Add a limits paragraph
Worked example. Report of about 370 words: abstract 50, introduction 60, methods 80, results 70, discussion 70, limits 40 (50 + 60 + 80 + 70 + 70 + 40 = 370). All 8 sections present, results kept apart from discussion.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "reporting findings for peer review" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (structure a report; cite sources; state limits; respond to a reviewer comment).

Standard to meet. Write a full short report from a result table, with all sections in place, results kept apart from discussion, and a limits paragraph.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Review by a colleague not on the study
  • Pass needs standard order, one style, honest limits
  • Share raw data so others can check
Worked example. Check: 8 of 8 sections present, 1 citation style, 3 limit sentences, 0 claims beyond the data. A colleague not on the study reads it and finds 1 unclear sentence, which is fixed before the check is passed.

Practise: Take the end check for "reporting findings for peer review": show that you can respond to a reviewer comment, given a sample, politely, with evidence or a change, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Pass the check by producing a short report in the standard order with one citation style, honest limits and no claims beyond the data.

Module 7 Second case: limits for a one-site study 45 min
  • Write limits for a new study
  • Say how far results apply
  • Offer a next test
Worked example. Case card 2: a small study with one site, 10 plants per group (20 in all), measured at day 14. Limits paragraph: "Only one site and one soil were tested, so results may not apply elsewhere. Only day 14 was measured. A repeat at a second site would help."

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: respond to a reviewer comment, given a sample, politely, with evidence or a change. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Write an honest limits paragraph for a small one-site study, stating what was not tested, the possible errors and what further test would help.

Module 8 Reviewer comments and portfolio 35 min
  • Read the comment fairly
  • Answer with evidence or a change
  • Keep the report as evidence
Worked example. Reviewer: "Results come from one site only." Reply: "Thank you. We have added this to the limits and stated that other sites were not tested." Change made, 1 of 1 comments answered politely. Final report passes all 8 checklist items.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: respond to a reviewer comment, given a sample, politely, with evidence or a change. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Respond to a reviewer comment politely with evidence or a change, revise the report, and compile it into a portfolio with a short reflection.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

What goes in limits?

Model answer: What was not tested and possible errors. (Covered in module 3: Limits and what was not tested. To pass that module: Write at least three honest sentences on limits, covering what was not tested and the possible errors, for a small one-site study.)

Cite how?

Model answer: One agreed style, every source. (Covered in module 2: Citing sources, one style. To pass that module: Cite every source in a report using one agreed style, kept the same throughout, so each claim can be traced.)

Where do results go?

Model answer: Results, not discussion. (Covered in module 1: Standard sections. To pass that module: Arrange a result table into a short report with the standard sections: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, limits and references.)

Why share raw data?

Model answer: So others can check. (Covered in: background fact, not taught in one module.)

Why state limits?

Model answer: Readers judge how far results apply. (Covered in module 3: Limits and what was not tested. To pass that module: Write at least three honest sentences on limits, covering what was not tested and the possible errors, for a small one-site study.)

One citation style?

Model answer: Yes, kept the same throughout. (Covered in module 2: Citing sources, one style. To pass that module: Cite every source in a report using one agreed style, kept the same throughout, so each claim can be traced.)

Where does data go?

Model answer: Results, with methods earlier. (Covered in module 1: Standard sections. To pass that module: Arrange a result table into a short report with the standard sections: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, limits and references.)

Who reviews first?

Model answer: A colleague not on the study. (Covered in: background fact, not taught in one module.)

Claims beyond data?

Model answer: Do not make them. (Covered in module 3: Limits and what was not tested. To pass that module: Write at least three honest sentences on limits, covering what was not tested and the possible errors, for a small one-site study.)

A report cites 3 sources in the text but its reference list has 2 entries. What is wrong and what do you do?

Model answer: One cited source is missing from the list, so add it, in the same style, so each citation matches an entry. (Covered in module 2: Citing sources, one style. To pass that module: Cite every source in a report using one agreed style, kept the same throughout, so each claim can be traced.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What is a report. Speaker note: A report tells others what was asked, done and found, so they can check it. At this level the learner knows that honest reporting means saying what the data show and nothing more.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: The standard sections. Speaker note: Show the eight sections: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, limits, references. The learner at this level can name each part and say where a table of results belongs.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Cite and state limits. Speaker note: Learners add citations in one agreed style under supervision and write one sentence on what was not tested. They follow a model report and a checklist for each section.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Write the report for review. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner writes a complete short report alone from a result table, cites every source in one style, states at least three limits and answers a reviewer politely.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Critical reviewing. Speaker note: The specialist reviews others' reports for claims beyond the data, missing sources and weak limits, and writes useful comments. They coach colleagues in answering reviewers with evidence or a change.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Quality across reports. Speaker note: The advanced worker sets report templates and review rules, samples reports for missing limits and style errors, and reports trends. Talk about how sharing raw data helps others re-check the work.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: A reporting standard. Speaker note: The super expert defines the organisation's reporting standard: templates, citation style, review steps, data sharing and training. They decide what reproducible means and prove it by having others re-run the work.

This course sits at L5 Specialist. Credits stack; aligned with, not accredited by, any framework.
