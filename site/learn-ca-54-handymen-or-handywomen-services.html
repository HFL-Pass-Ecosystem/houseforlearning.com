Handymen or Handywomen Services

Run a pilot of handymen or Handywomen Services in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small handymen or Handywomen Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: property, construction and smart buildings. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the handy trade 6 hUnderstand

Handy workers fix and improve homes: small repairs, fitting, painting, assembly. Know the limits of your trade first.

  • Name common jobs: patching walls, painting, shelves, door and tap repairs, furniture assembly.
  • Describe which jobs need a licensed trade, such as electrical, gas and some plumbing.
  • Name the basic tools: tape measure, level, drill, screwdrivers, stud finder.
  • List words: stud, anchor, caulk, primer, torque, shut-off valve.
  • Classify a job as handy, licensed trade or refer on, and write the reason.
  • Compare a small repair with a renovation and say where your limit sits.
  • Identify signs of a larger problem, such as mould, a sagging floor or repeated leaks.
Worked example. A customer asks for a new light fitting and a leaking shower. Light wiring may need a licensed electrician; a loose tap washer may be fine for a handy worker. The worker splits the request into two jobs and writes which one is referred on.

Activity. List ten common jobs and mark each: handy worker, or licensed trade needed (check locally).

Standard to meet. Task: sort jobs by scope. Condition: ten example jobs. Standard: each job marked with a reason and checked with the local authority.

Module 2 Work safely 6 hApply

Tools, ladders and dust can hurt. Safety comes before speed.

  • Apply tool safety: eye cover, gloves, hearing cover, dust mask where needed.
  • Use ladders correctly: firm base, three points of contact, do not overreach.
  • Check for hidden hazards before cutting or drilling: pipes, wires, old paint or insulation.
  • Explain why you stop and ask when you suspect something dangerous.
  • Wear a dust mask that suits the dust and check that it seals on the face.
  • Secure the work area with tape and signs, and keep cords out of walking paths.
  • Lift with the legs, ask for help with heavy items and use a trolley.
Worked example. Before drilling into a wall, the worker uses a detector, checks the other side, then drills a small pilot hole. Old paint in an old house is tested before sanding. To reach a point 4 m up, a ladder base sits about 1 m out from the wall (the one-in-four rule).

Activity. Write a safety checklist for ladder, drill and paint jobs and use it on a practice job.

Standard to meet. Task: complete a safe set-up. Condition: practice job. Standard: every checklist item done before work starts.

Module 3 Follow the rules in Canada 10 hApply

Trades licensing, permits, insurance and tax rules differ by province. Check with the local authority.

  • Explain that electrical, gas and many plumbing jobs need a licensed person; check your province.
  • Describe building permits: some work needs one; ask the city before you start.
  • Check insurance and compensation rules for working alone or hiring helpers.
  • Apply tax and contract rules: written quote, receipts, and sales tax rules checked with the federal tax agency.
  • Identify the city office to call for permit questions and write down the contact.
  • Describe when a landlord or condo board must approve work.
  • Keep a copy of the quote, permit and insurance for every job.
Worked example. Rules table row: Permit | city building office | ask before structural or electrical changes | before work | permit number in job file. A second row: Landlord approval | building owner | ask in writing before changes to walls or fixtures | before work | reply kept. Add a checked date to each row.

Activity. Build a rules table for your city and province with official pages and dates.

Standard to meet. Task: map the rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.

Module 4 Do core repairs to a standard 8 hEvaluate

Learn a few repairs well rather than many badly.

  • Build a method for a wall patch: clean, fill, dry, sand, prime, paint.
  • Apply a method for hanging a shelf: find studs, level, right anchors, load check.
  • Fix a dripping tap or running toilet by following the manufacturer steps.
  • Record before and after photos.
  • Sequence a repair in steps and write the tools and materials list before starting.
  • Check a finished repair with a level, a tug test or a water test.
  • Clean and put away tools, then note what to improve.
Worked example. Shelf job: stud found, level marked, two screws into the stud, anchor in one spot, load test, photo taken. If the shelf will hold three book sets of 2 kg and a 4 kg box, the planned load is 3 x 2 + 4 = 10 kg, and the load test uses that weight.

Activity. Complete each of three repairs twice on a practice board and compare the second run.

Standard to meet. Task: complete a repair. Condition: practice board. Standard: result level, firm and clean, and the second run is better.

Module 5 Read the problem and quote 8 hApply

Many handy jobs fail because the real problem was missed. Look first, then quote.

  • Ask questions: what, where, since when, what was tried.
  • Check the cause, not just the sign. A stain on a ceiling may come from above.
  • Write a quote with scope, materials, time, price and what is not included.
  • Explain a change order and why it must be agreed before extra work.
  • Take photos and measurements at the first visit.
  • Decide when to say 'I must look closer' instead of guessing a price.
  • Explain an allowance for hidden problems, such as extra time for an old house.
Worked example. Ceiling stain: the leak is from the bathroom above. The quote covers finding the cause (1 hour); painting is a second step after it is dry (3 hours). The two steps total 4 hours, and the client approves each one.

Activity. Visit two practice jobs, write the problem, cause and a written quote.

Standard to meet. Task: quote a job. Condition: practice site. Standard: scope, materials, price and exclusions all written.

Module 6 Price, materials and time 6 hCreate

Time and materials add up fast. Count the trip to the store too.

  • Compute job cost: labour, materials with a small mark-up, travel, tools wear, insurance.
  • Compare hourly and fixed prices.
  • Plan the day with buffer time for surprises.
  • Check supplier prices at three stores before buying.
  • Calculate materials with waste: add about 10% for cuts, then round up to whole packs.
  • Compute the mark-up on materials and tell the client.
  • Track real time against quoted time to improve the next quote.
Worked example. Price 120 for a 2-hour job, materials 15, travel 10. Margin = 120 - 15 - 10 = 95. If fixed costs are 600 a month, break-even is 600 / 95 = 6.3, so 7 jobs (teaching numbers only).

Activity. Build a cost sheet and a price list for five common jobs.

Standard to meet. Task: price jobs. Condition: all costs counted. Standard: margin and break-even correct and shown with units.

Module 7 Clients, trust and records 6 hEvaluate

You work in people's homes. Respect, tidiness and honesty bring referrals.

  • Apply site care: cover floors, clean up, take away waste.
  • Write a short agreement: scope, price, timing, payment, warranty on your work.
  • Keep a job file: quote, photos, receipts, notes.
  • Handle a mistake: say it, fix it, record it.
  • Take photos only with permission and never share the inside of a home.
  • Explain your warranty in plain words: its length and what is not covered.
  • Ask for payment on the date agreed and issue a receipt.
Worked example. Paint drips on a floor. The worker tells the client, cleans it at once and writes a note. The client keeps using the service, and the note goes in the job file.

Activity. Create a job file template and run one practice job through it from quote to final photo.

Standard to meet. Task: finish a job properly. Condition: practice job. Standard: site clean, file complete and client signs off.

Module 8 Run the service for real 8 hCreate

Now run your handy service with real clients, a plan and clear limits.

  • Run a 60-day pilot with a stop rule agreed in advance.
  • Review weekly: jobs, margin, repeat jobs, complaints, referrals.
  • Create a 12-month plan with training, insurance and tools.
  • Lead others: write job methods so a helper can do the same standard.
  • Calculate the share of quotes that become jobs.
  • Set a rule to decline jobs outside your skills or insurance.
  • Cost the tools and training in the 12-month plan and decide which to buy first.
Worked example. Stop rule: if fewer than 15 jobs in 60 days, stop and write lessons. Result: 22 jobs and 6 referrals, so 6 / 22 = about 27% of jobs came from referrals. 22 is above 15, so the pilot continues and shelf-and-assembly packages are added.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and present your plan to three people.

Standard to meet. Task: run a handy service pilot. Condition: 60 days, real clients. Standard: results compared with the stop rule and trade limits re-checked.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name two jobs that may need a licensed trade.

Model answer: Electrical and gas (also some plumbing); check locally. (Covered in module 1: Know the handy trade. To pass that module: Task: sort jobs by scope. Condition: ten example jobs. Standard: each job marked with a reason and checked with the local authority.)

Q2. What do you do before drilling a wall?

Model answer: Check for pipes and wires with a detector and plan the hole. (Covered in module 2: Work safely. To pass that module: Task: complete a safe set-up. Condition: practice job. Standard: every checklist item done before work starts.)

Q3. What are three points of contact on a ladder?

Model answer: Two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand, always touching. (Covered in module 2: Work safely. To pass that module: Task: complete a safe set-up. Condition: practice job. Standard: every checklist item done before work starts.)

Q4. Why ask the city about permits?

Model answer: Some work needs a permit and doing it without one can cause trouble. (Covered in module 3: Follow the rules in Canada. To pass that module: Task: map the rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.)

Q5. What should a quote include?

Model answer: Scope, materials, time, price and what is not included. (Covered in module 5: Read the problem and quote. To pass that module: Task: quote a job. Condition: practice site. Standard: scope, materials, price and exclusions all written.)

Q6. What is a change order?

Model answer: An agreed written change to scope or price before extra work. (Covered in module 5: Read the problem and quote. To pass that module: Task: quote a job. Condition: practice site. Standard: scope, materials, price and exclusions all written.)

Q7. Why find the cause, not just the sign?

Model answer: The stain or drip may come from another place. (Covered in module 5: Read the problem and quote. To pass that module: Task: quote a job. Condition: practice site. Standard: scope, materials, price and exclusions all written.)

Q8. How do you find break-even jobs?

Model answer: Fixed cost divided by margin per job. (Covered in module 6: Price, materials and time. To pass that module: Task: price jobs. Condition: all costs counted. Standard: margin and break-even correct and shown with units.)

Q9. What goes in a job file?

Model answer: Quote, photos, receipts and notes. (Covered in module 7: Clients, trust and records. To pass that module: Task: finish a job properly. Condition: practice job. Standard: site clean, file complete and client signs off.)

Q10. What if a job seems beyond your skill?

Model answer: Stop, decline it or refer it to a qualified person or licensed trade. (Covered in module 1: Know the handy trade. To pass that module: Task: sort jobs by scope. Condition: ten example jobs. Standard: each job marked with a reason and checked with the local authority.)

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
