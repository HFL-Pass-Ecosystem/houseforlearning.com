Artificial Intelligence: Mexico deep edition

Artificial Intelligence planned in detail for Mexico: sector lessons plus local rules, jobs and MXN examples. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real. All amounts are estimates for planning, not quotes. The module lines use rounded sample numbers; the Local example in module 6 uses separate local estimates.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a one-task automation pilot plan with a measured demo

Condition

Within 3 hours, using 10 sample cases, checked by a mentor and a peer

Standard

Time saved shown as minutes on 10 cases, error rate counted, a stop rule at 5%, and a one-page use policy.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read your local market 4 hUnderstand

Every plan starts with the place. Read Mexico as your customer does before you choose a sector.

  • Use AI to remove one slow task for a small firm; do not sell the word AI.
  • A large market with strong regional differences. Start in one city and one niche.
  • Many customers use bank transfers, cash-payment points and cards; a lot of buying is on messaging apps.
  • Electronic invoices are part of daily trade: learn them in week one.
  • Close to the northern trading partners: near-shoring creates demand for services, testing and training.
Worked example. Task: reply drafts for 40 daily emails cut from 4 hours to 1.5 hours. | Local example: Market page for Mexico: size in one line, three languages or fewer, main payment methods, three public holidays that affect delivery, and one risk. Each line has a source you can open and a date.

Activity. Write a one-page market page with sources and dates. Ask two local people to correct it.

Standard to meet. Every line has a source and a date; two local readers confirm the facts; one risk is named with a response.

Module 2 Choose your sector 4 hAnalyse

The global list has 75 sectors in 8 clusters. Pick by local fit, not by fashion.

  • Choose between a ready tool, a no-code build and custom work by cost and risk.
  • Cluster A, Online selling and marketing: Strong fit: messaging-app selling and marketplaces. Offer cash-payment and bank-transfer options.
  • Cluster B, Technology and digital services: Strong fit: near-shoring means firms need IT support, testing, security and cloud services in Spanish.
  • Cluster C, Health, care and wellbeing (regulated): Regulated. Practise only with the right licence and registration. This course teaches planning, not practice. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster D, Mobility, aerial and space (regulated): Regulated; airspace and transport rules apply. Charging points and shared mobility suit big cities. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster E, Energy, building and environment: Strong fit: sunshine, wind and water stress make solar and water services valuable. Hold permits first. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster F, Food and farming: Strong fit: food and farming are central; check health-risk rules for food and alternative proteins. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster G, Money services (regulated): Regulated money services; plan as a partner of a licensed firm only. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster H, People and business support: Coaching, coworking and outsourcing fit a young workforce; many founders need support in Spanish.
Worked example. Ready tool MXN 600 per month vs custom MXN 60,000 once; the ready tool pays back first. | Local example: Scoring example: pick three sectors, score each 1 to 5 on local demand, skills you hold, start-up cost, rules and partner access. A sector that scores 5 on demand but 1 on rules is a later project, not a first one. Pick the sector with the highest total and a rules score of 3 or more.

Activity. Rank six sectors from at least three clusters. Choose one and write the reason in three lines.

Standard to meet. Six sectors scored on five criteria; the choice has a stated reason; any regulated sector is marked as planning only.

Module 3 Register and license 4 hApply

Rules differ by place. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real.

  • Read the rules on automated decisions, advertising claims and cross-border data. Check with a local expert.
  • Register with the tax authority (a tax ID) before you invoice; choose sole trader or company. Check with a local expert.
  • A simplified small-taxpayer regime may fit a first year; read the current limits.
  • Issue electronic invoices as the tax rules require; learn the tool before the first customer. Check with a local expert.
  • Some activities need extra licences (food, health, tourism, money); list yours before the first order. Check with a local expert.
Worked example. Rule table: where the tool stores data, who owns outputs, what must be disclosed. | Local example: Licence list for a small Artificial Intelligence business: registration type, tax registration, address, any activity licence, insurance. Next to each, write who issues it, what it costs in MXN (from their public page), how long it takes, and the date you checked.

Activity. Build a licence table for your chosen sector with issuer, cost, time and the date checked. Do not apply; this is a plan.

Standard to meet. Table is complete; every row has a public source; the order of steps is logical; one adviser question is written.

Module 4 Protect data and customers 3 hApply

Trust is the real product. Protect customer data and keep your promises in writing.

  • Never paste confidential data into an unchecked tool; write a use policy of one page.
  • The private-sector data law needs a privacy notice (check the current text): who you are, what you collect, why and how to correct it. Check with a local expert.
  • Customers may ask to see, correct, remove or object to use of their data; set a reply time.
  • Keep marketing consent records; offer a clear way to stop messages.
  • Keep a list of every tool that stores customer data, including messaging apps.
Worked example. Policy: allowed data, forbidden data, review step before any output leaves. | Local example: Privacy notice in five lines: who we are; what we collect; why; who sees it; how to ask for a copy, correction or removal. Plus a data map listing each tool and where it stores data.

Activity. Write the five-line notice and a data map for your plan. Ask a peer to find any gap.

Standard to meet. Notice is plain words; data map lists every tool; refund and complaint steps are written and dated.

Module 5 Find and serve customers 4 hCreate

Go where your customers already are, in the language they use. Language: Spanish first; add English for tourism and trade customers.

  • Show a before and after demo with real time saved, measured.
  • Channels: messaging apps, social media and marketplaces; local fairs and markets for a first test.
  • Language: Spanish first, in plain words; add English only if customers ask.
  • Payments: bank transfer, cards and cash-payment points; show prices with the sales tax clearly. Check with a local expert.
  • Trust: tax ID, address, delivery days and a refund note on every page; consumer rules apply. Check with a local expert.
Worked example. Demo: 100 invoices sorted in 12 minutes instead of 3 hours, with 2 errors caught by a human check. | Local example: Test plan: show the offer to 20 real people through two channels over two weeks; count enquiries, replies and first orders. Example result: 20 shown, 6 enquiries, 2 orders: a 10% order rate. Change one thing (price or wording) and test again.

Activity. Test two channels with 20 people. Record results in a table and write the next change.

Standard to meet. Test has a start and end date; results are counted, not guessed; one change is chosen and justified.

Module 6 Price, tax and money 4 hEvaluate

Know your numbers in MXN. Tax rule in one line: Income tax plus a sales tax (IVA); a simple regime may fit small firms, and most sales need an electronic invoice.

  • Price per task or per month and count tool and review time.
  • Write MXN next to every number.
  • Contribution per unit = price minus the cost of each unit.
  • Break-even units = fixed monthly cost divided by contribution per unit; round up.
  • Add tax and duty explicitly; never bury them in a price. Check with a local expert.
  • Check against a worse case: if sales are 25% lower, do you still cover costs?
Worked example. Tool MXN 600 review 5 h at MXN 400 = MXN 2,000; total MXN 2,600; price MXN 8,000 leaves MXN 5,400. | Local example: For a small Artificial Intelligence business (all amounts are estimates): price MXN 600 per unit, cost MXN 240 per unit, fixed cost MXN 18,000 per month. Contribution = 600 - 240 = 360. Break-even = 18,000 / 360 = 50.0, so 50 subscriptions per month. If sales fall 25% from 100, you sell 75 and the margin is 9,000.

Activity. Build your own sheet in MXN: price, unit cost, fixed cost, contribution, break-even and a 25% worse case. Put the tax and duty rules in a separate line with a source.

Standard to meet. Sheet has all six lines in MXN; arithmetic is checked by a peer; the worse case is shown.

Module 7 People and partners 3 hApply

Plans become real through people. Hire, partner and write every promise down.

  • Train one person at the client to own the tool; write a one-page guide.
  • Employers register staff for social security and pay the year-end bonus; budget both.
  • Staff may share in profits; small and new firms can differ, so read the rule before you hire. Check with a local expert.
  • Written contracts and signed copies for every helper, even part-time.
  • Partner with a technical college or business chamber to find helpers and to practise.
Worked example. Guide: where to click, what never to paste, who to call. | Local example: Role card for a first helper: tasks, hours, pay in MXN, trial period, review date, and one measure of success. Signed by both sides.

Activity. Write one role card and one partner letter (what each side gives). Role-play a first meeting for 10 minutes.

Standard to meet. Role card has pay in local currency, hours and a review date; partner letter is balanced; both are signed copies.

Module 8 Launch plan and review 4 hCreate

Finish with a one-page launch plan and a plan to review it every month.

  • Launch with two pilots; stop if errors above 5% after correction.
  • Dates: first test, first order, first review.
  • One measure per week: enquiries, orders or revenue; pick one.
  • A monthly review: what we planned, what happened, what we change.
  • Keep a risk list with a response for each; include a rules change. Check with a local expert.
  • Local caution: A large country: start in one city, test one offer, then grow.
Worked example. Pilot review: error rate 3% after human check: go. | Local example: Launch page for Mexico: Week 1 licence table done; Week 2 test with 20 people; Week 3 numbers sheet checked; Week 4 launch decision with a go or no-go rule written in advance.

Activity. Write the launch page and present it to two people for 10 minutes. Record their questions and your answers.

Standard to meet. Plan has dates, one weekly measure, a go or no-go rule and a risk list; presenter answers three questions with evidence.

jobs

Where this takes you

  • Artificial Intelligence assistant. supports a small Artificial Intelligence business in Mexico.
  • Online seller. runs product pages, orders, invoices and delivery.
  • Digital marketing assistant. plans posts and reports results in Spanish.
  • IT and testing technician. supports near-shoring firms with testing and support.
  • Solar and water assistant. prepares surveys and quotes.
  • Food business assistant. plans menus, safety logs and orders.

Six steps from entry to senior

  1. Artificial Intelligence assistant (L2, Entry). helps with set tasks, keeps records and follows the checklist.
  2. Junior Artificial Intelligence coordinator (L3, Junior). runs small jobs end to end and reports each week.
  3. Artificial Intelligence practitioner (L4, Practitioner). plans and delivers the work alone, with a mentor check.
  4. Artificial Intelligence specialist (L5, Specialist). goes deep in one area, such as rules, numbers or customers.
  5. Artificial Intelligence team lead or manager (L6, Advanced). leads people and partners, owns the plan and the data.
  6. Senior Artificial Intelligence adviser or mentor (L7, Senior). designs the whole system, reviews others and publishes cases.

See how these steps connect to study and work in Career pathways and Pathways.
