Grade 12 Commerce: Economics

4 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5Post-secondary4 units36 teaching weeks36-week school yearBloom levels: Understand, Apply, Analyse
  • Apply Measure national income.
  • Understand Explain money creation and income determination.
  • Understand Describe the budget and exchange rates.
  • Analyse Analyse development, poverty and reforms.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 12 Commerce Economics, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 4 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

unit by unit

4 units with worked examples

Unit 1 National income and related aggregates Weeks 1 to 9Apply

In this unit you work on 'National income and related aggregates', taught in a way that is with exam-style questions and written reasoning. The target is clear: measure national income. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Place it: find where and when 'National income and related aggregates' happens, on a map, a timeline or in a local example.
  • Gather: read or listen to two short sources and note who is speaking and why.
  • Use it: measure national income. Give a reason and one piece of evidence for every point.
  • Weigh it: say what another person might think, and why, before you decide.
Worked example. Solved: GDP = C + I + G + (X - M) = 600 + 200 + 150 + (80 - 60) = 970. Method: Model run for 'National income and related aggregates'. Question: what happened, or what is the situation, and why? Sources: two short extracts, a map or table. Notes: who, what, where, when, why. Answer: three sentences, each with a reason and a piece of evidence. Check: is there a point of view that you have not considered? What a good answer looks like: facts are correct, reasons are linked to evidence, and one other point of view is named fairly.

Activity. About 40 to 50 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a timed paper and an error log.

Standard to meet. Measure national income: at least 70 percent on an exam-style task, with the method shown.

How you are checked

Evidence for each unit is marked with this rubric by a teacher, and by a peer where the unit allows. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet' after one retry.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
KnowledgeGapsMain facts rightFacts right and linked
Use of evidenceNo evidenceSome evidenceEvidence for every point
ReasoningStatements onlyReasons givenReasons weighed against another view
Maps, dates and termsOften wrongMostly rightAccurate and used well
Practice habitRarely practisesPractises weeklyPractises most days and reflects

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
