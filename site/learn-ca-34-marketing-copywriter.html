Marketing Copywriter

Run a real marketing Copywriter pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small marketing Copywriter offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: content, creative and marketing work. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know words, readers and the job of copy 6 hUnderstand

Copy is writing that helps a reader take one clear step. Start with the basic words and the people you write for.

  • Learn the terms: headline, body, call to action, benefit, feature, tone, brief.
  • A feature is what a thing has. A benefit is what the reader gains from it.
  • Name one reader in one line: who they are, what they want, what worries them.
  • Read five ads or web pages and mark the headline, the benefit and the call to action.
  • Rewrite two features as benefits.
  • Describe the reader's biggest worry in one line.
  • Compare the tone of two ads and name who each is written for.
Worked example. Feature: 'The mug keeps drinks hot for six hours.' Benefit: 'Your coffee is still hot when the meeting ends.' Call to action: 'Order today.' If a box lists 6 features, keep the 2 that matter most to the reader.

Activity. Pick five pieces of copy and mark headline, benefit and call to action on each. Write a one-line reader profile.

Standard to meet. Task: label the parts of five pieces of copy. Condition: with a printed or saved sample and no help. Standard: all parts labelled correctly and the reader profile fits one line.

Module 2 Explain how a copy job runs from brief to delivery 6 hApply

A job has steps. If you can name them, you can plan your time and avoid surprises.

  • The steps are brief, research, outline, draft, edit, review, final files.
  • A brief says who the reader is, the goal, the tone, the length, the deadline and the one action wanted.
  • Ask questions before you write; a short call saves a rewrite.
  • Agree how many rounds of changes are included.
  • Draft an outline with headings before any full text.
  • Record the answers to the brief questions in writing.
  • Plan your time: research, drafting and editing.
Worked example. A client asks for a web page. You ask: Who buys? What do they do now? What action do you want? What can we not say? A 4-hour job plans 1 hour research, 2 hours drafting and 1 hour editing: 1 + 2 + 1 = 4.

Activity. Write a one-page brief form with ten questions and test it on a friend who runs a small shop.

Standard to meet. Task: complete a brief for a real or pretend client. Condition: in one conversation of 30 minutes. Standard: the brief states reader, goal, action, tone, length, deadline and number of change rounds.

Module 3 Write a clear short piece to a standard 10 hApply

Now you apply the basics. Write short pieces that are clear, true and easy to scan.

  • Use short words and short sentences. Put the main point first.
  • Write three headline options and pick the one that is clear before it is clever.
  • Use real facts the client can prove. Do not make claims you cannot support.
  • Read aloud and cut every word that does nothing.
  • Set a word limit before you start, for example 150 words.
  • Replace vague words with numbers or facts.
  • Edit twice: first for meaning, then for spelling.
Worked example. Before: 'We offer a wide range of premium solutions.' (8 words) After: 'We fix leaking taps the same day. Call before 3 p.m.' (11 words). The second names the service, the speed and the step.

Activity. Write a 150-word product page and three headlines. Edit it twice and keep both drafts.

Standard to meet. Task: write a 150-word page. Condition: from a written brief, within two hours. Standard: one action only, every claim checkable, no spelling errors, and the second draft is shorter and clearer than the first.

Module 4 Follow Canadian rules for advertising and messages 8 hEvaluate

Canada has rules for honest advertising, email and language. Learn which ones touch your copy.

  • Advertising must not be false or misleading. A price, a discount or a result must be true and clear.
  • Commercial email and text messages need consent, a clear sender name and a working way to unsubscribe. Check the current anti-spam rules.
  • In Quebec, commercial advertising generally needs French. Check the current language rules with the provincial office.
  • Personal information you use in copy is covered by privacy law. Ask before using a customer name or story.
  • List the claims in your copy that need proof.
  • Add a working unsubscribe line to your email template.
  • Check that every price shows the full amount the reader will pay.
Worked example. A client wants 'Best in Canada'. You ask for proof. They have none, so you write 'Loved by 200 local families' only after they show you the count. Keep the proof with the job file.

Activity. Make a table of rule, who enforces it, what you must do, and where you checked, for ten claims you often write.

Standard to meet. Task: build a claims and rules table. Condition: using official public pages. Standard: each row has a dated source; no rule is guessed; unclear cases say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.

Module 5 Price your work and check it pays 8 hApply

You are paid for results and time. Work out your cost per hour and what a project must earn.

  • Time includes research, calls, drafting, edits and admin, not just typing.
  • Hourly cost = yearly costs and the pay you need, divided by hours you can really bill.
  • Compare per-hour, per-page and per-project pricing; use a project price only when the scope is clear.
  • Collect sales tax once registered; check the small-supplier limit with the tax agency.
  • Calculate your hourly break-even from yearly costs and your pay target.
  • Compare per-page pricing with hourly pricing.
  • Write a scope limit: pages, words and rounds of changes.
Worked example. A page takes 4 hours at $55 an hour: 4 x 55 = $220. If tools cost $5, margin is 220 - 5 = $215. If a per-page price is $200 and the job takes 4.5 hours, you earn 200 / 4.5 = about $44 an hour, below $55.

Activity. Time three pieces from start to finish. Compute your real hours per piece and set a price for each.

Standard to meet. Task: price three projects. Condition: from your own time log and a simple spreadsheet. Standard: hours, rate and total shown; a mentor can follow every number.

Module 6 Find clients and show proof 6 hCreate

Clients buy from writers they trust. Build a small portfolio and a steady way to be found.

  • Make three samples that match the kind of client you want, with the brief shown beside each.
  • Get written permission before showing client work.
  • Reach out to ten local businesses with one specific idea each, not a general pitch.
  • Keep a simple list: contact, date, reply, next step.
  • Write a sample with the brief shown beside it.
  • Ask each past client for permission in writing.
  • Track messages, replies and jobs.
Worked example. You email a bakery: 'Your online order page has no call to action. Here is a 60-word rewrite.' You attach it and ask for a 15-minute call. If 10 messages bring 2 calls and 1 job, the job rate is 1 / 10 = 10%.

Activity. Send ten tailored messages in two weeks and log the replies.

Standard to meet. Task: run a two-week outreach. Condition: using only legal, consent-based contact. Standard: ten messages sent with consent rules met, replies logged, one lesson written.

Module 7 Protect ownership, privacy and quality 6 hEvaluate

A professional writer is careful with rights, client secrets and facts.

  • Write down who owns the copy and what the client may do with it. Check with a legal adviser for large deals.
  • Do not copy other people's writing or images; use your own words or licensed material.
  • Keep client files safe: strong passwords, backups, access only for those who need it.
  • Fact-check names, prices, dates and quotes before delivery.
  • Keep source notes for every fact you use.
  • Use quotation marks and credit the source for any quote.
  • Name drafts by version so you can find the final one.
Worked example. Your contract says: 'The client owns the final copy once paid in full. The writer may show it in a portfolio unless the client objects.' A delivery checklist of 8 items at 1 minute each takes 8 minutes.

Activity. Write a one-page agreement template and a delivery checklist for facts, spelling, links and file formats.

Standard to meet. Task: produce a template and checklist. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: scope, fees, rounds, rights, payment date and privacy are all stated in plain words.

Module 8 Run your practice for real and grow it 8 hCreate

Now you run it: take real jobs, measure them and decide how to grow.

  • Set a 60-day pilot goal, such as three paid clients and one repeat job.
  • Each week record hours, income, late payments and client feedback.
  • Ask each client for a short review and one thing to improve.
  • At the end choose: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Calculate your effective hourly rate.
  • Compare late payments with on-time payments.
  • Evaluate which clients came back and why.
Worked example. After 60 days: 3 clients, 22 hours, $1,210 billed (22 x $55), one client came back. Your effective rate is 1,210 / 22 = $55 an hour. You decide to raise the rate and stop low-fee work.

Activity. Run the pilot, keep the weekly log and present a 12-month plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a 60-day practice pilot. Condition: real clients and paid or agreed work. Standard: the log is complete, the decision uses numbers, and a panel can repeat your plan.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is the difference between a feature and a benefit?

Model answer: A feature is what a thing has; a benefit is what the reader gains.

Q2. What should a good brief state?

Model answer: Reader, goal, action wanted, tone, length, deadline and change rounds.

Q3. Why write a headline three ways?

Model answer: To pick the one that is clearest, not just clever.

Q4. What must be true of every claim in your copy?

Model answer: It can be proved and is not misleading.

Q5. What do commercial emails and texts need in Canada?

Model answer: Consent, a clear sender and a working way to unsubscribe.

Q6. How do you find your real hourly cost?

Model answer: Yearly costs plus needed pay, divided by hours you can really bill.

Q7. Why get permission before showing client work?

Model answer: The work belongs to the client unless the agreement says otherwise.

Q8. What should a simple agreement list?

Model answer: Scope, fees, rounds of changes, rights, payment date and privacy.

Q9. What do you check before delivering copy?

Model answer: Facts, names, prices, spelling, links and file formats.

Q10. What do you do when you do not know a rule?

Model answer: Check the current rule with the local authority and keep the answer in writing.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
