Computer Services

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small computer Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: software, data and digital services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know computers and the service job 6 hUnderstand

Computer services keep devices and networks working for people and small firms. Start by learning the parts and the words.

  • Name the parts: processor, memory, storage, screen, network card, power.
  • Describe the difference between hardware, software and a network.
  • Explain what a service ticket is and why every job gets one.
  • List words: backup, update, password, router, malware.
  • Compare a hard drive and a solid-state drive by speed and cost in plain words.
  • Explain what an operating system does.
  • Describe the path of a keystroke to the screen in four steps.
Worked example. A laptop is slow. The ticket says: user, device, problem, date, steps taken, result. A 500 GB disk was 98% full (490 GB used); after moving 185 GB of old files to an external drive, 305 GB are used, which is 61%.

Activity. Open a spare computer or use a parts chart. Label eight parts and say what each does.

Standard to meet. Task: name computer parts. Condition: from a real or pictured device. Standard: eight parts named with one job each.

Module 2 Find faults step by step 6 hApply

Good technicians do not guess. They ask, test one thing at a time and write down the result.

  • Apply a fault method: ask what changed, reproduce, test one thing, record.
  • Check the simple things first: power, cable, restart, free space, updates.
  • Compare two possible causes and test the cheaper one first.
  • Explain why you copy the user's files before you change anything.
  • Record the exact error message before changing anything.
  • Test with a second cable, port or user account to narrow the cause.
  • Decide when to stop and refer: burning smell, swollen battery or water damage.
Worked example. No internet on one laptop. Other devices work, so the router is fine. Wi-Fi was switched off on the laptop. Fixed in one minute; ticket updated. When two causes are possible, test the cheaper one first: a 5-minute cable swap before a 60-minute reinstall.

Activity. Fix three practice faults on a test machine and write the steps for each.

Standard to meet. Task: find a fault. Condition: practice machine. Standard: one change at a time with the result written after each.

Module 3 Follow the rules in Canada 10 hApply

Technicians handle private data, so laws about privacy, licences and electronic waste matter. Check with the local authority.

  • Explain that federal and provincial privacy laws limit how personal data is collected and used; check which applies to your clients.
  • Describe software licence rules: do not install unlicensed software.
  • Check local rules for electronic waste; many provinces have recycling programs.
  • Apply the rule that you do not look at private files unless the job needs it and the owner agrees.
  • Ask the provincial e-waste program where drives and batteries go and write the drop-off address.
  • Choose a drive-wipe method and get written approval before a drive leaves the client's hands.
  • Check which privacy law applies when you handle staff records: federal or provincial.
Worked example. Rules table row: Personal data | privacy regulator | collect only what the job needs | every job | signed consent kept. If a drive holds staff records, open only the files the job needs and note which ones.

Activity. Make a rules table for your province covering privacy, licensing and e-waste, with official pages.

Standard to meet. Task: map the rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.

Module 4 Set up and fix devices and networks 8 hEvaluate

Now do real set-up work to a written method.

  • Build a new device set-up checklist: updates, user accounts, backup, security, printer, Wi-Fi.
  • Apply a home or office network plan: router, strong Wi-Fi password, guest network.
  • Install and test backups, including one restore test.
  • Record every setting you change so you can undo it.
  • Compare wired and wireless connections for an office by speed and reliability.
  • Test the signal in three rooms and move the router to improve the weakest one.
  • Draw a network map showing devices and label the cables.
Worked example. Set-up checklist for a new office laptop: update, create user, turn on disk encryption, install approved software, set backup, test restore, give password rules to the user. Planned time: 30 + 10 + 20 + 20 + 10 = 90 minutes for updates, accounts, backup, restore test and handover.

Activity. Set up a test computer and a test Wi-Fi network from the checklist and do one backup restore.

Standard to meet. Task: set up a device. Condition: written checklist. Standard: every item ticked and a restore test passed.

Module 5 Security and data safety 8 hApply

Most problems come from weak sign-in, old software and phishing. Fix these first.

  • Apply strong passwords, a password manager and two-step sign-in.
  • Check for phishing: odd sender, rush, link that does not match.
  • Explain least access: each user gets only what they need.
  • Create a breach plan: who is told, what is switched off, when.
  • Apply automatic updates and check that they actually ran.
  • Restore one file from backup once a month to prove it works.
  • Teach three staff how to report a suspicious email.
Worked example. An email says the bank account is locked with a link. The sender address is odd and the link points elsewhere. The staff member reports it and does not click. In a quiz of 5 emails, 3 phishing and 2 real, finding 4 of 5 is 80%.

Activity. Run a 20-minute safety talk for three people and a phishing quiz of five examples.

Standard to meet. Task: improve client security. Condition: small office or home. Standard: two-step sign-in on key accounts and a written breach plan.

Module 6 Price your work and plan your time 6 hCreate

Technicians often under-price. Count travel, waiting and learning time.

  • Compute your hourly cost: tools, insurance, vehicle, software, unpaid time.
  • Compare fee types: per hour, per job, monthly support plan.
  • Build a day plan with travel and a buffer for jobs that run long.
  • Evaluate which jobs pay well and which waste time.
  • Calculate the hourly rate as yearly costs plus wanted income divided by billable hours.
  • Compare a monthly support plan with per-job pricing for a client who calls often.
  • Calculate unbilled time: travel, quotes and notes.
Worked example. Price 85 per hour, direct cost 10. Margin 75 per hour. If fixed costs are 1,200 a month, break-even is 1,200 / 75 = 16 hours. A 2-hour job with 1 unpaid hour of travel earns 2 x 75 = 150 over 3 hours, or 50 per hour. Teaching numbers only.

Activity. Build a cost sheet and a price list for five common jobs.

Standard to meet. Task: price jobs. Condition: all costs counted. Standard: break-even hours correct and shown with units.

Module 7 Serve clients and keep records 6 hEvaluate

Clients return when you explain clearly, keep promises and leave good notes.

  • Explain faults in plain words, without blame or jargon.
  • Write a job report: problem, work done, parts, cost, advice.
  • Apply a follow-up check three days after each job to confirm the fix still works.
  • Handle a complaint: listen, restate, fix or refund as agreed.
  • Write next-step advice in three lines the client can follow.
  • Keep a log of repeat faults per client to find a deeper cause.
  • Identify the repeat faults from the pilot jobs and write one lasting fix for each.
Worked example. Job report: Printer would not connect. Cause: new Wi-Fi password. Work: re-connected, tested a print. Advice: write the new password in the office safe. Cost: one hour. A follow-up call on day 3 confirms it still works.

Activity. Write five job reports from practice jobs and call a practice client for a follow-up.

Standard to meet. Task: finish a job properly. Condition: practice job. Standard: client can read the report and knows what to do next.

Module 8 Run the service for real 8 hCreate

Now run your service with real clients, support plans and a growth plan.

  • Run a 60-day pilot with at least five clients or a volunteer help desk.
  • Review weekly: jobs, time per job, repeats, client feedback.
  • Create a 12-month plan with training goals and tools budget.
  • Lead others: write the checklists so a helper can repeat your standard.
  • Calculate average time per job and hours paid at the end of the pilot.
  • Compare client feedback with the stop rule.
  • Create a checklist for a helper and test it on one job.
Worked example. Stop rule: if fewer than 20 paid hours in 60 days, stop and write lessons. Result: 34 hours x 75 = 2,550 in margin, minus 2,400 fixed costs for two months = 150, and two monthly support clients; add a second service.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and present your plan to three people.

Standard to meet. Task: run a computer service pilot. Condition: 60 days, real clients. Standard: results compared with the stop rule and privacy rules re-checked.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
