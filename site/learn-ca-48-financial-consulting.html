Financial Consulting

Run a pilot of financial Consulting in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small financial Consulting offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: finance and accounting. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know what a financial consultant does 6 hUnderstand

A financial consultant helps people or small firms understand money and make better choices. Learn the basics first.

  • Define income, expense, asset, debt and net worth.
  • Describe the difference between advice, education and selling a product.
  • Explain who a consultant can serve: households, small firms, community groups.
  • List words: budget, cash flow, interest, risk, return.
  • Calculate net worth as assets minus debts for a practice household.
  • Write the questions you ask in a first meeting: goals, time frame and worries.
  • Explain what you will not do, such as promise returns or pick products you are not licensed to sell.
Worked example. A café owner earns 9,000 a month and spends 8,400. Income minus expense is 600, which is 600 / 9,000 = about 7% of income. The consultant first shows this in one simple table.

Activity. Write your own one-month income and expense list and mark what is fixed and what changes.

Standard to meet. Task: define core money terms. Condition: using your own month. Standard: five terms used correctly in a one-page table.

Module 2 Read basic money records 6 hApply

You cannot advise without reading numbers. Start with the three basic reports.

  • Describe the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow list in simple words.
  • Apply ratios: profit margin, debt to assets, days of cash.
  • Check that records match bank statements.
  • Explain why cash and profit are not the same.
  • Calculate profit margin as profit divided by sales.
  • Calculate days of cash as cash divided by daily costs.
  • Compare two months of records and write what changed and why.
Worked example. A shop shows 2,000 profit on paper but 6,000 of customer bills are unpaid. Cash is 1,500 and costs are 2,700 a month, or 90 a day, so days of cash = 1,500 / 90 = about 17. The consultant shows the gap in a cash list.

Activity. Read one sample set of small-firm records and write five plain-word findings.

Standard to meet. Task: read basic records. Condition: a sample set. Standard: ratios correct and five findings written in plain words.

Module 3 Follow the rules in Canada 10 hApply

Giving money advice is regulated. What you may say or sell depends on your licence and province. Check with the regulator.

  • Explain that advising on investments, insurance or loans often needs a provincial licence; check the securities or insurance regulator.
  • Describe the difference between a title like adviser and a protected title; check the current rule.
  • Apply the duty to know your client and to avoid conflicts of interest.
  • Check anti-money-laundering and privacy rules that apply to your work.
  • Ask the provincial regulator which titles are protected and what registration they need; write the answer.
  • Identify what you may do without a licence, such as teach budgeting, and what needs one, such as recommend a specific investment.
  • Ask the regulator how long client records must be kept.
Worked example. Rules table row: Investment advice | provincial securities regulator | registration needed before advising | before first paid advice | registration proof kept. If registration takes 8 weeks, the first paid advice cannot be before week 8.

Activity. Make a rules table for your province: what you can do unlicensed, and what needs a licence.

Standard to meet. Task: map the rules. Condition: official regulator pages. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.

Module 4 Build a client plan 8 hEvaluate

A plan starts with the client's goals and facts, not with products.

  • Build a client fact sheet: goals, income, costs, debts, savings, time frame.
  • Apply a simple budget: needs, wants, savings; adjust to the client.
  • Compare options for a goal such as an emergency fund or debt payoff.
  • Write advice in plain words with the reason for each step.
  • Calculate months of emergency fund as savings divided by monthly costs.
  • Rank goals by deadline and importance with the client.
  • Check the plan against the client's real bank records after one month.
Worked example. Client has 6,000 in card debt at a high rate, monthly costs of 2,400 and no savings. First goal: one month of costs, 2,400. Saving 300 a month takes 2,400 / 300 = 8 months; extra payments to the highest-rate debt follow. Reasons are written.

Activity. Build a plan for a practice client using a fact sheet and a one-page action list.

Standard to meet. Task: build a plan. Condition: practice client. Standard: goals, facts, options and reasons all written in plain words.

Module 5 Work with risk and numbers 8 hApply

Every choice has risk. Show it clearly and test the bad case.

  • Explain interest, compound growth and inflation in plain words.
  • Compute loan payments and savings growth with a free calculator.
  • Evaluate risk: loss, delay, fraud, change in rates; give a bad-case number.
  • Check claims such as very high safe returns; treat them as warnings.
  • Calculate the interest cost of a loan: payment x number of payments minus the amount borrowed.
  • Check a sales pitch by asking who is paid, what the fees are and what can be lost.
  • Test a plan against price rises of 2% and 4% a year to see the change in buying power.
Worked example. Saving 200 a month for 5 years is 200 x 60 = 12,000 plus a little interest. A promise of doubling in a year means a 100% return, which is a warning sign.

Activity. Test one savings goal and one loan at two interest rates and write what changes.

Standard to meet. Task: test a money choice. Condition: two rate cases. Standard: figures correct and the bad case explained.

Module 6 Find clients and set your fee 6 hCreate

Clients pay for clear results and trust. Be open about how you are paid.

  • Build a one-page offer: who, problem, result, process, fee.
  • Compare fee types: hourly, fixed, monthly; state them before work starts.
  • Write an engagement letter with scope, fee, privacy and what you do not do.
  • Track the funnel: contacts, calls, proposals, signed clients.
  • Calculate the hours a case really takes, including prep and notes, and set the fee from them.
  • Write what you do when a client asks for advice outside your licence: refer to a licensed person.
  • Ask three small firms what money problem costs them the most.
Worked example. Fixed fee for a cash-flow review: 4 hours work plus 2 hours meeting is 6 hours. At 150 an hour that is 900. If the review really takes 8 hours, 900 / 8 = 112.50 an hour, so build prep time into the fee. Teaching figures only; check local prices.

Activity. Write an offer and engagement letter and book five discovery calls.

Standard to meet. Task: win a first client. Condition: four weeks. Standard: offer under one page and a signed letter with scope and fee.

Module 7 Protect client money and data 6 hEvaluate

Trust is your main asset. Never mix client money with yours.

  • Apply the rule: keep client records private and locked, with strong sign-in.
  • Check scams: fake invoices, urgent transfers, identity theft; teach clients to spot them.
  • Explain why you never take client money into your own account unless the law and licence allow it.
  • Create a record of advice given, with dates and reasons.
  • List who can open client files and how long files are kept.
  • Confirm identity before sharing any account details.
  • Check that backups of client files are locked and tested.
Worked example. A client gets an urgent email from a fake supplier. The consultant has a call-back rule: confirm any new bank details by phone with a known number. The call takes 5 minutes and prevents a loss of the whole payment.

Activity. Write a privacy note, a call-back rule and a risk register of eight rows.

Standard to meet. Task: protect a client. Condition: practice case. Standard: each risk has a control, an owner and a review date.

Module 8 Run your practice for real 8 hCreate

Now serve real clients within your licence and keep learning.

  • Run a 90-day pilot with two or three clients, or a community workshop.
  • Review each case: plan, result, client feedback, mistakes.
  • Create a 12-month plan with licensing, learning and fee goals.
  • Lead others: train a helper to prepare fact sheets and follow the checklist.
  • Calculate hours worked, fees earned and cost per client at the end of the pilot.
  • Compare client results with the goals in each plan.
  • Identify one thing to learn next and add it to the 12-month plan.
Worked example. Stop rule: if the practice costs more than it earns after 90 days, change the offer. Three clients at a 900 fixed fee give 2,700; costs of 1,100 leave 1,600. Result and decision are recorded with numbers.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and present your plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a consulting pilot. Condition: 90 days, within licence limits. Standard: results compared with the stop rule and rules re-checked with the regulator.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is net worth?

Model answer: Assets minus debts. (Covered in module 1: Know what a financial consultant does. To pass that module: Task: define core money terms. Condition: using your own month. Standard: five terms used correctly in a one-page table.)

Q2. Why are cash and profit different?

Model answer: Profit includes unpaid bills; cash is money actually in hand. (Covered in module 2: Read basic money records. To pass that module: Task: read basic records. Condition: a sample set. Standard: ratios correct and five findings written in plain words.)

Q3. Who regulates investment advice in Canada?

Model answer: Provincial securities regulators; check yours. (Covered in module 3: Follow the rules in Canada. To pass that module: Task: map the rules. Condition: official regulator pages. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.)

Q4. What goes in a client fact sheet?

Model answer: Goals, income, costs, debts, savings and time frame. (Covered in module 4: Build a client plan. To pass that module: Task: build a plan. Condition: practice client. Standard: goals, facts, options and reasons all written in plain words.)

Q5. Why test the bad case?

Model answer: To see if the plan still works when things go wrong. (Covered in module 5: Work with risk and numbers. To pass that module: Task: test a money choice. Condition: two rate cases. Standard: figures correct and the bad case explained.)

Q6. How can you spot a likely scam?

Model answer: Promises of very high safe returns or urgent transfer requests. (Covered in module 7: Protect client money and data. To pass that module: Task: protect a client. Condition: practice case. Standard: each risk has a control, an owner and a review date.)

Q7. What must an engagement letter include?

Model answer: Scope, fee, privacy and what is not included. (Covered in module 6: Find clients and set your fee. To pass that module: Task: win a first client. Condition: four weeks. Standard: offer under one page and a signed letter with scope and fee.)

Q8. What is a call-back rule?

Model answer: Confirm any new bank details by phone using a known number. (Covered in module 7: Protect client money and data. To pass that module: Task: protect a client. Condition: practice case. Standard: each risk has a control, an owner and a review date.)

Q9. Why not mix client money with yours?

Model answer: It risks loss, breaks trust and may break the law. (Covered in module 7: Protect client money and data. To pass that module: Task: protect a client. Condition: practice case. Standard: each risk has a control, an owner and a review date.)

Q10. What if you are unsure whether you need a licence?

Model answer: Ask the regulator and keep the answer in writing. (Covered in module 3: Follow the rules in Canada. To pass that module: Task: map the rules. Condition: official regulator pages. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.)

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
