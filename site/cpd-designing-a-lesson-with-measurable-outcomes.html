CPD · Teaching

Designing a lesson with measurable outcomes

Writing lesson plans where every outcome starts with an action verb and can be checked.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 FoundationAbout 3 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playTeaching

Writing lesson plans where every outcome starts with an action verb and can be checked.

  • Write three outcomes, given a topic, each starting with an action verb and including a measure.
  • Sequence activities, given the outcomes, so each activity links to one outcome.
  • Design a short check, given an outcome, that a learner can pass or not yet pass.
  • Review a peer's plan, given a checklist, with two strengths and one improvement.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write measurable lesson outcomes, align each activity and check to an outcome, and design a short check a learner can pass or not yet pass

Condition

Given a topic, a checklist and peer plans, in a 3-hour planning workshop

Standard

Write three outcomes that each start with a Bloom verb, match every activity and check to one outcome, and give a peer two strengths and one improvement

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Outcomes and Bloom verbs 30 min
  • Start each outcome with an action verb you can observe (a Bloom verb comes from a standard list of thinking verbs).
  • Replace know and understand with identify, list or calculate.
  • Add a measure such as 4 of 5.
Worked example. Weak: "Understand fractions." Strong: "Compare two fractions with unlike denominators using the common-denominator method, getting 4 of 5 right." Verb: compare; measure: 4 of 5.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "outcomes and Bloom verbs": write three outcomes, given a topic, each starting with an action verb and including a measure.

Standard to meet. Write three lesson outcomes for a given topic, each starting with a measurable Bloom action verb, and replace vague verbs such as know or understand.

Module 2 Alignment: outcome, activity, check 35 min
  • Each activity leads to one outcome.
  • Each check matches an outcome.
  • Spot an activity that leads nowhere.
Worked example. Plan: 3 outcomes, 3 activities, 3 checks. A fourth activity, a word search, matches 0 outcomes, so it is removed. Alignment: 3 of 3 pairs.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "alignment: outcome, activity, check": sequence activities, given the outcomes, so each activity links to one outcome.

Standard to meet. Match each activity and each check to one outcome in a plan, and spot an activity that does not lead to its outcome.

Module 3 Designing a 5-item check 30 min
  • Write 5 items for one outcome (a quick in-lesson check may use 3).
  • One correct answer per item.
  • Decide the pass line before teaching.
Worked example. 5-question exit slip on comparing fractions. Pass line: 4 of 5. A learner with 3 of 5 is "not yet" and is re-taught, not failed.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "designing a 5-item check": design a short check, given an outcome, that a learner can pass or not yet pass.

Standard to meet. Design a five-item check for one outcome that a learner can pass or not yet pass, with one correct answer per item.

Module 4 Timing and flow 25 min
  • Give each activity a time.
  • Order activities from easy to hard.
  • Leave time for the check.
Worked example. 60-minute lesson: starter 5, worked pairs 15, practice 25, exit check 10, wrap-up 5 = 60 minutes. Practice follows 3 worked pairs.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "timing and flow": review a peer's plan, given a checklist, with two strengths and one improvement.

Standard to meet. Plan the timing and flow of a lesson so that each activity fits the time available and leads on to the next.

Module 5 Practice: draft your lesson 30 min
  • Draft a lesson with two to four outcomes.
  • Add one activity and one check for each.
  • Read it against your checklist.
Worked example. Topic: states of matter, Year 7. 3 outcomes (describe, compare, classify), 3 activities, 3 checks of 3 items each = 9 check items.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "designing a lesson with measurable outcomes" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (write three outcomes; sequence activities; design a short check; review a peer's plan).

Standard to meet. Draft a lesson of your own with two to four outcomes, one aligned activity for each, and a check for each outcome.

Module 6 Peer feedback and assessment 10 min
  • Review a peer's plan with a checklist.
  • Give two strengths and one improvement.
  • Check your own plan too.
  • If few learners meet the outcome, re-teach and re-check.
Worked example. Peer feedback: strengths: clear verbs, timed plan. Improvement: outcome 3 has no check. After the fix, 3 outcomes, 3 checks.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "peer feedback and assessment": review a peer's plan, given a checklist, with two strengths and one improvement. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Review a peer's plan against a checklist, giving two strengths and one improvement, and show your own lesson meets the outcome standard.

Module 7 Second worked case 10 min
  • Use the Year 7 science case card.
  • Write three outcomes and one check each.
  • Check the verbs.
Worked example. States of matter: Outcome 1 "Describe three properties of a solid in 3 sentences." Check item: write the 3 sentences; pass if all 3 are correct. Three outcomes with one check item each give 3 items in total.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second worked case": review a peer's plan, given a checklist, with two strengths and one improvement. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Write three outcomes with Bloom verbs for states of matter and one check item for each, then confirm each is measurable.

Module 8 Portfolio and reflection 10 min
  • Keep your best lesson plan.
  • Note the verbs you used.
  • Plan one change for your next lesson.
Worked example. Portfolio: 1 plan, 3 outcomes, 3 verbs (compare, describe, classify). Reflection: "I will replace 'learn about' in my next plan."

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "portfolio and reflection": review a peer's plan, given a checklist, with two strengths and one improvement. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Reflect on your lesson plan and justify one change that would make an outcome easier to measure.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Why use a Bloom verb?

Model answer: It names an observable action you can measure. (Covered in module 1: Outcomes and Bloom verbs. To pass that module: Write three lesson outcomes for a given topic, each starting with a measurable Bloom action verb, and replace vague verbs such as know or understand.)

Is "know" measurable?

Model answer: No; use identify, list, calculate. (Covered in module 1: Outcomes and Bloom verbs. To pass that module: Write three lesson outcomes for a given topic, each starting with a measurable Bloom action verb, and replace vague verbs such as know or understand.)

What does aligned mean?

Model answer: Each activity and each check matches an outcome. (Covered in module 2: Alignment: outcome, activity, check. To pass that module: Match each activity and each check to one outcome in a plan, and spot an activity that does not lead to its outcome.)

How many items to check one outcome?

Model answer: Enough to show a pattern; 3 to 5 is a good start. (Covered in module 3: Designing a 5-item check. To pass that module: Design a five-item check for one outcome that a learner can pass or not yet pass, with one correct answer per item.)

Rewrite "learn about volcanoes" as an outcome.

Model answer: "Describe three causes of eruptions in 3 sentences using terms from the word list." (Covered in module 1: Outcomes and Bloom verbs. To pass that module: Write three lesson outcomes for a given topic, each starting with a measurable Bloom action verb, and replace vague verbs such as know or understand.)

Why only one verb per outcome?

Model answer: Keeps measurement clear. (Covered in module 1: Outcomes and Bloom verbs. To pass that module: Write three lesson outcomes for a given topic, each starting with a measurable Bloom action verb, and replace vague verbs such as know or understand.)

What is misalignment?

Model answer: An activity that does not lead to the outcome. (Covered in module 2: Alignment: outcome, activity, check. To pass that module: Match each activity and each check to one outcome in a plan, and spot an activity that does not lead to its outcome.)

How many outcomes per lesson?

Model answer: Usually 2 to 4. (Covered in module 5: Practice: draft your lesson. To pass that module: Draft a lesson of your own with two to four outcomes, one aligned activity for each, and a check for each outcome.)

What to do if few learners meet the outcome?

Model answer: Re-teach and re-check. (Covered in module 6: Peer feedback and assessment. To pass that module: Review a peer's plan against a checklist, giving two strengths and one improvement, and show your own lesson meets the outcome standard.)

How can you tell whether an outcome is measurable?

Model answer: Check that its verb names an action you can see and that a measure, such as 4 of 5, shows when it is met. (Covered in module 1: Outcomes and Bloom verbs. To pass that module: Write three lesson outcomes for a given topic, each starting with a measurable Bloom action verb, and replace vague verbs such as know or understand.)

marking

How the practice and assessment are marked

LevelMeaningMarks of 4
SecureMeets the standard in the outcome every time, no prompts4
MeetingMeets the standard with one small slip, corrected when prompted3
DevelopingMeets part of the standard; needs a re-attempt2
Not yetStandard not met1

Pass = average 3 or more, and no outcome at 1. You may re-attempt after feedback. The subject expert sets any stricter pass mark.

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What a lesson outcome is. Speaker note: An outcome says what a learner will be able to do by the end of a lesson. The learner can tell an outcome from an activity and says why teachers write them first.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Verbs that can be measured. Speaker note: The learner knows that verbs such as compare, list and calculate can be observed, while know and understand cannot. They sort sample outcomes into measurable and vague.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Link activity to outcome. Speaker note: The learner can match a simple activity and a simple check to each outcome and see when an activity does not lead to any outcome.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Design a measurable lesson. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner writes three outcomes with Bloom verbs, aligns each activity and check, designs a five-item check and reviews a peer's plan.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Plan across a unit. Speaker note: The specialist plans a sequence of lessons so outcomes build from one lesson to the next, with checks that show progress across the unit. Each lesson's check feeds the next lesson's plan, so gaps are caught early rather than at the end of the term.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Coach and moderate. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner reviews other teachers' plans, spots misalignment, and moderates how outcomes and pass lines are applied across a department. They give feedback in the strength, strength, improvement pattern and share good examples with the team.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Lead curriculum design. Speaker note: The super expert designs outcome frameworks for a subject or school, trains teachers in alignment, and tracks whether outcomes lead to measurable learner gains.

assessment and certificate

How you are checked

Each outcome is checked against its standard with a checklist or a short test. A digital CPD record shows the hours. Keep evidence in My page. Aligned with common CPD practice; not accredited. Learners check their own body's rules.
