executive MBA subject 15

Global Operations Management

Year 2, term B. 5.0 ECTS, about 125 hours, 6 parts. Part 1 is free.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task, condition and standard

Task

Redesign one process and its supply network to improve cost, speed and reliability.

Condition

Given process data, demand forecasts and supplier locations.

Standard

Bottleneck, capacity and lean measures are correct and the network choice is justified with costs.

Who it is for: Managers who run processes, plants, service centres or supply networks.

how this subject works

Free entry, voice and your learning style

Part 1 is free to try. Read it, listen to it and answer it before you decide. Join the programme, or this subject on its own, to open the other parts.

  • Listen. Every part has a Listen button that reads it aloud.
  • Speak or type. Answer each part by talking (press Speak) or by typing. Your answers stay on this device only.
  • Learn your way. Every part has a See, Hear, Do and Read-and-write route. Not sure which suits you? Take the eight-question learning-style check.

6 parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 Operations strategy 21 hUnderstand: explain

Operations compete on cost, quality, speed, reliability or flexibility. You cannot be best at all; choose what matters most to customers.

  • Order winners and order qualifiers
  • Trade-offs between cost and flexibility
  • Align operations with strategy

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Customers rank on-time delivery first. On-time is 85%; a competitor is at 95%. Raising it is worth more than a 2% price cut.

Activity: Name the order winner for your product and its current level.

Standard: Winner chosen with evidence and current level measured.

Learn your way

  • See. Sketch "operations strategy" as a picture: boxes, arrows and the key number.
  • Hear. Press Listen, or explain the worked example aloud to someone.
  • Do. Do the activity with your own work or a practice case.
  • Read and write. Write the idea in three lines in your own words below.

Your answer. Name the order winner for your product and its current level.

Part 2 Process design 21 hAnalyse: find the bottleneck

A process is only as fast as its slowest step, the bottleneck. Improve the bottleneck first.

  • Map each step and its time
  • Capacity = time available / time per unit
  • Bottleneck sets the output of the whole line

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Steps take 4, 6 and 3 minutes per unit. The 6-minute step is the bottleneck: 480 minutes a day / 6 = 80 units a day.

Activity: Map a process and find its bottleneck and daily capacity.

Standard: All step times shown and capacity correct.

Part 3 Lean process improvement 21 hApply: remove waste

Lean removes waste: waiting, extra movement, overproduction, defects, extra stock, extra processing and unused skills.

  • Walk the process and spot waste
  • Measure lead time before and after
  • Fix causes, not symptoms

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Lead time 10 days, of which actual work is 1 day. Value-added share = 10%. Removing two waiting stages cuts lead time to 6 days.

Activity: Find three wastes in a process and estimate the time saved.

Standard: Wastes named and before and after times shown.

Part 4 Capacity management 21 hApply: plan

Capacity must meet demand without too much idle cost. Use utilisation and a buffer for peaks.

  • Utilisation = output / capacity
  • Plan for peaks with overtime, stock or partners
  • Very high utilisation causes queues

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Capacity 1,000 a week, demand 920: utilisation 92%. Peak demand 1,100 needs 100 extra units from overtime or stock built earlier.

Activity: Plan capacity for a 12-week demand forecast with one peak.

Standard: Utilisation correct and the peak covered.

Part 5 Supply network design 21 hEvaluate: choose locations

Where to make, store and buy affects cost, speed and risk. Compare total landed cost, not just the purchase price.

  • Landed cost = price + freight + duty + holding
  • Near suppliers are faster and often less risky
  • Dual sourcing reduces disruption

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Far supplier: price 10, freight 2, duty 1, holding 1 = 14. Near supplier: price 12, freight 0.5, duty 0, holding 0.3 = 12.8. The near supplier is cheaper overall.

Activity: Compare landed cost for two suppliers.

Standard: Both totals correct and the choice includes risk.

Part 6 Operations integrative case 21 hCreate: recommend

Combine strategy, process, lean, capacity and network into one plan for the case firm.

  • Answer first
  • Figures from earlier chapters
  • Implementation steps with owners

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. "Fix the 6-minute step, cut lead time to 6 days, move to the near supplier at 12.8 landed cost."

Activity: Write a two-page operations plan.

Standard: Plan is consistent and all figures are correct.

ten checks

Check yourself

Say or write your answer first, then read the model answer.

  1. Q1. Name five things operations can compete on.
    Model answer: Cost, quality, speed, reliability, flexibility. (Covered in part 1: Operations strategy.)
  2. Q2. What is an order winner?
    Model answer: The factor that makes customers choose you. (Covered in part 1: Operations strategy.)
  3. Q3. Steps 4, 6 and 3 minutes. Bottleneck?
    Model answer: The 6-minute step. (Covered in part 2: Process design.)
  4. Q4. 480 minutes / 6 minutes per unit. Capacity?
    Model answer: 80 units a day. (Covered in part 2: Process design.)
  5. Q5. Lead time 10 days with 1 day of work. Value-added share?
    Model answer: 10%. (Covered in part 3: Lean process improvement.)
  6. Q6. Name three kinds of waste.
    Model answer: Waiting, movement, overproduction, defects, stock, processing or unused skills. (Covered in part 3: Lean process improvement.)
  7. Q7. Output 920, capacity 1,000. Utilisation?
    Model answer: 92%. (Covered in part 4: Capacity management.)
  8. Q8. Why avoid utilisation near 100%?
    Model answer: Queues and delays grow fast. (Covered in part 4: Capacity management.)
  9. Q9. Price 12, freight 0.5, duty 0, holding 0.3. Landed cost?
    Model answer: 12.8. (Covered in part 5: Supply network design.)
  10. Q10. Why use dual sourcing?
    Model answer: To reduce the impact of disruption. (Covered in part 5: Supply network design.)
