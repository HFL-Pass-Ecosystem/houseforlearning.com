Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, start and run microgreens farming in São Paulo: serve a restaurant or home cook, deliver a tray of young salad greens, keep records, and show a working month of numbers.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. Own phone or computer, real customers or a real practice site, a peer and a mentor for the final review. Local rules and licences checked against official sources.

Standard

Every module standard is met; all claims are true and backed by records; peers and a mentor mark the evidence 'Meets' or better.

Who it is for: students, graduates, career changers, owners, workers and returners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, tracker, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know your customer and market 4 hAnalyse

Every sound business in microgreens farming begins with one clear customer and one clear problem. In São Paulo and the wider state, demand is real but crowded, so a small, well-described customer group wins before a broad one.

  • In microgreens farming: Chefs buy by freshness and consistency; ask for weekly volumes and delivery times.
  • Write the customer in one sentence: a restaurant or home cook. Add the place, the moment of need and what they use today.
  • List five rivals or substitutes within reach. For each, record price, strength, weakness and how people reach it.
  • Interview at least eight real customers with open questions. Record exact words, not your summary.
  • Count the market in units: how many trays could one area buy in a month? Use two public sources and your own count, then take the lower figure.
  • Choose one first segment and say why the others wait.
Worked example. Weak: 'Everyone needs a tray of young salad greens.' Strong: 'Our first customer is a restaurant or home cook within 8 km of the centre, who today pays R$ 20 per tray and complains about delay. We target 420 trays a month by month three.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: ten customers are interviewed for microgreens farming. Seven say price, two say speed, one says trust. Question: which first message do you write, and which interview record backs it? Model answer: lead with the tray price and speed promise, keep trust proof as the second line, and cite the seven price answers by date.

Activity. Write the one-sentence customer, a five-row rival table and eight interview records. Share them with a peer for a challenge round.

Standard to meet. Customer is specific; rival table has prices; every claim traces to an interview or a source.

Module 2 Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil 4 hUnderstand

Brazil asks every trader to be visible and accountable. Know which register, tax regime and sector rules apply before the first sale, and check each against the current official text.

  • In microgreens farming: Small producers register the business and follow food safety guidance for fresh produce; keep a water source test.
  • Choose a legal form: micro-entrepreneur (MEI) only if your activity appears on the official MEI list, or a company registered with a CNPJ. Regulated activities need their sector authorisation first. Check the current limits and lists on the official portal.
  • Learn the tax regime that fits your size and write down which taxes you will collect and pay, and when.
  • Find the sector rules for microgreens farming: licences, inspections, professional registers. List each with the office that issues it and the renewal date.
  • Personal data of customers falls under Brazil's data protection law (LGPD). Collect only what you need, say why, keep it safe, and name who answers questions.
  • Consumer law gives buyers clear rights on information, returns and complaints. Put your terms in plain Portuguese.
Worked example. Checklist for a new microgreens farming trader: 1) legal form (CNPJ, or MEI only if your activity is on the official list); 2) city operating licence; 3) sector licence or register; 4) tax set-up; 5) privacy notice; 6) written terms. Each has an owner and a date.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a neighbour says registration can wait until the first R$ 90 of sales. Question: what do you do? Model answer: do not delay; write the compliance table first, confirm the register, tax and sector licence with the official office, and start only where the table shows no open blocker.

Activity. Build your own six-line compliance table for your first location. Mark what is done, what is pending and who confirms it.

Standard to meet. Every line cites an official source; nothing is assumed; pending items have dates. Local law always wins over this course.

Module 3 Design the offer 4 hCreate

An offer states what the customer gets, how well, by when and at what price. For a tray of young salad greens, the offer must make the quality visible and the next step easy.

  • In microgreens farming: Offer a weekly standing order of defined tray sizes, three varieties, and a sample.
  • Describe the core offer in three lines: what is delivered (a tray of young salad greens), the standard it meets, and the time it takes.
  • Add one proof for each promise: a sample, a test result, a guarantee or a customer record.
  • Price from cost upward and from customer value downward, then pick a price inside both.
  • Write a simple one-page terms sheet: what is included, what is extra, how to cancel.
  • Create two options at most, so choice stays easy.
Worked example. Offer: 'A tray of young salad greens, delivered within agreed time, checked against a written standard, price R$ 18 per tray. Proof: a dated record the customer can check and sign. Changes or cancellation by written agreement before work begins.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a customer asks for a discount from R$ 18 to R$ 16 per tray. Question: decide with numbers. Model answer: contribution falls from R$ 11 to R$ 9 per tray; offer an extra of value (for example faster service) before cutting price, and accept only if the lower contribution still clears break-even.

Longer notes. Pick two or three crops, such as radish, sunflower and pea, and grow a test tray of each to learn days to harvest, yield and flavour. Prepare shallow trays, clean growing mix or mats, food-safe water and seed sold for sprouting. Write a weekly sowing calendar so a harvest is ready on every delivery day. Cut, weigh and pack 50-gram boxes, label them with the harvest date and keep them cold. List possible buyers: restaurants, market stalls and households. Calculate the cost per tray from seed, mix, packaging and water. Ask the local health office whether selling packed greens directly needs a registration, and what the label must say.

Extra worked example. A restaurant buys 40 trays a month at R$ 18 per tray, a worked-example order, so the income is 40 x R$ 18 = R$ 720. Seed, mix and packaging cost R$ 7 per tray, or R$ 280, which leaves R$ 440, or R$ 11 per tray (61,1%). Against R$ 3.500 of fixed cost a month for room, light and electricity, break-even is R$ 3.500 / R$ 11 = 318,2, so 319 trays. You would need about eight such restaurants, since 319 / 40 is roughly 8.

Activity. Write the offer card, the proof list and the terms sheet. Test them with three customers and note every point where they hesitated.

Standard to meet. Promise, proof and price are consistent; terms fit one page; three customer reactions are recorded.

Module 4 Run the work step by step 5 hApply

Good operations make results repeatable. For microgreens farming, write each step so a new person could follow it on their first day, and measure each step.

  • In microgreens farming: Cycle: soak, sow, blackout, light, water, harvest in 7 to 14 days; record each tray.
  • Map the process from first contact to paid and closed. Typical stages: enquiry, quote, preparation, delivery of the tray, check, payment, follow-up.
  • Name the key resource at each stage: a clean rack room with light and water. Note what happens if it is missing.
  • Write a one-page standard procedure per stage: who, what, tool, time, record.
  • Choose three measures: speed, quality (errors per 100) and customer rating. Review them every week.
  • Fix the cause of a defect, not the symptom. Ask 'why' five times and record the answer.
Worked example. Stage record: 'Prepare. Owner: lead. Time: 30 min. Tool: checklist. Record: signed sheet. Defect found on 1 of 175 trays last week; cause: missing check on a clean rack room with light and water; fix: add check at start, review next Friday.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: of 700 trays last month, 42 had a defect traced to a clean rack room with light and water. Question: what is the defect rate and what is your first action? Model answer: about 6 defects per 100; stop the cause (add a check at the start), correct affected customers, log the cause, and re-measure next week.

Activity. Draw the process map and write procedures for the two riskiest stages. Run each once and time it.

Standard to meet. Procedures are followable by a stranger; measures have weekly numbers; one defect is traced to a root cause.

Module 5 Safety, quality and risk 4 hEvaluate

The most serious risk in this field is mould or poor seed. A calm, written risk routine protects people first and the business second.

  • In microgreens farming: Mould and contaminated water are main risks; use clean seed, airflow and sanitised trays.
  • List ten things that could go wrong. Score each for how likely (1 to 5) and how serious (1 to 5). Multiply and sort.
  • For the top five, choose a control: remove the hazard, replace it, guard it, train people, or use protective gear, in that order of preference.
  • Write who does what in an emergency, and rehearse it once a quarter.
  • Keep an incident and near-miss log. Near-misses are free lessons.
  • Check that insurance and legal duties match the risk you actually carry.
Worked example. Risk row: 'Hazard: mould or poor seed. Likelihood 3, severity 5, score 15. Control: written check before each tray, trained staff, log sheet. Owner: site lead. Review monthly.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a near-miss occurs: mould or poor seed nearly happened but no one was hurt. Question: what do you do in the next 24 hours? Model answer: make safe, record the event, ask the five whys, add or tighten one control, brief the team, and set a review date; near-misses count as lessons, not blame.

Activity. Build a ten-row risk register and a one-page emergency plan. Run a ten-minute drill with a peer and note two improvements.

Standard to meet. Top risks have controls ranked by preference; drill done; log started. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.

Module 6 People, skills and service 4 hApply

People deliver the promise. Define roles, train to a standard and give feedback in the open.

  • In microgreens farming: Train staff on hygiene and a harvest quality check.
  • Write a role card for each person: purpose, five tasks, the standard for each, and who to ask for help.
  • Train by showing, doing with support, then doing alone with a check. Record sign-off per task.
  • Set service rules for answering customers: reply times, tone, how to apologise and fix.
  • Hold a ten-minute team check each day: yesterday's problem, today's plan, one risk.
  • Build a growth path: what a new person can do in week 1, month 3 and month 12.
Worked example. Training record: 'Task: handle a complaint. Shown 2 May; done with support 4 May; done alone 8 May, checked: apology, fix, follow-up call. Signed by lead.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a new hire completes training but a customer complaint shows a skipped step. Question: whose gap is it? Model answer: first the system: check the role card, the training sign-off and the checklist; then coach the person and fix the document so the step cannot be skipped.

Longer notes. Plan the daily rhythm of helpers: soak seeds, sow, mist, move trays to light, harvest, pack and wash. Write each step on a card with photos and the number of trays. Teach hygiene first: wash hands, clean scissors, sanitise benches and keep animals out. Show how to tell mould from root hairs and discard a tray when unsure. Ask helpers to write temperature and sowing date on a clipboard. Split tasks into morning and afternoon so harvest happens just before delivery. Test understanding by having each helper run a full tray cycle while you watch. Pay for the training hours.

Extra worked example. A part-time helper works 100 hours a month at R$ 20 per hour, both worked-example figures, costing R$ 2.000. Fixed cost rises from R$ 3.500 to R$ 5.500, and break-even moves to R$ 5.500 / R$ 11 = 500 trays. At 700 trays, profit falls from 700 x R$ 11 - R$ 3.500 = R$ 4.200 to R$ 2.200. The helper pays off only if you can sell 182 more trays a month, since R$ 2.000 / R$ 11 = 181,8.

Activity. Write two role cards and a training plan for one new hire. Role-play a hard customer conversation twice and review.

Standard to meet. Role cards have measurable standards; training shows sign-off steps; service rules are written in plain words.

Module 7 Money: cost, price and break-even 5 hAnalyse

A business lives or dies on a few numbers. Know your contribution per unit, your fixed costs and the volume you need.

  • In microgreens farming: Compute cost per tray including seed, medium, power and labour; check yield per rack.
  • Contribution per tray = price minus variable cost. Fixed costs are paid whether you sell or not.
  • Break-even units = fixed cost divided by contribution. Plan to pass it by a safe margin.
  • Make a 12-month cash plan. Cash timing matters as much as profit: customers may pay late, suppliers may want money first.
  • Keep money for business and money for the owner apart.
  • Test the plan: what if sales fall by a quarter, or cost rises by a tenth?
Worked example. Worked example, all figures in reais: price R$ 18, variable cost R$ 7, contribution R$ 11 per tray. Fixed cost R$ 3.500 a month. Break-even = R$ 3.500 / R$ 11 = 318,18, so 319 trays a month. At 700 trays a month, profit = R$ 11 x 700 - R$ 3.500 = R$ 4.200. These are teaching figures that belong to this example only; later examples and the case study use their own figures. Use your own.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: sales fall to 525 trays a month. Question: what is the monthly result? Model answer: R$ 11 x 525 - R$ 3.500 = R$ 2.275; compare with break-even of 319 trays and decide whether to cut fixed cost or raise volume first. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build your own table: price, variable cost, contribution, fixed cost, break-even and a downside case at 75% of sales. Show it to a peer or mentor.

Standard to meet. Arithmetic is correct; every figure has a source or is marked as an assumption; a downside case is shown.

Module 8 Grow, measure and renew 4 hEvaluate

Growth should follow proof. Measure, learn, then add; and keep returning to the customer.

  • In microgreens farming: Grow by adding shelves before adding rooms.
  • Pick one growth lever at a time: new customers, a higher repeat rate, a better price, or a new offer.
  • Set one measurable target for 90 days, such as 'raise repeat customers from 20% to 30%'.
  • Ask each customer how they found you, and record it. Spend more where the record shows results.
  • Review monthly: numbers, defects, complaints, cash. Write three decisions each time.
  • Plan the renewal of licences, equipment and skills a year ahead.
Worked example. 90-day plan: 'Lever: repeat customers. Action: follow-up call after every tray. Target: repeat rate from 20% to 30%. Check: weekly count. Review date: day 45 and day 90.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: you can add 140 trays a month by one of two levers: a follow-up call to past customers or a price rise of R$ 2 to R$ 20. Question: how do you choose? Model answer: test the cheaper reversible lever first (the call), measure for 30 days, and only then try the price change, so you can tell which one worked.

Longer notes. Compare crops and customers by margin per tray, not by sales alone. Record weekly trays sown, trays discarded and boxes sold, then calculate waste as a share of trays. Test a larger shelf only after two months of low waste. Rate each buyer by orders per week and payment time, and drop those who return many boxes. Trial a new crop on two trays before adding it. Plan an extra light and shelf from saved margin and work out how many weeks it needs to pay for itself. Keep a sheet of fridge temperature and delivery times.

Extra worked example. In this worked example you sow 700 trays and discard 35, so waste is 5%. A new shelf costs R$ 2.200 and holds 60 extra trays a month at a margin of R$ 11 each, adding 60 x R$ 11 = R$ 660 a month; the shelf price and the extra trays are invented numbers. The shelf pays for itself in R$ 2.200 / R$ 660 = 3,3, so 4 months, if every tray sells. Treat this as a test, since sales can fall.

Activity. Write a 90-day plan with one target, three actions and a review date. Present it in five minutes and take questions.

Standard to meet. Target is measurable and dated; actions are small enough to start this week; review date is in the calendar.

case study

A case to work through

Situation. A couple in a flat in Itaquera grows microgreens on shelves in a spare room and sells whole trays to three restaurants and a market stall. They sell about 700 trays a month at R$ 18 each. Seed, mix and packaging cost R$ 7 per tray sown, and room share, light, water and electricity cost R$ 3.500 a month. Last month they sowed 790 trays: 90 grew mould because the room was too humid, and one restaurant cancelled its 40 trays a month because the product looked dull. The mould count and the cancelled order are worked-example figures, not market data. The couple does not write down temperature, humidity or sowing dates, so they cannot find the cause. They want to protect their margin and deliver the same quality every week without buying an expensive machine.

Task. Say what you would do first, next and last, and what record you would keep.

Model answer. First, compute the sales: 700 x R$ 18 = R$ 12.600. Next, the variable cost on 790 sown trays is 790 x R$ 7 = R$ 5.530, and with fixed cost R$ 3.500 the profit is R$ 3.570. Without the mould, 700 sown trays would cost R$ 4.900 and profit would be R$ 4.200, so mould cost R$ 630. Mould was 11,4% of trays sown. The lost restaurant order cost 40 x R$ 11 = R$ 440 of margin a month. Each tray leaves R$ 11, so break-even is 319 trays. Then buy a cheap thermometer and humidity reader, and write sowing date, temperature, humidity and tray result in a daily sheet. Test one change at a time, such as a small fan, for two weeks, then recompute the mould rate. If mould then falls below 5%, which is fewer than 40 of 790 trays, the fix is working; the sheet will show which humid mornings explain any that still fail.

levels L1 to L7

Where this sits on the ladder

Seven levels, one slide each. Read the slide, then read the speaker notes under it. Credits stack and you can step in and out; this course is L4.

Slide 1 of 7: L1 Basic - Microgreens farming

  • Name common microgreens and their parts
  • Recall clean handling rules
  • Describe a growth cycle in simple steps
Notes for slide 1

Learners handle real trays of radish, sunflower and pea shoots, naming roots, stems, seed leaves and true leaves. They wash hands, sanitise tools and keep raw seed away from the finished product. They describe a cycle: soak, sow, blackout, light, water, cut. Hazards are mould and contamination. Evidence is a labelled photo sheet and a hygiene checklist; the routine must be clean and repeatable.

Slide 2 of 7: L2 Awareness - Microgreens farming

  • Explain light, water and airflow needs
  • Compare growing media and trays
  • Identify buyers such as restaurants and markets
Notes for slide 2

Learners explain why seedlings need even light, gentle bottom watering and moving air, and compare coco coir, hemp mat and soil trays. They visit or call a restaurant kitchen and a market stall to learn what each buyer wants in freshness and packaging. Hazard: damp, stagnant rooms. Evidence is a comparison of three media with photos; observations are written down, not guessed.

Slide 3 of 7: L3 Foundation - Microgreens farming

  • Sow, water and harvest a test tray
  • Record cycle days and yield
  • List the food safety and business rules to check
Notes for slide 3

Foundation work is one test tray grown and recorded: sow date, blackout days, harvest day, weight cut, and a note on smell and colour. The learner calculates yield per tray and lists food safety and business registration rules to check on official sites. Hazard: skipping notes. Evidence is a growth log with photos at day 3, day 6 and harvest; another grower could repeat it.

Slide 4 of 7: L4 Practitioner - Microgreens farming

  • Plan a microgreens business in Sao Paulo
  • Set up the room, supply, pricing and delivery
  • Run a month of sales and adjust
Notes for slide 4

A practitioner sets up a small room with shelves, lights and a sink, plans weekly sowing of several varieties and sets a price per tray or box. Delivery goes by bike or car in cold boxes. A month of sales is tracked on a sheet, and slow sellers are dropped. Hazards: overproduction and late deliveries. Evidence is a monthly sales sheet and an adjustment note.

Slide 5 of 7: L5 Specialist - Microgreens farming

  • Control mould, waste and quality
  • Design a weekly production calendar
  • Win and keep restaurant clients
Notes for slide 5

Specialists control mould with airflow, seed rinse and tray spacing, set a weekly calendar that staggers sowing so harvest happens daily, and track waste in grams. They pitch restaurants with samples and a one-page sheet, then follow up on feedback and consistency. Hazard: one bad batch losing a chef. Evidence is a waste log, a production calendar and a client file.

Slide 6 of 7: L6 Advanced - Microgreens farming

  • Scale to several rooms or sites
  • Model cost per tray and margin
  • Train staff and audit hygiene
Notes for slide 6

Scaling means a second room or partner site, hiring and training growers, and costing each tray on seed, mats, labour and electricity. Managers audit hygiene with a weekly checklist and visual checks, and record temperatures and corrective actions. Hazards: power failure and pest outbreaks. Evidence is a cost-per-tray sheet and an audit log; every deviation is corrected and dated.

Slide 7 of 7: L7 Super expert - Microgreens farming

  • Set farm strategy and product range
  • Mentor growers and managers
  • Advise on new crops and sales channels
Notes for slide 7

Farm leaders choose the product range, such as mustard, amaranth or edible flowers, and the channels, whether restaurants, subscriptions or grocery shelves. They coach growers and managers by reviewing batch records together. Hazard: spreading too thin. Evidence is a strategy paper, a small trial plan for a new crop and mentoring notes; each new line is tested small first.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, taxes, data duties and customer rights. This course is written for São Paulo. Check the current official text for your own place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registration: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registration: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registration: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registration: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registration: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registration: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registration: registration with ACRA. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia
