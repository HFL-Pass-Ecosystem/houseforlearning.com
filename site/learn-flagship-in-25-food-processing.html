flagship course

Food Processing and Agri-Tech

From harvest to packed product: hygiene, preservation, quality, cold chain, labelling and a small processing unit plan.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 35 h lessons plus practice

From harvest to packed product: hygiene, preservation, quality, cold chain, labelling and a small processing unit plan.

  • Define food hazards and hygiene rules.
  • Apply preservation and packing steps.
  • Analyse yield and losses.
  • Evaluate quality against a food-safety plan.
  • Create a small unit plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a safe batch of a processed food and write the batch record and unit plan.

Condition

About 12 weeks part-time. A clean kitchen or training unit, scales, thermometer, a mentor.

Standard

Hygiene rules are followed, critical points are measured and recorded, and labels are accurate.

Who it is for: Farmers, food entrepreneurs, diploma students. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Hygiene and food hazards 4 hDefine
  • Biological, chemical and physical hazards.
  • Handwashing, clothing, clean surfaces.
  • Pest control and water quality.
Worked example. A cut finger covered with a blue bandage and glove; the worker is moved off raw handling.

Activity. Write a hygiene code for a unit.

Standard to meet. Code covers people, place, water and pests.

Module 2 Raw material and post-harvest 4 hApply
  • Sort, wash, grade.
  • Reduce bruising and heat.
  • Record supplier and date.
Worked example. Tomatoes graded into three bins; losses fall from 18% to 9%.

Activity. Grade a lot and record losses.

Standard to meet. Losses calculated.

Module 3 Preservation methods 5 hApply
  • Drying, pickling, canning, freezing.
  • Time and temperature rules.
  • Packing and sealing.
Worked example. Mango pulp heated to 85 C for 2 minutes then sealed hot.

Activity. Make one preserved product.

Standard to meet. Temperature and time recorded.

Module 4 Quality and critical control points 5 hEvaluate
  • Find points where hazards are controlled.
  • Set limits and checks.
  • Corrective actions.
Worked example. Cooking to 75 C is a critical point; below it the batch is re-heated.

Activity. Draw a flow chart and mark control points.

Standard to meet. Limits and checks are listed.

Module 5 Cold chain and storage 4 hApply
  • Temperature zones.
  • First in, first out.
  • Monitor with logs.
Worked example. Cold store at 4 C checked twice a day.

Activity. Make a storage log.

Standard to meet. Log shows readings.

Module 6 Packaging and labels 4 hCreate
  • Material choice.
  • Label content by local law.
  • Batch and date codes.
Worked example. Label with name, weight, date, batch, ingredients.

Activity. Design a label.

Standard to meet. Label is accurate.

Module 7 Costs and yield 4 hAnalyse
  • Yield from raw to pack.
  • Cost per kg.
  • Price with margin.
Worked example. 100 kg fruit gives 62 kg product.

Activity. Compute yield and cost.

Standard to meet. Numbers right.

Module 8 Capstone: unit plan and batch record 5 hCreate
  • Plan a unit.
  • Run a batch.
  • Record.
  • Present.
Worked example. A pickle unit plan with a batch record.

Activity. Present.

Standard to meet. Plan and record complete.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
HygieneMissing or unclearMeets the stated checkCode followed

RecordsGapsCompleteComplete and reviewed by a peer
Safety and ethicsOverlookedFollowedFollowed and taught to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name three hazard types.

Model answer: Biological, chemical, physical.

Q2. Why record the supplier?

Model answer: Traceability.

Q3. Name two preservation methods.

Model answer: Drying and canning.

Q4. What is a critical control point?

Model answer: A step where a hazard is controlled.

Q5. What is FIFO?

Model answer: First in, first out.

Q6. Why batch codes?

Model answer: To trace and recall.

Q7. What is yield?

Model answer: Output compared with input.

Q8. What proves safe food?

Model answer: Controlled steps with records.

Q9. Why test water?

Model answer: It touches food.

Q10. Why keep samples?

Model answer: For checking issues.

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know hygiene rules.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain preservation.
  3. L3 Foundation. Help in a unit.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Run a batch.
  5. L5 Specialist. Design units and audits.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead plants.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set sector standards.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
