flagship course

Food Processing and Agri-Tech

Take produce from harvest to a safe packed product with good hygiene, correct preservation, measured critical points, cold chain, accurate labels and a plan for a small processing unit.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 42 h lessons plus practice

Take produce from harvest to a safe packed product with good hygiene, correct preservation, measured critical points, cold chain, accurate labels and a plan for a small processing unit.

  • Define food hazards and hygiene rules.
  • Apply preservation, heat treatment and packing steps.
  • Analyse yield, losses and cost per kilogram.
  • Evaluate a batch against a food-safety plan.
  • Create a labelled product and a small unit plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a safe batch of a processed food such as mango pickle or fruit jam, keep a batch record, and write a small unit plan.

Condition

About 12 weeks part-time. A clean kitchen or training unit, weighing scale, thermometer, pH strips or meter, clean jars, a mentor.

Standard

Hygiene rules are followed, critical limits are measured and recorded for the batch, losses are calculated, and the label shows all required details correctly.

Who it is for: Farmers, food entrepreneurs, self-help group members, diploma students. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Hygiene and food hazards 4 hDefine
  • Name the hazards: biological such as germs, chemical such as cleaning liquid, physical such as hair or glass.
  • Wash hands with soap for 20 seconds before work and after the toilet, touching the face, or handling raw produce.
  • Cover hair, wear clean clothes, and cover cuts with a waterproof bandage and glove.
  • Use safe water, keep pests out and store waste in covered bins away from food.
Worked example. A worker has a cut finger. It is covered with a blue bandage and a glove, and the worker is moved off raw handling. Water is tested and chlorinated to 0.2 to 0.5 parts per million.

Activity. Write a hygiene code for a small unit.

Standard to meet. Code covers people, place, water and pests with a check for each.

Module 2 Select and prepare raw material 5 hApply
  • Check produce for ripeness, damage, mould and pesticide waiting period.
  • Sort and grade by size and quality; remove spoilt pieces before washing.
  • Wash in running safe water, then peel or cut with clean tools.
  • Weigh before and after each step and record it.
Worked example. 100 kg of mangoes: 8 kg rejected at sorting, 22 kg lost to peel and stone. 70 kg of usable pulp remains, so usable yield is 70 percent.

Activity. Prepare 10 kg of raw produce and record weights at each step.

Standard to meet. Weights are recorded at each step and yield is calculated.

Module 3 Preservation by heat, drying and acid 6 hApply
  • Cook jam to 104 degrees Celsius, or until it reaches 65 percent sugar by a refractometer or the set test.
  • Use salt, oil and acid in pickles to stop growth of germs, with pH below 4.6.
  • Dry fruit or vegetables on clean trays at 55 to 60 degrees until crisp and moisture is low.
  • Keep boiling hot water, steam and open flames away from children and wear heat gloves.
Worked example. Jam from 5 kg pulp and 4 kg sugar is boiled to 104 degrees Celsius for 25 minutes. The final pH is 3.4, which is safe, and the hot fill temperature is 85 degrees.

Activity. Make one batch of jam or pickle and record time, temperature and pH.

Standard to meet. Critical limits are met and recorded at the time.

Module 4 Packing and sealing 5 hApply
  • Wash jars and lids and sterilise them in boiling water for 10 minutes.
  • Fill hot, leave 1 cm headspace, wipe the rim and seal at once.
  • Cool and check lids; reject any that bulge or leak.
  • Use food-grade packing and keep it covered until use.
Worked example. A batch of 40 jars of 250 g is filled at 85 degrees. Two leak at the lid and are removed, so 38 jars are passed.

Activity. Pack and seal one batch and inspect every jar.

Standard to meet. All sealed jars pass and rejects are recorded.

Module 5 Storage and cold chain 5 hApply
  • Store dry goods off the floor, away from walls, in a cool dry store with first in, first out.
  • Keep perishable items at 4 degrees Celsius or lower and frozen items at minus 18.
  • Check and log fridge temperature twice a day.
  • Transport in clean, covered vehicles with insulated boxes and ice packs.
Worked example. A cold room log shows 3 degrees in the morning and 9 degrees in the evening after a power cut. The stock is held and checked, and a generator is planned.

Activity. Set up a storage log and a stock rotation system.

Standard to meet. Logs are filled twice daily and oldest stock is used first.

Module 6 Quality and food safety plan 6 hEvaluate
  • List each step and its hazard, and mark the critical ones such as cooking, cooling, sealing and metal detection.
  • Set a limit, a check, a record and a correction for each critical step.
  • Take a sample of each batch for taste, colour, pH and seal checks.
  • Write a recall plan: batch number, buyers and how to stop sales.
Worked example. Critical step: cooking to 104 degrees. Limit: at least 104. Check: thermometer each batch. Correction: continue boiling. Record: batch sheet signed.

Activity. Write a safety plan for your product.

Standard to meet. Every critical step has limit, check, record and correction.

Module 7 Labelling, cost and yield 5 hAnalyse
  • Print on every label the name, ingredients, net weight, batch number, date of making, best before date and the maker name and address.
  • Show the licence number issued under the food law as required.
  • Work out yield as finished weight divided by raw weight.
  • Work out cost per jar from raw material, fuel, packing, labour and overhead.
Worked example. Cost of 38 jars: produce Rs 3,800, sugar Rs 1,800, packing Rs 760, fuel Rs 400, labour Rs 1,200 total Rs 7,960. Cost per jar is Rs 209, sold at Rs 280 for a margin of Rs 71.

Activity. Design a label and calculate cost per jar.

Standard to meet. Label has all details and the cost sheet adds up.

Module 8 Capstone: batch and unit plan 6 hCreate
  • Produce one batch with hygiene checks and a batch record.
  • Calculate yield, losses and cost.
  • Write a unit plan: space layout, equipment, licences, staff and monthly sales target.
  • Present the batch, label and plan to a mentor.
Worked example. A unit of 20 square metres making 100 jars a week with a start-up cost of Rs 1,20,000 and a monthly profit of Rs 12,000.

Activity. Present the batch pack and unit plan.

Standard to meet. Records are complete and the plan covers licences and costs.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name three hazard types.

Model answer: Biological, chemical and physical. (Covered in module 1: Hygiene and food hazards. To pass that module: Code covers people, place, water and pests with a check for each.)

Q2. Why weigh at every step?

Model answer: To find where losses occur. (Covered in module 2: Select and prepare raw material. To pass that module: Weights are recorded at each step and yield is calculated.)

Q3. Why measure pH in pickles?

Model answer: Below 4.6 stops harmful germs from growing. (Covered in module 3: Preservation by heat, drying and acid. To pass that module: Critical limits are met and recorded at the time.)

Q4. Why leave headspace?

Model answer: It allows expansion and helps a good seal. (Covered in module 4: Packing and sealing. To pass that module: All sealed jars pass and rejects are recorded.)

Q5. What does first in, first out mean?

Model answer: The oldest stock is used or sold first. (Covered in module 5: Storage and cold chain. To pass that module: Logs are filled twice daily and oldest stock is used first.)

Q6. What is a critical limit?

Model answer: A value that must be met to keep food safe. (Covered in module 6: Quality and food safety plan. To pass that module: Every critical step has limit, check, record and correction.)

Q7. Why print a batch number?

Model answer: So that a problem batch can be traced. (Covered in module 7: Labelling, cost and yield. To pass that module: Label has all details and the cost sheet adds up.)

Q8. What proves the batch was safe?

Model answer: The record of measured critical limits and passed checks. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: batch and unit plan. To pass that module: Records are complete and the plan covers licences and costs.)

Q9. Why not reuse old jars without washing?

Model answer: Residues can carry germs that spoil food or cause illness. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. What is shelf life?

Model answer: The time a food stays safe and good in stated storage conditions. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know hygiene rules and hazards.
  2. L2 Awareness. Prepare and preserve a small batch safely.
  3. L3 Foundation. Run batches with records and labels.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Operate a licensed small unit.
  5. L5 Specialist. Manage production, quality and a team.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead a processing business with traceability.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set standards for food processing and agri-tech training.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Hygiene and hazards

List 8 hygiene rules and 4 food hazards, and show correct handwashing and surface cleaning in under 2 minutes, with every step done.

Speaker notes. Teacher watches the handwash steps against a checklist. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Safe small batch

Prepare and preserve one batch of 10 jars safely using heat, drying or acid, with temperature and time written for each step.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the log shows the heating temperature and a clean sealed lid on each jar. Covered in module 3, Preservation by heat, drying and acid.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Batches with labels

Run 3 batches with a record sheet for each and a label showing name, date, ingredients and shelf life on every pack.

Speaker notes. Teacher reads 3 labels and checks every field is filled. Covered in module 7, Labelling, cost and yield.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Licensed small unit

Operate a small unit for 4 weeks following a written food safety plan with 5 control checks logged daily. Check local licence rules with an expert.

Speaker notes. Teacher inspects the daily log for gaps and checks storage temperatures. Covered in module 1, Hygiene and food hazards.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Production and quality lead

Manage a team of 4 over 20 production days, keeping rejects under 3 percent and every batch signed off by a quality check.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the reject count against total packs made.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Traceable processing business

Build a traceability system linking each pack to its raw material, so a test recall finds all affected packs within 1 hour.

Speaker notes. Teacher names a batch and times the learner finding every pack made from it.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Training standards

Write food processing and agri-tech training standards with a skills checklist, and train 5 workers until each passes a 20-point practical test.

Speaker notes. Teacher scores one practical test against the checklist. Check local rules with an expert.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
