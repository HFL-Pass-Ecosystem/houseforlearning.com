Grade 02: English

8 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L1Primary8 units36 teaching weeks36-week school yearBloom levels: Remember, Understand, Apply
  • Remember Read and spell words with common vowel pairs and blends.
  • Remember Read a short passage aloud and answer who, what, where questions.
  • Understand Tell common nouns from names that need capital letters.
  • Apply Use a full stop, comma and question mark correctly.

  • Apply Write four connected sentences about a picture.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 02 English, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 8 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

unit by unit

8 units with worked examples

Unit 1 Phonics set 2 and spelling patterns Weeks 1 to 5Remember

In this unit you work on 'Phonics set 2 and spelling patterns', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: read and spell words with common vowel pairs and blends. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Phonics set 2 and spelling patterns' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: read and spell words with common vowel pairs and blends, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 3, 7, 11, 15: add 4 each time, so next is 19. The nth term is 4n - 1; check n = 4: 15. Method: Model run for 'Phonics set 2 and spelling patterns'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Learn vowel pairs as sound teams. ai and ay say "ay": rain, tray. ee and ea say "ee": feet, leaf. oa says "oh": boat. Sort words by the sound, then check the spelling. Reason: a pair shares one sound.
  2. Step 2, Choose ai or ay by position. Use "ai" in the middle of a word (rain, paint) and "ay" at the end (play, day). Model: "We play in the rain." Reason: English words rarely end in "ai".
  3. Step 3, Read and spell blends. Blends: st, bl, cr, tr, sp. Word: "stamp" = s-t-a-m-p. Say the first two sounds quickly, then the vowel, then the end. Spell "crab": c-r-a-b.
  4. Step 4, Fix an error. A child spells "boat" as "bote" or "rain" as "rane". Correction: b-oa-t uses the vowel pair oa, and r-ai-n uses ai. Say the word, tap the sounds, and write each pair together as one sound team.
  5. Step 5, Spell with the "say, tap, write" method. Word: "green". Say it, tap g-r-ee-n (four sounds), and write it. Check: the ee pair is two letters for one sound. Cover and write it again.
  6. Step 6, Write a sentence using three pattern words. "The goat sat in the rain by the green tree." Pattern words: goat (oa), rain (ai), green (ee). Check spelling of each pair.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Spell these as you hear them: tray, leaf, coat. Model answer: t-r-ay, l-ea-f, c-oa-t. Then write one sentence: "I put my coat on the tray."

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Read and spell words with common vowel pairs and blends: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 2 Reading short passages Weeks 6 to 10Remember

In this unit you work on 'Reading short passages', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: read a short passage aloud and answer who, what, where questions. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Reading short passages' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: read a short passage aloud and answer who, what, where questions, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Passage: 'A boy has a red kite. He flies it on the hill.' Who? A boy. What? A red kite. Where? On the hill. Method: Model run for 'Reading short passages'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Read for sense. Passage: "A boy has a red kite. He flies it in the park on Sunday. His sister helps him." Read aloud smoothly, pausing at each full stop. Reason: stops give breath and meaning.
  2. Step 2, Answer a who question. "Who has a kite?" Answer: "The boy has a kite." Find the person in the text and give a complete sentence. Reason: who asks about a person.
  3. Step 3, Answer a what and where question. "What does the boy fly?" "He flies a red kite." "Where does he fly it?" "In the park." Reason: what asks about a thing; where asks for a place.
  4. Step 4, Use the text to check. Question: "When does the boy fly the kite?" Answer: "On Sunday." Point to the words in the passage. Reason: the answer must come from the text, not from a guess.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Question: "Who helps the boy?" Wrong answer: "The park." Right: "His sister helps him." A who question needs a person. Read the question word first before you answer.
  6. Step 6, Read with expression. A question ends with a rising voice: "Can you fly the kite?" An exclamation is stronger: "It is high!" Reason: punctuation tells you how to read.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Passage: "A girl has a cat. It sleeps on a mat by the door." Answer: Who has a cat? Where does it sleep? Model answer: "The girl has a cat." "It sleeps on a mat by the door."

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Read a short passage aloud and answer who, what, where questions: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 3 Nouns, common and proper Weeks 11 to 15Understand

In this unit you work on 'Nouns, common and proper', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: tell common nouns from names that need capital letters. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Nouns, common and proper' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: tell common nouns from names that need capital letters, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: In 'The girl visited Lahore', 'girl' is a common noun and 'Lahore' is a proper noun and needs a capital letter. Method: Model run for 'Nouns, common and proper'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Tell common from proper nouns. Common nouns name any one of a kind: girl, city, river. Proper nouns name a particular one: Lahore, Nile, Dubai. Proper nouns begin with capital letters. Reason: they are special names.
  2. Step 2, Sort a list. Words: boy, Lahore, school, Green Hill School, dog, Nile. Common: boy, school, dog. Proper: Lahore, Green Hill School, Nile. Reason: "dog" is any dog, "Nile" is one particular river.
  3. Step 3, Fix capital letters in a sentence. Wrong: "my friend lives in karachi." Right: "My friend lives in Karachi." Capitals: the first word and the city Karachi.
  4. Step 4, Capitalise days and months. Model: "On Monday in March we visit Cairo." Days, months and place names are proper nouns. But seasons are common: "We visit in spring."
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "We went to the Park with Our Dog." Right: "We went to the park with our dog." Only proper names get capitals.
  6. Step 6, Write a sentence with both. "The teacher gave a book to the girl in Karachi." Common: teacher, book, girl. Proper: Karachi. Reason: the name of one particular place is a proper noun.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Rewrite with correct capitals: "last tuesday a girl and her dog went to the river nile." Model answer: "Last Tuesday a girl and her dog went to the River Nile."

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Tell common nouns from names that need capital letters: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 4 Punctuation: full stop, comma, question mark Weeks 16 to 18Apply

In this unit you work on 'Punctuation: full stop, comma, question mark', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: use a full stop, comma and question mark correctly. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Punctuation: full stop, comma, question mark' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: use a full stop, comma and question mark correctly, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'what is your name' becomes 'What is your name?' - capital letter at the start and a question mark at the end. 'I have a pen a book and a bag' needs commas: 'I have a pen, a book and a bag.' Method: Model run for 'Punctuation: full stop, comma, question mark'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, A full stop ends a statement, which tells something. Write: I like to read. The full stop sits on the line after read. Check every statement you write for its full stop. Read aloud and drop your voice a little when you reach the dot.
  2. Step 2, A question mark ends a question, which asks something. Write: Where is my bag? Question sentences often start with who, what, where, when, why or do. Draw the mark carefully: a curl on top and a dot below. Do not use a full stop as well.
  3. Step 3, A comma separates things in a list. Write: I have a pen, a book and a ruler. Put a comma after pen, and use and before the last item, with no comma before and. Say the list aloud and take a small pause at each comma.
  4. Step 4, A comma also follows a word like Yes or No, or a name when you speak to someone. Write: Yes, I can swim. Write: Mum, can I play? The comma gives a short pause. Notice that the second one is a question, so it still ends with a question mark.
  5. Step 5, Use all three marks. Write: Do you like apples, pears or plums? The comma comes after apples and the question mark ends the sentence. Then write the answer: I like apples, but I do not like plums. Check each sentence for its capital letter and ending mark.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: add the capital letters and the punctuation to: can you see a cat a dog and a bird. Answer: Can you see a cat, a dog and a bird? It has a capital C, one comma after cat and a question mark.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Use a full stop, comma and question mark correctly: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 5 Verbs and describing words Weeks 19 to 23Apply

In this unit you work on 'Verbs and describing words', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: use verbs and adjectives to make a sentence richer. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Verbs and describing words' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: use verbs and adjectives to make a sentence richer, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'The girl runs.' Add describing words: 'The tall girl runs fast.' Tense: walk, walked, will walk. Method: Model run for 'Verbs and describing words'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Start with a plain sentence. "The dog barked." Add an adjective: "The big dog barked." Add a stronger verb: "The big dog howled." Reason: adjectives describe nouns and verbs show the action.
  2. Step 2, Choose strong verbs. Plain: "walk". Stronger: "stroll", "stride", "creep", depending on the meaning. Model: "The cat crept across the floor." Reason: a precise verb paints a clearer picture.
  3. Step 3, Choose adjectives that tell more than "nice". Instead of "a nice day", write "a bright, warm day". Use a comma between two adjectives of equal weight. Reason: precise adjectives give sensory details.
  4. Step 4, Add where and when. "Yesterday the big dog howled loudly at the gate." Added: when (yesterday), how (loudly), where (at the gate). Reason: each detail answers a question the reader may ask.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong: "The more big dog ran fast." Right: "The bigger dog ran fast." Another weak sentence: "The boy went." Better: "The tired boy trudged home."
  6. Step 6, Build a richer sentence from a simple one. Simple: "The bird sang." Richer: "The tiny red bird sang sweetly in the old tree." Underline verbs and adjectives to check you used both.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Make "The girl ran" richer with two adjectives and one stronger verb. Model answer: "The brave little girl raced down the dusty road."

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Use verbs and adjectives to make a sentence richer: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 6 Poems and rhymes Weeks 24 to 28Remember

In this unit you work on 'Poems and rhymes', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: read, recite and make a short rhyming poem. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Poems and rhymes' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: read, recite and make a short rhyming poem, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: cat, hat and mat rhyme. Make a couplet: 'The sun is high, a bird can fly.' High and fly rhyme. Method: Model run for 'Poems and rhymes'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Notice the rhyme. Poem lines (my own): "The cat sat down on the warm red mat, / and watched a small bird, plump and fat." Rhyming words: mat and fat. Reason: the end sounds match.
  2. Step 2, Read the poem with rhythm. Clap the beats: "The CAT sat DOWN on the WARM red MAT." Reason: reading with a steady beat shows the rhythm of the poem.
  3. Step 3, Recite from memory by learning one line at a time. Say line one three times, add line two, then say both. Use gestures. Reason: small steps and actions help the memory.
  4. Step 4, Find rhyming words for your poem. Pick "sun": run, fun, bun. Pick "light": night, bright, kite. Check that the end sounds match, even if the spelling differs ("blue" and "too").
  5. Step 5, Write two lines. "I see a bright and shining star, / It twinkles from so far." Check: "star" and "far" rhyme, and the lines have a similar length.
  6. Step 6, Fix an error. Wrong: "I like my dog, / he likes to play on the log, / and then he sleeps all day." The last line is long and has no rhyme. Right: "and then he curls up like a frog."
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Finish this couplet with a rhyming word and explain the rhyme. "I put on my boot / and ran to the..." Model answer: "fruit" (boot and fruit rhyme: both end with the "oot" sound).

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Read, recite and make a short rhyming poem: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 7 Picture composition Weeks 29 to 33Apply

In this unit you work on 'Picture composition', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: write four connected sentences about a picture. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Picture composition' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: write four connected sentences about a picture, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Picture of a boy with a kite. 'A boy has a kite. The kite is red. The wind is strong. He is happy.' Four connected sentences, each ending in a full stop. Method: Model run for 'Picture composition'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Look at the picture: a girl flies a red kite on a windy beach. Ask four questions: Who is in it? Where is it? What is happening? How do they feel? Write one or two words for each: girl, beach, flying a kite, happy. These notes become your sentences.
  2. Step 2, Sentence one introduces the picture, saying who and where. Write: A girl is on the beach. Start with a capital letter and end with a full stop. A good first sentence helps the reader to picture the scene straight away.
  3. Step 3, Sentence two says what is happening. Write: She is flying a red kite. Use an action word such as flying, running or jumping, and say which kite it is. Notice that She takes the place of A girl so we do not repeat words.
  4. Step 4, Sentence three adds a detail about weather or feelings. Write: The wind is strong and she is smiling. Then use a joining word to link your sentences: Then, Next, Soon or At last. Sentence four ends the story: At last, she holds the string and the kite flies high.
  5. Step 5, Check your four sentences. Count them: 1, 2, 3, 4. Does each start with a capital letter and end with a full stop? Do they tell about one picture in a good order? Read it aloud. The four sentences should sound like a tiny story.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: a boy with an umbrella stands in the rain. Write four connected sentences. Model answer: A boy is outside. It is raining hard. He holds a big yellow umbrella. Then he jumps in a puddle and laughs.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Write four connected sentences about a picture: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 8 Speaking and listening Weeks 34 to 36Understand

In this unit you work on 'Speaking and listening', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: retell, ask questions and respond politely. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Speaking and listening' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: retell, ask questions and respond politely, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Retell in order with first, next, then, last. Ask: 'What happened next?' Reply politely: 'Thank you, I agree because...' Method: Model run for 'Speaking and listening'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Retell a short story in order. Story: "A girl lost her ball. She asked her friend. Her friend found it under the bench." Retell: "First the girl lost her ball. Then she asked her friend. At last it was found under the bench."
  2. Step 2, Ask questions using who, what, where, why. "Who found the ball?" "Where did the friend find it?" "Why did the girl ask her friend?" Reason: question words show what kind of answer you need.
  3. Step 3, Give a polite response. "Thank you for telling me." "That is a good idea." "I did not know that." Reason: a polite reply shows you listened.
  4. Step 4, Listen then speak. Speaker: "I went to the market and bought a mango." Listener: "What colour was the mango?" Reason: a related question shows you were listening.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. Wrong response: "That is boring." Better: "I like the part about the market. Can you tell me more?" Another error: interrupting. Correction: wait until the speaker finishes, then speak.
  6. Step 6, Use a complete sentence and a polite voice. "Please can I ask a question?" "Yes, go ahead." Reason: "please" and "thank you" make a conversation friendly.
  7. Step 7, Your turn. Listen to: "I have a rabbit called Snow. It eats carrots." Ask one question and make one polite reply. Model answer: "What colour is Snow?" "That is a lovely name, thank you for telling me."

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Retell, ask questions and respond politely: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Letters, sounds and first reading (this course)

On the slide: L1 Basic · Letters, sounds and first reading

Notes: Match letters to sounds and read short words and simple sentences aloud. Notes: use the sounds s, a, t, p, i, n; learners blend them into sat, pin and tap, then read a three-word sentence and clap the syllables.

This course: Grade 02 English is at this level. Its units include Phonics set 2 and spelling patterns; Reading short passages; Nouns, common and proper; Punctuation: full stop, comma, question mark and more.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Sentences and simple writing

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Sentences and simple writing

Notes: Write a clear sentence with a capital letter and full stop, and read a short story with understanding. Notes: give a picture prompt; learners say the sentence first, write it, and check capital, spacing and full stop with a partner before reading it aloud.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Paragraphs, grammar and reading for meaning

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Paragraphs, grammar and reading for meaning

Notes: Write a paragraph of five connected sentences and identify nouns, verbs and adjectives in a text. Notes: colour nouns blue and verbs red in a short passage; learners then write about their street, using at least two adjectives and one linking word.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Essays, speaking and responding to texts

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Essays, speaking and responding to texts

Notes: Write a 300-word structured essay and give a short talk, quoting evidence from a text to support a view. Notes: model Point, Evidence, Explain; learners write one paragraph about a character, quote one line, and explain what it shows, then speak it aloud.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Analysis, argument and style

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Analysis, argument and style

Notes: Analyse a writer's choices of language and structure and write a sustained argument of 600 words. Notes: take two openings of different short stories; learners compare tone, sentence length and imagery in a table, then argue which opening works better and why.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Literary and linguistic study

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Literary and linguistic study

Notes: Evaluate texts through a critical lens, tracing how language changes across time and context in a researched essay. Notes: compare an older and a modern passage; learners list five differences in vocabulary and syntax and argue in 1,500 words what each reveals about its period.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Research, editing and authorship

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Research, editing and authorship

Notes: Produce an original piece of research or a polished long-form text, with a literature review and defended method. Notes: pick a narrow question about language in one genre; build a corpus of fifty samples, analyse patterns, write 5,000 words, and revise after external review.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

India (Bengaluru)

Use INR in money problems; deliver in English, Hindi, Kannada; personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Use AED in money problems; deliver in Arabic, English; personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Use BRL in money problems; deliver in Portuguese (Spanish and English support); personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Canada (Toronto)

Use CAD in money problems; deliver in English, French; personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

United States

Use USD in money problems; deliver in English, Spanish; personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Singapore

Use SGD in money problems; deliver in English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Australia

Use AUD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Use HKD in money problems; deliver in Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Use GEL or ILS in money problems; deliver in Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Use KYD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Data Protection Act; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Mexico

Use MXN in money problems; deliver in Spanish, English; personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; guardian contact for every learner under 18.
