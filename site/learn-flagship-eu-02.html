EU-02 · full course

Handmade Goods and Craft Enterprise

Turn a craft into a repeatable small business: batch making, honest pricing, product photos and a story that sells.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 150 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Turn a craft into a repeatable small business: batch making, honest pricing, product photos and a story that sells.

  • Understand Explain unit economics for handmade goods.
  • Apply Make and sell 20 units through a pilot channel.
  • Analyse Compare time, material cost and margin per unit.
  • Evaluate Judge which channel and price work best.
  • Create Produce product photos, a story and a customer-safe label.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Make and sell 20 units of one handmade product through a pilot channel and record time, materials and margin per unit.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3 to 6 months. Own tools and materials, a phone camera, free spreadsheet, a pilot channel (market stall, shop partner or online page), a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

20 units sold or placed; cost sheet reviewed; photos meet the checklist; product safety and consumer rules checked with the local authority; 70% on the check.

Check product-safety and consumer-goods rules for your product type and place (for example toys, cosmetics and candles have special rules). This course gives method only.

Requirements: None. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (150 h guided)

Module 1 Choose a product and a customer 6 hUnderstand

A product that a particular person wants beats a beautiful product nobody asked for.

  • List 5 things you can make well and 5 types of buyer.
  • Pick one pairing and write why this buyer would pay.
  • Look at 10 similar products: price, size, material, story.
  • Decide what is different: material, design, size, purpose, story.
Worked example. Maker of cotton pouch bags notes 10 market stalls sell plain pouches at EUR 8 to 12. Gap: pouches sized for reading glasses with a pocket, at EUR 14.

Activity. Complete the 10-product comparison and a one-page product idea.

Standard to meet. Ten comparisons; one clear difference; one named buyer.

Module 2 Time and materials 10 hApply

Handmade means time. Measure how long each step takes, because time is usually the biggest hidden cost.

  • Break the job into steps: cut, sew or shape, finish, pack.
  • Time five makes; use the average.
  • List each material with quantity, cost and waste.
  • Write a simple method so another person could make the same item.
Worked example. Times for five pouches: 42, 39, 41, 37, 36 min; average 39 min. Fabric 0.4 m at EUR 6/m = EUR 2.40, thread EUR 0.20, lining EUR 0.60.

Activity. Time five items and write the method card.

Standard to meet. Five times recorded; method card clear enough for a peer to follow.

Module 3 Quality and batch making 14 hApply

A batch is a group made together: faster and more consistent. Quality means every item meets the same standard.

  • Make jigs, templates and patterns so sizes repeat.
  • Write a quality checklist: size, finish, strength, look.
  • Make 20 in batches of 5 and compare times.
  • Reject and rework items that fail the checklist; log them.
Worked example. With a cutting template, cutting time drops from 9 to 4 minutes; batch of 5 takes 140 min against 195 min making one by one.

Activity. Build 20 units to your checklist.

Standard to meet. Batch time lower than single time; rejection rate recorded.

Module 4 Pricing for profit 14 hEvaluate

Price is not cost plus hope. Price covers materials, your time, tools, selling costs and a profit for growth.

  • Unit cost = materials + labour (time x fair hourly rate) + packaging + selling cost share.
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Test two prices with different buyers and watch what happens.
  • Check against competitors but do not copy their price without your own numbers.
Worked example. Pouch: materials EUR 3.20, labour 39 min at EUR 12/h = EUR 7.80, packaging EUR 0.40, stall fee share EUR 0.90; cost EUR 12.30. At EUR 14, margin = 12%; at EUR 18, margin 32%. Test 18.

Activity. Build your cost sheet and choose two test prices.

Standard to meet. Currency on all lines; margin computed for both prices.

Module 5 Photos, story and listing 12 hCreate

Buyers cannot touch your product online, so photos and words do the work.

  • Photograph in daylight near a window, plain background, 5 angles including scale and detail.
  • Write a short story: who made it, from what, why it is useful.
  • Describe size, material, care and delivery plainly.
  • Never use a photo of another maker's work.
Worked example. A listing shows 5 photos and the text 'Hand-sewn cotton pouch, 18 x 9 cm, fits glasses and a card. Wash cold. Ships in 3 days.'

Activity. Photograph and write one listing; get two peers to say what they would ask.

Standard to meet. Five photos meet the checklist; the listing answers likely questions.

Module 6 Sell in a pilot channel 50 hApply

Sell your 20 units where real buyers are: a stall, a shop partner or an online page.

  • Agree terms in writing with any shop (price, share, payment date).
  • Record every sale: date, item, price, buyer type (no personal data).
  • Ask each buyer one question: why did you pick this?
  • Note repeat buyers and requests.
Worked example. Stall day: 11 sold of 20 at EUR 18; four buyers asked for a different colour. Add two colours next batch.

Activity. Sell 20 units and keep the sales record.

Standard to meet. Sales table complete; two buyer comments acted on.

Module 7 Rules and safety 12 hAnalyse

Selling means obeying rules: consumer rights, product safety, tax and (for some products) labelling.

  • Find which rules apply to your product and place; ask the local authority or a small-business adviser.
  • Keep receipts and sales records.
  • Give a clear returns promise and keep it.
  • Do not claim materials or origin you cannot prove.
Worked example. A candle maker learns that labels must carry warning text and a batch code; she adds both and keeps a test log for every batch.

Activity. Write a one-page rules note naming each rule and its source.

Standard to meet. Each rule has a named source and a date.

Module 8 Review and plan 32 hEvaluate

After the pilot, decide how to grow without losing quality.

  • Compare time and margin for batch 1 and batch 4.
  • Write three lessons learned.
  • Choose: more of the same, a new colour or size, or a new channel.
  • Give two peers written feedback.
Worked example. Margin rose from 12% to 32% after templates and a price change; plan: one new stall and a 40-unit batch.

Activity. Present evidence at a review room.

Standard to meet. Plan with numbers and dates; two peer reviews given.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: your record, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why time each step?

Model answer: Time is often the biggest hidden cost. (Covered in module 2: Time and materials. To pass that module: Five times recorded; method card clear enough for a peer to follow.)

Q2. Define batch making.

Model answer: Making a group of items together for speed and consistency. (Covered in module 3: Quality and batch making. To pass that module: Batch time lower than single time; rejection rate recorded.)

Q3. Unit cost formula?

Model answer: Materials + labour + packaging + selling cost share. (Covered in module 4: Pricing for profit. To pass that module: Currency on all lines; margin computed for both prices.)

Q4. Margin when price is EUR 18 and cost is EUR 12.30?

Model answer: (18 - 12.30) / 18 = 31.7%, about 32%. (Covered in module 4: Pricing for profit. To pass that module: Currency on all lines; margin computed for both prices.)

Q5. What belongs in a listing?

Model answer: Photos, size, material, care, delivery, a short story. (Covered in module 5: Photos, story and listing. To pass that module: Five photos meet the checklist; the listing answers likely questions.)

Q6. Why a quality checklist?

Model answer: So every item meets the same standard and rejects are caught. (Covered in module 3: Quality and batch making. To pass that module: Batch time lower than single time; rejection rate recorded.)

Q7. What should you do when a shop sells for you?

Model answer: Agree price, share and payment date in writing. (Covered in module 6: Sell in a pilot channel. To pass that module: Sales table complete; two buyer comments acted on.)

Q8. Why ask buyers why they chose it?

Model answer: To learn what the market values and improve. (Covered in module 6: Sell in a pilot channel. To pass that module: Sales table complete; two buyer comments acted on.)

Q9. Name one product-safety step.

Model answer: Check the rules for your product type with the local authority. (Covered in module 7: Rules and safety. To pass that module: Each rule has a named source and a date.)

Q10. What decides growth?

Model answer: Margin and quality at the higher volume, not only demand. (Covered in module 8: Review and plan. To pass that module: Plan with numbers and dates; two peer reviews given.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.
