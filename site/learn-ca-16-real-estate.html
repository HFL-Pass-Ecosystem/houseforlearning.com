Real estate

Run a real real estate pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small real estate offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: property, construction and smart buildings. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read a local property market 6 hUnderstand

Property is local. Learn one neighbourhood deeply.

  • Collect 20 recent sales: price, size, days on market, condition.
  • Compute price per square foot and the spread.
  • Note rent levels, vacancy and new supply.
  • Write a one-page market note.
  • Record the source and date of every sale; use closed sales, not asking prices.
  • Use the median rather than the average, so one unusual sale does not distort the picture.
  • Compare with rent: annual rent divided by price gives a gross yield; work it out for three property types.
Worked example. 20 sales: median 640 per sq ft; range 560 to 760; median 28 days on market. A 900 sq ft unit at the median is 900 x 640 = 576,000, and the range gives 504,000 to 684,000. If it rents for 2,400 a month, annual rent is 28,800 and the gross yield is 28,800 / 576,000 = 5%.

Activity. Build a 20-sale table and market note.

Standard to meet. Numbers have sources and dates.

Module 2 Know licensing, disclosure and trust rules 6 hApply

Real estate is regulated.

  • Provincial regulators license agents and brokers; only licensed people may trade for others.
  • Deposits go into trust accounts with strict records.
  • Sellers and agents must disclose known material facts.
  • Anti-money-laundering rules need client identification.
  • Write the steps to become licensed in your province: course, exam, brokerage sponsor and insurance; check the regulator's current list.
  • Learn the difference between a conflict you must disclose and one you must avoid, and write an example of each.
  • Keep a client-identification file: what you check, when, and where it is stored; check the provincial and federal rule.
Worked example. Row: 'Accept a buyer deposit | provincial real-estate regulator | trust account and receipt | by the deadline in the provincial rule | trust ledger'. A second row: 'Disclose a known defect | seller and agent | written disclosure | before offer | signed form'. Date each source.

Activity. Build a rules table for your province.

Standard to meet. Rows sourced and dated.

Module 3 Value and analyse a property 10 hApply

Use comparable sales and income.

  • Comparable sales method: adjust for size, condition, location.
  • Income method: net operating income / capitalisation rate.
  • Check hidden costs: repairs, taxes, fees.
  • Write a short valuation note.
  • Choose three comparable sales that are recent and close by, and adjust each for size, condition and location, showing every adjustment in dollars.
  • For the income method list rent, vacancy allowance, taxes, insurance and repairs; net operating income is rent minus vacancy and costs.
  • Say which method you trust more for this property and why, in two sentences.
Worked example. Rent 40,000 a year less 5% vacancy (2,000) = 38,000; less costs 10,000 = 28,000 net income. At a 5% cap rate the value is 28,000 / 0.05 = 560,000. At 6% it is 28,000 / 0.06 = about 466,667, so the cap rate you choose matters a great deal.

Activity. Value one property two ways.

Standard to meet. Both methods shown.

Module 4 Cost a deal and test it 8 hEvaluate

Margin of safety.

  • Purchase price plus closing costs.
  • Repair and holding costs.
  • Sale or rent income.
  • Break-even and stress tests.
  • List closing costs: legal fees, land transfer tax where it applies, inspection and title insurance; check the provincial rule for each.
  • Add holding costs per month (interest, taxes, insurance, utilities) and multiply by the months you expect to hold.
  • Run three cases: base, a 5% price fall, and a sale that takes three months longer.
Worked example. Buy 360,000; costs 24,000, so total cost is 384,000. Sell 420,000; fees 21,000, so net is 399,000; profit 15,000, about 3.9% of cost. If the price falls 5% to 399,000, fees of 5% are 19,950, net is 379,050 and the result is a loss of 4,950. Teaching figures in CAD.

Activity. Build a deal sheet with a 5% fall case.

Standard to meet. Stress case shown.

Module 5 Run the transaction and file records 8 hApply

Process protects everyone.

  • Listing agreement, offer, conditions, closing.
  • Record every step.
  • Use lawyers and inspectors.
  • Handle conflicts of interest in writing.
  • Make a timeline from listing to offer, condition removal, closing and possession, and put every date in a calendar.
  • Keep a copy of every signed document and every message that changes terms.
  • Write a handover note that tells the client the next step in plain words.
Worked example. Closing checklist with 14 steps, each signed and dated. If only 12 are dated, the file is 12 / 14 = 86% complete, and the two blanks are the first things to chase.

Activity. Build a transaction checklist.

Standard to meet. All steps dated.

Module 6 Find clients and build trust 6 hCreate

Reputation is the business.

  • Referrals and local presence.
  • Plain-language guides.
  • Respond within hours.
  • Collect reviews honestly.
  • Write a plain guide for first-time buyers that lists every cost with an example figure.
  • Reply to every enquiry within a time you state, and log the reply time.
  • Ask for a review only after the client agrees, never offer a gift for one, and check the regulator's advertising rule.
Worked example. Funnel: 40 contacts, 10 viewings, 3 offers, 1 sale. Rates: 10 / 40 = 25% to viewing, 3 / 10 = 30% to offer, 1 / 3 = 33% to sale, and 1 / 40 = 2.5% overall. To expect four sales you would need about 160 contacts.

Activity. Run a 4-week funnel.

Standard to meet. Funnel recorded.

Module 7 Manage legal, fair-housing and market risk 6 hEvaluate

Treat everyone fairly.

  • Fair-housing and human-rights rules forbid discrimination.
  • Market downturn plan.
  • Keep records for the required years.
  • Insurance.
  • Write a short fair-treatment policy: same questions, same information and same service for every client; check the provincial human-rights code.
  • Set a downturn plan for a 10% price fall and for sales volume halving.
  • Check whether errors-and-omissions insurance is required in your province and what it covers.
Worked example. Risk: 'Price fall | 4 x 4 | margin of safety | owner | quarterly'. A 10% fall on a 420,000 property is 42,000 of value, so a deal with less than that margin of safety is skipped or re-priced.

Activity. Write a risk register.

Standard to meet. Owners named.

Module 8 Pilot with a paper deal 8 hCreate

Practise with a mock transaction.

  • Pick a listing, value it and plan the sale.
  • Track 60 days.
  • Stop rule: negative margin means no deal.
  • Review.
  • Pick a real listing from public records and write a value and sale plan without contacting anyone.
  • Keep a 60-day diary of price changes, new listings and days on market for the neighbourhood.
  • Compare your prediction with what happened and write one lesson.
Worked example. Mock deal margin 6%: on a total cost of 384,000 that is a profit of about 23,040 (384,000 x 0.06), so net proceeds after fees would need to be 407,040. The margin is above zero, so the decision is go.

Activity. Run the mock and present review.

Standard to meet. Review has decision.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is price per square foot?

Model answer: Price divided by area.

Q2. How is value found by income?

Model answer: Net operating income divided by cap rate.

Q3. Where are deposits held?

Model answer: In a trust account.

Q4. Who may trade for others?

Model answer: Licensed agents and brokers.

Q5. Why stress test a deal?

Model answer: To see if it survives a price fall.

Q6. What must sellers disclose?

Model answer: Known material facts.

Q7. Why keep records?

Model answer: Rules require it and it protects you.

Q8. What is a margin of safety?

Model answer: Buffer between price and value.

Q9. Why is fair housing important?

Model answer: Discrimination is illegal.

Q10. What triggers no-go in the mock?

Model answer: Negative margin.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
