CPD · Beauty and complementary therapies

Applying infection control

Hygiene steps before, during and after a treatment.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 FoundationAbout 2 hours (120 minutes) of CPDSelf-study plus role-playBeauty and complementary therapies

Hygiene steps before, during and after a treatment.

  • Demonstrate hand cleaning, given a checklist, with every step in order.
  • Prepare a clean workspace, given a layout, so clean and used items are kept apart.
  • Dispose of waste, given a list, using the correct containers.
  • Record cleaning, given a log, with date, time and initials.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Apply infection control in a treatment room by cleaning hands in order, choosing the cleaning level for each item, keeping clean and used items apart, and completing the cleaning log.

Condition

Given a hand-cleaning checklist, six sample items, a room layout and a cleaning log, working in a two-hour supervised session and following the local rule and product instructions.

Standard

Produce a result with every hand-cleaning step in order, the right cleaning level for all six items, clean and used items kept apart, and a log complete with date, item, method and initials.

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Why it matters 15 min
  • Missed steps can pass germs between people
  • Clients and staff are both at risk
  • A routine is proof of care
Worked example. A missed step has a cost to the client and to staff. With 3 clients in a session and one missed hand-clean each, there are 3 chances for germs to pass between people. Infection control aims for 0 missed steps.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "why it matters": demonstrate hand cleaning, given a checklist, with every step in order.

Standard to meet. Explain why infection control matters in a treatment room and list the risks to clients and staff when a step is missed.

Module 2 Hand hygiene order 20 min
  • Clean hands before and after each client
  • Clean hands after removing gloves
  • Follow the steps in order
Worked example. Hand cleaning with 3 clients and gloves each time: 3 before, 3 after, 3 after glove removal = 9 hand-cleaning moments. Steps in order scored on the checklist: 6 of 6.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "hand hygiene order": prepare a clean workspace, given a layout, so clean and used items are kept apart.

Standard to meet. Demonstrate hand cleaning with every step in order, before and after each client and after removing gloves, against a checklist.

Module 3 Cleaning levels for tools 25 min
  • Single-use items are used once, then discarded
  • Reusable items are cleaned to their level
  • Follow the item label and product instructions
  • Follow your local regulator and the product instructions.
Worked example. Six items on the case card: towel (launder), tweezers (clean, then disinfect or sterilise), nail file (single-use, discard), couch cover (single-use, discard), brush (clean, then disinfect), gloves (single-use, discard). 3 single-use + 3 reusable = 6.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "cleaning levels for tools": dispose of waste, given a list, using the correct containers.

Standard to meet. Choose the cleaning level for each of six items, such as towel, tweezers and gloves, and keep clean and used items apart.

Module 4 Cleaning log 15 min
  • Log date, item, method and initials
  • The lead checks the log weekly
  • A log is proof of the routine
  • If you miss a step, re-do it and note it in the log.
Worked example. Log line: "12 Jun, tweezers, disinfected, AB" has 4 parts: date, item, method, initials. A lead checks weekly: 5 days x 6 items = 30 expected entries; 28 found means 2 missing, so the lead asks why.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "cleaning log": record cleaning, given a log, with date, time and initials.

Standard to meet. Record cleaning on the log with date, item, method and initials, so there is proof of the routine for a lead to check.

Module 5 Demonstration 20 min
  • Choose the cleaning level for the tool
  • Keep clean and used items apart
  • Complete the log
Worked example. Case card 2: a reusable tool touched skin. Learner demonstrates 6 of 6 steps: wash hands, clean with approved cleaner, disinfect, dry, store covered, log date, item, method and initials. Clean items stay on the left, used on the right.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "demonstration": record cleaning, given a log, with date, time and initials. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Demonstrate the full routine on a live case where a reusable tool touched skin, choosing the cleaning level and completing the log.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Show hand cleaning, levels and log
  • Follow the local rule
  • Redo and note any missed step
Worked example. Check: hands 6 of 6 steps in order, 6 of 6 items sorted correctly, log has all 4 parts. If the local rule differs from the card, the local rule and the product instructions win.

Practise: Take the end check for "applying infection control": show that you can record cleaning, given a log, with date, time and initials, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Demonstrate hand cleaning, tool cleaning levels and a complete log, following the local rule and product instructions.

Module 7 Second case: a busy room 10 min
  • Plan for several clients in a row
  • Rotate tool sets
  • Log every use
Worked example. Case card 2: 4 clients in a row and 2 sets of tweezers. Set 1 for clients 1 and 3, set 2 for clients 2 and 4, so each set is cleaned while the other set is in use. Log entries: 4, one per client, each with date, item, method and initials.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second case: a busy room": record cleaning, given a log, with date, time and initials. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Apply the cleaning routine to a busy session by planning tool sets for four clients in a row and logging each use correctly.

Module 8 Spot the missed step and reflection 10 min
  • Find the step that was skipped
  • Redo it and note it
  • Reflect on your routine
Worked example. Observed routine shows 5 of 6 steps, with drying missed. The learner redoes the tool from the cleaning step and notes "missed drying, redone" in the log. Now 6 of 6 steps. Reflection: why the missed step was rushed.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "spot the missed step and reflection": record cleaning, given a log, with date, time and initials. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate an observed routine, identify a missed step, correct it and record it in the log, then reflect on one habit that prevents a repeat.

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What is infection control. Speaker note: Infection control is the set of cleaning habits that stop germs passing between people in a treatment room. At this level the learner knows the word hygiene and why clean hands come first.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Hands and single-use. Speaker note: Show the hand-cleaning moments: before, after, and after gloves. The learner at this level can name when to wash and which items are single-use, such as gloves and couch covers.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Cleaning levels. Speaker note: Learners sort the six items into clean, disinfect, sterilise or discard under supervision, keep clean and used apart, and fill in a log line with a date, item, method and initials.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Apply the routine and log it. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner demonstrates every hand-cleaning step in order, chooses the cleaning level for each item, keeps clean and used items apart, and completes the log without help.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Busy rooms and judgement. Speaker note: The specialist plans tool sets for several clients in a row, handles a reusable tool that touched skin, and coaches colleagues to redo a missed step and note it. They follow the local rule.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Auditing the routine. Speaker note: The advanced worker checks logs weekly for gaps, such as 28 of 30 expected entries, finds the cause of misses and reports trends. Talk about how the routine slips on the busiest days.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: A hygiene system for the business. Speaker note: The super expert designs the hygiene system for several rooms: layouts, tool sets, logs, training and audit measures. They decide what compliant looks like and prove it with weekly log checks.

assessment and certificate

How you are checked

Each outcome is checked against its standard with a checklist or a short test. A digital CPD record shows the hours. Keep evidence in My page. Aligned with common CPD practice; not accredited. Learners check their own body's rules.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own body's forms, units and currency. Local professional bodies set the rules for CPD hours; check them first.

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Credit and levels: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Credit and levels: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

United States

Singapore
