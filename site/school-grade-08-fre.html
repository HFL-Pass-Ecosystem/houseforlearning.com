Grade 08: French

4 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 08 French, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 4 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

unit by unit

4 units with worked examples

Unit 1 Past and future in simple form Weeks 1 to 9Understand

In this unit you work on 'Past and future in simple form', taught in a way that is with notes, a worked line and a self-mark. The target is clear: talk about past and future. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Past and future in simple form' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: talk about past and future, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Passe compose: j'ai parle, j'ai fini. Futur simple: je parlerai. Method: Model run for 'Past and future in simple form'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, The passé composé talks about finished actions. Form: avoir in the present plus the past participle. J'ai mangé [I ate], tu as fini [you finished], nous avons joué [we played]. Hier, j'ai joué au foot. [Yesterday I played football.]
  2. Step 2, Some verbs use être: aller, arriver, partir, venir, entrer. Je suis allé au marché. [I went to the market, a boy.] Elle est arrivée à huit heures. [She arrived at eight.] With être the participle agrees: allé, allée, allés, allées.
  3. Step 3, The near future uses aller plus the infinitive. Je vais manger. [I am going to eat.] Nous allons jouer demain. [We are going to play tomorrow.] The simple future adds endings to the infinitive: je mangerai, tu finiras, nous jouerons. [I will eat, you will finish, we will play.]
  4. Step 4, Dialogue. Élève A : Qu'est-ce que tu as fait hier ? [What did you do yesterday?] Élève B : J'ai regardé un film. [I watched a film.] Élève A : Et demain ? [And tomorrow?] Élève B : Demain, je jouerai au tennis. [Tomorrow I will play tennis.]
  5. Step 5, Fix errors. A student says j'ai allé. Correction: je suis allé, because aller takes être. Another says je vais mangé. Correction: je vais manger, with the infinitive. A third says elle est allé; correction: elle est allée.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: write yesterday, now and tomorrow with finir. Model answer: Hier, j'ai fini mes devoirs. Aujourd'hui, je finis mon livre. Demain, je finirai mon projet. [Yesterday I finished my homework. Today I finish my book. Tomorrow I will finish my project.]

Activity. About 30 to 40 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a worked task and a corrected copy.

Standard to meet. Talk about past and future: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer.

Unit 2 Daily routine Weeks 10 to 18Understand

In this unit you work on 'Daily routine', taught in a way that is with notes, a worked line and a self-mark. The target is clear: describe a day. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Daily routine' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: describe a day, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: 'Je me leve a sept heures. Je prends le petit dejeuner. Je vais a l'ecole.' Method: Model run for 'Daily routine'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Learn pronominal verbs for the morning. je me réveille [I wake up], je me lève [I get up], je me lave [I wash], je m'habille [I get dressed]. The me stays before the verb. Say: Je me réveille à sept heures. [I wake up at seven.]
  2. Step 2, Add other routine verbs. Je prends le petit déjeuner. [I have breakfast.] Je vais à l'école à huit heures et demie. [I go to school at half past eight.] Je mange à midi. [I eat at noon.] Je rentre à la maison à cinq heures. [I go home at five.]
  3. Step 3, Write the evening. Je fais mes devoirs. [I do my homework.] Je dîne avec ma famille. [I have dinner with my family.] Je me couche à neuf heures. [I go to bed at nine.] Use d'abord, ensuite, puis, enfin for order.
  4. Step 4, Connect in a short paragraph. D'abord, je me réveille à sept heures. Ensuite, je prends le petit déjeuner. Puis je vais à l'école. Enfin, le soir, je me couche tôt. [First I wake up at seven. Then I have breakfast. Then I go to school. Finally in the evening I go to bed early.]
  5. Step 5, Fix errors. A student says je réveille à sept heure. Correction: je me réveille à sept heures. Another writes tu te lèves with je: je te lève; correction: je me lève. Also keep the s on heures when the number is more than one.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: write four sentences about your day with times. Model answer: Je me lève à sept heures. Je vais à l'école à huit heures. Je rentre à quatre heures. Je me couche à dix heures. [I get up at seven. I go to school at eight. I come home at four. I go to bed at ten.]

Activity. About 30 to 40 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a worked task and a corrected copy.

Standard to meet. Describe a day: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer.

Unit 3 Listening practice Weeks 19 to 27Apply

In this unit you work on 'Listening practice', taught in a way that is with notes, a worked line and a self-mark. The target is clear: follow a longer dialogue. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Listening practice' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: follow a longer dialogue, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Ecoute: 'Le train part a huit heures quinze du quai trois.' Note: 8 h 15, quai 3. A dire: 'Bonjour, je m'appelle [mon prenom]. Je vais parler de ma ville.' Method: Model run for 'Listening practice'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Predict before listening. The title is On organise samedi [Planning Saturday]. Expect times, activities and a decision. Draw a three-column table: Qui [who], Quand [when], Quoi [what]. Decide to listen twice: once for the gist, once for the details.
  2. Step 2, Listen for the gist. Read aloud: Léa : Tu es libre samedi ? Tom : Le matin, j'ai un cours de guitare jusqu'à onze heures, mais l'après-midi, je suis libre. Léa : On pourrait aller au cinéma à quinze heures trente. Tom : Bonne idée ! Le film dure combien de temps ? Léa : Une heure quarante. Tom : Alors on sort à dix-sept heures dix. On mange une crêpe après ? Léa : D'accord ! Gist: two friends plan an outing.
  3. Step 3, Fill the table on the second listening. Qui : Léa and Tom. Quand : samedi, 15 h 30 (quinze heures trente). Quoi : cinéma, then une crêpe [a pancake]. Also note Tom's guitar lesson until 11 h (onze heures) and that the film lasts 1 h 40.
  4. Step 4, Check an inference with a calculation. 15 h 30 plus 1 h 40 equals 17 h 10. Tom says dix-sept heures dix, which is correct. When a speaker gives a start time and a length, work out the end time yourself and compare with what you hear.
  5. Step 5, Fix common slips. French often uses the 24-hour clock in speech: quinze heures trente is 3.30 p.m. Do not confuse quinze [15] with quarante [40] or dix-sept [17] with dix-huit [18]. Un quart d'heure [quarter of an hour] is different from quatorze heures [2 p.m.].
  6. Step 6, Your turn: hear Le cours commence à neuf heures quarante-cinq et dure une heure trente. À quelle heure finit-il ? Answer: 9 h 45 plus 1 h 30 is 11 h 15, so il finit à onze heures quinze. [It ends at 11.15.]

Activity. About 30 to 40 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a worked task and a corrected copy.

Standard to meet. Follow a longer dialogue: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer.

Unit 4 Speaking practice Weeks 28 to 36Apply

In this unit you work on 'Speaking practice', taught in a way that is with notes, a worked line and a self-mark. The target is clear: present. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Speaking practice' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: present, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Ecoute: 'Le train part a huit heures quinze du quai trois.' Note: 8 h 15, quai 3. A dire: 'Bonjour, je m'appelle [mon prenom]. Je vais parler de ma ville.' Method: Model run for 'Speaking practice'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Pick a topic and a plan. Topic: mon quartier [my neighbourhood]. Plan: introduce, location, what there is, what I like, conclude. A one-minute talk for a language learner at a careful pace is about 60 to 80 words, so about eight to ten short sentences.
  2. Step 2, Build the introduction and location. Bonjour à tous. Je vais vous présenter mon quartier. [Hello everyone. I am going to present my neighbourhood.] D'abord, il est situé près d'un grand parc. [First, it is near a big park.] Je vais plus infinitive is the near future.
  3. Step 3, Add the middle and the opinion. Ensuite, il y a une bibliothèque, un petit marché et deux écoles. [Then there is a library, a small market and two schools.] Enfin, ce que j'aime, c'est le calme et mes amis. [Finally, what I like is the calm and my friends.]
  4. Step 4, Close and deliver. Pour conclure, mon quartier est petit mais agréable. Merci de m'écouter. [To conclude, my neighbourhood is small but pleasant. Thank you for listening.] Use cue cards with one keyword each, look at the audience, and pause for a breath after each sequence word.
  5. Step 5, Fix errors. A child says Je vais présente. Correction: Je vais présenter, because after aller we use the infinitive. Another says il y a un bibliothèque. Correction: une bibliothèque, because it is feminine. Another reads every word; use keywords instead.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: present your school in five sentences. Model answer: Bonjour à tous. Je vais vous présenter mon école. D'abord, elle est près de ma maison. Ensuite, il y a une grande cour et un gymnase. Pour conclure, j'aime mon école parce que mes amis sont là. Merci !

Activity. About 30 to 40 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a worked task and a corrected copy.

Standard to meet. Present: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: First words and greetings

On the slide: L1 Basic · First words and greetings

Notes: Greet someone, give your name and count to ten in French. Notes: teach Bonjour (hello) and Je m'appelle... (my name is...) with a smile and gesture; learners greet five classmates and say their own name, with no reading or writing needed yet.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Sounds, spelling and simple phrases

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Sounds, spelling and simple phrases

Notes: Pronounce common French sounds and read short phrases about family, colours and food. Notes: show that final consonants are often silent, as in petit (small); learners read ten word cards aloud, then match each to a picture.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Present tense and simple sentences (this course)

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Present tense and simple sentences

Notes: Write and say ten sentences about yourself using regular verbs and the verbs être (to be) and avoir (to have). Notes: give a gap-fill on Je suis... and J'ai...; learners complete it with their own details and swap with a partner to check agreement.

This course: Grade 08 French is at this level. Its units include Past and future in simple form; Daily routine; Listening practice; Speaking practice.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Past and future in conversation

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Past and future in conversation

Notes: Talk about yesterday and next week using the passé composé and the near future. Notes: model J'ai mangé (I ate) and Je vais manger (I am going to eat); learners interview a partner for three minutes, then write six sentences in the past and the future.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Argument, texts and accuracy

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Argument, texts and accuracy

Notes: Write a 250-word opinion piece and summarise an authentic article, using the subjunctive where needed. Notes: practise Il faut que je parte (I must leave); learners mark every verb in their draft, fix agreement errors, and defend one opinion in a spoken minute.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Literature, linguistics and style

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Literature, linguistics and style

Notes: Analyse a literary or media text for style, register and historical context, and write a critical essay in French. Notes: choose one page of prose; learners find three stylistic choices, compare them with a modern translation, and argue in 600 words which reading is stronger.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Research, translation and teaching

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Research, translation and teaching

Notes: Plan and carry out an original piece of French-language research or translation with a documented method. Notes: choose a narrow question, such as how one word's meaning has shifted; collect twenty sources, code them, write up findings in French, and submit them for peer review.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

India (Bengaluru)

Use INR in money problems; deliver in English, Hindi, Kannada; personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Use AED in money problems; deliver in Arabic, English; personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Use BRL in money problems; deliver in Portuguese (Spanish and English support); personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Canada (Toronto)

Use CAD in money problems; deliver in English, French; personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

United States

Use USD in money problems; deliver in English, Spanish; personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Singapore

Use SGD in money problems; deliver in English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Australia

Use AUD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Use HKD in money problems; deliver in Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Use GEL or ILS in money problems; deliver in Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Use KYD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Data Protection Act; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Mexico

Use MXN in money problems; deliver in Spanish, English; personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; guardian contact for every learner under 18.
