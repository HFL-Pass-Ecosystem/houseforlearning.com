Micro-Mobility Services: Plan a small fleet with safety and parking rules.

  • Understand Explain who needs Micro-Mobility Services and name one real first user, with evidence from your own market scan.
  • Apply Build and run the practice task: Plan a small fleet with safety and parking rules.
  • Analyse Record every step, cost and problem, then compare the plan with what really happened.
  • Evaluate Judge your result against your goal using numbers, and write three lessons learned.
  • Create Present your evidence to a review room and give two peers written feedback.
  • Create Plan your next 90 days and contribute one template or worked example back to learners.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan a small fleet with safety and parking rules.

Condition

About 60 hours over 1-3 months. Own tools, free spreadsheet and form tools, and a real practice partner. Mentor and peer circle available. Currency written on every number.

Standard

Evidence reviewed by a mentor; at least 70% on the 10-question check; two written peer reviews given; every number carries its currency and unit.

Who it is for, requirements and materials

  • Who it is for: learners aged 16 and over, small-business starters, workers moving to a new field, and teams (see B2B, B2C, D2C).
  • Requirements: finish Biofuel Production Overview first, or show equivalent work.
  • Materials included: lesson text, worked examples, case exercise, 10-question check, templates in My page.
  • Teaching team: a trainer from our network, a mentor who reviews your evidence, and a peer circle. Names are given at enrolment.

lesson by lesson

9 modules, in the order you do them

Module 1 Short trips and who takes them IdeaStudy · 2 hUnderstand

Goal: find demand for a small fleet.

  1. Map a district: stations, campuses, markets, offices. Mark trips of 1 to 5 km.
  2. Count riders and pedestrians on two routes at two times (counts only).
  3. Interview 5 people about short trips: how they travel now, cost, time, what stops them using a shared bike or scooter.
  4. Write a one-page service idea: zone, vehicle type, price, hours.

Evidence you keep: the map, counts and five interview notes.

Module 2 Kick-off circle IdeaShare and mentoring · 3 hUnderstand

Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.

  1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
  2. Peers ask three questions: where do vehicles park, who is responsible after an accident, what do local rules say.
  3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a zone map, a fleet plan, a safety and parking plan, a cost sheet and a rules check.

Evidence you keep: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

Module 3 Plan the fleet and zone ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Goal: decide how many vehicles and where.

  1. Define the service zone and mark the main parking spots.
  2. Fleet size = expected rides per day divided by target rides per vehicle per day. Start low (for example 20 vehicles) and justify the number.
  3. Plan charging or battery swap rounds and maintenance days.
  4. Write the allowed and no-ride areas (pavements, schools, crowded streets).

Evidence you keep: zone map and fleet calculation.

Module 4 Safety, maintenance and local regulation ActionStudy · 2 hApply

Short modules:

  • Safety: helmets where required, speed limits, lights, brakes, no riding drunk, one rider only, age limits. Put the rules on every vehicle and in the app or sign.
  • Maintenance: daily check of brakes, tyres, lights, bell, battery condition; remove any faulty vehicle at once.
  • Regulation: licences for operators, insurance, allowed vehicles, parking rules. Write which rules apply and who confirmed them.
  • Community: complaints, blocked pavements, noise; a named contact to fix problems quickly.

Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

Module 5 Write the safety, parking and maintenance plan ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Goal: set routines that keep riders and the public safe.

  1. Write a 10-point daily vehicle check and test it on a borrowed bike.
  2. Design the parking plan: marked bays, a rule for blocked pavements and a time to move bad parking.
  3. Write the incident process: first aid, call emergency number, record, tell insurer, review the cause.
  4. Write rider rules in plain language.

Evidence you keep: the checklist, parking plan, incident process and rules.

Module 6 Operator panel ValueShare and mentoring · 6 hEvaluate

Goal: test your plan against real operators and officials.

  1. Present the plan to a panel with an operator or city officer.
  2. They ask about theft, vandalism, accidents, and complaints.
  3. Record challenges and answers.
  4. Update the plan.

Evidence you keep: panel notes and updated plan.

Module 7 Cost it and decide ValuePractice · 14 hEvaluate

Goal: check if the service can be fair and sustainable.

  1. Cost per vehicle per day: depreciation, battery, maintenance, staff time, insurance, parking fees, losses. Currency shown.
  2. Revenue per ride = unlock fee + minutes x price. Compare with cost per ride.
  3. Test with half the rides and a 10 percent loss rate.
  4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.

Evidence you keep: cost sheet and decision note.

Module 8 Review room EmpowermentShare and mentoring · 3 hCreate

Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.

  1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the zone, the fleet, the safety plan, the cost and the decision.
  2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
  3. Get a written note from a rider or a local officer.

Evidence you keep: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

Module 9 Check and next step RegenerationStudy · 2 hCreate

Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.

  1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
  2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: Car Sharing Business, Electric Car and EV Basics, Portable and Wireless Charging Solutions.
  3. Contribute back: share your daily vehicle check or incident process so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).

Evidence you keep: check result and the 90-day plan.

practice material and assessment

Worked examples, case, check and local law pointers

Worked examples
  1. Fleet size: expected 120 rides a day / 6 rides per vehicle per day = 20 vehicles.
  2. Revenue per ride: unlock 1.00 + 12 min x 0.20 = 3.40 (currency shown).
  3. Cost per vehicle-day: depreciation 2.00 + battery 1.00 + maintenance 1.50 + insurance 0.80 + parking 0.50 + loss 0.70 = 6.50. Rides per vehicle-day 6 gives revenue 20.40, margin 13.90.
  4. Stress test: half the rides (3 per vehicle-day) gives revenue 10.20, margin 3.70.
Case exercise

A district has a school, a market and a station within 3 km. Questions: Where are the no-ride areas? How do you stop blocked pavements? What is your incident process? Is the service fair and sustainable at half the rides? Write a one-page plan for an officer.

Check questions (draft set, 10)
  1. Why start with a small fleet? 2. Name three items in a daily check. 3. What do you do with a faulty vehicle? 4. Why have no-ride areas? 5. What does insurance cover? 6. How do you compute fleet size? 7. Why have a named complaints contact? 8. What if rides are half the plan? 9. Name one rider rule. 10. What is your decision?

Rules for this course

No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Paper plan only unless licensed. Counts must not record faces or plates. Confirm local licensing, insurance and parking rules.
