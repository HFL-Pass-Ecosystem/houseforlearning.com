flagship course · GL-20

Social Media Network Building

Build a small, safe and active online community around a real purpose, with clear rules, regular content, moderation and measured growth.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Build a small, safe and active online community around a real purpose, with clear rules, regular content, moderation and measured growth.

  • Understand Explain what makes a community useful, safe and worth joining.
  • Apply Set up a community space with rules, roles and a content calendar.
  • Analyse Analyse engagement numbers to see what members value.
  • Create Create welcome, moderation and event routines.
  • Evaluate Evaluate growth and health honestly, including who is not taking part.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Launch and run a small community of at least 30 members for 6 weeks, with rules, content plan, moderation log and a health report.

Condition

A free community or group tool, a real shared purpose, a content calendar in a spreadsheet, a mentor with community experience and a peer circle. About 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

Rules published before launch; every post and report handled within 24 hours; no private data shared; at least 30% of members active in week 6; a health report with five measures and three changes.

Who it is for: Teachers, small-business owners, club leaders, volunteers, marketers, students. Requirements: A personal account on a social tool. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Protect members: no sharing of private data, no pressure to join, clear rules on harassment, extra care with children. Follow the platform's rules and local law.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Why a community 4 hUnderstand

People join for a clear benefit. You will state a community's purpose in one short sentence and test it with five possible members before investing time in building.

  • Purposes: learn, sell, support, play, organise.
  • Describe the member in one sentence.
  • Promise: what do I get within a week?
  • Pick a name and a short description.
  • Write your community purpose in one sentence of fewer than 20 words, and name the first 20 people you could invite, because a community with a clear aim and 20 real members is ready to open.
  • Check that people need the community by asking five possible members what they would use it for; if fewer than three give a concrete answer, narrow the purpose.
  • Write who is welcome and who is not.
  • Choose two helpers early and share the tasks.
Worked example. Purpose: "Home bakers swap tips and sell locally." Promise: "A weekly tip and a Saturday swap."

Activity. Write purpose, member, promise.

Standard to meet. All three fit in 40 words.

Common mistake. A purpose that is "everyone".

Second worked example. Purpose: 'Night-shift nurses share rest tips and swap shifts.' Promise: 'A daily check-in and a Sunday shift swap.' Both fit on one card.

Check yourself. Who is your member in one sentence?

Module 2 Rules and safety 6 hUnderstand

Rules protect people. You will write five short rules and a three-step response ladder, so moderation is fair, visible and the same for everyone.

  • Short rules: be kind, no spam, no private data, no hate.
  • What happens when rules break: warn, remove, ban.
  • Report route and response time.
  • Extra care with children and sensitive topics.
  • Write rules as five short sentences that say what to do, such as 'be kind, stay on topic, no selling', and pin them where every new member sees them on joining.
  • Set a three-step response ladder: a friendly reminder, a temporary pause of 24 hours, then removal, and write down each action so decisions are fair and repeatable.
  • Prepare a warning message template that is kind and firm.
  • Ask new members why they joined and use their words.
Worked example. A member posts a phone number of a neighbour: remove at once, explain, keep a log.

Activity. Write six rules and a response plan.

Standard to meet. Rules short and enforceable.

Common mistake. Rules that are long and vague.

Second worked example. A member posts a neighbour's phone number. You remove it at once, explain the rule, log it and thank the person who reported it.

Check yourself. Can a new member read your rules in 30 seconds?

Module 3 Set up the space 8 hApply

Make joining easy. You will choose the one or two spaces that match members' habits and prepare a welcome kit, so the first visitor finds a living place.

  • Choose a platform members already use.
  • Welcome message, pinned guide, topics.
  • Roles: owner, helpers, members.
  • Privacy settings checked.
  • Choose a space type by matching it to your members' habits: a group chat for quick talk, a forum for lasting answers, a video room for events; avoid using more than two at the start.
  • Prepare a welcome post, a pinned rules post and three starter questions before opening, so the first visitor sees a space that already feels lived in.
  • Celebrate a member's win each week.
  • Track the busiest day and time.
Worked example. Pinned post lists rules, how to ask, how to report in 6 lines.

Activity. Set up the space and invite 10 friends to test.

Standard to meet. Test group completes joining in 2 minutes.

Common mistake. Setting up before testing joining.

Second worked example. The welcome message asks every new member to introduce themselves and read the three rules. A new member from another town replies within an hour and receives two answers.

Check yourself. How long does joining take?

Module 4 Content plan 8 hApply

Regular rhythm keeps people coming. You will plan a weekly posting rhythm backed by a bank of ten ideas, keeping the community active without exhausting its organiser.

  • Mix: teach, ask, show, celebrate.
  • Weekly calendar with owners.
  • Reuse member contributions with permission.
  • Quality over quantity.
  • Plan a weekly rhythm: one question on Monday, one useful resource on Wednesday and one member story on Friday, which gives three posts per week and takes under two hours.
  • Keep a bank of ten ideas ahead of time and reuse the best performing post format; if a type gets twice as many replies as others, do more of it.
  • Remove spam within an hour.
  • Offer help to members who are stuck.
Worked example. Mon tip, Wed question, Fri member story, Sat swap.

Activity. Plan six weeks of content.

Standard to meet. Calendar covers four content types.

Common mistake. Posting only offers.

Second worked example. Weekly plan: Tuesday a poll, Thursday a short how-to video, Sunday a photo challenge. Comments per post move from 2 to 5 over a month.

Check yourself. What are your four types?

Module 5 Grow honestly 8 hAnalyse

Invite, do not trick. You will invite members personally and ask them to bring one friend, building steady growth rooted in trust rather than bought numbers.

  • Invite people who will benefit.
  • No fake accounts, no bought followers.
  • Ask members to invite a friend.
  • Partner with related groups openly.
  • Invite members personally with a short message explaining the purpose, and aim to bring in 5 to 10 people per week instead of buying a sudden large crowd.
  • Ask each active member to invite one friend who would truly benefit, and thank people publicly, because growth by trust brings members who stay.
  • Make sure children are not contacted privately.
  • Share group rules every month.
Worked example. Aim 30 members: 10 friends, 10 customers, 10 from partner groups.

Activity. Run a 2-week invite plan.

Standard to meet. At least 30 real members joined.

Common mistake. Buying followers.

Second worked example. To grow from 20 to 60 members, ask 20 current members to invite one person each, post twice in two partner groups with permission, and never buy followers.

Check yourself. How did your 30 members join?

Module 6 Moderate and welcome 10 hCreate

The first reply sets the tone. You will welcome newcomers within a day and handle reports with a five-minute checklist, setting a friendly tone from the first reply.

  • Welcome every new member by name.
  • Answer questions within 24 hours.
  • Handle conflicts privately first.
  • Log actions and review monthly.
  • Welcome every new member within 24 hours by name, point them to one easy first action such as introducing themselves, and reply to their first post within a day.
  • Moderate with a checklist: read the report, check the rule, act on the ladder, record the decision, and message the member kindly, which takes under five minutes per case.
  • Back up key posts and files.
  • Keep a list of ideas members suggest.
Worked example. Two members argue; helper messages both, asks them to pause, thread locked for a day.

Activity. Keep a moderation log for the run.

Standard to meet. Log shows timings and outcomes.

Common mistake. Ignoring the first message.

Second worked example. A member posts an ad in a no-sales group. The moderator removes it, sends a kind note with the rule and where sales are allowed, and the member joins the sales thread.

Check yourself. What does your welcome say?

Module 7 Measure health 6 hEvaluate

Numbers show whether people value it. You will track active share, replies per post and thirty-day return, and read them to see whether people truly value the space.

  • Active rate = active members divided by all members.
  • Posts per week, replies per post, new members, leavers.
  • Ask three members what they value.
  • Note who is missing.
  • Track four numbers weekly: new members, active members who posted or replied, replies per post, and members who return after 30 days; focus on active share, not total size.
  • Aim for 20 percent of members active in a month; if you are at 10 percent, plan one change, such as a weekly question, and re-check after four weeks.
  • Ask quiet members one easy question.
  • Review how conflicts were handled and what to change.
Worked example. 34 members, 12 active = 35%; replies per post 2.4; 3 leavers.

Activity. Compute five measures weekly.

Standard to meet. Table updated for six weeks.

Common mistake. Measuring likes only.

Second worked example. After a month: 80 members, 22 post or comment (28%), 410 posts, 9 reports, 12 left. The active share is the number to improve next.

Check yourself. What is your active rate?

Module 8 Improve and sustain 4 hCreate

Communities need care. You will hold a monthly member review and recruit two volunteers, so the community improves and no longer depends on one person.

  • Share leadership with helpers.
  • Plan events: talk, challenge, meet.
  • Decide what to stop.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Hold a monthly review with members: ask what they like, what they would change and who might help run events, and note the answers in a shared list.
  • Share the work by inviting two volunteers to help with welcoming or events, giving them a clear task list so the community does not rest on one person.
  • Share your health report with helpers.
  • Plan the next 90 days with one event each month.
Worked example. Low replies on long posts: shorten posts and ask one question.

Activity. Write the health report and plan.

Standard to meet. Three changes with dates.

Common mistake. Doing everything alone.

Second worked example. Fewer members join on weekends. The team moves the 'swap' event from Wednesday to Saturday morning, and joins on Saturdays rise from 2 to 7.

Check yourself. Who are your helpers?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. The argument

Two members insult each other.

Question: First steps?

Model answer: Message both privately, pause the thread, apply the rule and log it.

Case 2. The quiet group

Only three members post.

Question: What do you try?

Model answer: Ask easier questions, feature member stories, invite quiet members to share short tips.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is a community promise?

Model answer: What a member gets within a week. (Covered in module 1: Why a community. To pass that module: All three fit in 40 words.)

Q2. Why publish rules first?

Model answer: So members know what is expected from day one. (Covered in module 2: Rules and safety. To pass that module: Rules short and enforceable.)

Q3. How do you compute active rate?

Model answer: Active members divided by all members. (Covered in module 7: Measure health. To pass that module: Table updated for six weeks.)

Q4. Why avoid fake followers?

Model answer: They harm trust and give false numbers. (Covered in module 5: Grow honestly. To pass that module: At least 30 real members joined.)

Q5. What to do with private data posted?

Model answer: Remove at once, explain and log. (Covered in module 2: Rules and safety. To pass that module: Rules short and enforceable.)

Q6. How fast should reports be handled?

Model answer: Within 24 hours. (Covered in module 6: Moderate and welcome. To pass that module: Log shows timings and outcomes.)

Q7. Name four content types.

Model answer: Teach, ask, show, celebrate. (Covered in module 4: Content plan. To pass that module: Calendar covers four content types.)

Q8. Why welcome by name?

Model answer: It makes people feel seen and more likely to stay. (Covered in module 6: Moderate and welcome. To pass that module: Log shows timings and outcomes.)

Q9. Who might be missing?

Model answer: People without access, with other languages or time zones. (Covered in module 7: Measure health. To pass that module: Table updated for six weeks.)

Q10. What do you do after a health report?

Model answer: Choose three changes with dates. (Covered in module 8: Improve and sustain. To pass that module: Three changes with dates.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Platform choice, age rules and content laws differ by place; check what members use and what your law requires. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a Brainport-area small firm.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Partner to practise with: SEBRAE small-business support, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or a SkillsFuture-linked training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a TAFE or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico (Central America)

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
