Stem Cell Therapy (Awareness Level)

Understand in plain words what stem cells are, what approved treatments exist, how to spot unproven clinics, and the ethics involved. Awareness only.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2 Awareness16:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain what stem cells are and the main types.
  • Apply Use a checklist to judge a clinic or advert.
  • Analyse Separate approved uses from experimental claims.
  • Evaluate Weigh hope against evidence and cost.
  • Create Draft a short guide for patients and families.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a one-page safe-choice guide on stem cell treatments with a clinic checklist and a glossary.

Condition

A computer, public information, two peers and a mentor. No medical advice is given and no patient records are used.

Standard

Facts are sourced and dated; approved and experimental uses are kept apart; no promise of cure appears; the guide tells readers to ask a qualified specialist; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Patients, carers, teachers, health writers and trainee lab staff. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What stem cells are 2 hUnderstand

Stem cells can copy themselves and can become other kinds of cell. That is why they matter in medicine.

  • Types: embryonic (from early embryos), adult or tissue (in bone marrow, skin, gut) and induced (adult cells reprogrammed in a lab).
  • Each type has different uses, limits and ethical questions.
  • Your body already uses stem cells every day, for example to renew blood and skin.
  • A stem cell in a lab is not a treatment until it has passed regulated testing.
Worked example. Blood stem cells in bone marrow keep making new red cells, white cells and platelets throughout life. A donor transplant replaces a patient's marrow when it is damaged.

Activity. Draw a picture of one stem cell dividing into one copy and one specialised cell, and label three types.

Standard to meet. Picture is accurate and labels are correct.

Module 2 Established and approved uses 2 hUnderstand

Some stem cell treatments are established. Many claims are not.

  • Blood stem cell transplants are used for certain blood cancers and inherited blood disorders.
  • Skin grafts and corneal surface treatments using stem cells exist for some conditions.
  • Approval means regulators reviewed trial evidence for a named condition.
  • Most other uses are experimental and belong in registered trials.
Worked example. A hospital matches a patient with a donor, checks tissue types, gives the transplant under specialist care and follows up for years. This is regulated, documented and repeatable.

Activity. List three approved uses and name a public source for each.

Standard to meet. Sources are regulators or recognised hospitals and are dated.

Module 3 Trials and evidence 2 hAnalyse

Medicine moves from idea to approval in stages, and each stage reduces risk.

  • Stages: laboratory work, small safety trials, larger effectiveness trials, regulator review, approval, monitoring.
  • Patient stories are not proof; control groups and large numbers are.
  • Registered trials appear in public registries.
  • Ask: Is this approved, or is it in a registered trial?
Worked example. A clinic advertises success stories only. The learner searches a trial registry and finds no registered trial for the claimed condition, so rates the claim unproven.

Activity. Check one claim in a public trial registry and write the result.

Standard to meet. Result states registry searched, terms used and date.

Module 4 Spotting unproven clinics 2 hEvaluate

Some clinics sell hope at high prices without evidence. Families can lose money and delay real care.

  • Red flags: cures many unrelated diseases, pay in advance, no registered trial, no follow-up, pressure to decide fast.
  • Risks include infection, unwanted growths and lost time.
  • Ask for the approval or trial number and check it yourself.
  • Talk to your own specialist before travelling.
Worked example. A family is offered an injection costing a year's income for a condition with no approved stem cell treatment. They ask for the trial number, receive none, and decline.

Activity. Score two real adverts with the red-flag list and justify each score.

Standard to meet. Each score cites the advert wording.

Module 5 Ethics and sources 2 hEvaluate

People hold different views on where cells come from and how they should be used.

  • Donors must give informed and free consent, with the right to withdraw.
  • Embryo use is debated; religions and cultures differ.
  • Cord blood banking is a personal choice with limited current uses.
  • Fairness: who can afford treatment, and who does research benefit?
Worked example. A parents' guide explains cord blood choices, costs and the limits of use without pressure and tells parents to ask their doctor.

Activity. Write a balanced paragraph on one ethical question presenting two views fairly.

Standard to meet. Both views are stated in terms their holders would accept.

Module 6 Talking with specialists 2 hApply

A prepared patient gets better answers.

  • Questions: what is this for, is it approved, what are the risks, what does it cost, what are the alternatives, what follow-up is needed.
  • Bring a family member and take notes.
  • Second opinions are normal.
  • Ask for written information.
Worked example. A patient brings a one-page question list to the haematologist, gets written answers and decides after a second opinion.

Activity. Prepare a question list of at least eight questions for a specialist visit.

Standard to meet. Questions are clear and cover approval, risk, cost and follow-up.

Module 7 Cost, travel and safety 2 hEvaluate

Medical travel adds risks beyond the procedure.

  • Check licence of clinic and doctor in the destination.
  • Count the full cost: fee, flights, stay, follow-up at home, complications.
  • Plan who provides follow-up care when you return.
  • Keep records of everything given.
Worked example. A traveller totals fee 12,00,000 INR (figures are estimates; check current prices), flights and stay 1,50,000 INR, and complication cover 2,00,000 INR (total 15,50,000 INR), then decides against it after comparing with an approved local option.

Activity. Cost a hypothetical trip and list five safety questions.

Standard to meet. Total includes all cost items with currency.

Module 8 Write the safe-choice guide 2 hCreate

Turn the course into one useful page for patients and families.

  • One page, plain words, large print.
  • Include the clinic checklist and local regulator contacts.
  • Add a glossary of five terms.
  • Test with two readers and fix confusing parts.
Worked example. Readers say they would first ask "is it in a registered trial?" and know where to look it up.

Activity. Finish the guide and test it.

Standard to meet. Readers can repeat three red flags without notes.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: guide, clinic checklist, glossary. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Red-flag useNoneListedApplied to real adverts
Balanced ethicsOne-sidedBoth viewsBoth views with respect
Evidence stageTreatments described without their stageEach treatment is marked approved, trial-only or unproven, with sourceEach stage is checked against a public register on a stated date
Clinic red flagsNo clinic checksChecklist covers licence, approval, advertised cures and feesChecklist tried on two real clinic websites, with findings recorded

Model answer: Adult (tissue) and embryonic (or induced). (Covered in module 1: What stem cells are. To pass that module: Picture is accurate and labels are correct.)

Q2. Name one long-established use.

Model answer: Blood stem cell transplant for some blood disorders. (Covered in module 2: Established and approved uses. To pass that module: Sources are regulators or recognised hospitals and are dated.)

Q3. Are patient stories proof?

Model answer: No. (Covered in module 3: Trials and evidence. To pass that module: Result states registry searched, terms used and date.)

Q4. Name two red flags.

Model answer: Cures many diseases; pay up front or fast pressure. (Covered in module 4: Spotting unproven clinics. To pass that module: Each score cites the advert wording.)

Q5. What question tests a clinic?

Model answer: Is it approved or in a registered trial? (Covered in module 4: Spotting unproven clinics. To pass that module: Each score cites the advert wording.)

Q6. Name one risk.

Model answer: Infection, tumours, wasted money or delayed care. (Covered in module 4: Spotting unproven clinics. To pass that module: Each score cites the advert wording.)

Q7. Why consent?

Model answer: Donors must freely agree with full information. (Covered in module 5: Ethics and sources. To pass that module: Both views are stated in terms their holders would accept.)

Q8. Is a second opinion normal?

Model answer: Yes. (Covered in module 6: Talking with specialists. To pass that module: Questions are clear and cover approval, risk, cost and follow-up.)

Q9. What cost parts does travel add?

Model answer: Flights, stay and follow-up. (Covered in module 7: Cost, travel and safety. To pass that module: Total includes all cost items with currency.)

Q10. Who gives medical advice?

Model answer: Qualified specialists, not this course. (Covered in module 6: Talking with specialists. To pass that module: Questions are clear and cover approval, risk, cost and follow-up.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Stem cell treatment rules and licensing differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
