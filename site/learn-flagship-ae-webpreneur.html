Webpreneur

Learn webpreneur for the UAE: rules, workflow, costing in AED and a recorded 30-day practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 177 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Understand Explain the UAE market, rules and hazards for webpreneur.
  • Apply Run the workflow: pick a problem, build a simple offer, test, sell and support.
  • Analyse Measure paying customers after 30 days and find the biggest cause of loss.
  • Evaluate Cost and price in AED and judge scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a record pack a supervisor can audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a real or simulated 30-day pilot for a small web-based business in the UAE and keep a full record of every step, cost and result.

Condition

About 177 hours over 3 to 6 months. A partner site or a simulated customer, a free spreadsheet, a phone, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Hazards named with controls; every number has a unit and AED; records complete; rules confirmed with the local authority; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better.

Process learning only. Main risk to manage: weak privacy and false claims. Follow UAE federal and emirate rules and any licence needed before you charge a customer. Start small and prove one paying customer first.

Requirements: none. Currency: AED on every number.

lesson by lesson

11 modules with worked examples (177 h guided)

Module 1 Map the market and the people 5 hUnderstand

Start with who needs a small web-based business and why. Around niche customers online drive demand in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other emirates.

  • List 10 offers or suppliers in the UAE with price per customer in AED and who buys.
  • Interview 5 people from niche customers online: what do they buy now, how often, what do they pay, what annoys them?
  • Learn three terms: Offer (what you sell and to whom); Landing page (a page built to get one action); Churn (customers who stop paying).
  • Write a one-page brief: user, need, hoped-for price, and the rule that matters most.
Worked example. Five interviews all say delay is the main pain. You write: 'Reliable a small web-based business, answer in one day, price near AED 240 per customer.'

Activity. Complete the market table, five interview notes and the brief.

Standard to meet. Ten offers priced; five users heard; one need stated in one sentence.

Module 2 Rules, hazards and licences 6 hApply

Know the rule before the work. Main hazards here: weak privacy and false claims. Hazard thinking means list each step, ask what could go wrong, and decide the control.

  • Ask your local authority or free zone which licence, permit or inspection applies. Write the answer and the date you asked.
  • For each step (pick a problem; build a simple offer; test; sell and support) write the hazard and the control.
  • Use a hierarchy: remove the hazard, replace it, separate people from it, change the way of work, then protective equipment last.
  • Keep a one-page incident rule: who you call, what you stop, what you record.
Worked example. Step 'pick a problem': hazard = weak privacy; control = written check before start and a named supervisor; record = signed log line.

Activity. Draw the flow and mark hazard and control at each step.

Standard to meet. Every step has a hazard and a control; licence question answered in writing.

Module 3 Core workflow, step by step 12 hApply

The work is a short chain: pick a problem, build a simple offer, test, sell and support. Learn each link, then link them with records.

  • Write a checklist for each of the 4 steps: input, action, check, output.
  • Time each step three times and note the slowest.
  • Record a defect or delay each time it happens: what, where, cause, fix.
  • Measure paying customers after 30 days; this is your main number.
Worked example. Three timed runs of 'build a simple offer' take 42, 38 and 51 minutes. Average 43.7; the 51-minute run had a missing tool. Fix: a tool tray checked at start.

Activity. Run the chain three times (simulated or real) and fill the log.

Standard to meet. Three complete logs; the slowest step has a cause and a fix.

Module 4 Quality and measurement 10 hAnalyse

What you do not measure you cannot improve. Your main measure is paying customers after 30 days.

  • Define paying customers after 30 days: what counts, what does not, and over what period.
  • Draw a simple chart of the last 10 results and mark the average.
  • Find the top two causes of loss with a tally sheet, then fix the biggest one first.
  • Check measuring tools: are they accurate, clean and in date?
Worked example. Tally of 40 faults: 22 from late inputs, 9 from wrong settings, 9 other. Fix late inputs first: that is 55% of faults.

Activity. Collect a tally for one week and make a Pareto chart.

Standard to meet. Tally complete; biggest cause named; one fix planned with a date.

Module 5 Costing and pricing in AED 12 hEvaluate

Know the cost of one customer before you set the price. Include materials, labour, energy, transport, licence share, waste and a safety margin.

  • Cost per customer = total cost divided by customers sold (not made).
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs divided by gain per unit.
  • Test the price with two users and write down the answer.
Worked example. At AED 240 per customer and cost AED 90, gain is AED 150 and margin 62.5%. At 40 customers a month, gross gain is AED 6,000. If fixed costs are AED 720, break-even is 5 customers.

Activity. Build the cost sheet with AED on every line and have your mentor check it.

Standard to meet. No missing cost found; margin calculated twice by different routes.

Module 6 Serve and sell responsibly 12 hApply

Customers return when you keep promises. Promise only what your workflow can deliver.

  • Write a one-page offer: what is included, what is not, price, time, how you fix mistakes.
  • Write a short script for the first call or visit.
  • Agree dates in writing; confirm each change.
  • Handle a complaint in four steps: listen, apologise for the impact, fix, record what changed.
Worked example. Offer: 'A small web-based business delivered within one working day; price AED 240 per customer; faults corrected free within 3 days.'

Activity. Present the offer to two users and note changes.

Standard to meet. Offer has scope, price, time and fix rule; two users commented.

Module 7 Run the 30-day pilot 55 hCreate

Small, real and recorded. Change one thing at a time and learn quickly.

  • Keep a daily log: date, units, cost, delays, defects, safety notes.
  • Hold a 15-minute review each week with your mentor.
  • Write down every complaint and what you changed.
  • Take photos only where allowed and never of people without consent.
Worked example. Week 2 shows paying customers after 30 days at 71%; week 4 at 84% after the tool tray and an earlier input check.

Activity. Complete 30 days of the log and four weekly reviews.

Standard to meet. 30 days logged; each defect has a cause; weekly notes kept.

Module 8 Review, decide and plan 38 hEvaluate

A decision beats a feeling. Compare plan with result and choose to scale, change or stop.

  • Make a before and after table: cost per unit, defects, delays, repeat users.
  • Write three lessons.
  • Decide scale, change or stop, with the numbers behind it.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
Worked example. Result: cost per customer fell from AED 90 to AED 84.60; repeat users 6 of 9. Decision: scale to a second site; keep price.

Activity. Present to a review room and collect one written comment from a user.

Standard to meet. Decision backed by numbers; two peer reviews given.

Module 9 Validate before you build 9 hAnalyse

Prove one paying customer first.

  • Interview 10 people about the problem.
  • Offer a simple paid test.
  • Measure sign-ups and payments.
  • Stop or change if no one pays.
Worked example. 10 interviews, 3 say they would pay, 1 pays AED 100 for a test.

Activity. Run a smoke test page.

Standard to meet. Numbers recorded.

Module 10 Support, privacy and growth 9 hEvaluate

Support and trust keep customers.

  • Collect only needed data.
  • Reply within a day.
  • Track churn monthly.
  • Improve the top complaint.
Worked example. Churn 8% per month; fixing onboarding brings it to 5%.

Activity. Draft a privacy note and support plan.

Standard to meet. Data minimised.

Module 11 Terms, consent and honest claims 9 hEvaluate

An online business is judged on what it promises. Clear terms, honest marketing and proper consent keep customers and help you avoid complaints and penalties.

  • State price, what is included, and the refund rule before the customer pays.
  • Do not invent reviews, fake scarcity or results you cannot show.
  • Ask permission before sending marketing messages and make stopping easy.
  • Keep a log of complaints and how each was solved.
Worked example. A course page says "only 3 places left" but 40 places are open. You change it to "next class starts 15 March, 40 places". Sales fall from 12 to 9 a week at first, but refund requests drop from 4 to 1 a month, saving AED 600 (3 fewer refunds at AED 200 each).

Activity. Audit one real sales page and list every claim that cannot be proved, with a corrected wording.

Standard to meet. Every claim is either proved, reworded or removed; refund rule is visible before payment.

levels L1 to L7

Slide by slide, with notes

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: What a web business sells

On the slide: L1 Basic · What a web business sells

Notes: Name the offer, the customer and the landing page of one small web business. Notes: pick a real example and ask who pays and for what; the course word for customers who stop paying is churn.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Privacy and honest claims

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Privacy and honest claims

Notes: Describe the two main risks, weak privacy and false claims, and one rule for each. Notes: collect only the data you need, state price, what is included and the refund rule before payment, and never invent reviews or fake scarcity.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Write a one-page offer

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Write a one-page offer

Notes: Write a one-page offer with who it is for, what is included, the price and how mistakes are fixed. Notes: check that a stranger can read it in one minute; the course example is AED 240 per customer.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Prove one paying customer

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Prove one paying customer

Notes: Run a simple paid test: interview 10 people, offer the test and record sign-ups and payments. Notes: the course case is 10 interviews, 3 who say they would pay and 1 who pays AED 100; stop or change if no one pays.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Judge the numbers and choose

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Judge the numbers and choose

Notes: Analyse cost, margin and customers after 30 days and decide to scale, change or stop. Notes: at AED 240 and AED 90 cost, the gain is AED 150 (62.5 per cent) and break-even on AED 720 fixed costs is 5 customers; compare paying customers after 30 days with that target.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Coach support and consent

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Coach support and consent

Notes: Set a support routine and a consent check for a small team and review it weekly. Notes: sample five customer messages a week against a checklist for clear terms, permission to send marketing and an easy way to stop.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Design the web business system

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Design the web business system

Notes: Design the full system for a web business with terms, privacy, support, growth targets and a 12-month plan. Notes: audit claims and consent records for gaps; at 40 customers a month the gross gain is AED 6,000, so set the yearly target and one risk limit against it.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
