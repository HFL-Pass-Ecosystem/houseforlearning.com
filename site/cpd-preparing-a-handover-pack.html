CPD · Building, construction and property

Preparing a handover pack

Collecting the records an owner needs at completion.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 3 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playBuilding, construction and property

Collecting the records an owner needs at completion.

  • List handover items, given a contract, in a complete checklist.
  • Collect documents, given a sample project, with each named, dated and filed.
  • Verify completeness, given the checklist, with no missing item.
  • Present the pack, given an owner role-play, explaining where to find each item.

Task, condition and standard

Task

List the handover items a client needs, check a pack against the contract list, build an index, and present the pack to the client for signed acceptance

Condition

Given a contract list, a sample pack with planted gaps, an index template and a client role-play, in a 3-hour practical session

Standard

Find every missing item in a pack, list each outstanding item with a chase date, index every document by name, date and location, and obtain a signed acceptance with no item missing from the checklist

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 What clients need to keep 20 min
  • The client keeps records of what was built and how to run it.
  • Use the contract list as the master checklist.
  • Group items by type.
Worked example. Contract list of 7 items: as-built drawings, test certificates, manuals, warranties, maintenance schedule, contact list, permits. The checklist has 7 lines, each to be ticked when found.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: list handover items, given a contract, in a complete checklist.

Standard to meet. List the handover items a client needs to keep, given a contract, such as as-built drawings, test certificates, manuals, warranties and permits, in a complete checklist.

Module 2 Checking for gaps 30 min
  • Compare the pack with the contract list line by line.
  • A missing or undated item is a gap.
  • List each gap with a chase date.
Worked example. Sample pack: 7 items required, 4 found, 3 planted gaps. Outstanding: warranties, one test certificate, maintenance schedule. Each gets an owner and a chase date 5 working days away.

Practise: Take the end check for "preparing a handover pack": show that you can collect documents, given a sample project, with each named, dated and filed, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Check a sample pack against the contract list, finding every missing item, and list each outstanding item with a chase date.

Module 3 Building the index 20 min
  • Every item has a name, a date and a place.
  • Number the index so items are easy to find.
  • Keep the index in the same order as the checklist.
Worked example. Index line: 4.2 | Boiler warranty | dated 2026-02-10 | folder W3, page 1. Seven items, seven index lines, in the checklist order.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: verify completeness, given the checklist, with no missing item.

Standard to meet. Build an index of the pack, given the documents, with each item named, dated and filed, so the client can find any item quickly.

Module 4 Walk-through with the client 15 min
  • Walk the client through the index.
  • Show where each item is kept.
  • Get the signed acceptance and give the client a copy.
Worked example. Role-play: client asks where the boiler warranty is. Answer: index 4.2, folder W3, page 1. Then signed acceptance, one client copy and one project archive copy: 2 copies, 1 signature page.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: present the pack, given an owner role-play, explaining where to find each item.

Standard to meet. Present the pack to a client in a role-play, explaining where to find each item, and obtain the signed acceptance and a client copy alongside the project archive copy.

Module 5 Practice 10 min
  • Name, date, file and index every document.
  • Check the checklist before you hand over.
  • No item missing.
Worked example. Sample project pack: 7 of 7 items named, dated, filed and indexed, with the index matching the checklist. Result: complete, ready to present.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "preparing a handover pack" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (list handover items; collect documents; verify completeness; present the pack).

Standard to meet. Prepare a pack for a sample project, with every document named, dated, filed and indexed, and with no item missing from the checklist.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Check a finished pack against the list.
  • Find all three planted gaps.
  • Confirm that every item is present at the end.
Worked example. Pack with 3 planted gaps: find 3 of 3, list each with a chase date, and after the chase confirm 7 of 7 items present before sign-off.

Practise: Take the end check for "preparing a handover pack": show that you can present the pack, given an owner role-play, explaining where to find each item, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Verify a finished pack with three planted gaps, finding every gap and confirming that every item on the contract list is present.

Module 7 Second worked case: missing warranty and test record 40 min
  • Find both gaps by comparing with the contract list.
  • List each as outstanding with an owner and a date.
  • Do not mark the pack complete.
Worked example. Case card 2: the pack lacks the warranty papers and the test record for circuit 5. Outstanding list: warranty papers, supplier, chase 24 March; circuit 5 record, electrician, chase 24 March. Status: 5 of 7 items present.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: present the pack, given an owner role-play, explaining where to find each item. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Find two missing items in a pack, list each as outstanding with an owner and a chase date, and state that the pack is not complete until both arrive.

Module 8 Teach-back and peer check 40 min
  • Swap packs with a partner.
  • Count items against the contract list.
  • Say what is missing in one clear sentence.
Worked example. A partner says the pack is complete, but 6 of the 7 contract items are present. The maintenance schedule is missing, so the pack is not complete and the schedule goes on the outstanding list.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template: present the pack, given an owner role-play, explaining where to find each item. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate a partner's pack against the contract list, find every missing item, and state clearly whether the pack is ready for client acceptance.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Why an index?

Model answer: So the client finds items fast. (Covered in module 3: Building the index. To pass that module: Build an index of the pack, given the documents, with each item named, dated and filed, so the client can find any item quickly.)

Name 3 documents.

Model answer: Warranties, test certificates, manuals. (Covered in module 1: What clients need to keep. To pass that module: List the handover items a client needs to keep, given a contract, such as as-built drawings, test certificates, manuals, warranties and permits, in a complete checklist.)

Who accepts?

Model answer: The client or their representative, signed. (Covered in module 4: Walk-through with the client. To pass that module: Present the pack to a client in a role-play, explaining where to find each item, and obtain the signed acceptance and a client copy alongside the project archive copy.)

When is it complete?

Model answer: When every item on the contract list is present. (Covered in module 2: Checking for gaps. To pass that module: Check a sample pack against the contract list, finding every missing item, and list each outstanding item with a chase date.)

Name three documents.

Model answer: Drawings, warranties, test records. (Covered in module 1: What clients need to keep. To pass that module: List the handover items a client needs to keep, given a contract, such as as-built drawings, test certificates, manuals, warranties and permits, in a complete checklist.)

Why an index?

Model answer: So the client can find items later. (Covered in module 3: Building the index. To pass that module: Build an index of the pack, given the documents, with each item named, dated and filed, so the client can find any item quickly.)

Who signs the handover?

Model answer: The client and the lead. (Covered in module 4: Walk-through with the client. To pass that module: Present the pack to a client in a role-play, explaining where to find each item, and obtain the signed acceptance and a client copy alongside the project archive copy.)

What if a document is late?

Model answer: List it as outstanding with a date. (Covered in module 2: Checking for gaps. To pass that module: Check a sample pack against the contract list, finding every missing item, and list each outstanding item with a chase date.)

Where stored?

Model answer: Both client copy and project archive. (Covered in module 4: Walk-through with the client. To pass that module: Present the pack to a client in a role-play, explaining where to find each item, and obtain the signed acceptance and a client copy alongside the project archive copy.)

A contract lists 7 handover items and the pack holds 5. How many are outstanding and what do you do?

Model answer: Two are outstanding; list each with an owner and chase date, and do not mark the pack complete. (Covered in module 2: Checking for gaps. To pass that module: Check a sample pack against the contract list, finding every missing item, and list each outstanding item with a chase date.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What handover means. Speaker note: Handover is the point when a finished building passes to its owner. At this first step the learner can say what a handover pack is and why the owner needs warranties, drawings and test records.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: What a client keeps. Speaker note: The learner can name the usual pack items: as-built drawings, test certificates, manuals, warranties, a maintenance schedule, a contact list and permits. They know the contract lists exactly what must be in the pack.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Check and index. Speaker note: The learner can tick a pack against a checklist and write an index line with name, date and location, with help. They follow the worked example where three planted gaps are found.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Prepare and present a pack. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner checks a pack against the contract, lists outstanding items with chase dates, builds the index, presents it to the client in a role-play and obtains a signed acceptance.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Complex projects. Speaker note: The specialist assembles packs for projects with many trades and phases. They track late documents, keep revisions straight, and agree acceptance of part-complete packs without hiding what is outstanding.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Audit and improve. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner audits packs from several projects, finds repeated gaps, and sets earlier collection points so documents are gathered during the work and not left to the end.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Own the handover system. Speaker note: The super expert designs the pack standard, index template and acceptance process for a company. They train teams, review client feedback and decide how records are kept after completion.
