flagship course

Hobby and Craft Course Teaching

Teach hobbies such as music, painting, cooking, gardening or photography so beginners finish something they are proud of, safely and at a fair cost.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 35 h lessons plus practice

Teach hobbies such as music, painting, cooking, gardening or photography so beginners finish something they are proud of, safely and at a fair cost.

  • Define a beginner outcome that can be seen.
  • Apply demonstrate, copy, vary and share teaching.
  • Analyse learner progress and common mistakes.
  • Evaluate materials, cost and safety.
  • Create a four-session course and a showcase.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and teach a four-session hobby course for beginners and run a small showcase of finished work.

Condition

About 10 weeks part-time. A group of six to twelve learners, a safe space, basic materials, a mentor.

Standard

Every session ends with a finished item, safety rules are written and followed, costs per learner are known, and each learner receives specific feedback.

Who it is for: Hobbyists, teachers, retirees, small studio owners. Requirements: Solid skill in one hobby. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Choose a beginner outcome 3 hDefine
  • Pick a small item a beginner can finish in one session.
  • Write the outcome so anyone can see if it is done.
  • Fit the task to age, time and budget.
  • Plan a stretch version for those who finish early.
Worked example. Painting outcome: a simple flower in three colours on a postcard-size card in 60 minutes.

Activity. Write outcomes for four sessions.

Standard to meet. Each outcome is visible and fits time and budget.

Module 2 Break the skill into steps 5 hAnalyse
  • List the steps in the order a beginner needs them.
  • Mark where beginners usually go wrong.
  • Create a quick drill for the hardest step.
  • Test your steps on one beginner and fix them.
Worked example. Cooking: wash, chop evenly, heat oil, add spices in order, taste, serve; the drill is chopping onions to equal size.

Activity. Break down one skill and test it with a beginner.

Standard to meet. Steps are in order and one drill is tested.

Module 3 Materials, cost and sourcing 4 hEvaluate
  • Make a kit list with quantities for each learner.
  • Find safe, low-cost sources and reuse materials.
  • Compute cost per learner and a fair fee for your time.
  • Plan clean up and storage.
Worked example. Kit for ten learners: paper, brushes, three paints; cost Rs 2,500, or Rs 250 each.

Activity. Cost a kit for a group.

Standard to meet. Totals are right and sources are named.

Module 4 Teach a session 6 hApply
  • Use four beats: demonstrate, copy, vary, share.
  • Demonstrate slowly with the camera or learners close.
  • Walk around and fix one thing for each learner.
  • Praise effort and a specific detail.
Worked example. After copying the flower, learners change colours and add a second flower, then show it to a neighbour.

Activity. Teach 30 minutes to peers.

Standard to meet. Peers note clear steps and at least one correction per learner.

Module 5 Mixed ages and abilities 4 hApply
  • Give an extension for fast finishers and an easier start for those who struggle.
  • Pair strong learners with new ones.
  • Keep the pace gentle and no one sits out.
  • Respect different languages and abilities.
Worked example. A learner with a weak grip uses a thicker brush with a foam grip.

Activity. Plan two adjustments for a mixed group.

Standard to meet. Each adjustment is realistic and respectful.

Module 6 Safety and care 4 hApply
  • Name the hazards: sharp, hot, chemical, electrical, dust.
  • Keep first aid, water and a named adult ready.
  • Teach safe habits in the first five minutes.
  • Record incidents and fix causes.
Worked example. Knife station only with an adult nearby; hot pans carried with cloth and called out.

Activity. Write a one-page safety sheet and use it in a session.

Standard to meet. Sheet covers main hazards and the contact plan.

Module 7 Feedback and the showcase 4 hEvaluate
  • Give feedback in three parts: what works, one change, next step.
  • Display finished work with names.
  • Invite families to a short showcase.
  • Collect a simple feedback form.
Worked example. Feedback: your colours are bold; leave a little more white around the petals; next time try a second leaf.

Activity. Run a mini showcase and collect feedback.

Standard to meet. Feedback is specific; form results are summarised.

Module 8 Capstone: four-session course 5 hCreate
  • Write four session plans.
  • Teach at least one with a mentor present.
  • Gather feedback and costs.
  • Write a report with changes for next time.
Worked example. A weekend course in four sessions ending with a small exhibition.

Activity. Present the plan and the report.

Standard to meet. Plans have outcomes, steps, kit, safety and costs; report names three changes.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why choose a small finished item?

Model answer: It builds confidence and motivation. (Covered in module 1: Choose a beginner outcome. To pass that module: Each outcome is visible and fits time and budget.)

Q2. Why test with a real beginner?

Model answer: You see steps you skipped without noticing. (Covered in module 2: Break the skill into steps. To pass that module: Steps are in order and one drill is tested.)

Q3. How is cost per learner found?

Model answer: Total cost divided by number of learners. (Covered in module 3: Materials, cost and sourcing. To pass that module: Totals are right and sources are named.)

Q4. Name the four teaching beats.

Model answer: Demonstrate, copy, vary, share. (Covered in module 4: Teach a session. To pass that module: Peers note clear steps and at least one correction per learner.)

Q5. How help a fast finisher?

Model answer: Give an extension task. (Covered in module 5: Mixed ages and abilities. To pass that module: Each adjustment is realistic and respectful.)

Q6. Name one safety habit to teach first.

Model answer: Safe handling of tools and heat. (Covered in module 6: Safety and care. To pass that module: Sheet covers main hazards and the contact plan.)

Q7. What are the three parts of feedback?

Model answer: What works, one change, next step. (Covered in module 7: Feedback and the showcase. To pass that module: Feedback is specific; form results are summarised.)

Q8. What belongs in the final report?

Model answer: Outcomes met, feedback, costs and changes. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: four-session course. To pass that module: Plans have outcomes, steps, kit, safety and costs; report names three changes.)

Q9. Why plan an extension?

Model answer: Keeps fast finishers engaged. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why keep an incident record?

Model answer: To prevent repeats. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know your own hobby well.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain steps to a friend.
  3. L3 Foundation. Help others in a class.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Teach a four-session course.
  5. L5 Specialist. Teach advanced groups and design workshops.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead a studio and train teachers.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set standards and build a network of studios.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Know your hobby

Show your hobby with 3 finished pieces and name the tools and materials used.

Speaker notes. Teacher inspects the pieces and asks about tools. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Explain steps

Explain a hobby task in 6 clear steps so a friend can repeat it.

Speaker notes. A friend follows the steps and the teacher notes any gap. Covered in module 2.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Help in a class

Help in a class for 3 sessions, preparing materials and answering simple questions.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the materials list. Covered in module 3.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Teach a short course

Teach a four-session course for 6 learners, each leaving with a finished piece.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the session plans and finished pieces. Covered in module 8.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Teach advanced groups

Teach an advanced group and design a workshop for mixed ages with a costed materials list.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the cost sheet and the safety notes.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Lead a studio

Lead a studio with 3 teachers, a shared safety list of 8 points and a showcase each term.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the safety list and showcase plan.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Build a studio network

Set standards and build a network of 3 studios with a common teaching checklist.

Speaker notes. Teacher tests the checklist on a visit.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
