flagship course

Water Management and Conservation

Measure, save and manage water at home, farm, school or business, with leak checks, rainwater, reuse and fair sharing.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner32:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain the water cycle and local water risks.
  • Apply Do a water audit and leak test.
  • Analyse Find the biggest savings.
  • Evaluate Judge quality, safety and cost of reuse.
  • Create Write a water-saving plan with targets.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Audit water use at one site, plan savings, design a rainwater or reuse option and set a measurable target.

Condition

A real site or a practice case, a bucket and timer or meter, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor.

Standard

Audit lists all uses in litres; leak test is documented; savings and payback are calculated; water safety rules are stated; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Facility managers, farmers, school staff, residents, community groups. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Water cycle and local risk 3 hUnderstand

Know where your water comes from and what could go wrong.

  • Rain, rivers, lakes, groundwater and storage.
  • Droughts, floods and quality problems.
  • Know your supply source, schedule and rules.
  • Cost and availability vary by season.
Worked example. A town cuts supply in summer; households that store water cope better.

Activity. Describe your water supply and one risk.

Standard to meet. Sources named and risk explained.

Module 2 Water audit 5 hApply

Measure first.

  • Read the meter at the same time daily.
  • List uses: taps, toilets, showers, cleaning, irrigation, laundry.
  • Convert to litres per person per day.
  • Compare with benchmarks.
Worked example. A home uses 180 L per person per day; the benchmark target is 135 L.

Activity. Do an audit for one week.

Standard to meet. Table complete.

Module 3 Finding leaks 4 hApply

Small drips cost a lot.

  • Night meter test: stop all use, check the meter does not move.
  • Dye test for toilets.
  • A drip per second wastes thousands of litres per year.
  • Fix quickly.
Worked example. A toilet leak is found with food dye and repaired with a new valve, saving 20 L per day.

Activity. Run a leak test and record results.

Standard to meet. Result recorded with fix.

Module 4 Saving at the tap 4 hAnalyse

Cheap measures work.

  • Aerators, efficient flushes, full loads, shorter showers.
  • Behaviour and signs.
  • Track savings.
  • Maintenance matters.
Worked example. Aerators cut tap flow by 25 per cent.

Activity. Choose five measures and estimate savings.

Standard to meet. Savings estimated with numbers.

Module 5 Rainwater harvesting 5 hCreate

Catch and store rain.

  • Roof area x rainfall x efficiency = litres per year.
  • First-flush diverter, filter and covered tank.
  • Use for non-drinking unless treated.
  • Maintain tanks.
Worked example. A 100 m2 roof and 800 mm rain with 80 per cent efficiency gives about 64,000 L a year.

Activity. Design a system.

Standard to meet. Calculation correct.

Module 6 Reuse and water quality 4 hEvaluate

Grey water and treatment need care.

  • Grey water from basins can irrigate non-edible plants with care.
  • Avoid contact with edible leaves where unsafe.
  • Test and treat drinking water.
  • Know local rules.
Worked example. Bath water irrigates ornamental plants but not salad crops.

Activity. List safe uses and unsafe uses.

Standard to meet. Rules clear.

Module 7 Irrigation and farming 3 hApply

Use less, grow more.

  • Drip lines, mulching, timing.
  • Soil moisture checks.
  • Crop choice for local rain.
  • Reduce evaporation.
Worked example. Drip cuts water use by about 40 per cent compared with flood irrigation.

Activity. Plan a small plot.

Standard to meet. Plan complete.

Module 8 Plan and target 4 hCreate

Make it stick.

  • Targets, owners, dates.
  • Report monthly.
  • Share with the community.
  • Celebrate wins.
Worked example. A school sets a 20 per cent cut in one year.

Activity. Present the plan.

Standard to meet. Target measurable.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: audit sheet, leak test record, plan, target. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
AuditIncompleteCompleteComplete with benchmarks
Savings planVagueNumbersNumbers with payback
SafetyMissingStatedTested
Honesty and evidenceClaims without proofEvery claim has a recordEvery claim linked to a dated record and checked by a peer
Safety, ethics and local rulesNot consideredChecked and written downChecked, dated and explained to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Litres per person per day?

Model answer: Total use / people / days. (Covered in module 1: Water cycle and risk. To pass that module: Sources named.)

Q2. Night meter test?

Model answer: No use but meter moves means a leak. (Covered in module 2: Water audit. To pass that module: Table complete.)

Q3. Rainwater formula?

Model answer: Area x rain x efficiency. (Covered in module 3: Finding leaks. To pass that module: Result recorded.)

Q4. Safe grey water use?

Model answer: Irrigation of non-edible plants. (Covered in module 4: Saving at the tap. To pass that module: Savings estimated.)

Q5. Aerator benefit?

Model answer: Reduces flow. (Covered in module 5: Rainwater harvesting. To pass that module: Calculation correct.)

Q6. Why first flush?

Model answer: Removes dirt. (Covered in module 6: Reuse and quality. To pass that module: Rules clear.)

Q7. Mulch use?

Model answer: Keeps moisture. (Covered in module 7: Irrigation and farming. To pass that module: Plan complete.)

Q8. Drinking water rule?

Model answer: Treat and test. (Covered in module 8: Plan and target. To pass that module: Target measurable.)

Q9. Why meters?

Model answer: To measure savings. (Covered in module 8: Plan and target. To pass that module: Target measurable.)

Q10. Why share plan?

Model answer: Community buy-in. (Covered in module 8: Plan and target. To pass that module: Target measurable.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Water rights, rainwater and reuse rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
