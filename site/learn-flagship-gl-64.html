flagship course

Water Management and Conservation

Measure, save and manage water at home, farm, school or business, with leak checks, rainwater, reuse and fair sharing.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner32:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain the water cycle and local water risks.
  • Apply Do a water audit and leak test.
  • Analyse Find the biggest savings.
  • Evaluate Judge quality, safety and cost of reuse.
  • Create Write a water-saving plan with targets.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Audit water use at one site, plan savings, design a rainwater or reuse option and set a measurable target.

Condition

A real site or a practice case, a bucket and timer or meter, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor.

Standard

Audit lists all uses in litres; leak test is documented; savings and payback are calculated; water safety rules are stated; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Facility managers, farmers, school staff, residents, community groups. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Water cycle and local risk 3 hUnderstand

Know where your water comes from and what could go wrong.

  • Sources: rain, rivers, lakes, groundwater and storage.
  • Risks: drought, flood, pollution and failing pipes.
  • Know your supplier, tariff, quality report and any local limits.
  • Storage can protect you during supply cuts.
Worked example. A town cuts supply for three hours a day in summer. Households that store 150 litres per person cope; those that do not buy tanker water at five times the price.

Activity. Describe your water supply, its schedule and one risk with source.

Standard to meet. Sources named; risk and storage size explained.

Module 2 Water audit 5 hApply

Measure before you save.

  • Read the meter at the same time daily for a week.
  • List uses: taps, toilets, showers, cleaning, laundry, irrigation and leaks.
  • Convert to litres per person per day.
  • Compare with a benchmark from your supplier or national guidance (litres per person per day).
Worked example. A household of five uses 900 litres a day: 180 per person. Garden use is 40 per cent in summer.

Activity. Complete a one-week audit and split indoor and outdoor use.

Standard to meet. Table is complete and totals match the meter.

Module 3 Finding and fixing leaks 4 hApply

Small leaks waste a lot.

  • Night meter test: stop all use; if the meter moves, there is a leak.
  • Dye test for toilets; check taps, hoses and float valves.
  • A drip per second wastes thousands of litres per year.
  • Measure flow by timing a bucket fill.
Worked example. A toilet leak is found with food dye. A new valve saves about 20 litres a day. A float valve in a roof tank wasted 60 litres a day before it was adjusted.

Activity. Run a leak test and record results and fixes.

Standard to meet. Result recorded with fix and saving.

Module 4 Saving at the tap 4 hAnalyse

Cheap measures give quick savings.

  • Aerators, low-flow shower heads, efficient flushes and full loads.
  • Behaviour: shorter showers, tap off while brushing, reuse of rinse water.
  • Signs and feedback help.
  • Rank measures by litres saved per INR spent.
Worked example. A low-flow shower head saves 6 litres per minute; an aerator cuts tap flow by 25 per cent.

Activity. Choose five measures and estimate savings.

Standard to meet. Savings are estimated with numbers.

Module 5 Rainwater harvesting 5 hCreate

Catch rain where it falls.

  • Roof area x rainfall x efficiency = litres per year.
  • Gutters, mesh, first-flush diverter, filter, covered tank and overflow route.
  • Use for non-drinking unless treated.
  • Clean the tank yearly.
Worked example. A 100 m2 roof, 800 mm rain and 80 per cent efficiency give about 64,000 litres a year. A 5,000-litre tank fills after about 60 mm of rain.

Activity. Design a system with a calculation.

Standard to meet. Calculation is correct and the design has overflow and cleaning.

Module 6 Reuse and water quality 4 hEvaluate

Grey water and treated water can be reused with care.

  • Grey water from basins and showers can irrigate non-edible plants where permitted.
  • Never use untreated grey water on salad crops.
  • A simple sand and gravel filter can help.
  • Test and treat drinking water; boil or disinfect if unsure.
Worked example. A school filters hand-wash water and uses it for flushing toilets, cutting use by 20 per cent.

Activity. List safe and unsafe uses and a treatment step.

Standard to meet. Rules are clear and a test is noted.

Module 7 Irrigation and farming 3 hApply

Grow more with less.

  • Drip lines, mulching, timing and crop choice for local rain.
  • Water early morning or evening.
  • Check soil moisture with a finger or a simple sensor.
  • Group plants by water need.
Worked example. Drip lines with mulch use about 40 per cent less water than flood irrigation and keep soil moist twice as long.

Activity. Plan a small plot with a watering timetable.

Standard to meet. Plan is complete and justified.

Module 8 Target, plan and progress 4 hCreate

Make saving a habit.

  • Set targets such as 20 per cent less in a year.
  • Assign owners and dates.
  • Display progress where everyone can see.
  • Report monthly and celebrate wins.
Worked example. A school sets a 20 per cent cut in one year. A notice board shows monthly litres saved by class and the school reaches 17 per cent in the first six months.

Activity. Present the plan and progress display.

Standard to meet. Target is measurable and the display is clear.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: audit sheet, leak test record, plan, target. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
AuditIncompleteCompleteComplete with benchmarks
Savings planVagueNumbersNumbers with payback
SafetyMissingStatedTested
Meter dataSavings guessedMeter or bucket readings recorded for at least a weekReadings compared with a benchmark and the biggest use named
Water rights and health rulesRules not checkedAbstraction, reuse and drinking-water rules for the place are written downRules checked with the local water body and a safe-use sign drafted

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Litres per person per day?

Model answer: Total use / people / days. (Covered in module 2: Water audit. To pass that module: Table is complete and totals match the meter.)

Q2. Night meter test?

Model answer: No use but meter moves means a leak. (Covered in module 3: Finding and fixing leaks. To pass that module: Result recorded with fix and saving.)

Q3. Rainwater formula?

Model answer: Area x rain x efficiency. (Covered in module 5: Rainwater harvesting. To pass that module: Calculation is correct and the design has overflow and cleaning.)

Q4. Safe grey water use?

Model answer: Irrigation of non-edible plants. (Covered in module 6: Reuse and water quality. To pass that module: Rules are clear and a test is noted.)

Q5. Aerator benefit?

Model answer: Reduces flow. (Covered in module 4: Saving at the tap. To pass that module: Savings are estimated with numbers.)

Q6. Why first flush?

Model answer: Removes dirt. (Covered in module 5: Rainwater harvesting. To pass that module: Calculation is correct and the design has overflow and cleaning.)

Q7. Mulch use?

Model answer: Keeps moisture. (Covered in module 7: Irrigation and farming. To pass that module: Plan is complete and justified.)

Q8. Drinking water rule?

Model answer: Treat and test. (Covered in module 6: Reuse and water quality. To pass that module: Rules are clear and a test is noted.)

Q9. Why meters?

Model answer: To measure savings. (Covered in module 2: Water audit. To pass that module: Table is complete and totals match the meter.)

Q10. Why share plan?

Model answer: Community buy-in. (Covered in module 8: Target, plan and progress. To pass that module: Target is measurable and the display is clear.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Water rights, rainwater and reuse rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
