CPD · Medical and healthcare

Recording care notes to the standard

Writing care notes that are clear, dated, signed and fair to the patient.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 FoundationAbout 1.5 hours of CPDSelf-study plus role-playMedical and healthcare

Writing care notes that are clear, dated, signed and fair to the patient.

  • Record an event, given a short scenario, with date, time, facts only and signature, meeting every checklist item.
  • Correct an error in a sample note, given the rule set, without hiding the original entry.
  • Distinguish fact from opinion, given 10 sample lines, with at least 9 correct.
  • Protect patient privacy in a note, given a sample with personal details, by removing everything not needed.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Record a care event as a clear, dated, signed, fact-only note, correct errors without hiding the original, and protect patient privacy

Condition

Given short scenarios, sample notes with personal details, 10 sample lines and a rule set, in a 90-minute practical session

Standard

Write a note meeting every checklist item, correct an error with the original still readable, and distinguish fact from opinion in at least 9 of 10 lines

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 What good notes do and why 15 min
  • A good note is clear, fair and useful to the next reader.
  • Accuracy protects the patient and the writer.
  • Notes show the order of events.
Worked example. Poor note: "Patient grumpy, pain bad, gave him something." It has 0 times, 0 numbers and no signature, so the next reader cannot act on it.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 1.

Standard to meet. Describe what makes a care note clear, fair and useful, and give two reasons why a note must be accurate for the next reader.

Module 2 Facts, time, signature 25 min
  • Write the date and time of each entry.
  • State facts you saw or were told, with numbers.
  • Sign with initials and role.
Worked example. "14 Mar, 10:20. Mr A reported pain in the right knee, 7 out of 10. Dressing dry and intact. Told nurse in charge at 10:25. Signed: initials, role." That ticks 6 of 6 checklist items.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 2.

Standard to meet. Record an event from a short scenario with date, time, facts only and signature, meeting every item on the checklist.

Module 3 Correcting errors and late entries 20 min
  • Draw one line through the error so it can be read.
  • Write error, then initials and date.
  • Mark a late entry with the real time of writing.
Worked example. At 10:40 you find 7 should be 6. Line through 7, write "error", initials, 14 Mar 10:40, then add: "Correct: 6 out of 10." Original still readable.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 3.

Standard to meet. Correct an error in a sample note with one line, the word error, initials and date, keeping the original readable, and mark a late entry.

Module 4 Privacy and need-to-know 15 min
  • Share only what the reader needs for care.
  • Leave out home address and family detail unless needed.
  • Only people with a care need may read the note.
Worked example. Sample note has 5 personal details. Keep 2 needed for care (pain score, allergy) and remove 3 (home address, phone, family dispute).

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Protect privacy by removing every personal detail not needed for care from a sample note, and say who may read a note.

Module 5 Write-and-score practice 10 min
  • Write the note from the fall scenario.
  • Score it on the 6-item checklist.
  • Name any gap.
Worked example. Fall with no injury, 2:15 pm: "Found sitting on the floor by bed. No injury seen. Helped to chair. Told nurse in charge 2:20." Score 5 of 6, gap: no signature.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Write a note for a short scenario about a fall with no injury, then score it against the checklist and name any gaps.

Module 6 Check and reflection 5 min
  • Sort 10 lines into fact or opinion.
  • Pass is 9 of 10.
  • Reflect on one habit to change.
Worked example. "He was crying" is fact; "He seemed upset" is opinion. A learner gets 9 of 10 right, meets the pass line, and plans to replace "appeared to" with what they saw.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Distinguish fact from opinion in 10 sample lines, getting at least 9 correct, and reflect on one way to improve your own notes.

Module 7 Second worked case: the refused meal 10 min
  • Write the second case card as a note.
  • Quote the person's words.
  • Record both offers with times.
Worked example. Resident refused a meal at 12:30: "I'm not hungry." Re-offered 13:15, accepted soup. Note: 12:30 declined, 13:15 soup accepted, 45 minutes between offers.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Write a note for the refused-meal case with both times, the quoted words and a signature, and score it against the checklist.

Module 8 Peer review and portfolio 10 min
  • Swap notes with a partner.
  • Score each other's note.
  • Keep your best two notes in a portfolio.
Worked example. Partner scores your note 5 of 6 on the checklist. After fixing the missing time, you re-score it 6 of 6 and file it in your portfolio.

Practise: a case card or template exercise linked to outcome 4.

Standard to meet. Review a partner's note against the checklist, name one strength and one gap, and revise your own note to reach every item.

practice

Worked example and case cards

Worked example (a good note)

"14 Mar, 10:20. Mr A reported pain in the right knee, 7 out of 10. Dressing dry and intact. Offered a rest and a drink; he accepted both. Told nurse in charge at 10:25. Signed: initials, role."

A poor note (do not copy)

"Patient grumpy, pain bad, gave him something." Opinion word, no number, no time, unclear action, unsigned.

Correcting an error

draw one line through the wrong entry so it can still be read, write "error", initial and date it, then add the correct entry. Never erase or cover it. (Check your workplace's rule.)

Case card for practice

a short scenario about a fall with no injury; learners write the note, then score it on the checklist.

Case card 2

A resident refused a meal at 12:30 and said, "I'm not hungry." Staff offered again at 13:15; accepted soup. Write the note.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Which is fact: "He seemed upset" or "He was crying"?

Model answer: "He was crying." (Covered in module 2: Facts, time, signature. To pass that module: Record an event from a short scenario with date, time, facts only and signature, meeting every item on the checklist.)

How do you correct a mistake in a paper note?

Model answer: Single line through, "error", initials and date, then the right entry. (Covered in module 3: Correcting errors and late entries. To pass that module: Correct an error in a sample note with one line, the word error, initials and date, keeping the original readable, and mark a late entry.)

Why include the time of each entry?

Model answer: So the order of events is clear to anyone who reads it. (Covered in module 2: Facts, time, signature. To pass that module: Record an event from a short scenario with date, time, facts only and signature, meeting every item on the checklist.)

Name two details you should leave out of a note shared with a wider team.

Model answer: Anything not needed for care, such as home address or family details. (Covered in module 4: Privacy and need-to-know. To pass that module: Protect privacy by removing every personal detail not needed for care from a sample note, and say who may read a note.)

Who may read your note?

Model answer: Only people with a care need to know. (Covered in module 4: Privacy and need-to-know. To pass that module: Protect privacy by removing every personal detail not needed for care from a sample note, and say who may read a note.)

What is a late entry?

Model answer: A note added after the event; mark it as late with the real time of writing. (Covered in module 3: Correcting errors and late entries. To pass that module: Correct an error in a sample note with one line, the word error, initials and date, keeping the original readable, and mark a late entry.)

May you write for someone else?

Model answer: Only with a clear policy; normally each author records their own. (Covered in: background fact, not taught in one module.)

Why avoid "appeared to"?

Model answer: It mixes opinion with fact unless backed by what you saw. (Covered in module 2: Facts, time, signature. To pass that module: Record an event from a short scenario with date, time, facts only and signature, meeting every item on the checklist.)

How long do notes stay?

Model answer: Per local retention rules. (Covered in: background fact, not taught in one module.)

What if you spot a colleague's error?

Model answer: Tell them and the lead; do not alter their entry. (Covered in module 3: Correcting errors and late entries. To pass that module: Correct an error in a sample note with one line, the word error, initials and date, keeping the original readable, and mark a late entry.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: Why we write care notes. Speaker note: A care note is a written record of what happened to a person in care. At this first step the learner can say why notes are written, who reads them and why they must be true.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Fact, opinion and privacy. Speaker note: The learner knows the difference between a fact such as "he was crying" and an opinion such as "he seemed upset", and knows that notes are private and shared only with people who need to know.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Date, time, signature. Speaker note: The learner can copy a good note pattern: date and time, what was seen, what was done, who was told and a signature. They find the gaps in a poor sample note with help.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Write notes to the standard. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner writes a full note from a scenario meeting every checklist item, corrects an error with one line and keeps personal details to what care needs.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Hard notes. Speaker note: The specialist records harder events: refusals, falls, late entries and shared records. They choose exact words, quote the person and decide what to leave out for a wider team.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Audit and coach. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner audits a set of notes against the checklist, spots patterns such as missing times, and coaches colleagues with specific, kind feedback. They report the audit results as simple class-style patterns, not as blame, so that staff can see where notes improve.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Shape recording practice. Speaker note: The super expert sets recording standards for a service, links note quality to safer care, and trains others to teach fact-based writing and privacy-aware sharing.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Canada (Toronto)

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Credit and levels: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Australia
