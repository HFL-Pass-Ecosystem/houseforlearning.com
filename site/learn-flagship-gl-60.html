flagship course

Portable and Wireless Charging Solutions

Understand power banks, wireless pads and shared charging kiosks: safety, standards, battery care and a simple service business.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation27:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain how batteries, chargers and wireless pads work.
  • Apply Calculate capacity and charge time.
  • Analyse Compare devices on real capacity.
  • Evaluate Judge safety and quality.
  • Create Plan a small charging service.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Test and compare three portable chargers, write a safety and quality note, and plan a small shared charging point with price and care rules.

Condition

Three chargers (practice or real), a meter or phone app, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. No opening of battery packs.

Standard

Capacity test is repeated and averaged; safety rules are followed; unsafe items are rejected; business plan shows costs; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Shop owners, repair techs, event hosts, students. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Batteries, units and capacity 3 hUnderstand

You cannot compare power banks without understanding the units.

  • Energy in watt-hours: Wh = volts x amp-hours; a 10,000 mAh cell at 3.7 V holds 37 Wh.
  • Conversion to 5 V and charging loses 20 to 40 per cent as heat.
  • Cycles of charge and discharge age batteries.
  • Labels often give capacity at cell voltage, not usable output.
Worked example. A 37 Wh bank with 70 per cent efficiency delivers about 26 Wh, enough for roughly two charges of a 12 Wh phone.

Activity. Calculate usable energy for three banks from their labels.

Standard to meet. Maths correct and assumptions stated.

Module 2 Wired charging standards 3 hUnderstand

Cables and plugs affect speed and safety.

  • Connector types and power delivery levels.
  • Thin cables overheat and limit current.
  • Match charger, cable and device.
  • Use certified products from reliable sellers.
Worked example. A 65 W charger with a cheap cable charges a laptop slowly because the cable cannot carry the current.

Activity. Make a compatibility table for five devices.

Standard to meet. Entries are correct.

Module 3 Wireless charging 3 hUnderstand

Coils and magnetic fields transfer energy without a plug.

  • Pad and device must align; metal objects between can heat up.
  • Efficiency is lower than wired charging; more heat.
  • Standards exist for compatibility.
  • Good for convenience, not always for speed.
Worked example. A wireless pad at 70 per cent efficiency wastes 30 per cent as heat, so a 15 Wh phone charge draws about 21 Wh from the wall.

Activity. Explain how wireless charging works to a peer.

Standard to meet. Peer can repeat the main idea and one limit.

Module 4 Testing real capacity 4 hApply

Measure, do not trust the label.

  • Discharge fully into a known load while recording watt-hours with a meter.
  • Repeat three times and average.
  • Compare with the label and note the percentage.
  • Record temperature and time.
Worked example. A 20,000 mAh bank labelled 74 Wh delivers 55 Wh, 74 per cent of the claim.

Activity. Test three banks and record results in a table.

Standard to meet. Results recorded with averages and percentage of label.

Module 5 Safety and quality 4 hEvaluate

Poor batteries can overheat or catch fire.

  • Look for certification marks and protection circuits.
  • Avoid swollen, hot or damaged units.
  • Follow airline and transport rules for carrying batteries.
  • Dispose of faulty batteries at a collection point.
Worked example. A swollen bank is isolated in a safe container away from flammable items and taken to a collection point.

Activity. Write a safety note and a disposal procedure.

Standard to meet. Includes inspection, isolation and disposal.

Module 6 A shared charging service 4 hCreate

Kiosks and lending services can serve events and cafes.

  • Locations: cafes, events, transport hubs.
  • Pricing by time or deposit.
  • Return, cleaning and loss control.
  • Staff training and insurance.
Worked example. A cafe rents banks for 50 INR with a refundable deposit of 300 INR and tracks serial numbers.

Activity. Plan a small service with prices.

Standard to meet. Costs and prices shown with currency.

Module 7 Care, reuse and recycling 3 hApply

Extend the life of your devices.

  • Avoid heat; store at about half charge.
  • Recycle at collection points.
  • Repair only with trained people.
  • Label and track batteries.
Worked example. A cafe has a labelled battery recycling box and a monthly collection.

Activity. Write care rules.

Standard to meet. Rules are specific.

Module 8 Pilot and review 3 hCreate

Test the idea for a week.

  • Run a trial with a small number of users.
  • Record use and complaints.
  • Improve based on data.
  • Decide whether to continue.
Worked example. Week one: 40 rentals and 2 losses; a deposit is added and losses stop.

Activity. Present your review.

Standard to meet. Numbers are real.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Formula for Wh?

Model answer: Volts x amp-hours. (Covered in module 1: Batteries and units. To pass that module: Maths correct.)

Q2. Why losses?

Model answer: Conversion and heat. (Covered in module 2: Wired charging standards. To pass that module: Entries correct.)

Q3. What marks safe goods?

Model answer: Certification marks and protection circuits. (Covered in module 3: Wireless charging. To pass that module: Peer understands.)

Q4. What do you do with a swollen bank?

Model answer: Isolate and dispose safely. (Covered in module 4: Testing capacity. To pass that module: Results recorded.)

Q5. Wireless pad efficiency issue?

Model answer: Lower efficiency, more heat. (Covered in module 5: Safety and quality. To pass that module: Includes disposal.)

Q6. Why store at half charge?

Model answer: Slows ageing. (Covered in module 6: Shared charging service. To pass that module: Costs and prices shown.)

Q7. How measure capacity?

Model answer: Discharge into a known load and measure Wh. (Covered in module 7: Care and recycling. To pass that module: Rules specific.)

Q8. Why repeat tests?

Model answer: To average errors. (Covered in module 8: Plan and review. To pass that module: Numbers real.)

Q9. Name a loss control.

Model answer: Deposit. (Covered in module 8: Plan and review. To pass that module: Numbers real.)

Q10. Where recycle batteries?

Model answer: Collection points. (Covered in module 8: Plan and review. To pass that module: Numbers real.)

levels L1 to L7

The ladder, slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Batteries and units

L1: learner can name charger parts. Focus module: Batteries and units (Understand, 3 h). mAh, V, Wh. Key points: Wh = V x Ah. A 10,000 mAh at 3.7 V is 37 Wh. Losses of 20 to 40% in conversion. Charge and discharge cycles age cells.

Speaker notes. Teach: open with the worked example. A 37 Wh bank charges a 12 Wh phone about 2.5 times. Then set the activity: Calculate three examples. Mark against the standard: Maths correct. Check at this level: Formula for Wh? Model answer: Volts x amp-hours. Show examples.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Wired charging standards

L2: learner knows safety rules. Focus module: Wired charging standards (Understand, 3 h). Cables and speeds. Key points: Connector types and power delivery levels. Use quality cables; thin cables overheat. Match charger and device.

Speaker notes. Teach: open with the worked example. A 65 W charger with the wrong cable charges slowly. Then set the activity: Make a compatibility table. Mark against the standard: Entries correct. Check at this level: Why losses? Model answer: Conversion and heat. Safety.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Wireless charging

L3: learner can calculate Wh. Focus module: Wireless charging (Understand, 3 h). Coils and fields. Key points: Pad and device must align. Efficiency is lower; more heat. Metal objects between can heat up.

Speaker notes. Teach: open with the worked example. A wireless pad is 70% efficient, wasting 30%. Then set the activity: Explain how it works. Mark against the standard: Peer understands. Check at this level: What marks safe goods? Model answer: Certification marks and protection circuits. Sums.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Testing capacity

L4: learner can test a bank. Focus module: Testing capacity (Apply, 4 h). Measure, do not trust labels. Key points: Discharge fully into a known load and measure Wh. Repeat three times and average. Compare to the label.

Speaker notes. Teach: open with the worked example. A 20,000 mAh bank delivers 55 Wh instead of 74 Wh claimed. Then set the activity: Test three banks. Mark against the standard: Results recorded. Check at this level: What do you do with a swollen bank? Model answer: Isolate and dispose safely. Test.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Safety and quality

L5: learner can compare devices. Focus module: Safety and quality (Evaluate, 4 h). Cheap packs can fail. Key points: Look for certification marks and protection circuits. Avoid swollen, hot or damaged units. Do not carry on aircraft against airline rules; check limits.

Speaker notes. Teach: open with the worked example. A swollen bank is isolated in a safe container and disposed of properly. Then set the activity: Write a safety note. Mark against the standard: Includes disposal. Check at this level: Wireless pad efficiency issue? Model answer: Lower efficiency, more heat. Compare.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Care and recycling

L6: learner can run a kiosk team. Focus module: Care and recycling (Apply, 3 h). Extend life. Key points: Avoid heat; store at half charge. Recycle batteries at collection points. Repair only with trained people.

Speaker notes. Teach: open with the worked example. A cafe has a labelled battery recycling box. Then set the activity: Write care rules. Mark against the standard: Rules specific. Check at this level: Why store at half charge? Model answer: Slows ageing. Team.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Plan and review

L7: learner can audit safety. Focus module: Plan and review (Create, 3 h). Pilot the idea. Key points: Run a one-week trial. Record use and complaints. Improve.

Speaker notes. Teach: open with the worked example. Week one: 40 rentals, 2 losses; add deposit. Then set the activity: Present your review. Mark against the standard: Numbers real. Check at this level: How measure capacity? Model answer: Discharge into a known load and measure Wh. Peer audit.

ten locations and global

Your place

Battery transport and recycling rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
