flagship course

Portable and Wireless Charging Solutions

Understand power banks, wireless pads and shared charging kiosks: safety, standards, battery care and a simple service business.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation27:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain how batteries, chargers and wireless pads work.
  • Apply Calculate capacity and charge time.
  • Analyse Compare devices on real capacity.
  • Evaluate Judge safety and quality.
  • Create Plan a small charging service.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Test and compare three portable chargers, write a safety and quality note, and plan a small shared charging point with price and care rules.

Condition

Three chargers (practice or real), a meter or phone app, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. No opening of battery packs.

Standard

Capacity test is repeated and averaged; safety rules are followed; unsafe items are rejected; business plan shows costs; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Shop owners, repair techs, event hosts, students. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Batteries, units and real capacity 3 hUnderstand

You cannot compare power banks without understanding the units, and the number on the label is not what you can use.

  • Energy is measured in watt-hours: Wh = volts x amp-hours. A 10,000 mAh cell at 3.7 V holds 37 Wh.
  • Converting the voltage to 5 V and charging a phone wastes 20 to 40 per cent as heat.
  • Cycles of charge and discharge age batteries; heat ages them faster.
  • Labels often quote capacity at cell voltage, not usable output.
Worked example. A 37 Wh bank at 70 per cent efficiency delivers about 26 Wh, enough for roughly two charges of a 12 Wh phone, not three.

Activity. Calculate usable energy for three banks from their labels and assumed efficiency.

Standard to meet. Maths is correct and assumptions are stated.

Module 2 Wired charging, cables and standards 3 hUnderstand

Cables and plugs decide speed and safety.

  • Connector types and power delivery levels set the maximum watts.
  • Thin or damaged cables overheat and limit current.
  • Match charger, cable and device; use certified products.
  • Fast charging needs both ends to agree on voltage.
Worked example. A 65 W charger with a cheap cable charges a laptop slowly because the cable cannot carry the current; a certified cable restores full speed.

Activity. Make a compatibility table for five devices, chargers and cables.

Standard to meet. Entries are correct and sourced.

Module 3 Wireless charging 3 hUnderstand

Coils and magnetic fields transfer energy without a plug.

  • Pad and device must align; metal objects between them can heat up.
  • Efficiency is lower than wired charging, so more heat and wasted energy.
  • Standards exist for compatibility.
  • Good for convenience, not always for speed.
Worked example. A wireless pad at 70 per cent efficiency wastes 30 per cent as heat, so a 15 Wh phone charge draws about 21 Wh from the wall.

Activity. Explain how wireless charging works and calculate wall energy for one charge.

Standard to meet. A peer can repeat the idea and one limit.

Module 4 Testing real capacity 4 hApply

Measure, do not trust the label.

  • Fully discharge into a known load while recording watt-hours with a meter.
  • Repeat three times and average.
  • Compare with the label and record the percentage.
  • Record temperature and time.
Worked example. A 20,000 mAh bank labelled 74 Wh delivers 55 Wh on average, 74 per cent of the claim. Its price per delivered Wh is higher than a competitor with an honest label.

Activity. Test three banks and record results in a table with price per delivered Wh.

Standard to meet. Results include averages, percentage of label and cost per Wh.

Module 5 Safety, quality and transport 4 hEvaluate

Poor batteries can overheat or catch fire.

  • Look for certification marks and protection circuits.
  • Avoid swollen, hot or damaged units; isolate them in a safe container.
  • Follow airline and transport rules for carrying batteries.
  • Dispose of faulty batteries at a collection point.
Worked example. A swollen bank is isolated in a metal tin away from flammable items and taken to a collection point the same day.

Activity. Write a safety note, inspection routine and disposal procedure.

Standard to meet. Includes inspection, isolation, transport rule and disposal.

Module 6 A shared charging service 4 hCreate

Kiosks and lending services serve events, cafes and transport hubs.

  • Locations: cafes, events, transport hubs and gyms.
  • Pricing by time or deposit; refund on return.
  • Return, cleaning, serial tracking and loss control.
  • Staff training, insurance and a complaint route.
Worked example. A cafe rents banks for 50 INR with a refundable deposit of 300 INR (estimates; check your market) and tracks serial numbers. Loss falls to zero after deposits are introduced.

Activity. Plan a small service with prices, loss assumptions and break-even.

Standard to meet. Costs and prices are shown with currency and break-even is calculated.

Module 7 Care, reuse and recycling 3 hApply

Extend the life of your devices.

  • Avoid heat; store at about half charge.
  • Recycle at collection points; never put batteries in general waste.
  • Repair only with trained people.
  • Label and track batteries.
Worked example. A cafe has a labelled battery recycling box and a monthly collection. Banks are retired after 500 cycles or when capacity falls below 70 per cent.

Activity. Write care rules and a retirement rule.

Standard to meet. Rules are specific and measurable.

Module 8 Pilot and review 3 hCreate

Test the idea for a week.

  • Run a trial with a small number of users.
  • Record use, complaints and losses.
  • Improve based on data.
  • Decide whether to continue.
Worked example. Week one: 40 rentals and 2 losses; a deposit is added and losses stop. Average rental time is 4 hours, so one bank earns about 12 INR an hour.

Activity. Present your pilot review.

Standard to meet. Numbers are real and one improvement is dated.

ten locations and global

Your place

Battery transport and recycling rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
