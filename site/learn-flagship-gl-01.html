flagship course · GL-01

Lead Generation for Small Businesses

Build a simple, lawful way to attract people who want what a small business sells, measure the cost of each lead, and hand good leads to a person who can help them.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner60 h of lessons plus practiceLong Module, 1-3 months

Build a simple, lawful way to attract people who want what a small business sells, measure the cost of each lead, and hand good leads to a person who can help them.

  • Understand Explain the difference between a lead, a prospect and a customer, and describe the funnel in plain words.
  • Apply Build a landing page and an opt-in form that ask for only what is needed and record consent.
  • Analyse Compare three traffic sources by cost per lead and lead quality.
  • Evaluate Judge a 14-day test against targets and decide to scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a lead system with a follow-up routine and a one-page report for the business owner.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Build and run a 14-day lead test for one real small business and report cost per lead and cost per customer.

Condition

A real owner who agrees in writing, a free page or form tool, a spreadsheet, a budget of USD 60 or less (an estimate; check your market, or use the equivalent in your own currency), two peers and a mentor. About 60 hours over 4 to 8 weeks.

Standard

Every lead has recorded consent; every number carries its currency; the report shows spend, leads, cost per lead, customers won and the decision; two peers rate it Meets or better.

Who it is for: Small-business owners, shop and clinic managers, freelancers, students who want a first marketing project. Requirements: none. A phone or computer and a real small business to help. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: No purchased or scraped contact lists. No messages to anyone who has not agreed. No health, money or miracle claims in any offer. This course is not legal advice.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Who is a lead, and who is your ideal customer 6 hUnderstand

A lead is a person who has shown interest and given you a way to contact them. A prospect is a lead who fits your customer profile. A customer has paid. Lead generation is the work of moving people along that line honestly.

  • Write the ideal customer profile: who, where, what problem, what they already try, what they pay now. Keep it to ten lines.
  • The funnel has four steps: notice, interest (lead), decision (prospect), purchase (customer). Count people at each step; the drop between steps shows where to work.
  • Interview 5 real people who match the profile. Ask what they did the last time they needed this, what annoyed them, and what made them trust a seller.
  • A small business wins on being specific: a named place, a named group, a named problem.
Worked example. Corner tailoring shop. Profile: office workers within 3 km of the shop who need trousers shortened quickly. Problem: shops take a week. Offer idea: 24-hour alterations. Five interviews show people care about speed more than price.

Activity. Write the profile and five interview notes for your chosen business and keep them in My page.

Standard to meet. Profile has all ten lines; each interview note has a quote and a fact; the problem is in the customer's own words.

Module 2 The offer: a promise worth giving contact details for 6 hCreate

People share contact details when they get something useful straight away. That useful thing is the lead magnet: a checklist, a price guide, a free first check, a sample, a short guide.

  • A good offer is specific, quick to use and linked to the paid service. "Free fit check in 10 minutes" beats "Contact us".
  • Write one clear promise in one line: who it is for, what they get, how fast.
  • Never promise what you cannot deliver on the day. State any limits (for example, "first 20 customers this month").
  • Test two versions of the promise on five people and keep the one they understand in under 5 seconds.
Worked example. Alteration shop: Version A "Quality tailoring". Version B "Trousers shortened in 24 hours, USD 10, pick-up at the door". Four of five people pick B as clearer.

Activity. Write three promise lines, test them on five people, and record which wins and why.

Standard to meet. Winning line names the audience, the result and the time; the test notes show who said what.

Module 3 Consent and the rules 6 hApply

Trust is the asset. Contact people only with their clear agreement, tell them what you will send, and let them leave at any time. Local data-protection law sets the detail: check yours.

  • Good consent is active (a box the person ticks), specific (what you will send, how often) and recorded (date, time, wording they saw). Check the exact rule for your place.
  • Collect the minimum: name and one contact method are usually enough. Every extra field lowers sign-ups.
  • Every message carries who you are and a one-step way to stop receiving messages. Honour a stop request the same day.
  • Keep a simple register: person, date, source, consent wording, stop date if any. Delete data you no longer need.
  • Never buy lists, never scrape contact details, never add people because they gave a card at an event without saying what you will do.
Worked example. Form text: "Send me the free fit-check slot times and occasional offers from Corner Tailors (one message a week at most). I can unsubscribe at any time." with an unticked box. The register records form version 1, tick time 14:32, source: poster QR.

Activity. Write your consent sentence, build the register sheet, and test unsubscribe on a friend's number or address.

Standard to meet. Consent box is unticked by default; the register records date, source and wording; unsubscribe works and is logged.

Module 4 The landing page and form 8 hCreate

A landing page has one job: persuade one kind of visitor to take one action. Remove everything else.

  • Structure: headline with the promise; two lines of proof; a short list of what they get; the form; a contact fallback.
  • Use plain words at reading age 12 or below, a large button with a verb ("Book my fit check"), and a page that loads in under 3 seconds on a phone.
  • Proof beats claims: a real photo, a real number ("612 pairs shortened this year"), a short quote with permission.
  • Conversion rate = leads divided by visitors. If 200 visitors give 10 leads, conversion is 10 / 200 = 5%.
  • Check it on a phone first: most small-business visitors use one.
Worked example. Version 1: 200 visitors, 10 leads (5%). Version 2 shortens the form from 6 fields to 3: 200 visitors, 17 leads (8.5%). Keep version 2.

Activity. Build the page and form with a free tool. Ask three people to find the button in 5 seconds.

Standard to meet. One action per page; three fields or fewer; works on a phone; three of three testers succeed.

Module 5 Getting visitors: own network, referral, search, social, small paid test 8 hAnalyse

Start with sources that cost time, not money, then run one small paid test to learn.

  • Own network: customers, neighbours, local groups, shop counter, poster with a QR code. Ask for referrals with a small thank-you.
  • Search: a useful page with the words customers use, a map listing with correct opening hours and photos.
  • Social posts: show the work, answer questions, post the offer on a regular day.
  • Paid test: set a cap before you start, for example USD 30 over 7 days, one audience, one offer, one page.
  • Track every source with its own link or code so you know which one produced leads.
Worked example. Poster QR: 14 leads, USD 4 for printing, USD 0.29 per lead. Local group post: 9 leads, cost USD 0 but 2 hours of time. Paid test: USD 30, 12 leads, USD 2.50 per lead. Poster wins on cost; time must be counted too.

Activity. Run two sources for 7 days each with separate tracking and fill a comparison table.

Standard to meet. Table shows source, spend, time, leads and cost per lead; the winner is chosen using numbers, not feeling.

Module 6 Follow-up: email and message basics 6 hApply

Most leads are lost in the first day. A fast, human, helpful first reply beats a clever campaign.

  • Reply within one hour in working hours. Use the name they gave; answer their question first; offer one clear next step.
  • A welcome sequence of three short messages: day 0 (what they asked for), day 2 (a useful tip or answer), day 5 (an invitation to book).
  • Subject lines tell the truth in under 8 words. No capital-letter shouting, no tricks.
  • Keep it plain text and short. One link per message. Say who you are and where you are.
Worked example. Day 0: "Hello [first name], here are the slot times for your free fit check this week: Tue 5pm, Wed 6pm, Thu 5pm. Reply with the one you want. - [your name], Corner Tailors." 9 of 30 leads reply the same day.

Activity. Write the three-message sequence and send it to two peers (not strangers) for feedback.

Standard to meet. Each message has one action, a stop line and the sender; peers confirm they understand what to do next.

Module 7 Measure: cost per lead, per customer, and the decision 8 hEvaluate

Numbers turn opinion into decisions. Use four: visitors, leads, customers, spend.

  • Cost per lead (CPL) = spend divided by leads. Cost per customer = spend divided by customers.
  • Lead-to-customer rate = customers divided by leads.
  • Compare with the profit per customer. If one customer brings USD 40 profit and costs USD 25 to win, margin is USD 15.
  • Decide with a rule written before the test: scale if cost per customer is under half the profit; change if between half and full; stop if above.
  • Report honestly, including what failed.
Worked example. Spend USD 60 (including printing and USD 30 paid test). 41 leads, CPL = 60 / 41 = USD 1.46. 8 customers: lead-to-customer 19.5%, cost per customer = 60 / 8 = USD 7.50. Profit per customer USD 20 over a year of repeat hems; USD 7.50 is under half of USD 20, so scale.

Activity. Compute the four numbers and the three rates for your test in a sheet.

Standard to meet. No arithmetic errors; units and currency on every figure; the decision matches the pre-written rule.

Module 8 Hand-off, the 14-day report and what next 12 hCreate

A lead is only worth something when a person follows it up well. Close the loop and present your result.

  • Keep one shared sheet: lead, date, source, status (new, contacted, booked, won, lost), next step, owner.
  • Qualify politely: need, timing, budget. Not every lead fits, and that is fine.
  • Write a one-page report: goal, what you did, numbers, lessons, decision, next 14 days.
  • Present to the owner and a peer circle for 10 minutes. Ask for one improvement and record it.
Worked example. Report line: "We spent USD 60 and gained 41 consented leads and 8 customers. Poster QR was cheapest. Next 14 days: two more shop counters, drop the paid test." The owner signs that they saw the report.

Activity. Prepare the report and present it. Update My page with all evidence.

Standard to meet. Report is one page; numbers match the sheet; the owner confirms in writing; two peer reviews given.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: customer profile and interviews, consent register, landing page, source comparison, number sheet, 14-day report and owner sign-off. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is the difference between a lead and a prospect?

Model answer: A lead has shown interest and shared contact details; a prospect is a lead who also fits your customer profile. (Covered in module 1: Who is a lead, and who is your ideal customer. To pass that module: Profile has all ten lines; each interview note has a quote and a fact; the problem is in the customer's own words.)

Q2. Name the three properties of valid consent.

Model answer: Active (ticked by the person), specific (what and how often), recorded (date, wording, source). (Covered in module 3: Consent and the rules. To pass that module: Consent box is unticked by default; the register records date, source and wording; unsubscribe works and is logged.)

Q3. 200 visitors give 14 leads. What is the conversion rate?

Model answer: 14 / 200 = 7%. (Covered in module 4: The landing page and form. To pass that module: One action per page; three fields or fewer; works on a phone; three of three testers succeed.)

Q4. You spent USD 45 and won 6 customers. What is the cost per customer?

Model answer: 45 / 6 = USD 7.50. (Covered in module 7: Measure: cost per lead, per customer, and the decision. To pass that module: No arithmetic errors; units and currency on every figure; the decision matches the pre-written rule.)

Q5. Why is buying a contact list a bad idea?

Model answer: People have not agreed to hear from you, it can break data law, and it harms trust and delivery. (Covered in module 3: Consent and the rules. To pass that module: Consent box is unticked by default; the register records date, source and wording; unsubscribe works and is logged.)

Q6. Give two ways to reduce form drop-off.

Model answer: Fewer fields; a clear verb on the button; a fast phone-friendly page; trust proof. (Covered in module 4: The landing page and form. To pass that module: One action per page; three fields or fewer; works on a phone; three of three testers succeed.)

Q7. How soon should you reply to a new lead in working hours?

Model answer: Within about one hour. (Covered in module 6: Follow-up: email and message basics. To pass that module: Each message has one action, a stop line and the sender; peers confirm they understand what to do next.)

Q8. What must every message include?

Model answer: Who you are and a one-step way to stop receiving messages. (Covered in module 3: Consent and the rules. To pass that module: Consent box is unticked by default; the register records date, source and wording; unsubscribe works and is logged.)

Q9. Why track each source separately?

Model answer: To know which one produces leads and what each lead costs. (Covered in module 5: Getting visitors: own network, referral, search, social, small paid test. To pass that module: Table shows source, spend, time, leads and cost per lead; the winner is chosen using numbers, not feeling.)

Q10. State a decision rule written before a test.

Model answer: For example: scale if cost per customer is under half the profit per customer. (Covered in module 7: Measure: cost per lead, per customer, and the decision. To pass that module: No arithmetic errors; units and currency on every figure; the decision matches the pre-written rule.)

On the slide: L1 Basic · What a lead is

Notes: Say in one sentence what a lead is and name one place a small shop can find them. Notes: no tools needed; use a local shop the learner knows and ask who has asked about prices this week.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Consent and honest promises

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Consent and honest promises

Notes: Know that people must agree before you contact them and that offers must be true. Notes: show one consent tick box and one false offer; learners mark which is fair and say why.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: A simple offer and form

On the slide: L3 Foundation · A simple offer and form

Notes: Write one offer and a form with three fields or fewer. Notes: check the offer names who it is for and what they get; count the fields and the words on the button.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Run a 14-day lead test

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Run a 14-day lead test

Notes: Run the test for one real business and report conversion rate and cost per customer. Notes: use 200 visitors giving 14 leads as the practice case (7 per cent); learners redo the sums on their own numbers.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Compare sources and decide

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Compare sources and decide

Notes: Compare at least three sources by cost per lead and choose what to scale, change or stop using a rule written before the test. Notes: scale if cost per customer is under half the profit per customer; learners defend one decision.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Coach a team on follow-up

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Coach a team on follow-up

Notes: Set reply targets, templates and a weekly review for a small team. Notes: target a reply within about one hour in working hours; sample five replies and mark them against a checklist.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Design the whole lead system

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Design the whole lead system

Notes: Design a lead system for several businesses with data rules, audit and a yearly plan. Notes: review consent records and source tracking for gaps; present one risk, one saving and a 12-month plan.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Partner to practise with: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties.
