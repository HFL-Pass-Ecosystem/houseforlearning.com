Online Currency Exchange Concepts

Understand how currency exchange works online, compare rates and fees honestly, and design a safe, transparent exchange-service concept without handling real funds.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Understand how currency exchange works online, compare rates and fees honestly, and design a safe, transparent exchange-service concept without handling real funds.

  • Understand Explain exchange rates, spread, fees and why they differ between providers.
  • Apply Calculate the true cost of a transfer from rate, fee and spread.
  • Analyse Analyse providers on cost, speed, safety and rules.
  • Create Create a customer guide and a transparent price display concept.
  • Evaluate Evaluate the risks of fraud, rate change and legal duties.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Compare five public exchange routes for one real need, calculate the true cost, and design a transparent price display and risk plan (no real money handled).

Condition

A computer, public rate data, a spreadsheet, practice transactions only, a mentor with finance experience and a peer circle. About 8 weeks part-time.

Standard

Every cost shown in both currencies; true cost % calculated for five routes; fees and spread separated; scam checklist completed; concept reviewed by a mentor as honest and clear.

Who it is for: Small traders, travellers, finance learners, support staff, students. Requirements: Basic arithmetic. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Concepts and practice only. Offering exchange services needs a licence in most places. Never take or hold real customer money in this course. Not financial or legal advice.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How exchange works 4 hUnderstand

A rate says how much of one currency you get for another. You will read a quoted rate in both directions and spot the gap from the mid-market rate, which is where much of the real cost hides.

  • Quote like EUR 1 = INR 90 means 1 euro buys 90 rupees.
  • Mid-market rate is the middle of buy and sell prices.
  • Providers add a spread and fees.
  • Rates move every second.
  • Treat the mid-market rate as the halfway point between buy and sell prices; if it is 1.10 and a provider offers 1.07, the gap of 0.03 is a hidden cost of about 2.7 percent.
  • Write down the currency pair both ways, such as A per B and B per A, and check which direction you are quoted, since mixing them causes errors of several percent.
  • Convert five amounts both ways and check with a second calculator.
  • Write which currency is quoted and which is the base.
Worked example. Mid-market 1 EUR = 90.00 INR (an example rate; check live rates and fees). Provider offers 88.20: spread = (90 - 88.2) / 90 = 2%.

Activity. Convert five amounts in both directions.

Standard to meet. All conversions correct with currency shown.

Common mistake. Confusing buy and sell rates.

Second worked example. 1 USD = 83.00 INR at mid-market. A provider offers 81.00, so the spread is (83 - 81) / 83 = 2.4%, which costs 2.40 rupees on every 100.

Check yourself. Which rate applies to you?

Module 2 True cost 6 hUnderstand

The advertised rate is not the whole price. You will combine fixed fee, percentage fee and rate margin into one honest percentage, so two quotes can be compared fairly.

  • True cost = (mid-market value - what receiver gets) / mid-market value.
  • Add fixed fees, card fees and receiver bank fees.
  • Compare same amount, same day.
  • Watch for "zero fee" offers with poor rates.
  • Add up three costs for any transfer: the fixed fee, the percentage fee, and the rate margin; then divide the total by the amount sent to get one honest percentage.
  • Compare two offers on a 1,000 unit transfer: a low fee with a poor rate may cost more than a higher fee with a rate near the mid-market, so always compute both.
  • Record the date and time of every quote.
  • Find the regulator's public list of licensed providers.
Worked example. Send EUR 500: mid value INR 45,000. Receiver gets INR 43,925 after a 3 EUR fee and 1.8% spread. True cost = 1,075 / 45,000 = 2.4%.

Activity. Calculate true cost for three real quotes.

Standard to meet. Percentages correct.

Common mistake. Forgetting fixed fees on small amounts.

Second worked example. A student abroad sends GBP 300 home. The family receives INR 30,300 where mid value is INR 31,200, after one flat fee and a weak rate. Divide the 900 lost by 31,200 and the true cost is 2.9%.

Check yourself. What is the fee as a percent?

Module 3 Comparing routes 8 hApply

Bank, card, cash, online provider, wallet: each has trade-offs. You will build a side-by-side grid of four routes for the same sum and see which one actually delivers the most to the receiver.

  • Cost, speed, limits, safety, convenience.
  • Public comparison tools and their limits.
  • Time of day and weekends.
  • Who regulates each route.
  • Build a comparison grid with at least four routes, such as bank, card, specialist transfer service and cash exchange, and record fee, rate, speed and receiver amount for the same sum.
  • Check the offer at two different times on the same day and note the difference, since quotes move and ask whether the provider will lock a quoted rate for 30 minutes.
  • Never share one-time codes or passwords with anyone.
  • Compare the cost for a small and a large amount.
Worked example. Bank wire: 3 days, 3.5%. Online provider: 1 day, 1.4%. Cash agent: instant, 4%.

Activity. Build a five-route comparison table.

Standard to meet. Table has all five measures.

Common mistake. Comparing different days.

Second worked example. Sending money home: bank transfer 4 days at 3%; app 1 day at 1.2%; pickup counter, same hour, 4.5%. A migrant worker choosing for an urgent medical bill takes the counter and accepts the higher cost.

Check yourself. When did you take each quote?

Module 4 Safety and scams 8 hApply

Exchange attracts fraud. You will check a provider's registration and recognise pressure tactics, protecting yourself and customers from the most common exchange frauds.

  • Fake rates too good to be true; pressure to act now.
  • Unlicensed providers; checks on the regulator's public list.
  • Never send money to personal accounts for a "business".
  • Keep records of every transfer.
  • Check any provider by searching its registration on an official register, confirming the website address letter by letter, and refusing anyone who asks for payment in gift cards.
  • Treat urgency as a warning: if a message says 'send within one hour for a special rate', stop, wait a day, and call the provider on a number you found yourself.
  • Look at how long each route takes on weekends.
  • Check whether the receiver pays a fee too.
Worked example. An offer promises a rate 8% above market: reject it; check the public register; report if fake.

Activity. Complete a scam checklist on three adverts.

Standard to meet. Red flags named for each.

Common mistake. Trusting a too-good rate.

Second worked example. A message offers 'guaranteed profit, double your money in a week'. You search the public register, find no licence, check the website age, and report it instead of paying.

Check yourself. Which red flag did you find?

Module 5 Rules and duties 8 hAnalyse

Providers must follow identity and anti-crime rules. You will list the identity and record-keeping duties that apply to exchange services, so a concept is designed to meet them from day one.

  • Licence, identity checks, limits and reports.
  • Record-keeping periods.
  • Taxes and declared limits at borders.
  • Consumer protection and complaints routes.
  • Keep records of each exchange for at least the period your local rules require, noting date, amount, rate, fee and reason, since these may be needed for reporting or tax.
  • Learn the identity checks providers ask for and why they exist, and prepare documents in advance so a first transfer is not delayed by days.
  • Keep screenshots of every quote.
  • Learn which documents are needed to send money.
Worked example. A provider asks for ID above a set amount: that is a legal duty, not an insult.

Activity. Summarise the rules for your place in one page.

Standard to meet. Page names the regulator and key limits.

Common mistake. Ignoring identity rules.

Second worked example. A provider refuses a first transfer until a passport is shown, and keeps the copy for the legal period. The customer is told why, and the check takes 5 minutes.

Check yourself. What limit applies where you live?

Module 6 Design a transparent display 10 hCreate

Show the total before the customer commits. You will design a screen that leads with the amount received and shows rate, fee and quote timer, so customers decide with full knowledge.

  • Show mid-market rate, our rate, fee and total received.
  • Show the time to arrive.
  • Lock the rate for a short time and say how long.
  • Show how to complain.
  • Display the final receiver amount in the largest text on the screen, and show the rate, the fee and the mid-market rate on the same line so nothing is hidden.
  • Show a countdown for how long a quote stays valid, such as 'rate held for 10:00', and say what happens when it expires so users are not surprised.
  • Write what happens if a transfer fails.
  • List how to complain, step by step.
Worked example. Screen: "You send EUR 500. Mid-market 90.00. Our rate 89.10. Fee EUR 3. You get INR 44,283. Rate held 5 minutes."

Activity. Sketch the price screen.

Standard to meet. Every number labelled with currency.

Common mistake. Showing the rate but not the total.

Second worked example. A mock-up shows total cost in large type at the top: 'You pay USD 200. They receive INR 16,216. All fees included. Arrives tomorrow.' Details open below for anyone who wants them.

Check yourself. What is the first number your user sees?

Module 7 Risk plan 6 hEvaluate

Rates move and systems fail. You will rate five exchange risks and attach a protective step to each, including a plan for when a rate moves against a transfer.

  • Rate risk: hold time and limits.
  • Operational risk: down time and wrong entries.
  • Fraud risk: checks and limits.
  • Liquidity: having enough of each currency.
  • List five risks with a rating: rate moves before sending, wrong recipient details, delayed delivery, fraud and provider failure; write one protective step for each.
  • Set a plan for rate moves, such as locking the rate for large amounts or splitting a transfer into two parts a week apart so a single bad day matters less.
  • Draw the money flow including fees.
  • Remember this course holds no real money.
Worked example. A rate falls 1.5% during a 10-minute delay: the price lock protects the customer and a limit protects the business.

Activity. Write a risk table with six rows.

Standard to meet. Each row has a control and an owner.

Common mistake. No rate-lock time.

Second worked example. A business owner agrees a rate for a payment due in 30 days; the rate moves 2% against her. A forward contract with the provider fixes the rate and removes the surprise.

Check yourself. How long is your hold?

Module 8 Present and plan 4 hCreate

Explain the concept clearly and honestly. You will present a cost comparison, display design and risk list, then set a four-week plan for testing the concept with invented figures.

  • One page: need, routes compared, display, risks, rules.
  • State what is concept only.
  • Gather feedback from a trader or traveller.
  • Plan 90 days: licence path, advisers.
  • Prepare a five-minute presentation with the cost comparison, your display design, your top three risks and a clear next step, such as a pilot with invented figures.
  • End with a short action plan dated over four weeks, with one task per week, so your concept moves from idea to something that can be reviewed.
  • Write a short customer guide in plain words.
  • Ask a finance adviser to review your concept.
Worked example. Feedback: "Show total in rupees first." Change made and retested.

Activity. Present to peers and mentor.

Standard to meet. Feedback recorded and applied.

Common mistake. Presenting concept as ready.

Second worked example. A tester says the fee seems hidden. You move the total cost to the top of the screen, and the next three testers state the cost correctly in under 10 seconds.

Check yourself. What did you state as limits?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. The cheap-looking transfer

A provider shows "0 fee".

Question: How check?

Model answer: Compare received amount with mid-market and compute true cost.

Case 2. The pressure call

A caller says the rate expires in ten minutes and asks for a personal transfer.

Question: What do you do?

Model answer: Stop, verify the provider on the regulator's list, and report.

Glossary

Mid-market rate
Middle of buy and sell prices.
Spread
Gap between mid and offered rate.
True cost
All costs as a percent of the amount.
Rate lock
A fixed rate for a short time.
Regulator
The authority that licenses providers.
Remittance
Money sent abroad.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is a spread?

Model answer: The gap between mid-market and the rate you are offered. (Covered in module 1: How exchange works. To pass that module: All conversions correct with currency shown.)

Q2. Calculate true cost.

Model answer: (Mid-market value minus received) divided by mid-market value. (Covered in module 2: True cost. To pass that module: Percentages correct.)

Q3. Why compare the same day?

Model answer: Rates change constantly. (Covered in module 2: True cost. To pass that module: Percentages correct.)

Q4. Red flag in an offer?

Model answer: A rate far better than the market or pressure to hurry. (Covered in module 4: Safety and scams. To pass that module: Red flags named for each.)

Q5. Why are ID checks needed?

Model answer: Legal duties to prevent crime. (Covered in module 5: Rules and duties. To pass that module: Page names the regulator and key limits.)

Q6. What should a display show?

Model answer: Rate, fee, total received, time and complaint route. (Covered in module 6: Design a transparent display. To pass that module: Every number labelled with currency.)

Q7. What is rate risk?

Model answer: The rate moves before the deal completes. (Covered in module 7: Risk plan. To pass that module: Each row has a control and an owner.)

Q8. What does "zero fee" hide?

Model answer: Possibly a poor rate. (Covered in module 2: True cost. To pass that module: Percentages correct.)

Q9. May this course hold real money?

Model answer: No, concepts only. (Covered in module 8: Present and plan. To pass that module: Feedback recorded and applied.)

Q10. Where to check licences?

Model answer: The regulator's public register. (Covered in module 4: Safety and scams. To pass that module: Red flags named for each.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Exchange licences, limits and tax declarations differ by country: always check the local regulator before any service idea. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Partner to practise with: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.
