Learning Platform Administration

Choose, build and run a learning platform: from course packages to security, with all 29 key features covered.

Task

Write a needs brief, build a sample learning path, design the people and automation set-up, and score one platform against a checklist.

Condition

A practice platform (free trial or open demo), a real or sample organisation, a mentor review. About 5 weeks part-time.

Standard

All 29 features in the map below are covered by a decision or a reason; a test learner completes your path; your scoring has evidence for every line.

Who it is for: training managers, HR staff, school and college administrators, trainers who run an online academy. Requirements: none. Source: built from the owner's "top 29 platform features" article, rewritten in our own words; no supplier is named.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Choosing the right kind of platform 3 hAnalyse

Before features, decide which KIND of learning platform you need. Five choices shape everything else.

  • Size: a small organisation values fast set-up and ease; an enterprise (for example a few hundred staff or more, possibly customers and partners too) needs automation, segmenting of users and links to other systems.
  • Open source or paid: open source has no licence fee, but you must install, host, secure and upgrade it, which can cost more than a subscription. Count the staff time.
  • Cloud or installed: cloud is the norm; installed is chosen only when a law or network rule requires it.
  • Integrated or standalone: can it exchange data with HR, CRM, payment and meeting tools?
  • Course builder or not: some platforms only play ready-made packages; most also let you build courses from slides, documents, video and audio.
Worked example. A 40-person training company: cloud, paid, quick set-up, builder included, payment link. A 5,000-person manufacturer: cloud, automation, HR link, groups by plant and role, single sign-in.

Activity. Write a one-page needs brief for a real organisation: size, five choices, three must-haves, three nice-to-haves.

Standard to meet. Brief names a decision on all five choices with one reason each; must-haves are testable.

Module 2 Building and packaging courses 4 hCreate

Covers course management and learning-package standards.

  • Course management: drag, drop and order content; mix PDFs, slides, video, audio and live sessions.
  • Package standards: SCORM and xAPI are the common formats that let a course made in one tool run on another platform and report results. Test an upload before you promise a date.
  • Learning paths: sequenced (one after another) when order matters; learner choice (finish any 3 of 5) when it does not.
  • Blended learning: pair online study with a live session; schedule it inside the platform so records stay in one place.
Worked example. Path for new sales staff: Product basics (must finish first), then choose 2 of 4 skills modules, then a live role-play session, then a short exam.

Activity. Build a three-step learning path in a practice platform with one uploaded package, one live session and one exam.

Standard to meet. Path runs end to end; a test learner completes it; the completion record appears in the report.

Module 3 Organising people: portals, groups, roles 4 hApply

Covers learning portals, roles and permissions, and groups.

  • Portals: a top-level portal controls several sub-portals, one per audience (staff, customers, partners). Copy a course between portals instead of rebuilding.
  • Roles: give each person only the rights they need: view, edit, create, delete. Mirror your real structure: director, manager, trainer, learner.
  • Groups: collect people by team, role or place. Use filter rules so people move in and out automatically, for example 'joined in the last 30 days' feeds the onboarding group.
  • Report by group so each manager sees only their own people.
Worked example. New-hire rule: if joined date is within 30 days, add to Onboarding group and assign the three starter courses. After 30 days, remove from Onboarding.

Activity. Design the portal, role and group map for a real organisation on one page; write three group filter rules.

Standard to meet. Every audience has a portal or group; no role has more rights than its tasks need; rules are testable.

Module 4 Automating the work 4 hApply

Covers integrations, automation, dynamic rules and notifications.

  • Integrations: link HR, CRM, meeting tools and payment. Single sign-in lets people use one login. An open data interface lets other systems add users and pull results.
  • Automation: add and remove users from the HR list, enrol by role, send due-date reminders.
  • Dynamic rules are 'if this, then that': if onboarding is complete then award the certificate and enrol in the next course.
  • Notifications: personal, timely, not endless. Use the person's name, one clear action and one date.
Worked example. Rule: IF course 'Safety basics' is complete THEN issue certificate AND enrol in 'Site safety practical' AND send message to line manager.

Activity. Write five automation rules for your brief, each with trigger, action and a test case.

Standard to meet. Each rule has a trigger, an action and a way to check it fired; no rule can send more than one message a day to a person.

Module 5 Assessing, certifying and reporting 4 hEvaluate

Covers exams, surveys, reporting, skills and certificate tracking, and selling courses.

  • Exams: several question types; auto-marking gives fast feedback. Match the question verb to the course outcome verb.
  • Surveys inside a course show what to fix. Ask one question per idea.
  • Reports: progress, results, survey answers; schedule and export them. Look for patterns (for example, written lessons pass better than video).
  • Certificates: auto-issue on completion; set expiry dates and automatic re-enrolment for renewals; keep records audit-ready.
  • Selling: a built-in store or payment link, bundles of courses, plans for groups, member discounts.
Worked example. Pattern found in a sample report (example values): video lessons pass at 62%, written lessons at 81%. Action: add a written summary to each video lesson and compare next month.

Activity. Choose three measures for your brief (for example completion rate, first-attempt pass rate, time to certificate) and sketch the report that shows them.

Standard to meet. Each measure has a definition, a target and an action to take if it is missed.

Module 6 Engagement, community and gamification 3 hApply

People finish courses when the experience is social, clear and rewarding. Learn simple engagement tools and how to check they help.

  • Points, badges and progress bars show effort; keep rules public and fair.
  • Forums, groups and shared projects make learners talk to each other; assign a moderator and house rules.
  • Leaderboards can discourage some people; make them optional and show personal progress too.
  • Measure the effect: compare completion before and after a change, with the same kind of learners.
Worked example. Illustration with example values: a 30-learner onboarding group has 18 completions in a month (60 per cent). After a progress bar and a weekly group message the next group has 24 of 30 (80 per cent). The change is noted, and the team repeats it with a third group before calling it proven.

Activity. Choose two engagement tools for your brief, write the rule for each and a way to measure whether it works.

Standard to meet. Each tool has a fair public rule, a measure and a stop condition; the claim of benefit is based on a comparison.

Module 7 Pilot, roll-out and review 4 hCreate

Test with a small group first, then roll out in steps, then review with data.

  • Pilot with a small group of real learners and one friendly manager.
  • Write success measures before the pilot: completion, first-attempt pass, learner score, support requests.
  • Roll out in steps by group; keep a rollback plan and a support rota.
  • Review at 30 and 90 days and update the brief.
Worked example. Example values: a pilot with 30 learners reaches 27 completions: 27 / 30 = 90 per cent against a target of 85 per cent. Three support requests were all about login, so a one-page login guide is added before roll-out.

Activity. Write a one-page pilot and roll-out plan for your brief with measures, dates and a rollback step.

Standard to meet. Plan has at least three measures with targets, three dates and a rollback step.

Module 8 Experience, security and reliability 4 hEvaluate

Covers learner experience, branding, language, mobile, security, reliability and migration.

  • Learner experience: easy to find, works on any device, offers extra optional courses so learners steer their own learning. Social tools (forums, sharing) keep people engaged.
  • Branding: your colours and logo; white-labelling also removes the supplier's marks and sets your own web address.
  • Languages and mobile: the interface in learners' own languages, and a design that works on phones and weak connections.
  • Security: ask about independent security audits, data-protection law compliance, staff training and encryption. Keep personal data to what you need.
  • Reliability: ask for the uptime record and maintenance windows; plan what happens when the platform is down.
  • Migration: moving platforms is like moving house; list what must move (users, records, courses), test with a sample first.
Worked example. Checklist question: 'Show us your last independent security audit and how you handle a data-protection request from a learner.' A good answer is specific, dated and documented.

Activity. Complete a 15-line due-diligence checklist for a platform you could choose and score it.

Standard to meet. Each line has evidence, a score and a reason; two risks are named with a mitigation.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Give two costs of an open source platform that a licence fee hides.

Model answer: Hosting, installing, securing, upgrading and staff time.

Q2. When would you pick a sequenced path and when learner choice?

Model answer: Sequenced when order matters; learner choice when order does not.

Q3. What does SCORM or xAPI let you do?

Model answer: Move a course between tools and report results to the platform.

Q4. Why give each role only the rights it needs?

Model answer: Less risk and less clutter for each person.

Q5. Write a dynamic rule for onboarding.

Model answer: IF onboarding course complete THEN issue certificate AND enrol in the next course.

Q6. Name two single-sign-in benefits.

Model answer: Fewer password resets for IT; one verified login for learners.

Q7. What should a notification contain?

Model answer: Person's name, one action, one date.

Q8. How do you make a certificate renew?

Model answer: Set an expiry date and automatic re-enrolment with a reminder.

Q9. Name three questions for a security review.

Model answer: Latest independent audit; data-protection compliance; how requests and breaches are handled.

Q10. Why test a small sample before migrating?

Model answer: To find missing users, records or course errors before the full move.

ten locations and global

Your place

Choose a platform that can store learner data in a place your law allows.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Credit and level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Credit and level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Credit and level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Credit and level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Credit and level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Credit and level frame: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Credit and level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Credit and level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Credit and level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Credit and level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Credit and level frame: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Credit and level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
