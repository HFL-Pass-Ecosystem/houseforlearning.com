ERP and CRM Business Applications: Sales, Operations and Finance

Run one connected business application for sales, stock, purchasing and finance, and tailor it with low-code tools.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run one connected business application for sales, stock, purchasing and finance, and tailor it with low-code tools. Finish all 8 modules on a practice set-up.

Condition

A free trial or practice account, practice data only (never real customer data), a peer circle and a mentor for the final review.

Standard

Every module standard is met; records are kept; a peer and a mentor rate the work 'Meets' or better on the rubric.

Who it is for: Business analysts, operations staff, finance staff, consultants, managers. Requirements: a computer and internet. Materials: slides with notes, worked examples, activities, quiz, rubric.

How the ladder works: modules climb from L1 (remember) to L7 (create); the ladder section shows each level as a slide. Take them in order; the first three suit new learners, 4 and 5 suit practitioners, 6 and 7 suit advanced staff.

Module 8 Post-go-live review and coaching the team 4 hEvaluateL7 Super expert

A system is not finished on go-live day. In the first months you check whether it really helps, fix what hurts and teach others to use it well.

  • Compare results with the goals set in the plan: orders per day, stock errors, days to close the month.
  • Review the first 90 days: open issues, user complaints, data quality and slow screens.
  • Rank improvements by benefit and effort, then pick the top three.
  • Coach users with short guides, a practice session and a named super-user in each team.
Worked example. Three months after go-live, stock errors fell from 8 per week to 2, but 30 percent of quotes are still typed in a side spreadsheet. The review finds the quote form is too long, so the team trims it and runs a 30 minute coaching session.

Activity. Write a one-page review of a practice system with three measured results, three problems ranked by benefit and effort, and a 20 minute lesson plan for new users.

Standard to meet. Review uses real numbers; top three actions are justified; lesson plan has a goal, steps and a check that learners can do the task.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. A sales order is confirmed and the goods are shipped. Which records change in a joined-up business system?

Model answer: Stock goes down, an invoice is created and the ledger and customer balance are updated. This is the benefit of one connected set of records. (Covered in module 1: One business, one system. To pass that module: Every step and record named.)

Q2. Name two examples of master data and two of transactions.

Model answer: Master data: customer and item (also supplier). Transactions: a sales order and a purchase receipt. Master data changes rarely; transactions happen every day. (Covered in module 1: One business, one system. To pass that module: Every step and record named.)

Q3. A guided sales flow stops a user from moving a deal to Won. What is the likely reason and who should fix it?

Model answer: A required step is missing, such as a named decision maker. The user completes the step; the flow should not be bypassed. (Covered in module 2: Sales and customer engagement. To pass that module: Order created; no step skipped.)

Q4. An item has a reorder point of 20 units and 18 are on hand. What should happen?

Model answer: The system should suggest a purchase for that item, because stock is below the reorder point. The buyer checks the quantity and confirms. (Covered in module 3: Stock, buying and warehouse. To pass that module: Stock matches count; variances explained.)

Q5. A stock count shows 96 units but the system says 100. Explain what to do.

Model answer: Find the reason first, such as a missed receipt or a damaged item, then post an adjustment of minus 4 with a note. Never change the number without a reason. (Covered in module 3: Stock, buying and warehouse. To pass that module: Stock matches count; variances explained.)

Q6. Why do posting profiles matter in finance setup?

Model answer: They decide which ledger accounts a transaction uses. A wrong profile sends money to the wrong account and makes reports untrue. (Covered in module 4: Finance and projects. To pass that module: Entries land in the right dimension.)

Q7. A flow must ask a manager to approve any discount above 10 percent. Name its trigger, condition and action.

Model answer: Trigger: a quote is saved. Condition: discount is greater than 10 percent. Action: send an approval request to the manager. (Covered in module 5: Low-code: forms, flows and apps. To pass that module: Tested in sandbox; works end to end.)

Q8. Why should cutover to a new system be rehearsed twice before the real weekend?

Model answer: Each rehearsal shows steps that fail or take too long, so you can fix them and time the work. By the real weekend the team knows the order, the time needed and the rollback point. (Covered in module 7: Implementation and architecture. To pass that module: Plan has fit-gap, cutover rehearsal, rollback and owners.)

Q9. A regional manager must see only their own region in a shared report. How is this done?

Model answer: Use row-level access, so the same report shows only the rows that match the viewer's region. Test it with a real manager account. (Covered in module 6: Data, reports and dashboards. To pass that module: Five numbers; access test passed.)

Q10. Compare a fit-gap review with a post-go-live review.

Model answer: A fit-gap review is done before the project to see what the system already does and what is missing. A post-go-live review is done after, to measure results and plan improvements. (Covered in module 8: Post-go-live review and coaching the team. To pass that module: Review uses real numbers; top three actions are justified; lesson plan has a goal, steps and a check that learners can do the task.)

levels L1 to L7

The ladder, slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: L1 Basic: know the words

Remember the main parts of a business application: sales, stock, buying, finance and projects. Recognise the difference between master data and transactions. Identify where to click to find a customer or an item.

Speaker notes. Show one order moving from quote to invoice on a diagram. Check that learners can name each record. A common mistake is mixing up an item (master data) with an order line (transaction).

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: L2 Awareness: see how it connects

Understand how one action in sales changes stock and finance. Explain why a clean customer list helps every team. Describe what a guided process does.

Speaker notes. Use a simple story: one sale, three departments. Ask learners to say which team sees the change. Watch for learners who think each module has its own separate data.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: L3 Foundation: do daily tasks

Apply the standard steps to create a lead, a quote, a purchase order and a stock count. Follow checklists for every task. Fix simple data entry errors.

Speaker notes. Let learners complete the tasks on a practice company. Check that each record is saved and linked. A common mistake is skipping a required step and then editing the result by hand.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: L4 Practitioner: run a whole cycle

Apply the full order-to-cash and purchase-to-pay cycles without help. Analyse a stock variance and explain it. Set reorder points and dimensions correctly.

Speaker notes. Give a case with a few planted errors. Ask learners to find and explain each one. Common mistake: adjusting stock numbers to match, without finding the cause.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: L5 Specialist: build small tools

Create a flow, a form and a small app that solve a real team problem. Analyse who should see which data and set access to match. Test in a separate environment before release.

Speaker notes. Review each build with the trigger, condition, action rule. Ask what happens if the flow fails. Common mistake: building straight in the live system.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: L6 Advanced: judge and improve

Evaluate dashboards, data models and flows for accuracy and safety. Compare options by benefit and effort. Review a live system after go-live and recommend the top improvements.

Speaker notes. Ask learners to defend five dashboard numbers and drop the rest. Check that every recommendation is backed by a measured result. Common mistake: adding more reports instead of fixing bad data.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: L7 Super expert: design the programme

Create an implementation plan with fit-gap review, migration, rehearsal, rollback, training and benefits tracking. Evaluate trade-offs across the whole business. Coach others to run the system on their own.

Speaker notes. Ask for a plan that two peers can challenge. Check owners, dates and a rollback path. Common mistake: planning the technology but forgetting training and support.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local law changes how you store customer data and issue invoices. Check it for your place first.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Use INR for amounts. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Use EUR for amounts. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Use AED for amounts. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Use BRL for amounts. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Use CAD for amounts. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Use USD for amounts. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Use SGD for amounts. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Use AUD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Use HKD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Use GEL or ILS for amounts. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Use KYD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Use MXN for amounts. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
