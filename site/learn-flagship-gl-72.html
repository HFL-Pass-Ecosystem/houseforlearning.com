flagship course

VR Live Events

Plan and run a live virtual-reality or virtual event: platforms, access, moderation, safety and measures of success.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner29:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain event formats and platforms.
  • Apply Prepare tech checks and run sheets.
  • Analyse Read attendance data.
  • Evaluate Judge safety, privacy and access.
  • Create Run a small pilot event.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and run a 30-minute virtual event for 10 to 20 people, with rehearsal, moderation, accessibility, feedback and report.

Condition

Computers or VR headsets as available, a free event platform, two peers and a mentor.

Standard

Rehearsal passes; moderation rules enforced; accessibility options present; feedback from 80% of attendees; report with data; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Event planners, teachers, community groups, trainers. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Formats, platforms and fit 3 hUnderstand

A virtual event can run on a flat screen, a headset or both. The best format is the one your audience can use.

  • Desktop 3D spaces and video rooms are easiest to join.
  • Headsets give presence but need equipment, space and comfort checks.
  • Hybrid events stream a live room to virtual guests.
  • Check bandwidth, device ownership and time zones.
Worked example. A training session uses a desktop 3D room so that every learner can join from a laptop. A product launch offers a headset option to early adopters after a survey shows 5 per cent own headsets.

Activity. Compare three formats for one event goal.

Standard to meet. Table includes device needs, access and cost.

Module 2 Goals, audience and plan 3 hCreate

Define success before choosing tools.

  • Write one goal with a number, such as 60 attendees with 70 per cent staying to the end.
  • Write a one-page event plan: audience, format, date, agenda, roles and budget.
  • Plan how guests join: link, app or invitation.
  • Prepare an event page with what to expect.
Worked example. A community group aims for 60 attendees and a 4 out of 5 satisfaction score. It sends one link 24 hours before and a reminder one hour before.

Activity. Write goals and a one-page plan.

Standard to meet. Goals are measurable and the plan covers roles and timing.

Module 3 Technical checks and fall-backs 4 hApply

Test everything on real devices before the event.

  • Accounts, links, audio, avatars and screen sharing.
  • Test on each device type and connection speed.
  • Prepare a backup video link and a helper desk.
  • Record known issues and fixes.
Worked example. A fallback video link saves a session when the 3D room fails to load for ten attendees.

Activity. Make a ten-item checklist and test it on three devices.

Standard to meet. Checklist is tested and issues are logged.

Module 4 Moderation and safety 4 hEvaluate

Virtual spaces need clear rules and active moderators.

  • Code of conduct; tools to mute, block and report.
  • Age rules, parental consent for minors and no sharing of personal data.
  • Trained moderators with a script: warn, mute, remove, report.
  • Keep an incident log.
Worked example. A disruptive participant is muted, removed and reported according to the code. The log records time, action and follow-up.

Activity. Write rules, a moderator script and an escalation plan.

Standard to meet. Rules are clear and tested in a mock session.

Module 5 Accessibility and comfort 4 hEvaluate

Everyone should be able to take part.

  • Captions, transcripts and keyboard options.
  • Motion-sickness settings: teleport movement, short sessions and breaks.
  • Time zones, language and sign-up form questions about needs.
  • Alternatives for those without headsets.
Worked example. Teleport movement and 10-minute breaks reduce discomfort for headset users. A form asks about captions and break needs.

Activity. Write an access plan.

Standard to meet. Measures cover vision, hearing, motion and device access.

Module 6 Run sheet and rehearsal 4 hApply

Smooth delivery needs a minute-by-minute plan.

  • Roles: host, tech, moderator, content lead.
  • Rehearse twice with real devices and record it.
  • Plan transitions and time for questions.
  • Keep a printed copy of the run sheet.
Worked example. A rehearsal reveals audio echo; the host uses headphones for the live run. A 30-second silence at the start is cut.

Activity. Run a rehearsal and log fixes.

Standard to meet. Fixes are logged and the second rehearsal is clean.

Module 7 Live run 4 hCreate

Deliver your pilot event calmly.

  • Welcome, rules, content, Q and A, close.
  • Handle problems calmly and use the helper desk.
  • Collect feedback and record attendance.
  • Keep a live issue log with times.
Worked example. The pilot finishes on time with 14 attendees and three technical issues, all solved within 5 minutes.

Activity. Run the pilot event.

Standard to meet. Mentor observes and signs the run.

Module 8 Measure, report and improve 3 hAnalyse

Learn from the data.

  • Attendance, time in event and feedback scores.
  • Fix the top issues and plan the next event.
  • Send a thank-you and a survey within 24 hours.
  • Share a one-page report.
Worked example. Average rating 4.3 out of 5 and 80 per cent stayed to the end. The report lists three fixes.

Activity. Write the report.

Standard to meet. Report has numbers, feedback and three dated improvements.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: plan, run sheet, tech checklist, feedback report. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Tech readinessGapsCheckedTested twice
Safety and accessGapsPlansPlans applied
ReportingNo dataDataData and actions
Honesty and evidenceClaims without proofEvery claim has a recordEvery claim linked to a dated record and checked by a peer
Safety, ethics and local rulesNot consideredChecked and written downChecked, dated and explained to others

ten locations and global

Your place

Privacy and child-protection rules differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
