Online Car Dealership Operations

Run the online side of a car dealership honestly: list vehicles with true condition, price fairly, handle enquiries and test drives, check paperwork and keep buyers safe.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Run the online side of a car dealership honestly: list vehicles with true condition, price fairly, handle enquiries and test drives, check paperwork and keep buyers safe.

  • Understand Explain how online car sales work and the legal duties of a seller.
  • Apply Describe vehicles accurately with photos and checks.
  • Analyse Analyse price, margin and days in stock.
  • Create Create enquiry, viewing and handover processes.
  • Evaluate Evaluate trust through reviews, returns and complaints.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prepare and run an online listing and enquiry process for 5 vehicles (real or practice) with condition reports, pricing, viewing checklist and paperwork checklist.

Condition

Vehicle data and photos you may use, a listing page or spreadsheet, a checklist template, a mentor with motor-trade experience and a peer circle. About 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

Every listing states true condition, mileage, history and faults with evidence; price within a market range with reasons; ownership and paperwork verified; no pressure sales; complaints answered within 2 working days.

Who it is for: Dealership staff, small traders, online sales assistants, students. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Check ownership and finance status before listing. Never hide faults or wind back mileage. Follow consumer, safety and licensing rules for traders. Keep test drives safe and insured.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How online car sales work 4 hUnderstand

Online adds reach, not shortcuts. You will map the online buying journey from search to handover and set stock standards, so you can promise realistic delivery times and list only suitable vehicles.

  • Listing, enquiry, viewing, test drive, paperwork, payment, handover.
  • Seller duties: accurate description; right to sell.
  • Buyer rights: returns, warranty as per local law.
  • Trust is the product.
  • Map the full customer journey from search to handover, listing who does what at each step, and time each step so you can promise realistic delivery dates.
  • Decide which stock you will handle, such as vehicles under ten years old, and write the standards a vehicle must meet before it is listed.
  • Learn the local steps to transfer ownership and register a change of keeper.
  • Keep copies of all documents for the legal period.
Worked example. A listing that hides a repainted door loses trust and may break the law.

Activity. Map the sales steps.

Standard to meet. Duties named at each step.

Common mistake. Thinking online sales are different law.

Second worked example. The sales map has seven steps: listing, enquiry, viewing, test drive, paperwork, payment, handover, with the seller duty named at each step.

Check yourself. Which rules apply?

Module 2 Check the vehicle and paperwork 6 hUnderstand

Know what you sell. You will verify identity, registration, history and outstanding finance, and complete a written inspection, so every vehicle you list has documented checks behind it.

  • Ownership proof, registration, finance check.
  • History and service records.
  • Inspection checklist: body, tyres, lights, fluids, brakes, test drive.
  • Safety recalls.
  • Verify identity details, the registration number and service history against the paperwork, and check that no money is owed on the vehicle before you list it.
  • Use a written inspection list covering tyres, brakes, lights, fluid levels and warning lights, and record the date and name of the person who completed it.
  • Add a short line on the warranty you offer, in plain words.
  • Check recalls and open safety notices for the model.
Worked example. Mileage on the dash is 62,000 km but service records show 78,000 km earlier: investigate before listing.

Activity. Complete a checklist for two vehicles.

Standard to meet. Findings recorded with photos.

Common mistake. Skipping ownership checks.

Second worked example. The inspection finds a service record showing 78,000 km two years ago while the dash reads 62,000. The car is not listed until the history is checked.

Check yourself. What did you verify?

Module 3 Honest descriptions and photos 8 hApply

Show what a buyer would see. You will photograph vehicles thoroughly and write descriptions from the inspection list, which builds buyer trust because the listing matches what they later see.

  • Title: make, model, year, mileage.
  • 20+ photos: all sides, interior, faults.
  • Plain condition notes.
  • Equipment list.
  • Photograph every vehicle from at least 20 angles, including damage, in daylight, and write the description from the inspection list so wording matches what you can show.
  • State the mileage, previous owners and any known faults plainly, as an honest note of a small scratch builds more trust than a discovered surprise.
  • Invite the buyer to bring their own inspector if they wish.
  • Never promise features you cannot prove.
Worked example. Photos include a scratch on the rear bumper with a note.

Activity. Create three listings.

Standard to meet. Every fault shown.

Common mistake. Hiding faults.

Second worked example. Listing: 'Hatchback, 2018, 62,000 km, one scratch on rear bumper (photo 14), full service history, two keys', with 24 photos.

Check yourself. Which faults did you show?

Module 4 Pricing 8 hApply

Fair and defendable. You will compare similar listings, set prices above total cost with a stated margin and schedule reviews, replacing guesswork with a repeatable pricing routine.

  • Compare similar cars locally.
  • Adjust for mileage, condition, history.
  • Margin and costs: reconditioning, fees, finance.
  • Days in stock cost money.
  • Research at least five comparable listings, note the spread between the lowest and highest, and set your price with a stated margin above your total costs.
  • Review prices every two weeks and reduce by a planned step, such as 2 percent, for vehicles unsold after 30 days instead of guessing.
  • Avoid pressure phrases such as 'only today'.
  • Describe tyre tread in millimetres, not in words like 'good'.
Worked example. Cost 7,200 + prep 400; market 8,600 (an estimate; check your market); price 8,500; margin 900.

Activity. Price five vehicles.

Standard to meet. Reasons for each price.

Common mistake. Pricing from memory.

Second worked example. A used van costs 11,500 and needs 600 of repairs. Local listings for similar vans run from 12,800 to 13,900, so a price of 13,200 leaves about 1,100 margin.

Check yourself. What range?

Module 5 Enquiries and trust 8 hAnalyse

Fast, clear and respectful. You will answer enquiries within two hours using templates and offer video walk-rounds, helping distant buyers feel informed and respected before they travel.

  • Reply within 2 hours in opening times.
  • Answer with facts and offer a video walk-around.
  • Avoid pressure; never take deposits without terms.
  • Keep a log.
  • Reply to every enquiry within two hours during opening times, using a template that answers the common questions and invites a call or viewing.
  • Offer a live video walk-round for distant buyers, and keep a list of questions asked so you can improve the listing text.
  • Keep keys, manuals and spare wheels listed in the sale.
  • Insure test drives with a clear policy.
Worked example. A buyer asks for rust photos: send underbody pictures and a mechanic's note.

Activity. Answer ten practice enquiries.

Standard to meet. Replies factual and polite.

Common mistake. Slow replies.

Second worked example. Enquiry reply in under 2 hours: 'Here are underbody photos and the mechanic's note. Would a video call suit you?'

Check yourself. What is your target?

Module 6 Viewings and test drives 10 hCreate

Safe for everyone. You will arrange viewings and test drives with written confirmation, licence checks and a second person present, keeping buyers, staff and the vehicle safe.

  • ID, licence and insurance checks.
  • Route and speed rules.
  • Accompany the buyer.
  • Record condition before and after.
  • Confirm viewings in writing with address, time and what to bring, check the driver's licence and insurance before a test drive, and set a fixed route and time.
  • Have a second person present for each test drive, and record fuel level and mileage before and after in a short checklist.
  • Keep a log of enquiries and outcomes.
  • Reply to complaints within two working days.
Worked example. The buyer's licence is checked and copied securely; the route avoids busy roads.

Activity. Write a test-drive procedure.

Standard to meet. Procedure covers insurance.

Common mistake. Unsafe test drives.

Second worked example. Test drive: licence and insurance checked, route avoids busy roads, accompanied, condition recorded before and after.

Check yourself. What is your procedure?

Module 7 Contracts, payment and handover 6 hEvaluate

Paperwork that protects both sides. You will prepare a written contract, accept traceable payment and finish a handover checklist, so both sides leave with clear records of what was agreed.

  • Sales agreement: price, condition, warranty, date.
  • Payment methods: traceable, no large cash.
  • Transfer of ownership and insurance.
  • Handover checklist and receipt.
  • Provide a written sales contract listing the price, deposit, vehicle details, known faults and handover date, and give the buyer a copy before any payment is taken.
  • Accept payment only by traceable methods, issue a receipt, and complete a handover checklist covering keys, documents, fuel level and a final walk-round.
  • Offer to take back a car if a hidden major fault is found, per the law.
  • Check that your adverts do not mislead on finance costs.
Worked example. Payment by bank transfer confirmed before keys are released.

Activity. Make a paperwork checklist.

Standard to meet. Checklist complete.

Common mistake. Cash handling.

Second worked example. On handover the seller and buyer sign the sale paper, the buyer shows proof of insurance, and the ownership change is filed the same day. A copy of each document goes in the file.

Check yourself. What payments do you accept?

Module 8 After-sales and growth 4 hCreate

Reputation lasts. You will follow up with buyers, log complaints and review days-to-sell and margin each month, using real figures to decide which stock to buy next.

  • Follow-up call.
  • Handle faults under law and warranty.
  • Reviews and referrals.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Contact each buyer after one week and one month to ask whether anything needs attention, and keep a log of complaints with how and when each was resolved.
  • Review monthly numbers for days to sell, margin and enquiries per listing, and use them to decide which stock to buy next.
  • Review monthly the cars that take longest to sell.
  • Share lessons with your trade peers.
Worked example. A brake light fault in week 1 is fixed free and the buyer posts a good review.

Activity. Write after-sales rules and a plan.

Standard to meet. Rules fair and clear.

Common mistake. No after-sales.

Second worked example. A buyer returns after two weeks because a window button sticks. The shop repairs it free in an hour, notes it in the record, and asks permission before posting the buyer's kind comment.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name two seller duties.

Model answer: Accurate description and right to sell. (Covered in module 1: How online car sales work. To pass that module: Duties named at each step.)

Q2. Why check finance status?

Model answer: The car may be owned by a lender. (Covered in module 2: Check the vehicle and paperwork. To pass that module: Findings recorded with photos.)

Q3. Why show faults?

Model answer: Honesty and legal duty. (Covered in module 3: Honest descriptions and photos. To pass that module: Every fault shown.)

Q4. Pricing method?

Model answer: Compare similar cars and adjust for condition. (Covered in module 4: Pricing. To pass that module: Reasons for each price.)

Q5. Why log enquiries?

Model answer: To follow up and prove fair handling. (Covered in module 5: Enquiries and trust. To pass that module: Replies factual and polite.)

Q6. Safe test-drive rule?

Model answer: Check licence and insurance and accompany the buyer. (Covered in module 6: Viewings and test drives. To pass that module: Procedure covers insurance.)

Q7. Why avoid large cash?

Model answer: Fraud and safety risk. (Covered in module 7: Contracts, payment and handover. To pass that module: Checklist complete.)

Q8. What if mileage looks wrong?

Model answer: Investigate before listing. (Covered in module 2: Check the vehicle and paperwork. To pass that module: Findings recorded with photos.)

Q9. Why after-sales?

Model answer: Reputation and legal duties. (Covered in module 8: After-sales and growth. To pass that module: Rules fair and clear.)

Q10. What does handover include?

Model answer: Receipt, ownership transfer and insurance. (Covered in module 7: Contracts, payment and handover. To pass that module: Checklist complete.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Trader licences, vehicle registration, warranty and consumer-return rules differ by place; check them first. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Partner to practise with: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.
