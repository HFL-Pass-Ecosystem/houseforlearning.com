Ride and delivery driving

Run a real ride and delivery driving pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small ride and delivery driving offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: local, logistics and field services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the words and the kinds of driving work 6 hUnderstand

Ride and delivery driving means moving people or goods for pay through an app or a company. Start with the basics.

  • Learn the terms: trip, fare, tip, surge, fee, shift, route, active time, self-employed.
  • Kinds of work: rides, food delivery, parcel delivery, courier.
  • Most app drivers are paid per trip and pay their own costs.
  • Name the hours and zones with the most demand near you.
  • Read how the app calculates fares and what share you keep.
  • List what you need to start: licence, vehicle, phone, insurance and account approval.
  • Compare delivery by car, bike and on foot where allowed, and say which suits you.
Worked example. Lunch hours in a business area have many short delivery trips; late nights near venues have more rides. If a lunch hour gives 4 trips at 5 dollars each, 4 x 5 = 20 dollars before costs.

Activity. Write a table of work type, pay style, vehicle and hours for three kinds of driving.

Standard to meet. Task: compare three kinds of work. Condition: from public information. Standard: table complete and accurate.

Module 2 Explain a driving shift from start to end 6 hApply

A shift has steps. If you can name them, you plan time, fuel and safety.

  • The steps: vehicle check, go online, accept trips, pickup, drop-off, break, go offline, log.
  • Check tyres, lights, brakes, fluids and cleanliness before you start.
  • Plan breaks and rest; tired driving is dangerous.
  • Record start and end time, kilometres and earnings every shift.
  • Set a stop time before you start and keep to it.
  • Keep water, a phone charger and a first aid kit in the vehicle.
  • Write down each trip's start and end place so you can review the day.
Worked example. Before a shift you check tyres and lights, charge your phone, and plan a rest at the fourth hour. An 8-hour shift with a 30-minute break gives 8 - 0.5 = 7.5 hours of driving time.

Activity. Make a pre-shift checklist and a shift log.

Standard to meet. Task: make two forms. Condition: one page each. Standard: all checks present and usable on a real shift.

Module 3 Drive safely and serve people well 10 hApply

Now you apply the basics. Drive safely and treat customers with care.

  • Follow all traffic laws; never use a hand-held phone while driving.
  • Keep a calm voice, confirm the pickup and say the destination.
  • Keep the car clean and handle food or parcels with care.
  • Report any problem with a trip through the app support.
  • Park legally and never block traffic, hydrants or bike lanes.
  • Offer help with bags only when it is safe and agreed.
  • Decide in advance what you do if a trip feels unsafe: refuse or end it, and report.
Worked example. A rider is not at the pickup. You wait the allowed time, call, and then follow the app's no-show steps. If the allowed wait is 5 minutes and you arrived at 6:10, you may leave at 6:15.

Activity. Role-play ten pickups with a friend and write what you would change.

Standard to meet. Task: practise ten trips. Condition: parked, with a friend as customer. Standard: greeting, confirmation and handover done every time.

Module 4 Follow Canadian rules for drivers 8 hEvaluate

Rules for driving for pay differ by province and city. Learn which ones touch you.

  • You need the right licence class for your vehicle; some places need extra checks or permits for ride work. Check the current rule with the provincial transport office.
  • Ordinary personal insurance may not cover paid driving; ask your insurer what cover you need.
  • Cities can set rules for taxis and ride services; check the current rule with the local authority.
  • App drivers are usually self-employed; income is taxable and sales tax rules may apply. Check with the tax agency.
  • Ask whether your vehicle must pass an inspection for this work; check the provincial rule.
  • Ask the provincial transport office whether your vehicle needs any special plates or permits for paid driving.
  • Keep your licence, registration and insurance papers current and in the vehicle.
Worked example. You ask your insurer in writing: 'Am I covered when I drive for a ride app?' and keep the reply. If the reply says you need extra cover, you buy it before your next shift. You file the reply with your insurance papers.

Activity. Make a table of rule, who sets it, what you must do and where you checked, for ten items.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: from official public pages. Standard: each row has a dated source; unknowns say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.

Module 5 Work out real earnings 8 hApply

Your pay is what is left after vehicle costs. Work the numbers.

  • Costs: fuel or charging, insurance, repairs, tyres, depreciation, phone, parking and fees.
  • Cost per kilometre = yearly vehicle costs / yearly kilometres.
  • Real hourly pay = (earnings - costs) / total hours, including waiting.
  • Plan for slow weeks by keeping a small cash buffer.
  • Count unpaid time: waiting, driving to a pickup and charging.
  • Spread large costs, such as tyres, over the kilometres they last.
  • Set aside an amount for tax from every payout.
Worked example. Earnings 154, fuel and wear 42, hours 7: net = 154 - 42 = 112, and real pay = 112 / 7 = 16 per hour. If yearly vehicle costs are 6,000 and you drive 20,000 km, cost = 6,000 / 20,000 = 0.30 per km. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Log five shifts and compute real pay per hour for each.

Standard to meet. Task: compute real pay. Condition: from your own log. Standard: all costs shown and a mentor can follow.

Module 6 Find the best times and keep a good record 6 hCreate

Better planning raises pay and lowers risk. Use your log to decide.

  • Compare pay per hour by day and time of day.
  • Avoid long unpaid waits and trips that end far from demand.
  • Keep ratings steady by being on time and polite.
  • Keep receipts and a mileage log for tax.
  • Mark the zones where you wait longest and test a different zone.
  • Compare whole weeks, not single days, before you change your hours.
  • Note weather and events that change demand.
Worked example. Your log shows Friday lunch pays 20 per hour and Tuesday afternoon 11. Working 4 hours of Friday lunch instead of Tuesday afternoon earns 4 x 20 - 4 x 11 = 36 more. You work more Friday lunch.

Activity. Compare ten shifts and write a plan for next month.

Standard to meet. Task: analyse ten shifts. Condition: with simple spreadsheet. Standard: the plan uses numbers.

Module 7 Protect yourself, your passengers and your data 6 hEvaluate

Safe habits protect you, others and your account.

  • Wear a seat belt, rest when tired and never drive after alcohol or drugs.
  • Know the emergency steps: pull over safely, call emergency services, report in the app.
  • Keep customer addresses private.
  • Know how to stay safe at night and in unknown places.
  • Share your trip with a trusted person when working at night, if the app allows.
  • Check the provincial rule before you record passengers with a camera.
  • Plan for a breakdown: safe stopping place, tow number and warning lights.
Worked example. A customer is rude. You stay calm, end the trip at a safe place and report it to the app. You write the time and place in your log so you can show what happened.

Activity. Write an emergency card, a pre-trip check and a privacy note.

Standard to meet. Task: write three documents. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: each is clear and ready to use.

Module 8 Run a real pilot and decide 8 hCreate

Now you run it for real. Drive, measure and decide whether it is worth it.

  • Set a 60-day goal, such as 60 hours and a real hourly pay target.
  • Every week record hours, kilometres, income, costs and safety events.
  • Ask: do I earn enough after costs and tax?
  • At the end choose: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Compare real pay with your pay target after costs and tax.
  • List the hours you will stop working.
  • Check your vehicle's condition and costs after 60 days.
Worked example. After 60 days: 62 hours, real pay 16 per hour, no incidents. Total real pay = 62 x 16 = 992. You decide to drive only the best 20 hours a week.

Activity. Run the pilot, keep the weekly log and present a plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a 60-day pilot. Condition: legal, insured driving. Standard: log complete, decision uses numbers, a panel can repeat the plan.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
