Veterinary and Pet Care Business

Start and run a small pet care business, from grooming, boarding and walking to records, hygiene and when to refer to a vet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain animal welfare needs and the limits of a non-vet service.
  • Apply Run safe handling, hygiene and booking steps.
  • Analyse Cost services and capacity.
  • Evaluate Judge risks, bites, disease and legal duties.
  • Create Launch a 30-day pilot.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and pilot a small pet care service with welfare rules, hygiene plan, booking and price sheet, owner forms and emergency steps.

Condition

Practice animals from willing owners or a shelter partner, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor veterinarian. Medical treatments are for qualified vets only.

Standard

Every pet has an owner form and vaccine record; welfare needs are met; injuries and bites have a response plan; costs and price are shown; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Pet lovers, vet clinic assistants, groomers, walkers and boarders. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Welfare basics and the limits of your service 4 hUnderstand

Every animal has welfare needs, whatever the species. A pet business protects them and knows where its own skills stop.

  • Learn the five needs: a suitable place to live, a suitable diet with clean water, freedom to behave normally, suitable company or separation, and protection from pain, fear and disease.
  • Spot stress early: panting, hiding, shaking, pacing, growling, or refusing usual food.
  • Never leave an animal in a parked vehicle, in direct sun, or without fresh water.
  • Write which species you accept and which you refuse, since each needs different space and skills.
Worked example. A walker finds a dog panting hard after 15 minutes in hot weather. She moves to shade, offers water, ends the walk at 20 minutes and shifts later walks to 7 am, then logs the change.

Activity. Write a one-page welfare card for two species with one action for each need, plus a list of animals you refuse.

Standard to meet. All five needs are covered with no wrong facts, and a mentor vet confirms the card.

Module 2 Owner forms, consent and records 4 hApply

You must know the pet and the owner before you accept responsibility. A clear form protects the animal, the owner and your business.

  • Collect pet name, age, species, vaccine dates, medicines, allergies, behaviour notes, the owner's vet and two emergency contacts.
  • Get written consent for each service and a signed spending limit for urgent vet care.
  • Store records securely, share them only when needed, and update them at every visit.
  • Decline the booking when vaccine proof or contact details are missing, and explain kindly why.
Worked example. A boarding form for a puppy shows one vaccine dose given 10 weeks ago. The owner is asked to check with the vet and the booking pauses until a vet-signed record arrives. The decision and date go in the file.

Activity. Design a one-page owner form, fill it for a practice pet, and write a three-line rule for empty fields.

Standard to meet. The form has vaccine, vet, emergency, consent and spending-limit fields, and a peer finds nothing missing.

Module 3 Safe handling of animals and people 5 hApply

Most injuries happen when handlers rush, corner a frightened animal or use the wrong gear. Calm, planned handling keeps everyone safe.

  • Approach from the side, speak softly, let the animal sniff you, and stop if it stiffens, growls or hides.
  • Match gear to the animal: a fitted harness and lead, a secure carrier, and separate crates for each species.
  • Before the first job, ask the owner about triggers such as other dogs, loud noise or touch near the tail.
  • Tell the owner about any bite, scratch or fall the same day, log it, and send anyone hurt to a doctor.
Worked example. A walker meets a nervous dog near a busy road. She uses a double lead, keeps 3 metres from other dogs, crosses early and rewards calm behaviour. The 30-minute walk ends with no pulling and no bolt.

Activity. Practise leading, crating and a calm approach on one practice animal under a mentor, with a five-step observation sheet.

Standard to meet. The mentor signs off all five steps as safe, with no injuries and no lasting stress.

Module 4 Hygiene and disease control 4 hApply

Clean spaces and hands stop illness spreading between animals. A written routine turns this into habit.

  • Clean and disinfect kennels, bowls, brushes and tables between animals, using a product made for animal areas and following its label.
  • Separate at once any animal that coughs, vomits, has loose stools or looks very tired, then call the owner and vet.
  • Wash hands before and after each animal, bag waste, and keep food in sealed containers against pests.
  • Keep a signed cleaning log with date, area, method and person.
Worked example. A boarding dog coughs at 9 am. It is moved to a separate room, the owner is called by 9:10 and the vet by 9:20. Shared bowls and floors are disinfected by noon and each step is logged.

Activity. Create a daily and weekly hygiene plan for a small boarding space with a one-week log template.

Standard to meet. The plan names task, product type, frequency and person, and a peer can run a day from it.

Module 5 Knowing when to refer to a vet 4 hEvaluate

A pet business is not a veterinary clinic. You notice problems early, keep the animal safe and bring in a licensed vet quickly.

  • Phone a vet at once for suspected poisoning, serious injury, trouble breathing, repeated vomiting, seizures, collapse or heavy bleeding.
  • Do not diagnose or give medicine unless the owner or vet has told you in writing what to do.
  • Keep your clinic and an out-of-hours emergency number on the wall and in your phone.
  • Record the time, symptoms and what the animal ate or touched.
Worked example. A dog eats dark chocolate at 4:05 pm. The carer keeps the wrapper, calls the emergency clinic at 4:10 with the dog's weight, follows the vet's instructions, phones the owner at 4:20 and logs every step.

Activity. Write an emergency card with numbers, owner contacts and a six-step action list, then run a timed mock call.

Standard to meet. The mock call takes under five minutes, and a mentor vet confirms the card refers serious cases and gives no treatment advice.

Module 6 Services, capacity and pricing 5 hAnalyse

A fair price covers every cost, fits the time and space you really have, and leaves a margin for slow weeks.

  • List each service and the time per pet, including cleaning, setup and travel.
  • Add monthly costs: wages, supplies, transport, insurance, space, laundry and advertising.
  • Divide costs by realistic bookings for break-even, add a margin and compare with nearby prices.
  • Cap daily numbers so each animal stays safe, for example four dogs per walker.
Worked example. A groom takes 60 minutes of work plus 30 minutes of cleaning, so 1.5 hours of table time. At 400 INR per staff hour (wages and overheads; an estimate, check your market) plus 120 INR supplies, cost is 720 INR. A markup of about 50 percent gives a price near 1,100 INR.

Activity. Cost three services in a sheet, show break-even bookings per month and set one price each, with currency beside every number.

Standard to meet. Totals are correct, the capacity limit is stated, and the price covers cost with a visible margin.

Module 7 Law, insurance and ethics 4 hEvaluate

Know the rules before your first client. Local law sets licences, insurance and standards, and a licensed professional can confirm them.

  • Look up the business licence, animal-welfare law, tax and boarding or grooming rules, noting the office or document for each.
  • Ask an insurance adviser what cover is needed for injury, damage and animals in your care, and file the policy.
  • Never neglect or harm an animal, and report suspected cruelty to the proper authority.
  • Advertise only what you deliver, write clear terms and keep owners' details private.
Worked example. A provider lists four rules for her town, each with the office name and check date. She asks an adviser about public-liability cover, buys it before taking clients and files the policy number and renewal date.

Activity. List every rule, licence and insurance need for your place with source and date, and mark items to confirm with a professional.

Standard to meet. Each rule has a named source and date, no guess is written as fact, and open questions go to a licensed adviser.

Module 8 Pilot, review and improve 5 hCreate

Run a 30-day pilot with a few clients to prove your service is safe, fairly priced and wanted before you grow.

  • Take only the clients your plan allows and log bookings, complaints, injuries, near misses and rebookings daily.
  • Send each owner a short report after every visit with one photo and one fact about the day.
  • Rank the top three problems by how often or badly they happened and fix each with a date.
  • Write a 90-day plan for price, capacity, training and one community action.
Worked example. A pilot records 24 bookings, 2 complaints and 0 injuries. After photo reports begin, rebookings rise from 35 percent to 55 percent. The top issue, late pickups, is fixed with a set pickup time on the form.

Activity. Present your pilot log, price sheet and 90-day plan to peers and a mentor in a ten-minute talk.

Standard to meet. Figures match the log, three fixes are dated, and peers rate the evidence Meets or better.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name the five welfare needs.

Model answer: A suitable place to live, a suitable diet and clean water, freedom to behave normally, suitable company or separation, and protection from pain, fear and disease. (Covered in module 1: Welfare basics and the limits of your service. To pass that module: All five needs are covered with no wrong facts, and a mentor vet confirms the card.)

Q2. Why do you ask for vaccine records before a booking?

Model answer: They protect other animals in your care, show the pet has had basic protection and tell you when to ask the owner's vet before accepting the pet. (Covered in module 2: Owner forms, consent and records. To pass that module: The form has vaccine, vet, emergency, consent and spending-limit fields, and a peer finds nothing missing.)

Q3. Name two signs of stress in an animal.

Model answer: Panting, hiding, shaking, pacing, growling or refusing food; any of these means slow down, give space and tell the owner. (Covered in module 1: Welfare basics and the limits of your service. To pass that module: All five needs are covered with no wrong facts, and a mentor vet confirms the card.)

Q4. When must you call a vet straight away?

Model answer: For suspected poisoning, serious injury, trouble breathing, repeated vomiting, seizures, collapse or heavy bleeding; record the time and symptoms. (Covered in module 5: Knowing when to refer to a vet. To pass that module: The mock call takes under five minutes, and a mentor vet confirms the card refers serious cases and gives no treatment advice.)

Q5. Can you give a pet medicine on your own judgement?

Model answer: No. Give medicine only as the owner or vet instructs in writing, because only qualified vets may diagnose and treat. (Covered in module 5: Knowing when to refer to a vet. To pass that module: The mock call takes under five minutes, and a mentor vet confirms the card refers serious cases and gives no treatment advice.)

Q6. What belongs on an owner form?

Model answer: Pet details, vaccine dates, medicines, allergies, behaviour notes, vet and emergency contacts, signed consent and a spending limit for urgent care. (Covered in module 2: Owner forms, consent and records. To pass that module: The form has vaccine, vet, emergency, consent and spending-limit fields, and a peer finds nothing missing.)

Q7. Why separate a sick animal from the others?

Model answer: It stops infection spreading to other animals; then clean the area, call the owner and vet, and note the actions in the log. (Covered in module 4: Hygiene and disease control. To pass that module: The plan names task, product type, frequency and person, and a peer can run a day from it.)

Q8. Why should a pet business carry insurance?

Model answer: It helps cover injury or damage claims involving animals or people; ask a licensed adviser which cover local rules and your services need. (Covered in module 7: Law, insurance and ethics. To pass that module: Each rule has a named source and date, no guess is written as fact, and open questions go to a licensed adviser.)

Q9. What is table time and why does it matter for pricing?

Model answer: It is the full time an animal occupies a table or room, including setup and cleaning; it sets how many pets you can serve and what you must charge. (Covered in module 6: Services, capacity and pricing. To pass that module: Totals are correct, the capacity limit is stated, and the price covers cost with a visible margin.)

Q10. What do you track in a 30-day pilot?

Model answer: Bookings, income and costs, complaints, injuries and near misses, and rebookings, so fixes and prices rest on real numbers. (Covered in module 8: Pilot, review and improve. To pass that module: Figures match the log, three fixes are dated, and peers rate the evidence Meets or better.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Licences and animal-welfare laws differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.
