flagship course

Veterinary and Pet Care Business

Start and run a small pet care business, from grooming, boarding and walking to records, hygiene and when to refer to a vet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner35:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain animal welfare needs and the limits of a non-vet service.
  • Apply Run safe handling, hygiene and booking steps.
  • Analyse Cost services and capacity.
  • Evaluate Judge risks, bites, disease and legal duties.
  • Create Launch a 30-day pilot.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and pilot a small pet care service with welfare rules, hygiene plan, booking and price sheet, owner forms and emergency steps.

Condition

Practice animals from willing owners or a shelter partner, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor veterinarian. Medical treatments are for qualified vets only.

Standard

Every pet has an owner form and vaccine record; welfare needs are met; injuries and bites have a response plan; costs and price are shown; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Pet lovers, vet clinic assistants, groomers, walkers and boarders. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Welfare basics 4 hUnderstand

Five needs: food and water, shelter, health, behaviour and company.

  • Know species needs and daily routines.
  • Signs of stress: panting, hiding, aggression.
  • Never leave animals in hot vehicles.
Worked example. A dog left in shade with fresh water stays calm; one in a parked car does not.

Activity. Write a welfare card for two species.

Standard to meet. Each need is covered.

Module 2 Owner forms and records 4 hApply

Know the pet before you take it.

  • Name, age, vaccines, medicines, allergies, vet contact, emergency contact.
  • Written consent for care and emergency treatment.
  • Keep records securely.
Worked example. A boarding form shows an unvaccinated pup, so it is declined.

Activity. Design an owner form.

Standard to meet. Includes vaccine and vet details.

Module 3 Safe handling 5 hApply

Protect animals and people.

  • Approach calmly; read body language.
  • Use correct leads and carriers; keep species apart.
  • First-aid for bites and scratches; report them.
Worked example. A walker uses a double lead near traffic.

Activity. Practise handling steps.

Standard to meet. Mentor observes without safety issues.

Module 4 Hygiene and disease control 4 hApply

Clean spaces prevent disease.

  • Clean and disinfect kennels, tools and bowls between animals.
  • Separate sick animals.
  • Safe waste and hand washing.
Worked example. A cough in one dog leads to isolation and a vet call.

Activity. Create a daily hygiene plan.

Standard to meet. Plan lists frequency and method.

Module 5 When to refer to a vet 4 hEvaluate

Know your limits.

  • Poisoning, injury, breathing trouble, long vomiting, seizures need a vet at once.
  • Do not give medicines unless a vet or owner instructs in writing.
  • Keep the clinic number visible.
Worked example. A boarder calls the vet within 10 minutes for a dog that ate chocolate.

Activity. Write an emergency card.

Standard to meet. Includes numbers, steps and owner contacts.

Module 6 Services, scheduling and pricing 5 hAnalyse

Match services to capacity.

  • Grooming, walking, boarding, day care; time per pet.
  • Costs: staff, supplies, transport, insurance, space.
  • Price to cover costs and margin.
Worked example. A 60-minute groom has 90 minutes of table time.

Activity. Cost three services.

Standard to meet. Totals correct; currency shown.

Module 7 Law, insurance and ethics 4 hEvaluate

Check licences and duties.

  • Business licence, animal-welfare rules and insurance.
  • No cruelty or neglect; report abuse.
  • Honest advertising.
Worked example. A provider obtains public-liability insurance before taking clients.

Activity. List rules for your place.

Standard to meet. Sources listed.

Module 8 Pilot and improve 5 hCreate

Run 30 days with a few clients.

  • Track bookings, complaints, injuries, rebookings.
  • Fix top 3 issues.
  • Plan owner education and community tie-ups.
Worked example. Rebookings reach 55% after report cards with photos.

Activity. Present your review.

Standard to meet. Numbers real; fixes dated.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: service menu, owner form, hygiene plan, price sheet, pilot log. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Animal welfareGapsNeeds metNeeds met and monitored
Owner recordsMissingCompleteComplete and securely stored
Referral readinessNo planCardCard tested with vet
Honesty and evidenceClaims without proofEvery claim has a recordEvery claim linked to a dated record and checked by a peer
Safety, ethics and local rulesNot consideredChecked and written downChecked, dated and explained to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name the five welfare needs.

Model answer: Food and water, shelter, health, behaviour, company.

Q2. Why vaccine records?

Model answer: To protect other animals.

Q3. Name a stress sign.

Model answer: Panting, hiding or aggression.

Q4. When call a vet?

Model answer: Poisoning, injury, breathing trouble, seizures, long vomiting.

Q5. Can you give medicine freely?

Model answer: No, only as the owner or vet instructs in writing.

Q6. What goes on an owner form?

Model answer: Details, vaccines, medicines, allergies, contacts, consent.

Q7. Why separate sick animals?

Model answer: To stop spread.

Q8. Why insurance?

Model answer: To cover injuries or damage.

Q9. What is table time?

Model answer: Time in the room including cleaning.

Q10. What do you track in a pilot?

Model answer: Bookings, complaints, injuries, rebookings.

ten locations and global

Your place

Licences and animal-welfare laws differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
