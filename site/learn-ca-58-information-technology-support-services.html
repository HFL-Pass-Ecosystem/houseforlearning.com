Information Technology Support Services

Run a real information Technology Support Services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small information Technology Support Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: software, data and digital services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Find a real user and a real need 6 hUnderstand

Every good information Technology Support Services offer starts with a person who will use it, not with an idea. This module gives you a way to find that person and write their need in one line.

  • Interview five people who would use a information Technology Support Services service. Ask: what do you do today, what does it cost in time and money, what breaks?
  • Choose one narrow first user (for example a clinic, a small shop, a neighbourhood group) and write their problem in one sentence.
  • Write a one-line value statement: 'For [who] who need [what], our information Technology Support Services gives [result], unlike [what they do now].'
  • Score three possible first users out of 5 on need, ability to pay and ease of reaching them; pick the highest and say why.
Worked example. Value statement example: 'For small clinics that lose visits to missed appointments, our information Technology Support Services gives a weekly list and call script, unlike paper notes.' Scores: clinics 4/4/5, schools 3/2/3, gyms 3/4/4; clinics chosen.

Activity. Speak with five possible users, fill the score table and write the value statement.

Standard to meet. Five conversations recorded with dates; scores have reasons; value statement fits one line.

Module 2 Know the rules in Canada 6 hApply

Rules differ by federal, provincial and local level. Your task is to find which apply to information Technology Support Services, who enforces them, and what you must do first.

  • Federal private-sector privacy law (PIPEDA) and provincial laws set how you collect, use, store and share personal data; Quebec has its own stricter law.
  • Anti-spam law (CASL) needs consent before you send commercial messages; keep proof of consent.
  • Accessibility: aim for WCAG 2.1 AA so people with disabilities can use what you build; Ontario and federal rules apply to many organisations.
  • Contracts: agree scope, ownership of code and data, support hours and what happens on termination in writing.
  • Make a rules table: rule, who enforces it, what you must do, by when, evidence you hold. Update it every quarter.
Worked example. Rules table row: 'Sales tax | federal tax agency | register once taxable sales pass the small-supplier limit | before the next sale after the limit | registration number kept on file'.

Activity. Build your rules table for your province, citing the official page for each row and the date you checked it.

Standard to meet. Every row has an enforcer, an action and a dated source; no rule is guessed.

Module 3 Do the core work to a standard 10 hApply

The core craft of information Technology Support Services is learned by doing small, checked pieces of real work.

  • Write the user story: 'As a ___ I want ___ so that ___' and a test that proves it works.
  • Build in small releases of one to two weeks; each release is used by one real person.
  • Use version control, automated tests and a staging copy before you change anything live.
  • Document in plain language: how to run it, how to fix common faults, who to call.
Worked example. Story: 'As a clinic receptionist I want a reminder list so that fewer patients miss visits.' Test: 20 sample patients, list shows exactly the ones booked tomorrow. Release 1 in one week; no-shows tracked for a month.

Activity. Complete one core task for information Technology Support Services with a written method, a measurement and a before/after record.

Standard to meet. Task done twice; second run is better than first; method and measures are written down.

Module 4 Price it and prove it pays 8 hEvaluate

A business that does not cover its costs closes. You will build a cost sheet and a break-even number, and test a price with real people.

  • Variable cost is the cost each extra hour adds (materials, fees, direct time). Fixed cost is paid whatever you sell (rent, insurance, tools, software).
  • Margin per hour = price - variable cost. Margin percent = margin / price.
  • Break-even volume = fixed cost / margin per hour. If you cannot reach it within your capacity, change price, cost or scope.
  • Test the price with at least five real people before you commit; note the reaction, not only the answer.
Worked example. Price 85 per hour, variable cost 30: margin 55.00 per hour (65%). Fixed cost per month 9,900 gives break-even = 9,900 / 55.00 = about 180 hours a month. Figures are teaching numbers in CAD, not market data.

Activity. Build the cost sheet in a free spreadsheet, compute break-even, and write what you would change if you were 30% short.

Standard to meet. Units and currency shown; formulas visible; three what-if cases; a mentor checks the arithmetic.

Module 5 Run it well every day 8 hApply

Operations turn a good plan into steady results for information Technology Support Services.

  • Measure availability (percent of time working), response time and defects found per release.
  • Back up data on a schedule and test a restore at least once.
  • Write a runbook for the three most likely faults.
  • Review access: who can see what, and remove access when people leave.
Worked example. Weekly check: three numbers on one page (volume, quality, cash) and one action chosen from them.

Activity. Run a 4-week log with the weekly one-page check and one improvement.

Standard to meet. Log has four weekly pages; each improvement is dated and its result noted.

Module 6 Sell, partner and be found 6 hCreate

Customers find you through people they trust. Build two or three routes that you can run every week.

  • Pick channels: direct contact, local partners, online listing, referrals, public tenders where they exist; choose at most three at first.
  • Write a one-page offer: who, what, result, proof, price basis, how to start.
  • Keep it bilingual (English and French) where your customers or the law need it.
  • Track a funnel: contacts, conversations, offers, accepted; improve the weakest step.
Worked example. Funnel for four weeks: 40 contacts, 12 conversations, 5 offers, 2 accepted. The weakest step is contact to conversation (30%); test a new first message.

Activity. Run a 4-week funnel, send 20 messages and book 5 conversations.

Standard to meet. Funnel numbers recorded; offer page under one page; one test and its result written.

Module 7 Keep people, data and the community safe 6 hEvaluate

Trust is earned by how you handle risk, personal data and your community.

  • Security basics: strong sign-in, least access, encryption, software updates, a breach plan with who tells whom and when.
  • Bias and fairness: check that a model or rule does not treat groups unfairly; keep a record of the check.
  • Vendor risk: note what happens if a supplier changes price or closes.
  • Privacy: collect only what you need, tell people why, store it safely, delete it on schedule, and have a person who answers questions.
  • Reconciliation and inclusion: ask who is missing from the table, including Indigenous, Francophone, newcomer and disabled people, and invite them in.
Worked example. Risk row: 'Customer list stolen | likely 2, harm 4 | strong sign-in, encrypted backup, access list reviewed monthly | owner | review each month'.

Activity. Write a risk register of eight rows and a one-page privacy note.

Standard to meet. Each risk has likelihood, harm, control, owner and review date; the privacy note is in plain words.

Module 8 Pilot, review and grow 8 hCreate

A pilot proves the idea at small size before you put more money and time in.

  • Define the pilot: scope, length (30 to 90 days), measures and a stop rule agreed in advance.
  • Record weekly: what was planned, what happened, what was learned.
  • At the end compare results with the standard, decide: stop, change, or grow, with reasons.
  • Write a 12-month plan with three milestones, a budget and who helps you.
Worked example. Stop rule: 'If fewer than 90 hours are sold in 60 days, we stop and write lessons.' Result at day 60: 180 hours; decision: grow in steps of 25%.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and the 12-month plan, and present to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Pilot has measures and a stop rule; review decides with evidence; panel can repeat the plan.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What belongs in a one-line value statement?

Model answer: Who it is for, the need, the result and how it differs from what they do now.

Q2. Why make a rules table with a date and an official source?

Model answer: Rules change and differ by province; a dated source shows what you checked and when.

Q3. Why test a restore, not only make a backup?

Model answer: A backup you have never restored may not work when you need it.

Q4. How do you find break-even volume?

Model answer: Fixed cost divided by margin per unit.

Q5. What is margin percent?

Model answer: Margin per unit divided by price.

Q6. Name the three measures on a weekly one-page check.

Model answer: Volume, quality and cash.

Q7. Which funnel step do you improve first?

Model answer: The weakest step, found from the conversion numbers.

Q8. What two things must a privacy note state?

Model answer: What data you collect and why, and how you protect and delete it.

Q9. What must be agreed before a pilot starts?

Model answer: Scope, length, measures and a stop rule.

Q10. What is the safest response when you cannot find a rule for your case?

Model answer: Ask the official body or a licensed adviser and keep their answer in writing.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
