IT Business and Managed Services

Start and run a small IT services business: define services, price them, onboard a client and deliver reliable support.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 150 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Start and run a small IT services business: define services, price them, onboard a client and deliver reliable support.

  • Understand Explain what managed services are and how contracts work.
  • Apply Onboard one real client and set up monitoring and backup.
  • Analyse Read tickets and response times to find weak spots.
  • Evaluate Judge price, risk and service levels.
  • Create Write a service catalogue, a simple agreement and a monthly report.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Deliver a managed IT service to one real small client for 30 days and report the results.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3 to 6 months. A computer, free or trial tools for tickets and monitoring, a friendly small client, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Service catalogue written; client onboarded; 30-day ticket log; monthly report delivered; security and privacy checks done; 70% on the check.

Only access systems you have written permission to access; protect client data; never use a client's passwords outside the agreed task.

Requirements: None. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (150 h guided)

Module 1 What managed services are 8 hUnderstand

A managed service means you look after a client's IT every month for a fixed fee, instead of fixing things only when they break.

  • Break-fix: client calls, you fix, you bill by the hour. Managed: you prevent problems and promise response times.
  • Typical services: help desk, device set-up, updates, backup, security checks, small projects.
  • Clients value predictable cost and fewer outages.
  • You value steady income, but must keep promises.
Worked example. A 10-person office pays EUR 450 per month for a help desk, updates, backup checks and a monthly report.

Activity. Write a one-page description of your service in plain words.

Standard to meet. Explain what is included and what is not so that a non-technical reader understands.

Module 2 Service catalogue and service levels 14 hCreate

List what you do, how fast, and what it costs. Clear limits protect both sides.

  • Package services in 2 or 3 levels: Basic, Standard, Plus.
  • Define response targets: urgent in 1 hour, normal in 1 working day.
  • State exclusions: what needs a separate quote.
  • Agree support hours and how to reach you.
Worked example. Basic EUR 25 per user per month (help desk, updates); Standard EUR 40 (adds backup and security checks); Plus EUR 55 (adds monthly review).

Activity. Write the catalogue with prices and response targets.

Standard to meet. List inclusions, exclusions, targets and price for each level.

Module 3 Pricing and the business case 16 hEvaluate

Price must cover your time, tools, risk and profit.

  • Cost of delivery: hours x hourly cost + tools + insurance + learning time.
  • Break-even clients = fixed monthly costs divided by margin per client.
  • Include onboarding time in the first month price.
  • Test the price with two possible clients.
Worked example. Monthly fixed costs EUR 900; margin per client EUR 260; break-even = 900/260 = 3.5 so 4 clients.

Activity. Build a simple cost and break-even sheet.

Standard to meet. Show currency on every line and calculate break-even twice.

Module 4 Agreements, risk and rules 14 hApply

A clear agreement prevents arguments. Get legal advice before using any template.

  • Parts: scope, price, term, response targets, exclusions, data protection, how to end.
  • Written permission to access systems is essential.
  • Insurance and limits of liability: ask an adviser.
  • Keep records of changes and approvals.
Worked example. A one-page agreement checked by an adviser lists scope, price, 3-month term, 30-day notice and a data-protection clause.

Activity. Draft the agreement and get an adviser's comment.

Standard to meet. Draft covers all parts; adviser's comments recorded.

Module 5 Onboard a client 30 hApply

Onboarding is the first impression. Do it in a clear order.

  • Inventory: devices, accounts, software, licences, backups.
  • Fix the basics: updates, passwords policy, backup, antivirus or equivalent.
  • Set up a ticket system and tell users how to get help.
  • Hold a short kick-off meeting and give a one-page guide.
Worked example. Inventory finds 12 devices, 3 old laptops and no backup. Plan: replace backups first, then update laptops.

Activity. Onboard one real or practice client and keep a checklist.

Standard to meet. Complete the checklist and deliver a one-page guide.

Module 6 Run the service for 30 days 40 hApply

Deliver the promises every day and record everything.

  • Log each ticket: time received, urgency, first response, fix, closed.
  • Check backups weekly and test one restore.
  • Apply updates in a planned window and tell users.
  • Keep a change log.
Worked example. Week 2: 14 tickets, 12 answered within target. Two late: both after hours. Adjust cover or the promise.

Activity. Run 30 days with a full ticket log.

Standard to meet. Complete the ticket log and test a backup restore.

Module 7 Report and improve 20 hAnalyse

A monthly report shows value: what you did, what is at risk, what to do next.

  • Numbers: tickets, response times, outages, updates done, backup tests.
  • Top three repeat problems and a fix.
  • Risks: old devices, weak passwords, missing backups.
  • One-page plan for next month.
Worked example. Report shows 22 tickets, 91% within target, repeated printer issue; recommendation: replace the printer (EUR 180).

Activity. Write and present the report to the client.

Standard to meet. Report uses numbers and ends with actions.

Module 8 Grow the business and your skills 8 hEvaluate

Plan growth without breaking quality.

  • How many clients can you serve well alone?
  • Which tool or skill saves the most time?
  • Three lessons learned.
  • Decide: grow, change or stop.
Worked example. Capacity: 8 clients at 6 hours each per week. Plan: automate updates to serve 12.

Activity. Present at a review room; give two peers feedback.

Standard to meet. Plan has numbers and dates.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: your record, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Difference between break-fix and managed?

Model answer: Break-fix reacts and bills by the hour; managed prevents and bills a fixed fee. (Covered in module 1: What managed services are. To pass that module: Explain what is included and what is not so that a non-technical reader understands.)

Q2. Why exclusions in a catalogue?

Model answer: To protect both sides from unclear scope. (Covered in module 2: Service catalogue and service levels. To pass that module: List inclusions, exclusions, targets and price for each level.)

Q3. Break-even clients for fixed costs 900, margin 260?

Model answer: 4 (3.5 rounded up). (Covered in module 3: Pricing and the business case. To pass that module: Show currency on every line and calculate break-even twice.)

Q4. Why written permission?

Model answer: It is the legal basis to touch the client's systems. (Covered in module 4: Agreements, risk and rules. To pass that module: Draft covers all parts; adviser's comments recorded.)

Q5. What do you do in onboarding?

Model answer: Inventory, fix basics, set up tickets, kick-off and guide. (Covered in module 5: Onboard a client. To pass that module: Complete the checklist and deliver a one-page guide.)

Q6. Why test a restore?

Model answer: A backup you have not restored is unproven. (Covered in module 6: Run the service for 30 days. To pass that module: Complete the ticket log and test a backup restore.)

Q7. What belongs in a monthly report?

Model answer: Tickets, response times, outages, updates, backup tests, risks, next actions. (Covered in module 7: Report and improve. To pass that module: Report uses numbers and ends with actions.)

Q8. Name two response targets.

Model answer: Urgent within 1 hour, normal within 1 working day. (Covered in module 2: Service catalogue and service levels. To pass that module: List inclusions, exclusions, targets and price for each level.)

Q9. Why get an adviser to check the agreement?

Model answer: Laws differ and mistakes are costly. (Covered in module 4: Agreements, risk and rules. To pass that module: Draft covers all parts; adviser's comments recorded.)

Q10. What limits growth?

Model answer: Hours per client and your capacity to keep promises. (Covered in module 8: Grow the business and your skills. To pass that module: Plan has numbers and dates.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.
