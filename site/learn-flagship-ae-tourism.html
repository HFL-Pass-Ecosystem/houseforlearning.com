Tourism

Learn tourism for the UAE: rules, workflow, costing in AED and a recorded 30-day practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 176 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Understand Explain the UAE market, rules and hazards for tourism.
  • Apply Run the workflow: design the experience, brief the guide, run the tour, collect feedback.
  • Analyse Measure guests rating 4 or 5 out of 5 and find the biggest cause of loss.
  • Evaluate Cost and price in AED and judge scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a record pack a supervisor can audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a real or simulated 30-day pilot for tours and visitor experiences in the UAE and keep a full record of every step, cost and result.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3 to 6 months. A partner site or a simulated customer, a free spreadsheet, a phone, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Hazards named with controls; every number has a unit and AED; records complete; rules confirmed with the local authority; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better.

Process learning only. Main risk to manage: heat illness and unsafe activity. Follow UAE federal and emirate rules and any licence needed before you charge a customer. Summer heat means indoor or evening options matter.

Requirements: none. Currency: AED on every number.

lesson by lesson

11 modules with worked examples (176 h guided)

Module 1 Map the market and the people 5 hUnderstand

Start with who needs tours and visitor experiences and why. Around visitors, hotels and cruise lines drive demand in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other emirates.

  • List 10 offers or suppliers in the UAE with price per guest in AED and who buys.
  • Interview 5 people from visitors, hotels and cruise lines: what do they buy now, how often, what do they pay, what annoys them?
  • Learn three terms: Itinerary timing (the minute-by-minute plan of a tour); Capacity (the safe number of guests per tour); Shoulder season (the weeks between peak and low season).
  • Write a one-page brief: user, need, hoped-for price, and the rule that matters most.
Worked example. Five interviews all say delay is the main pain. You write: 'Reliable tours and visitor experiences, answer in one day, price near AED 260 per guest.'

Activity. Complete the market table, five interview notes and the brief.

Standard to meet. Ten offers priced; five users heard; one need stated in one sentence.

Module 2 Rules, hazards and licences 6 hApply

Know the rule before the work. Main hazards here: heat illness and unsafe activity. Hazard thinking means list each step, ask what could go wrong, and decide the control.

  • Ask your local authority or free zone which licence, permit or inspection applies. Write the answer and the date you asked.
  • For each step (design the experience; brief the guide; run the tour; collect feedback) write the hazard and the control.
  • Use a hierarchy: remove the hazard, replace it, separate people from it, change the way of work, then protective equipment last.
  • Keep a one-page incident rule: who you call, what you stop, what you record.
Worked example. Step 'design the experience': hazard = heat illness; control = written check before start and a named supervisor; record = signed log line.

Activity. Draw the flow and mark hazard and control at each step.

Standard to meet. Every step has a hazard and a control; licence question answered in writing.

Module 3 Core workflow, step by step 12 hApply

The work is a short chain: design the experience, brief the guide, run the tour, collect feedback. Learn each link, then link them with records.

  • Write a checklist for each of the 4 steps: input, action, check, output.
  • Time each step three times and note the slowest.
  • Record a defect or delay each time it happens: what, where, cause, fix.
  • Measure guests rating 4 or 5 out of 5; this is your main number.
Worked example. Three timed runs of 'brief the guide' take 42, 38 and 51 minutes. Average 43.7; the 51-minute run had a missing tool. Fix: a tool tray checked at start.

Activity. Run the chain three times (simulated or real) and fill the log.

Standard to meet. Three complete logs; the slowest step has a cause and a fix.

Module 4 Quality and measurement 10 hAnalyse

What you do not measure you cannot improve. Your main measure is guests rating 4 or 5 out of 5.

  • Define guests rating 4 or 5 out of 5: what counts, what does not, and over what period.
  • Draw a simple chart of the last 10 results and mark the average.
  • Find the top two causes of loss with a tally sheet, then fix the biggest one first.
  • Check measuring tools: are they accurate, clean and in date?
Worked example. Tally of 40 faults: 22 from late inputs, 9 from wrong settings, 9 other. Fix late inputs first: that is 55% of faults.

Activity. Collect a tally for one week and make a Pareto chart.

Standard to meet. Tally complete; biggest cause named; one fix planned with a date.

Module 5 Costing and pricing in AED 12 hEvaluate

Know the cost of one guest before you set the price. Include materials, labour, energy, transport, licence share, waste and a safety margin.

  • Cost per guest = total cost divided by guests sold (not made).
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs divided by gain per unit.
  • Test the price with two users and write down the answer.
Worked example. At AED 260 per guest and cost AED 160, gain is AED 100 and margin 38.5%. At 300 guests a month, gross gain is AED 30,000. If fixed costs are AED 9,600, break-even is 96 guests.

Activity. Build the cost sheet with AED on every line and have your mentor check it.

Standard to meet. No missing cost found; margin calculated twice by different routes.

Module 6 Serve and sell responsibly 12 hApply

Customers return when you keep promises. Promise only what your workflow can deliver.

  • Write a one-page offer: what is included, what is not, price, time, how you fix mistakes.
  • Write a short script for the first call or visit.
  • Agree dates in writing; confirm each change.
  • Handle a complaint in four steps: listen, apologise for the impact, fix, record what changed.
Worked example. Offer: 'Tours and visitor experiences delivered within one working day; price AED 260 per guest; faults corrected free within 3 days.'

Activity. Present the offer to two users and note changes.

Standard to meet. Offer has scope, price, time and fix rule; two users commented.

Module 7 Run the 30-day pilot 55 hCreate

Small, real and recorded. Change one thing at a time and learn quickly.

  • Keep a daily log: date, units, cost, delays, defects, safety notes.
  • Hold a 15-minute review each week with your mentor.
  • Write down every complaint and what you changed.
  • Take photos only where allowed and never of people without consent.
Worked example. Week 2 shows guests rating 4 or 5 out of 5 at 71%; week 4 at 84% after the tool tray and an earlier input check.

Activity. Complete 30 days of the log and four weekly reviews.

Standard to meet. 30 days logged; each defect has a cause; weekly notes kept.

Module 8 Review, decide and plan 38 hEvaluate

A decision beats a feeling. Compare plan with result and choose to scale, change or stop.

  • Make a before and after table: cost per unit, defects, delays, repeat users.
  • Write three lessons.
  • Decide scale, change or stop, with the numbers behind it.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
Worked example. Result: cost per guest fell from AED 160 to AED 150.40; repeat users 6 of 9. Decision: scale to a second site; keep price.

Activity. Present to a review room and collect one written comment from a user.

Standard to meet. Decision backed by numbers; two peer reviews given.

Module 9 Design a safe, enjoyable experience 9 hCreate

A tour balances story, pace, comfort and safety.

  • Plan timing with breaks and shade.
  • Set capacity and guide ratios.
  • Brief on water, sun and local etiquette.
  • Prepare a wet or hot weather plan.
Worked example. A desert evening tour moves the walking section to sunset, with water stops every 20 minutes.

Activity. Plan a half-day tour with a safety brief.

Standard to meet. Timings, capacity and weather plan set.

Module 10 Feedback, price and repeat guests 9 hEvaluate

Guests rate quickly. Ask well and act on it.

  • Ask three short questions after the tour.
  • Track average rating and top complaint.
  • Test a price change with a small group.
  • Thank guests and invite them back.
Worked example. Average rating 4.3; top complaint is waiting at pickup. A 10-minute earlier meeting lifts the rating to 4.6.

Activity. Collect 10 feedback forms and summarise.

Standard to meet. Top issue and fix named.

Module 11 Emergencies and responsible tourism 8 hEvaluate

A guide is responsible for guests' safety and for the place being visited. Both need a plan made before the trip.

  • Carry a first-aid kit, a list of guest health notes with consent, and emergency numbers on every trip.
  • Brief guests before moving off: heat, water, sun, safe distances from animals and cliffs.
  • In a medical event, call for help first, then give only the first aid you are trained for.
  • Protect the site: take rubbish out, keep to paths, respect local customs and dress rules.
Worked example. A group of 12 guests on a 4-hour desert trip needs 2 litres of water each: 12 x 2 = 24 litres, plus a 20% spare, giving 28.8 litres, so carry 30 litres.

Activity. Draw up an emergency card and a 5-point guest briefing for one trip.

Standard to meet. Numbers correct; briefing under 3 minutes; water and first-aid items checked off.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name the main hazards in this work.

Model answer: Heat illness and unsafe activity.

Q2. Why ask your local authority about licences?

Model answer: Rules differ by emirate and activity; written answers protect you.

Q3. Gain per guest if price is AED 260 and cost is AED 160?

Model answer: AED 100.

Q4. Margin for the same figures?

Model answer: 38.5%.

Q5. Name the steps of the workflow.

Model answer: Design the experience; brief the guide; run the tour; collect feedback.

Q6. What is Itinerary timing?

Model answer: The minute-by-minute plan of a tour.

Q7. What is Capacity?

Model answer: The safe number of guests per tour.

Q8. What is Shoulder season?

Model answer: The weeks between peak and low season.

Q9. Which control comes first in the hierarchy?

Model answer: Remove the hazard; protective equipment comes last.

Q10. What three options end the pilot?

Model answer: Scale, change or stop, with reasons from the numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a Brainport-area small firm.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: SEBRAE small-business support, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or a SkillsFuture-linked training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a TAFE or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico (Central America)

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
