Courier Services

Run a real courier Services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small courier Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: local, logistics and field services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know courier work 6 hUnderstand

Couriers move parcels and documents from one place to another on time and safely. Learn the main types and words first.

  • Name types of courier jobs: same-day, next-day, scheduled routes, medical or legal documents.
  • Describe the steps: pick-up, scan or sign, carry, deliver, proof.
  • Explain what proof of delivery is and why it matters.
  • List words: waybill, manifest, route, dispatch, signature on delivery.
  • Compare same-day and scheduled routes by price, stress and planning.
  • Identify what a waybill must show: sender, receiver, contents and time.
  • Check who is responsible if a parcel is lost by reading the agreement.
Worked example. A law office needs a document at a court by 3 pm. The courier collects at 1 pm, which leaves a 2 hour buffer, gets a signature at the court and sends the proof to the office.

Activity. Follow a delivery you receive. Note every step from order to door.

Standard to meet. Task: name the steps of a delivery. Condition: from a real parcel. Standard: five steps and the proof clearly listed.

Module 2 Plan routes and keep time 6 hApply

A good route saves fuel, time and stress. Plan before you drive, bike or walk.

  • Apply route planning: group stops by area, deadlines first, use a map tool.
  • Compute time per stop including parking and waiting.
  • Check traffic, weather and construction before the first stop.
  • Compare two routes using distance and time, not only distance.
  • Calculate stops per hour from your practice route.
  • Add a buffer of about 15% to planned time for traffic and waiting, then adjust from your records.
  • Compare a loop route with going back and forth.
Worked example. Six stops: two have deadlines by noon. The route does those two first, then the rest in a loop. Planned total is 3 hours 20 minutes (200 minutes), about 33 minutes per stop. If the real run takes 3 hours 50 minutes (230 minutes), it is 30 minutes or 15% over plan, so add a buffer.

Activity. Plan and drive or walk a six-stop practice route and compare plan with real time.

Standard to meet. Task: plan a route. Condition: six stops, two deadlines. Standard: all deadlines met and time difference explained.

Module 3 Follow the rules in Canada 10 hApply

Road, vehicle, tax and goods rules apply to couriers. They differ by province. Check with the local authority.

  • Explain that a driver licence class and a vehicle registration and insurance are needed; check the use type with your insurer and provincial licensing office.
  • Describe hours-of-work and safe driving rules for commercial vehicles; check the current rule.
  • Check rules for dangerous goods, medical samples and cash; some need training or permits.
  • Apply tax rules: record income and costs, and check sales tax rules with the federal tax agency.
  • Ask the provincial licensing office which licence class your vehicle needs.
  • Check local rules for bike or on-foot couriers about sidewalks and bike lanes.
  • Keep a copy of your insurance letter and permit documents on your phone or in the vehicle.
Worked example. Rules table row: Vehicle insurance | provincial insurer | tell the insurer you use the vehicle for deliveries | before the first paid job | letter kept. If the insurer takes 5 days to reply, ask at least 5 days before the first job.

Activity. Make a rules table for your province covering licence, insurance, goods and tax, with official pages.

Standard to meet. Task: map the rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.

Module 4 Handle parcels and prove delivery 8 hEvaluate

Care and proof protect you and the customer.

  • Apply handling rules: check the label, lift safely, secure items so they do not move.
  • Check the address, phone and name before leaving.
  • Build a proof routine: photo, signature or code, time, note.
  • Handle a failed delivery: try the contact, leave a notice, return or hold as agreed.
  • Leave parcels only in a place the sender has agreed to in writing.
  • Photograph the parcel at the door with the address visible, showing no more private information than needed.
  • Refuse items you cannot carry safely, such as fragile or heavy loads without help.
Worked example. Customer is not home. Courier calls, waits five minutes, leaves a notice with the time and the next try, and records it in the job log. In ten practice deliveries, 9 had full proof, which is 90%; the missing one is fixed.

Activity. Complete ten practice deliveries and record proof for each.

Standard to meet. Task: deliver with proof. Condition: ten practice stops. Standard: every stop has a time and proof, and failed ones have a note.

Module 5 Safety for you and the load 8 hApply

A courier who stays safe stays in business. Rest, fitness and a safe vehicle matter.

  • Apply safe driving: seat belt, no phone in hand, rest breaks, no rush.
  • Check the vehicle each day: tyres, lights, brakes, fluids, load straps.
  • Explain how to lift without back injury and when to ask for help.
  • Create an incident plan: who to call, photos, written report.
  • Plan rest breaks and check the rule for commercial drivers.
  • Prepare for winter: tyres, scraper, boots and extra time.
  • Report any near miss in the log with date and place.
Worked example. Daily check: tyres good, lights work, brakes fine, straps tight. Ten minutes a day is 50 minutes over a five-day week, and prevents a breakdown at noon.

Activity. Make a daily vehicle checklist and use it for one five-day week.

Standard to meet. Task: complete safety checks. Condition: one five-day week. Standard: five dated checklists with no skipped items.

Module 6 Price, costs and cash 6 hCreate

Courier income looks big until fuel, repair, insurance and waiting time are counted.

  • Compute cost per kilometre: fuel, repairs, tyres, insurance, depreciation.
  • Compute pay per hour after costs, including time between jobs.
  • Compare per-stop price, per-km price and per-hour price.
  • Check tax set-aside for self-employment; ask an adviser.
  • Calculate yearly fuel cost from kilometres and fuel use.
  • Compare owning, leasing and renting a vehicle by total yearly cost.
  • Calculate the weekly profit of a client route after all costs.
Worked example. Price 12 per delivery, cost 5 in fuel and wear. Margin 7. If fixed costs are 400 a month, break-even is 400 / 7 = 57.1, so 58 deliveries. In a week, 60 deliveries x 7 = 420 over 25 hours including waiting is 16.80 an hour before fixed costs. Teaching numbers only.

Activity. Build a cost sheet for one week and find your true pay per hour.

Standard to meet. Task: find true pay. Condition: one week of records. Standard: all costs counted and pay per hour correct.

Module 7 Win clients and keep them 6 hEvaluate

Businesses want couriers who are on time and easy to reach. Reliability wins repeat work.

  • Build a one-page offer: area, speed, price, proof, hours.
  • Contact local offices, clinics, shops and online sellers who need regular delivery.
  • Write a simple agreement: pick-up window, price, cancellation, liability.
  • Track the funnel: contacts, trials, regular clients.
  • Write the price per stop and per kilometre in the one-page offer.
  • Ask each trial client what would make them switch to you.
  • Keep a record of on-time rate to show new clients.
Worked example. Four weeks: 30 contacts, 8 trials (27%), 3 regular clients (38% of trials). The weakest step is contact to trial; offer a free first delivery.

Activity. Contact 20 businesses and book 5 trial deliveries.

Standard to meet. Task: win regular clients. Condition: four weeks. Standard: funnel recorded and a written agreement with each regular client.

Module 8 Run the courier service for real 8 hCreate

Now run the service with real clients, safe habits and a plan to grow.

  • Run a 60-day pilot with a stop rule agreed in advance.
  • Review weekly: deliveries, on-time rate, cost per km, complaints.
  • Create a 12-month plan with vehicle, insurance and growth steps.
  • Lead others: train a second courier with your checklists and proof routine.
  • Calculate on-time rate as on-time deliveries divided by all deliveries.
  • Compare income per hour in week one and week eight.
  • Identify one route to add, drop or change.
Worked example. Stop rule: if on-time rate stays under 90% after 60 days, fix the plan before adding clients. Result: 192 of 200 on time is 96%; add one route.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and present your plan to three people.

Standard to meet. Task: run a courier pilot. Condition: 60 days, real clients. Standard: results compared with the stop rule and insurance and licence re-checked.

assessment

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is proof of delivery?

Model answer: A photo, signature or code with time that shows the parcel arrived. (Covered in module 4: Handle parcels and prove delivery. To pass that module: Task: deliver with proof. Condition: ten practice stops. Standard: every stop has a time and proof, and failed ones have a note.)

Q2. Why do deadline stops go first on a route?

Model answer: So the time-limited deliveries are not missed. (Covered in module 2: Plan routes and keep time. To pass that module: Task: plan a route. Condition: six stops, two deadlines. Standard: all deadlines met and time difference explained.)

Q3. What must you tell your insurer?

Model answer: That you use the vehicle for paid deliveries. (Covered in module 3: Follow the rules in Canada. To pass that module: Task: map the rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.)

Q4. Who sets driver licence and vehicle rules?

Model answer: The province; check your provincial office. (Covered in module 3: Follow the rules in Canada. To pass that module: Task: map the rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.)

Q5. What do you do with dangerous goods or medical samples?

Model answer: Check the rules; some need training or permits. (Covered in module 3: Follow the rules in Canada. To pass that module: Task: map the rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, an action and a checked date.)

Q6. What do you do after a failed delivery?

Model answer: Leave a notice, record it and follow the agreed return or hold step. (Covered in module 4: Handle parcels and prove delivery. To pass that module: Task: deliver with proof. Condition: ten practice stops. Standard: every stop has a time and proof, and failed ones have a note.)

Q7. Name three daily vehicle checks.

Model answer: Tyres, lights, brakes, fluids, straps (any three). (Covered in module 5: Safety for you and the load. To pass that module: Task: complete safety checks. Condition: one five-day week. Standard: five dated checklists with no skipped items.)

Q8. How do you find true pay per hour?

Model answer: Income minus all costs, divided by all hours worked including waiting. (Covered in module 6: Price, costs and cash. To pass that module: Task: find true pay. Condition: one week of records. Standard: all costs counted and pay per hour correct.)

Q9. How do you find break-even deliveries?

Model answer: Fixed cost divided by margin per delivery. (Covered in module 6: Price, costs and cash. To pass that module: Task: find true pay. Condition: one week of records. Standard: all costs counted and pay per hour correct.)

Q10. What belongs in a courier agreement?

Model answer: Pick-up window, price, cancellation and liability. (Covered in module 7: Win clients and keep them. To pass that module: Task: win regular clients. Condition: four weeks. Standard: funnel recorded and a written agreement with each regular client.)

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
