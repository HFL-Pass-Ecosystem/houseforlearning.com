Module 10: Operational ethics and cross-cultural moral architecture

A 2.5 ECTS executive module, 75 hours.

what you take

Module 10

  • Size

    2.5 ECTS, 75 hours of study.

  • Credit

    Counts in 15 countries. See the table.

how it is marked

Assessment

  • Written memo 30%
  • Live interactive simulation 35%
  • Boardroom presentation 35%

Marking bands: A, B or fail. Presentations use at most 10 slides, with a short live defence.

Weights shown follow the shared pattern for modules 3 to 14, and will be confirmed.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task, condition and standard

Task

Apply a four-lens moral test, handle gift and payment cases, audit a supplier sample and design a code with conduct-linked pay.

Condition

Given dilemma cases, a gift limit, an audit sample and a bonus plan. All money figures are in one currency of your choice.

Standard

Each case is decided with stated reasons, audit figures are correct and the code separates fixed from local rules.

Who it is for: Managers who work in countries with different norms and must keep one standard.

eight parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 Four lenses for a moral choice 9 hUnderstand: explain

A hard choice can be viewed through results, duties, fairness and care. Using all four stops a person from relying on a single habit.

  • Results: who gains and who is harmed
  • Duty: which rule or promise applies
  • Fairness and care: who is left out

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A plant manager must decide whether to keep a line running with a known fault. Results: running earns 40,000 a week, but 3 workers face a risk of injury. Duty: the safety rules say stop. Fairness: the workers did not choose the risk. Care: the 3 workers have families. Three of four lenses say stop, so the line is stopped, and the loss of 40,000 a week is treated as a cost of doing the job properly.

Activity: Take a real or case dilemma and write one sentence under each of the four lenses, then state the decision.

Standard: All four lenses are used and the final choice follows the majority of lenses with a reason for any lens that is overruled.

Part 2 Moral norms that differ between countries 10 hAnalyse: compare

Some practices are normal in one place and a crime in another. A manager must know which differences are real customs and which are just excuses.

  • Separate custom from corruption
  • Ask what the local law says
  • Ask what the home law says

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. In a host country, a small present of about 20 is given at a festival. The home-country rule sets a gift limit of 50 and needs a log entry. The present is 30 under the limit, so it is only logged. Later a supplier offers a 'thank you' of 2,000 during a contract talk. That is 40 times the limit and is linked to a decision, so it is refused and reported.

Activity: List three gifts with value and timing. For each, say allowed, allowed with a log, or refused, using a 50 limit.

Standard: Each gift is placed in the correct class using the limit and the timing of the decision.

Part 3 Gifts, facilitation payments and bribery 9 hApply: decide

A bribe is a payment to get an unfair gain. A small payment to speed up a normal service is still risky and is banned in many places.

  • Test: is it a gain the payer has no right to?
  • Never pay to skip a rule
  • Record and report every request

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A shipment is held at a border and an officer hints that 150 will release it today. Waiting costs 400 a day in storage for 3 days, which is 1,200. The payment is cheaper at 150, but it is still a bribe. The manager refuses, asks for the official fee list and files a request, and logs the incident. The cost to the firm is 1,200 and the firm keeps its licence.

Activity: Work through a case where a payment saves money. List the cost of refusal and the risk of paying.

Standard: The cost of refusal is calculated, the payment is named as a bribe and a lawful route is chosen.

Part 4 Labour standards in supply chains 10 hApply: audit

A firm is responsible for how its suppliers treat workers. A simple audit checks pay, hours, age and safety.

  • Set a minimum standard in every contract
  • Audit a sample of sites
  • Fix first, end the contract last

Worked example: A firm has 40 suppliers and audits 25% each year, which is 10 sites. At the 10 audited sites, 3 show hours above the firm's limit of 60 a week. That is 30%. If the same rate applies to all 40, about 12 suppliers may break the rule. The firm gives each of the 3 a 90-day fix plan and re-audits. Any site that fails again is dropped.

Activity: Take 30 suppliers and a 20% audit sample. Work out the sample size, the failure rate and the likely total number failing.

Standard: Sample size is 6, the failure rate is calculated from the findings and the projection to 30 is shown.

Part 5 A repeatable test for a hard decision 9 hApply: use a method

A written test helps people decide the same way each time. It can be used in any country.

  • Check law, then policy, then conscience
  • Ask if you would be happy to see it in the news
  • Ask a colleague before acting

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A sales lead may offer a customer a 12% discount, but the limit is 10%. Law: no breach. Policy: breached by 2 points. News test: a 12% discount for one buyer and 10% for others looks unfair. The lead asks the director. The deal is 500,000, so 2 points is 10,000. The director refuses the extra 2%, and the lead offers 10% plus free delivery worth 3,000.

Activity: Write a three-step test and apply it to a case with a number limit that is breached.

Standard: The test has three steps in order, the money effect is calculated and the choice stays inside the policy.

Part 6 Codes of conduct that work in many places 10 hCreate: design

A code sets one global floor and lets local teams add detail. It must be short enough to read and clear enough to apply.

  • Global floor that cannot be changed
  • Local detail that cannot lower the floor or lift a ceiling
  • Plain words in each language

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A firm writes a code with 8 rules. 5 are fixed globally (bribes, safety, child labour, fair pay, records). 3 can be set locally (gift limit up to the global ceiling of 50, working hours within the local legal limit, holiday gifts). A site sets a gift limit of 30, which is below the global ceiling of 50, so it is accepted. Another site sets 80, which is above the ceiling, so it is rejected.

Activity: Write a code with five fixed rules and three local rules and test it with two local proposals.

Standard: The code separates fixed and local rules and the two proposals are checked against the ceiling.

Part 7 Training and incentives 9 hEvaluate: judge

People follow what is rewarded. If bonuses reward only sales, staff may bend the rules, so bonuses must include conduct.

  • Pay for how as well as how much
  • Train with real cases
  • Test understanding after training

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A sales bonus is 10,000 for hitting target. The firm changes it to 8,000 for results and 2,000 for passing a conduct review, which is 20% of the bonus. Of 50 staff, 46 pass a short case test, which is 92%. The 4 who fail redo the training in 30 days. The firm checks that conduct complaints fall from 9 to 5 in a year.

Activity: Redesign a 12,000 bonus so that 25% depends on conduct and set a pass mark for the case test.

Standard: The split is 9,000 and 3,000, a pass mark is stated and one result is tracked.

Part 8 Monitoring and reporting concerns 9 hEvaluate: assess

A rule with no checks is only words. Monitoring means sampling, reporting lines and a record of action.

  • Check a sample of cases each quarter
  • Offer a private reporting line
  • Log what was done and by when

Worked example: Each quarter the ethics team checks 5% of 1,200 expense claims, which is 60 claims. 3 claims break the gift rule, a rate of 5%. That projects to 60 claims out of 1,200, if all were checked. The team follows up the 3 within 14 days and logs each outcome. The following quarter the rate drops to 2 of 60, or 3.3%.

Activity: Take 800 claims and a 7.5% sample. Work out the sample, the failure rate for 4 breaches and the projection.

Standard: Sample is 60, failure rate is 6.7% and the projection is about 53 claims.

ten checks

Check yourself

  1. List the four lenses used to view a moral choice.
    Results, duty, fairness and care. (Covered in part 1: Four lenses for a moral choice. To pass that part: All four lenses are used and the final choice follows the majority of lenses with a reason for any lens that is overruled.)
  2. A gift limit is 50. A supplier gives a gift worth 35 at a festival. What do you do?
    It is under the limit, so it is allowed, and it is logged if the policy needs a log. (Covered in part 2: Moral norms that differ between countries. To pass that part: Each gift is placed in the correct class using the limit and the timing of the decision.)
  3. A border officer asks for 200 to release goods. Storage is 300 a day for 2 days. Is paying right?
    No. It is a bribe even though paying (200) costs less than waiting (600). (Covered in part 3: Gifts, facilitation payments and bribery. To pass that part: The cost of refusal is calculated, the payment is named as a bribe and a lawful route is chosen.)
  4. A firm audits 20% of 50 suppliers and finds 4 failures in the sample. What is the failure rate?
    10 sites are audited, so 4 / 10 = 40%. (Covered in part 4: Labour standards in supply chains. To pass that part: Sample size is 6, the failure rate is calculated from the findings and the projection to 30 is shown.)
  5. In the three-step test, which step comes first?
    Check the law. (Covered in part 5: A repeatable test for a hard decision. To pass that part: The test has three steps in order, the money effect is calculated and the choice stays inside the policy.)
  6. A local team proposes a gift limit of 90 when the global ceiling is 50. What happens?
    It is rejected, because local rules cannot lift the global ceiling. (Covered in part 6: Codes of conduct that work in many places. To pass that part: The code separates fixed and local rules and the two proposals are checked against the ceiling.)
  7. A 10,000 bonus has 20% tied to conduct. How much is that?
    2,000. (Covered in part 7: Training and incentives. To pass that part: The split is 9,000 and 3,000, a pass mark is stated and one result is tracked.)
  8. Why must bonuses include conduct?
    Because people repeat what is rewarded, so pay for results alone can push staff to break rules. (Covered in part 7: Training and incentives. To pass that part: The split is 9,000 and 3,000, a pass mark is stated and one result is tracked.)
  9. A 5% sample of 2,000 claims finds 6 breaches. What is the sample and the failure rate?
    Sample is 100 and the rate is 6%. (Covered in part 8: Monitoring and reporting concerns. To pass that part: Sample is 60, failure rate is 6.7% and the projection is about 53 claims.)
  10. A site works a 65-hour week and the standard is 60. What does the firm do first?
    Give a fix plan with a time limit and re-audit, and end the contract only if it fails again. (Covered in part 4: Labour standards in supply chains. To pass that part: Sample size is 6, the failure rate is calculated from the findings and the projection to 30 is shown.)

slides L1 to L7

The seven-level ladder, slide by slide

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: Why ethics at work. A slide shows one case where a cheap choice led to a high later cost. Speaker note: Say: ethics is part of the job, not an extra. Check: ask for one example from the group. Mistake: treating it as a matter of opinion only.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Basic: The four lenses. Four boxes for results, duty, fairness and care sit around one dilemma. Speaker note: Say: use all four. Check: fill the boxes for a case. Mistake: using only the result lens.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Core: Custom or bribe. A table lists gifts by value and timing with allowed, log or refuse. Speaker note: Say: size and timing matter. Check: sort three gifts. Mistake: calling a bribe a custom.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Core: Pressure payments. A cost sum compares the bribe, the delay and the legal route. Speaker note: Say: cheaper is not the test. Check: work out the cost of waiting. Mistake: paying to save a small delay.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Applied: Supplier audits. A sample sum shows sites audited, failures found and a projection. Speaker note: Say: sample, then project, then fix. Check: ask for the projected number. Mistake: ending contracts before offering a fix.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Applied: Codes and incentives. A code with fixed and local rules sits beside a bonus split. Speaker note: Say: pay what you want to see. Check: split a bonus. Mistake: a code that nobody can read.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Advanced: Monitoring. A trend of sample failure rates over four quarters is shown with follow-up times. Speaker note: Say: check, record, follow up. Check: ask what trend means success. Mistake: running a check once and stopping.

jobs this leads to

Where this takes you

  • Ethics and compliance officer. Writes the code and runs checks.
  • Supply chain auditor. Samples suppliers and reports failures.
  • Country manager. Applies the code and handles pressure from local partners.
  • Training lead. Builds case training and tests understanding.
