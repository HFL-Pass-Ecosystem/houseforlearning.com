Online fulfilment business: Georgia and Israel edition

The global course "Online fulfilment business" with local rules, jobs and examples for Georgia and Israel.

about this course

Online fulfilment business: Georgia and Israel edition

L1 to L4 location edition8 modules

Online fulfilment business from the global list, applied to Georgia and Israel: local rules, jobs and examples in GEL.

Task

Plan a small Online fulfilment business business for Georgia and Israel from fit check to launch plan.

Condition

About six weeks part-time; public sources, two local readers and a mentor.

Standard

All module standards met; every claim has a public source and date; mentor and peer mark 'Meets' or better.

Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real. Draft; not legal, tax, medical or financial advice. All amounts are estimates for planning, not quotes.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read your local market 4 hUnderstand

Every plan starts with the place. Read Georgia and Israel as your customer does before you choose a sector.

  • Two different countries, two sets of rules. Pick the country first, then use that country’s list in every module.
  • Both have strong technology and start-up activity; many small firms sell abroad from day one.
  • Languages differ by customer: write the page in the local language plus English.
  • Public holidays and the working week differ; put the working days on every delivery promise.
Worked example. Market page for Georgia and Israel: size in one line, three languages or fewer, main payment methods, three public holidays that affect delivery, and one risk. Each line has a source you can open and a date.

Activity. Write a one-page market page with sources and dates. Ask two local people to correct it.

Standard to meet. Every line has a source and a date; two local readers confirm the facts; one risk is named with a response.

Module 2 Check the fit of this sector 4 hAnalyse

Online fulfilment business sits in the cluster "Online selling and marketing". Judge its fit for Georgia and Israel before spending money.

  • Local angle: Strong fit in both: small firms sell online to the region. Check delivery rules and returns for each country.
  • Score demand, skills you hold, start-up cost, rules and partner access from 1 to 5.
  • A rules score below 3 makes this a later project.
  • Not on the regulated list, but check any activity licence.
Worked example. Fit table for Online fulfilment business in Georgia and Israel: list three local customer groups, one local competitor type (no names), one rule that could stop you and one partner type. Score each criterion with a reason.

Activity. Write a fit table for Online fulfilment business with scores and reasons. Ask one local reader to challenge two scores.

Standard to meet. Five criteria scored with reasons; one rule risk named; regulated status stated.

Module 3 Register and license 4 hApply

Rules differ by place. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real.

  • Name the country first. Each has its own registration route: an individual-entrepreneur or small-business type in one, a dealer type or a company in the other.
  • Compare the types by tax, liability and filing work before you choose; write the table.
  • Licences vary by activity (food, health, money, education centres); list yours before the first order.
  • Keep a local address and a named contact in the country where you trade.
Worked example. Licence list for a small Online fulfilment business: registration type, tax registration, address, any activity licence, insurance. Next to each, write who issues it, what it costs in GEL (from their public page), how long it takes, and the date you checked.

Activity. Build a licence table for your chosen sector with issuer, cost, time and the date checked. Do not apply; this is a plan.

Standard to meet. Table is complete; every row has a public source; the order of steps is logical; one adviser question is written.

Module 4 Protect data and customers 3 hApply

Trust is the real product. Protect customer data and keep your promises in writing.

  • Both countries have a personal-data law; read the one for the country where the customer lives.
  • Say in plain words what you collect and why; give a way to see and correct it.
  • Keep marketing consent records; customers must be able to say no.
  • Moving data between the two countries or to a third place may need a safeguard; check with a local expert.
Worked example. Privacy notice in five lines: who we are; what we collect; why; who sees it; how to ask for a copy, correction or removal. Plus a data map listing each tool and where it stores data.

Activity. Write the five-line notice and a data map for your plan. Ask a peer to find any gap.

Standard to meet. Notice is plain words; data map lists every tool; refund and complaint steps are written and dated.

Module 5 Find and serve customers 4 hCreate

Go where your customers already are, in the language they use. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, Russian and English depending on the country and customer.

  • Channels: messaging apps and social media are strong in both; add a local marketplace.
  • Language: one page in the local language, one in English; never mix currencies on one price.
  • Payments: bank transfer and local payment methods; show the currency code next to every price.
  • Trust: registration number, address, delivery days and a refund note on every page.
Worked example. Test plan: show the offer to 20 real people through two channels over two weeks; count enquiries, replies and first orders. Example result: 20 shown, 6 enquiries, 2 orders: a 10% order rate. Change one thing (price or wording) and test again.

Activity. Run a two-channel test with 20 people. Record results in a table and write the next change.

Standard to meet. Test has a start and end date; results are counted, not guessed; one change is chosen and justified.

Module 6 Price, tax and money 4 hEvaluate

Know your numbers in GEL. Tax rule in one line: Local sales tax and income tax differ in each country; a small-business regime may apply in one and a dealer type in the other.

  • Write GEL next to every number.
  • Contribution per unit = price minus the cost of each unit.
  • Break-even units = fixed monthly cost divided by contribution per unit; round up.
  • Add tax and duty explicitly; never bury them in a price.
  • Check against a worse case: if sales are 25% lower, do you still cover costs?
Worked example. For a small Online fulfilment business (all amounts are estimates): price GEL 75 per unit, cost GEL 44 per unit, fixed cost GEL 4,800 per month. Contribution = 75 - 44 = 31. Break-even = 4,800 / 31 = 154.8, so 155 boxes per month. If sales fall 25% from 310, you sell 232 and the margin is 2,392.

Activity. Build your own sheet in GEL: price, unit cost, fixed cost, contribution, break-even and a 25% worse case. Put the tax and duty rules in a separate line with a source.

Standard to meet. Sheet has all six lines in GEL; arithmetic is checked by a peer; the worse case is shown.

Module 7 People and partners 3 hApply

Plans become real through people. Hire, partner and write every promise down.

  • Employment rules, notice and social contributions differ; read the one for the country of the worker.
  • Written terms and signed copies for every helper, even part-time.
  • Freelancers are common: write scope, price, language and date in one page.
  • Partner with a local college, chamber or training body to find helpers and to practise.
Worked example. Role card for a first helper: tasks, hours, pay in GEL, trial period, review date, and one measure of success. Signed by both sides.

Activity. Write one role card and one partner letter (what each side gives). Role-play a first meeting for 10 minutes.

Standard to meet. Role card has pay in local currency, hours and a review date; partner letter is balanced; both are signed copies.

Module 8 Launch plan and review 4 hCreate

Finish with a one-page launch plan and a plan to review it every month.

  • Dates: first test, first order, first review.
  • One measure per week: enquiries, orders or revenue; pick one.
  • A monthly review: what we planned, what happened, what we change.
  • Keep a risk list with a response for each; include a rules change.
  • Local caution: the final country choice will be confirmed before opening.
Worked example. Launch page for Georgia and Israel: Week 1 licence table done; Week 2 test with 20 people; Week 3 numbers sheet checked; Week 4 launch decision with a go or no-go rule written in advance.

Activity. Write the launch page and present it to two people for 10 minutes. Record their questions and your answers.

Standard to meet. Plan has dates, one weekly measure, a go or no-go rule and a risk list; presenter answers three questions with evidence.

jobs

Where this takes you

  • Online fulfilment business assistant. supports a small Online fulfilment business in Georgia and Israel.
  • Subscription shop operator. runs boxes, orders and delivery for a small seller.
  • Export assistant. prepares documents and delivery notes for cross-border sales.
  • Security and privacy assistant. keeps customer data safe in small firms.
  • Food business assistant. plans menus, safety logs and orders.
  • Solar and energy assistant. prepares site data and quotes.

Six steps from entry to senior

  1. Online fulfilment business assistant (L2, Entry). helps with set tasks, keeps records and follows the checklist.
  2. Junior Online fulfilment business coordinator (L3, Junior). runs small jobs end to end and reports each week.
  3. Online fulfilment business practitioner (L4, Practitioner). plans and delivers the work alone, with a mentor check.
  4. Online fulfilment business specialist (L5, Specialist). goes deep in one area, such as rules, numbers or customers.
  5. Online fulfilment business team lead or manager (L6, Advanced). leads people and partners, owns the plan and the data.
  6. Senior Online fulfilment business adviser or mentor (L7, Senior). designs the whole system, reviews others and publishes cases.

See how these steps connect to study and work in Career pathways and Pathways.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Name fulfilment steps

Name the 6 steps (receive, store, pick, pack, ship, return) and say what can go wrong at each.

Speaker notes. Walk through a parcel journey and ask for the failure at each step. Check returns are included.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Stock, labels and customs

Describe how a SKU, a barcode and a shipping label link to an order and when customs forms are needed.

Speaker notes. Show a label with an error. Check local rules with an expert for customs and tax.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Pilot for small-parcel sellers

Plan a pilot for sellers shipping 20 to 100 orders a week with a pick list, a pack standard and a same-day dispatch cut-off.

Speaker notes. Review the pick list. Check it is sorted by shelf location.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Run a pick and pack test

Run a test of 50 orders and record pick accuracy, pack time and damage rate.

Speaker notes. Hand over a tally. Check accuracy is stated as a percentage with a 99 percent target.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Design layout and pricing

Design a shelf layout and a price per order in GEL covering space, labour and materials with a margin.

Speaker notes. Check the sums. Ask what happens at double volume.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Analyse carrier and error data

Analyse delivery times and error causes across 200 orders and pick one carrier or process change.

Speaker notes. Provide a data sheet. Check the cause is traced to numbers.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Lead a scaling plan

Lead a plan to handle 5 times the volume with a staffing rota, a stock count each week and a service agreement.

Speaker notes. A peer plays a seller after a lost parcel. Check the claim steps are followed.
