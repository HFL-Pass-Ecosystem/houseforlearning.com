Biofuel Production Overview

Understand how biofuels are made from crops, waste and algae, with sustainability, safety, cost and policy.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2 Awareness22:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain biofuel types and basic production steps.
  • Apply Do simple mass and energy balances.
  • Analyse Compare feedstocks and emissions.
  • Evaluate Judge sustainability and food-versus-fuel questions.
  • Create Write a balanced overview for a community or investor.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a three-page overview of one biofuel pathway with process chart, balance, emissions estimate, risks and recommendation.

Condition

A computer, public data, a spreadsheet, two peers and a mentor. No production work is done.

Standard

Process steps and balances are correct; emissions use stated assumptions; risks and safety are listed; sources dated; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Students, farm cooperatives, energy staff, policy assistants. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Types of biofuel 2 hUnderstand

Biofuels are fuels made from recently grown plant or waste material. Different types suit different engines.

  • Ethanol is made by fermenting sugars and blended into petrol.
  • Biodiesel is made from oils and fats and blended into diesel.
  • Biogas is made by digesting wet waste without air and burns like natural gas.
  • Advanced fuels use woody waste or algae and are still costly.
Worked example. A bus fleet running a low biodiesel blend such as 20 per cent often needs no engine change if the engine maker allows it, while 100 per cent biodiesel may need new seals and heated tanks in cold places.

Activity. Build a table of the four types with raw material, product and use.

Standard to meet. Complete the table correctly with one limit for each type.

Module 2 Feedstocks 2 hAnalyse

The feedstock decides cost, emissions and whether food or land is harmed.

  • Waste and residues such as used oil and crop stalks compete least with food.
  • Dedicated crops need land, water and fertiliser.
  • Collection and transport cost rise with distance.
  • Supply must be steady all year.
Worked example. Used cooking oil from 200 restaurants gives about 60 litres each a month, 12,000 litres, enough for a small plant, with no new land used.

Activity. Rank three feedstocks for your place on cost, supply and land use.

Standard to meet. Justify the ranking with one number per feedstock.

Module 3 Production steps 3 hUnderstand

Each pathway has stages: prepare, convert, separate and clean.

  • Prepare: clean, dry and size the material.
  • Convert: ferment, react with alcohol, or digest.
  • Separate: distil, wash or settle to isolate the fuel.
  • Test quality before sale.
Worked example. In biodiesel, oil reacts with methanol and a catalyst to give fuel and glycerol. Glycerol is settled out and sold or used.

Activity. Draw a process chart for one pathway.

Standard to meet. Chart shows inputs, steps, products and by-products in order.

Module 4 Mass and energy balance 3 hApply

Track what goes in and what comes out to check yields.

  • Mass in must equal mass out including waste.
  • Yield = fuel output / feedstock input.
  • Energy ratio = energy in fuel / energy used to make it.
  • A ratio below 1 means the process uses more energy than it gives.
Worked example. 100 kg of oil plus 11 kg of alcohol gives about 96 kg of fuel and 15 kg of glycerol and loss. Yield is 96 per cent of oil input.

Activity. Complete a mass balance for 1,000 litres of fuel.

Standard to meet. Balance inputs and outputs within 2 per cent and show the yield.

Module 5 Emissions and sustainability 3 hEvaluate

Compare emissions over the whole life, not only at the tailpipe.

  • Count growing, collecting, making, moving and burning.
  • Compare with the fossil fuel it replaces.
  • Watch for land clearing, which can cancel the benefit.
  • Use a recognised method and state it.
Worked example. A fuel from waste oil may cut life-cycle emissions by a large share, while one from cleared forest land may give no saving at all.

Activity. Estimate the saving per litre for one pathway.

Standard to meet. State the method, list the stages and give the result as a range.

Module 6 Safety and quality 3 hApply

Alcohols, gases and heat create fire and health risks; quality failures damage engines.

  • Alcohol vapour is flammable; ventilate and avoid sparks.
  • Biogas can collect and explode; install detection and venting.
  • Test water content, acidity and purity before sale.
  • Keep records of every batch.
Worked example. A batch with high water content is held back, because water causes rust and microbe growth in tanks.

Activity. Write a safety and quality checklist.

Standard to meet. Write a checklist of at least eight checks with limits and who signs.

Module 7 Economics and policy 3 hEvaluate

Plants live or die on cost, price and rules.

  • Capital cost, running cost and feedstock cost drive price.
  • By-products can improve income.
  • Blending rules and incentives vary by country.
  • Test the plan against a fall in the fossil fuel price.
Worked example. A small plant selling fuel and glycerol earns enough only when feedstock stays under a set price per litre; the plan shows that price.

Activity. Build a simple cost per litre estimate.

Standard to meet. Add up the costs and show the break-even price.

Module 8 Write the overview 3 hCreate

Bring the work together as a three-page document for a decision maker.

  • Page one: pathway, feedstock and process chart.
  • Page two: balance, emissions and risks.
  • Page three: economics and recommendation.
  • State what you do not know yet.
Worked example. The overview recommends a waste-oil biodiesel pilot of 1,000 litres a month with a six-month review.

Activity. Write the three-page overview.

Standard to meet. Include all sections, make numbers agree across pages, and end the document with a clear recommendation.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: process chart, balance sheet, emissions estimate, overview. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Science accuracyErrorsCorrectCorrect and sourced
Balance and emissionsMissingDoneDone with assumptions
Risk reviewMissingListedListed with controls
Pathway numbersYields and emissions stated bareYield and emission figures cite a source and unitFigures compared across two sources, with the gap explained
Land, food and fuel rulesPolicy not checkedFuel standard, land use and subsidy rule for the place are written downRules checked on the regulator's page and the date recorded

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name three biofuels.

Model answer: Ethanol, biodiesel, biogas. (Covered in module 1: Types of biofuel. To pass that module: Complete the table correctly with one limit for each type.)

Q2. Best feedstock type?

Model answer: Waste and residues. (Covered in module 2: Feedstocks. To pass that module: Justify the ranking with one number per feedstock.)

Q3. What is a co-product?

Model answer: Another useful output such as glycerol. (Covered in module 3: Production steps. To pass that module: Chart shows inputs, steps, products and by-products in order.)

Q4. What is a balance?

Model answer: Inputs equal outputs plus losses. (Covered in module 4: Mass and energy balance. To pass that module: Balance inputs and outputs within 2 per cent and show the yield.)

Q5. Name an emissions source in the chain.

Model answer: Land change, fertiliser, processing, transport. (Covered in module 5: Emissions and sustainability. To pass that module: State the method, list the stages and give the result as a range.)

Q6. Name a safety hazard.

Model answer: Flammable liquids or methanol. (Covered in module 6: Safety and quality. To pass that module: Write a checklist of at least eight checks with limits and who signs.)

Q7. Name a quality test.

Model answer: Water content, acidity, flash point. (Covered in module 6: Safety and quality. To pass that module: Write a checklist of at least eight checks with limits and who signs.)

Q8. Biggest cost?

Model answer: Feedstock. (Covered in module 7: Economics and policy. To pass that module: Add up the costs and show the break-even price.)

Q9. Food versus fuel question?

Model answer: Avoid competing with food supply. (Covered in module 2: Feedstocks. To pass that module: Justify the ranking with one number per feedstock.)

Q10. Why check sources?

Model answer: Emissions claims vary. (Covered in module 5: Emissions and sustainability. To pass that module: State the method, list the stages and give the result as a range.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Fuel standards, licences and incentives differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.
