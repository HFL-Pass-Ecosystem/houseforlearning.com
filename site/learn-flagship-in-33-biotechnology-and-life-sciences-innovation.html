flagship course

Biotechnology and Life Sciences Innovation

Move a life-science idea from lab result to a tested, lawful and fundable product: validation, quality, intellectual property, regulation and a small-venture plan.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5 Specialist8 modules · 41 h lessons plus practice

Move a life-science idea from lab result to a tested, lawful and fundable product: validation, quality, intellectual property, regulation and a small-venture plan.

  • Define a problem a life-science product can solve and who pays for it.
  • Apply validation steps and quality records.
  • Analyse trial data with simple statistics.
  • Evaluate regulatory, ethical and biosafety risk.
  • Create a venture plan and a pitch.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Take one lab-scale idea to a validation report, a protection plan and a venture plan.

Condition

About 12 weeks part-time. Access to lab data or a virtual lab, a mentor from industry or research, a team of two to four.

Standard

Claims are backed by repeated data, risks are listed with controls, ethics approval steps are named, and the plan shows costs and milestones.

Who it is for: Biotechnology graduates, research associates, founders, agri-biotech staff. Requirements: Biotechnology Foundations or equivalent. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 From problem to opportunity 5 hDefine
  • Start from a need in health, farming, food or environment, not from a technique.
  • Size the need: how many people, how often, what they pay today.
  • Write a one-sentence problem and a one-sentence solution.
  • Check what already exists in papers and public records.
Worked example. Need: post-harvest rot hitting banana growers. Idea: a natural coating from plant extract. Market check: three growers each lose about a fifth of the crop.

Activity. Write a problem sheet with numbers and sources.

Standard to meet. Problem is specific, sized and sourced; solution is one sentence.

Module 2 Validation: repeat and control 6 hApply
  • Set a hypothesis and a pass limit before the test.
  • Use controls and at least three repeats.
  • Record conditions: temperature, batch, date, operator.
  • Report the spread, not only the average.
Worked example. Treated bananas keep firm for 9 days against 5 days for controls, over 3 batches; the spread is one day.

Activity. Design and run a validation with three repeats.

Standard to meet. Hypothesis, controls, repeats and limits are written first; results meet or miss the limit honestly.

Module 3 Data and statistics for decisions 5 hAnalyse
  • Mean, spread and a simple comparison of two groups.
  • Charts that show every point.
  • What a small sample can and cannot show.
  • Keep raw data and the steps that produced each chart.
Worked example. With 3 repeats, the gap of 4 days is larger than the spread of 1 day, so the effect is likely real but needs more repeats.

Activity. Analyse a data set and write a short finding.

Standard to meet. Numbers are right; limits of the sample are stated.

Module 4 Quality and traceability 5 hApply
  • Standard operating procedures: who, what, how, when.
  • Batch records and sample labels.
  • Calibration of balances, pipettes and meters.
  • Change control: write down any change and why.
Worked example. A batch record lists lot numbers of extract, date, operator, volume, pH and result.

Activity. Write one procedure and one batch record.

Standard to meet. Another person can repeat the work from your procedure.

Module 5 Intellectual property and data 4 hEvaluate
  • Patent, trademark, copyright and trade secret: what each protects.
  • Prior art search before you file.
  • Share data and materials under written terms.
  • Keep dated lab notebooks signed by a witness.
Worked example. A coating recipe is kept as a trade secret while a patent search is done.

Activity. Write a protection plan for your idea.

Standard to meet. Plan names the right tool for each asset and the next dated step.

Module 6 Biosafety, ethics and regulation 6 hEvaluate
  • Risk groups and containment levels.
  • Consent for human samples and ethics review for animals.
  • Approvals for food, drug, agri and device products follow different rules.
  • Local authority and national guidelines come first.
Worked example. A food-contact coating needs a food-safety review before any sale.

Activity. Map the approvals your product needs.

Standard to meet. Map names each authority, step and document.

Module 7 Scale-up and cost 5 hAnalyse
  • Lab, pilot and production scale differ.
  • Cost of goods: materials, labour, utilities, loss.
  • Find the step that fails first at scale.
  • Use partners for pilot runs.
Worked example. A pilot at 20 litres raises cost per litre by 30% because of cooling.

Activity. Cost one product at lab and pilot scale.

Standard to meet. Costs add up and the first scale risk is named.

Module 8 Venture plan and pitch 5 hCreate
  • Customer, product, proof, money, team.
  • Milestones with dates and funds needed.
  • Grants, incubators and customers as funding sources.
  • Pitch in five minutes with one slide on proof.
Worked example. A plan asking for a modest grant to run a field trial on 10 farms.

Activity. Write the plan and pitch it.

Standard to meet. Plan has milestones, costs and proof; pitch fits five minutes.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
ValidationMissing or unclearMeets the stated checkRepeated with controls
ComplianceGuessingEvidence keptApprovals mapped and tracked
RecordsGapsCompleteComplete and reviewed by a peer
Safety and ethicsOverlookedFollowedFollowed and taught to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why start from a need?

Model answer: Products succeed when they solve a real problem. (Covered in module 1: From problem to opportunity. To pass that module: Problem is specific, sized and sourced; solution is one sentence.)

Q2. Why set the pass limit first?

Model answer: To avoid choosing the limit to fit the result. (Covered in module 2: Validation: repeat and control. To pass that module: Hypothesis, controls, repeats and limits are written first; results meet or miss the limit honestly.)

Q3. What can a small sample not show?

Model answer: That the effect is certain or applies everywhere. (Covered in module 3: Data and statistics for decisions. To pass that module: Numbers are right; limits of the sample are stated.)

Q4. What is a batch record for?

Model answer: It lets anyone trace and repeat a batch. (Covered in module 4: Quality and traceability. To pass that module: Another person can repeat the work from your procedure.)

Q5. What is prior art?

Model answer: Earlier public knowledge that can block a patent. (Covered in module 5: Intellectual property and data. To pass that module: Plan names the right tool for each asset and the next dated step.)

Q6. Who decides approval for your product?

Model answer: The authority for its product type, such as food, drug or agri. (Covered in module 6: Biosafety, ethics and regulation. To pass that module: Map names each authority, step and document.)

Q7. What is cost of goods?

Model answer: Direct cost to make one unit. (Covered in module 7: Scale-up and cost. To pass that module: Costs add up and the first scale risk is named.)

Q8. What must the proof slide show?

Model answer: The validated data and its limits. (Covered in module 8: Venture plan and pitch. To pass that module: Plan has milestones, costs and proof; pitch fits five minutes.)

Q9. Why keep raw data?

Model answer: So results can be checked. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why name a witness on notebooks?

Model answer: It dates and supports your records. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L5 Specialist. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know basic biology words.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain how products are tested.
  3. L3 Foundation. Help run lab work.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Validate an idea alone.
  5. L5 Specialist. Take a product to pilot and plan its protection.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead a research and development team.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set innovation and quality strategy for an institute.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Biology words

Define 10 basic biology words (cell, gene, enzyme, protein and others) and use each in a correct sentence.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks sentences for correct use. Covered in module 1.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Product testing explained

Explain how a product is tested using a control and repeats, and describe the 4 stages from idea to trial with one check at each.

Speaker notes. Teacher asks how a new soil booster would be tested and checks the control is named. Covered in module 2.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Lab work help

Help run lab work for 2 weeks, keeping a notebook with dated entries, witnessed pages and raw data saved for every run.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the notebook for dates and a witness name. Covered in modules 3 and 4.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Validate an idea

Validate an idea alone with 3 repeats, a control and a statistic, and state a go or no-go decision backed by numbers.

Speaker notes. Teacher reruns one average and checks the decision follows the data. Covered in modules 2 and 3.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Pilot and protection

Plan a pilot of 100 units with a cost table, a scale-up risk list and a protection plan for the idea. Check local rules with an expert.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the cost per unit and the filing steps listed.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: R and D team lead

Lead an R and D team of 5 with stage gates, a monthly data review and a stop decision made on evidence for at least one weak project.

Speaker notes. Teacher reads the gate record for the stopped project.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Institute strategy

Set an innovation and quality strategy for an institute with 4 measured goals, an ethics process and a yearly audit plan.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks each goal has an owner and a number. Check local rules with an expert.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
