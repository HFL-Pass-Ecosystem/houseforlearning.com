Selecting a cable to the standard

Choosing a cable size using current rating, voltage drop and protection. Verified by a named engineer before use.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 4 h 10 min of CPDSelf-study plus role-playEngineering

Choosing a cable size using current rating, voltage drop and protection. Verified by a named engineer before use.

  • Calculate design current, given a load schedule, with correct units and no errors.
  • Select a cable, given tables and installation method, meeting current rating and voltage-drop limits.
  • Check protection, given a protective device, that it protects the cable.
  • Record the calculation, given a template, so another engineer can verify it.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Calculate the design current for a three-phase load, select a cable size and type that meets current rating and voltage-drop limits, and record the calculation so another engineer can verify it

Condition

Given a load schedule, sample rating tables, an installation method and a calculation template, in a 4-hour practical session with three practice loads

Standard

Calculate current with correct units and no errors, show a corrected rating above the design current, keep voltage drop within the stated limit, cite the clause and table, and complete a sheet ready for checking

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Load, current and the safety margin 35 min
  • Three-phase current is I = P / (sqrt3 x V x pf).
  • Keep units clear: watts, volts, then amps.
  • A margin covers heat, grouping and ageing.
Worked example. 22 kW at 400 V, power factor 0.85: I = 22,000 / (1.732 x 400 x 0.85) = 22,000 / 588.9 = about 37.4 A. A sample 25 percent margin gives 37.4 x 1.25 = 46.7 A.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "load, current and the safety margin": calculate design current, given a load schedule, with correct units and no errors.

Standard to meet. Calculate the design current for a three-phase load such as 22 kW at 400 V, given a load schedule, using the correct formula and units, and state why a safety margin on current is applied.

Module 2 Voltage drop and tolerance 45 min
  • Drop in volts = mV per amp per metre x current x length / 1000.
  • Compare it with the limit on the sheet, in volts or percent.
  • Over the limit: choose a larger size or shorten the run.
Worked example. Case card 2: 22 kW load, 60 m from the board, 16 mm2 at 2.5 mV/A/m (sample table). Drop = 2.5 x 37.4 x 60 / 1000 = 5.6 V, or 1.4 percent of 400 V, under a 4 percent limit (16 V). If the limit were 1 percent (4 V), 25 mm2 at 1.6 mV/A/m gives 3.6 V, or 0.9 percent.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "voltage drop and tolerance": select a cable, given tables and installation method, meeting current rating and voltage-drop limits.

Standard to meet. Check voltage drop for a cable run, given its length and the limit on the sheet, and decide whether a larger size or a shorter run is needed to stay within tolerance.

Module 3 Reading the sample table 35 min
  • Find the size row, then the installation-method column.
  • Multiply the tabulated rating by every correction factor.
  • The corrected rating must be above the design current.
Worked example. Sample table: 10 mm2 = 52 A, 16 mm2 = 69 A. Temperature factor 0.87 x grouping factor 0.80 = 0.696. 10 mm2: 52 x 0.696 = 36.2 A, below 37.4 A, so it fails. 16 mm2: 69 x 0.696 = 48.0 A, above 37.4 A, so it passes.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "reading the sample table": check protection, given a protective device, that it protects the cable.

Standard to meet. Interpret the sample rating table, given the installation method and temperature, and apply the correction so the corrected rating is shown to be above the design current.

Module 4 Choosing size and type, citing the clause 40 min
  • Pass current first, then voltage drop, then short-circuit withstand.
  • Pick the smallest size that passes every check.
  • Write the clause and table number beside the choice.
Worked example. Choose 16 mm2: corrected rating 48.0 A is above 37.4 A, and the drop of 5.6 V (1.4 percent) is within the 4 percent limit. Record sample Table B and clause 7, and note that short-circuit withstand is still to be checked.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "choosing size and type, citing the clause": record the calculation, given a template, so another engineer can verify it.

Standard to meet. Select a cable size and type, given tables and an installation method, meeting current rating and voltage-drop limits, and cite the clause and table number that support the choice.

Module 5 Calculation sheet 30 min
  • Every line has an input, a unit and a result.
  • Cite the clause so a checker can find the rule.
  • Another competent person must be able to verify without asking.
Worked example. Sheet line: 22 kW | 400 V | pf 0.85 | I = 37.4 A | method C | factor 0.696 | 16 mm2 | corrected 48.0 A | drop 5.6 V (1.4 percent) | Table B, clause 7 | checker signed.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "calculation sheet": record the calculation, given a template, so another engineer can verify it. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Record the calculation on the template, with inputs, units, results and clause, so another competent person can verify it without asking questions.

Module 6 Three practice loads 45 min
  • Work each load from current to drop to clause.
  • Use that load's own length and method.
  • Check units before moving to the next load.
Worked example. Three loads at 400 V: A 11 kW, pf 0.80, I = 11,000 / (1.732 x 400 x 0.80) = 19.8 A; B 15 kW, pf 0.90, I = 15,000 / (1.732 x 400 x 0.90) = 24.1 A; C 22 kW, pf 0.85, I = 37.4 A.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "three practice loads": record the calculation, given a template, so another engineer can verify it. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Calculate and select the cable for three practice loads, each with its own length and installation method, with correct units and no errors.

Module 7 Assessment 10 min
  • Work alone, with the tables and the template.
  • Do not copy an old sheet without re-checking inputs.
  • Hand in a sheet that is ready for a checker.
Worked example. New load: 30 kW, 400 V, pf 0.85, 80 m. I = 30,000 / (1.732 x 400 x 0.85) = 50.9 A. Then correct the rating, check the drop, cite the clause and fill the sheet.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "assessment": record the calculation, given a template, so another engineer can verify it. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Complete a full cable selection for a new load, from current to voltage drop to clause, with no calculation errors and a sheet ready for checking.

Module 8 Teach-back and peer check 10 min
  • Swap sheets with a partner and check the inputs first.
  • Recalculate the current before reading the cable choice.
  • Say what is wrong in one sentence, with the right value.
Worked example. A partner's sheet gives 31.8 A for the 22 kW load. Recalculation: 22,000 / (1.732 x 400) = 31.8 A, so the 0.85 power factor was left out. The correct value is 37.4 A, which is 5.6 A higher.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "teach-back and peer check": record the calculation, given a template, so another engineer can verify it. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate a partner's calculation sheet by recalculating the current, find any wrong input or missing factor, and state the correct value with a one-sentence explanation.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Formula for three-phase current?

Model answer: P / (sqrt3 x V x pf). (Covered in module 1: Load, current and the safety margin. To pass that module: Calculate the design current for a three-phase load such as 22 kW at 400 V, given a load schedule, using the correct formula and units, and state why a safety margin on current is applied.)

Why correct the rating?

Model answer: Heat, grouping and method change what the cable can carry. (Covered in module 3: Reading the sample table. To pass that module: Interpret the sample rating table, given the installation method and temperature, and apply the correction so the corrected rating is shown to be above the design current.)

What else after current?

Model answer: Voltage drop and short-circuit withstand. (Covered in module 2: Voltage drop and tolerance. To pass that module: Check voltage drop for a cable run, given its length and the limit on the sheet, and decide whether a larger size or a shorter run is needed to stay within tolerance.)

Where is the rule?

Model answer: Your national standard; cite clause and table. (Covered in module 4: Choosing size and type, citing the clause. To pass that module: Select a cable size and type, given tables and an installation method, meeting current rating and voltage-drop limits, and cite the clause and table number that support the choice.)

Why a margin on current?

Model answer: Heat, grouping and ageing reduce safe capacity. (Covered in module 1: Load, current and the safety margin. To pass that module: Calculate the design current for a three-phase load such as 22 kW at 400 V, given a load schedule, using the correct formula and units, and state why a safety margin on current is applied.)

What if voltage drop is over the limit?

Model answer: Use a larger size or shorten the run. (Covered in module 2: Voltage drop and tolerance. To pass that module: Check voltage drop for a cable run, given its length and the limit on the sheet, and decide whether a larger size or a shorter run is needed to stay within tolerance.)

Where is the choice recorded?

Model answer: On the calculation sheet with the clause. (Covered in module 5: Calculation sheet. To pass that module: Record the calculation on the template, with inputs, units, results and clause, so another competent person can verify it without asking questions.)

Who checks the sheet?

Model answer: A second competent person. (Covered in module 5: Calculation sheet. To pass that module: Record the calculation on the template, with inputs, units, results and clause, so another competent person can verify it without asking questions.)

Can you copy last year's sheet?

Model answer: Only after re-checking the inputs. (Covered in module 5: Calculation sheet. To pass that module: Record the calculation on the template, with inputs, units, results and clause, so another competent person can verify it without asking questions.)

A partner's sheet shows 31.8 A for a 22 kW, 400 V, power factor 0.85 load. What went wrong?

Model answer: The power factor was left out; with it the current is about 37.4 A. (Covered in module 8: Teach-back and peer check. To pass that module: Evaluate a partner's calculation sheet by recalculating the current, find any wrong input or missing factor, and state the correct value with a one-sentence explanation.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What a cable does. Speaker note: A cable carries current from the board to a load. At this first step the learner can say what current, voltage and load mean, and why a cable that is too small gets hot and can fail.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Why size matters. Speaker note: The learner can name the three checks behind a cable choice: current rating, voltage drop and protection. They know the national standard holds the rules and that a named engineer verifies the choice before use.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Current and the table. Speaker note: The learner can calculate current for a simple load and read a rating table with help. They follow the 22 kW worked example, including the power factor and the correction for installation method.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Select and record a cable. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner calculates design current, corrects the table rating, checks voltage drop, selects a size, cites the clause and table, and records every step so another engineer can verify it.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Long runs and groups. Speaker note: The specialist handles long runs, grouped cables, high ambient temperature and several loads on one feeder. They test two or three sizes, compare the results, and justify the final choice in writing.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Review other sheets. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner checks other engineers' sheets, finds missing factors and wrong inputs, and confirms that the chosen cable is protected by its device, including short-circuit withstand.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Own the method. Speaker note: The super expert maintains the calculation templates and teaches the method. They compare editions of the standard, set review rules for sheets, and mentor engineers to verify design work instead of copying old sheets.
