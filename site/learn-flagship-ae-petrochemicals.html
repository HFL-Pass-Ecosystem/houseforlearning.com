Petrochemicals

Learn petrochemicals for the UAE: rules, workflow, costing in AED and a recorded 30-day practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 177 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Understand Explain the UAE market, rules and hazards for petrochemicals.
  • Apply Run the workflow: feed, react, cool and separate, then bag or tank the product.
  • Analyse Measure tonnes in specification per day and find the biggest cause of loss.
  • Evaluate Cost and price in AED and judge scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a record pack a supervisor can audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a safety-first process brief, a costed throughput model and a 30-day observation log for plastics, resins and chemical building blocks, in a simulated or supervised setting.

Condition

About 177 hours over 3 to 6 months. A supervised site visit or virtual plant walk-through, a free spreadsheet, a mentor and a peer circle. You do not operate live plant alone.

Standard

Hazards named with controls; every number has a unit and AED; records complete; rules confirmed with the local authority; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better.

Process learning only. Main risks: toxic release, fire and runaway reaction. You work only under a competent supervisor and follow the site permit and safety rules.

Requirements: none. Currency: AED on every number.

lesson by lesson

11 modules with worked examples (177 h guided)

Module 1 Map the market and the people 5 hUnderstand

Start with who needs plastics, resins and chemical building blocks and why. Around plastics converters, packaging makers and construction firms drive demand in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other emirates.

  • List 10 offers or suppliers in the UAE with price per tonne in AED and who buys.
  • Interview 5 people from plastics converters, packaging makers and construction firms: what do they buy now, how often, what do they pay, what annoys them?
  • Learn three terms: Feedstock (the raw material fed into a plant); Specification (the written limits a product must meet to be sold); Batch record (the log of what was made, when and how).
  • Write a one-page brief: user, need, hoped-for price, and the rule that matters most.
Worked example. Five interviews all say delay is the main pain. You write: 'Reliable plastics, resins and chemical building blocks, answer in one day, price near AED 4,200 per tonne.'

Activity. Complete the market table, five interview notes and the brief.

Standard to meet. Ten offers priced; five users heard; one need stated in one sentence.

Module 2 Rules, hazards and licences 6 hApply

Know the rule before the work. Main hazards here: toxic release, fire and runaway reaction. Hazard thinking means list each step, ask what could go wrong, and decide the control.

  • Ask your local authority or free zone which licence, permit or inspection applies. Write the answer and the date you asked.
  • For each step (feed; react; cool and separate; then bag or tank the product) write the hazard and the control.
  • Use a hierarchy: remove the hazard, replace it, separate people from it, change the way of work, then protective equipment last.
  • Keep a one-page incident rule: who you call, what you stop, what you record.
Worked example. Step 'feed': hazard = toxic release, fire; control = written check before start and a named supervisor; record = signed log line.

Activity. Draw the flow and mark hazard and control at each step.

Standard to meet. Every step has a hazard and a control; licence question answered in writing.

Module 3 Core workflow, step by step 12 hApply

The work is a short chain: feed, react, cool and separate, then bag or tank the product. Learn each link, then link them with records.

  • Write a checklist for each of the 4 steps: input, action, check, output.
  • Time each step three times and note the slowest.
  • Record a defect or delay each time it happens: what, where, cause, fix.
  • Measure tonnes in specification per day; this is your main number.
Worked example. Three timed runs of 'react' take 42, 38 and 51 minutes. Average 43.7; the 51-minute run had a missing tool. Fix: a tool tray checked at start.

Activity. Run the chain three times (simulated or real) and fill the log.

Standard to meet. Three complete logs; the slowest step has a cause and a fix.

Module 4 Quality and measurement 10 hAnalyse

What you do not measure you cannot improve. Your main measure is tonnes in specification per day.

  • Define tonnes in specification per day: what counts, what does not, and over what period.
  • Draw a simple chart of the last 10 results and mark the average.
  • Find the top two causes of loss with a tally sheet, then fix the biggest one first.
  • Check measuring tools: are they accurate, clean and in date?
Worked example. Tally of 40 faults: 22 from late inputs, 9 from wrong settings, 9 other. Fix late inputs first: that is 55% of faults.

Activity. Collect a tally for one week and make a Pareto chart.

Standard to meet. Tally complete; biggest cause named; one fix planned with a date.

Module 5 Costing and pricing in AED 12 hEvaluate

Know the cost of one tonne before you set the price. Include materials, labour, energy, transport, licence share, waste and a safety margin.

  • Cost per tonne = total cost divided by tonnes sold (not made).
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs divided by gain per unit.
  • Test the price with two users and write down the answer.
Worked example. At AED 4,200 per tonne and cost AED 3,600, gain is AED 600 and margin 14.3%. At 200 tonnes a month, gross gain is AED 120,000. If fixed costs are AED 144,000, break-even is 240 tonnes.

Activity. Build the cost sheet with AED on every line and have your mentor check it.

Standard to meet. No missing cost found; margin calculated twice by different routes.

Module 6 Serve and sell responsibly 12 hApply

Customers return when you keep promises. Promise only what your workflow can deliver.

  • Write a one-page offer: what is included, what is not, price, time, how you fix mistakes.
  • Write a short script for the first call or visit.
  • Agree dates in writing; confirm each change.
  • Handle a complaint in four steps: listen, apologise for the impact, fix, record what changed.
Worked example. Offer: 'Plastics, resins and chemical building blocks delivered within one working day; price AED 4,200 per tonne; faults corrected free within 3 days.'

Activity. Present the offer to two users and note changes.

Standard to meet. Offer has scope, price, time and fix rule; two users commented.

Module 7 Run the 30-day observation 55 hCreate

Small, real and recorded. Change one thing at a time and learn quickly.

  • Keep a daily log: date, units, cost, delays, defects, safety notes.
  • Hold a 15-minute review each week with your mentor.
  • Write down every complaint and what you changed.
  • Take photos only where allowed and never of people without consent.
Worked example. Week 2 shows tonnes in specification per day at 71%; week 4 at 84% after the tool tray and an earlier input check.

Activity. Complete 30 days of the log and four weekly reviews.

Standard to meet. 30 days logged; each defect has a cause; weekly notes kept.

Module 8 Review, decide and plan 38 hEvaluate

A decision beats a feeling. Compare plan with result and choose to scale, change or stop.

  • Make a before and after table: cost per unit, defects, delays, repeat users.
  • Write three lessons.
  • Decide scale, change or stop, with the numbers behind it.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
Worked example. Result: cost per tonne fell from AED 3,600 to AED 3,384; repeat users 6 of 9. Decision: scale to a second site; keep price.

Activity. Present to a review room and collect one written comment from a user.

Standard to meet. Decision backed by numbers; two peer reviews given.

Module 9 From feedstock to product: the reaction chain 8 hUnderstand

Plants turn simple gases and liquids into building blocks, and then into plastics. Knowing the chain explains why one upset affects many products.

  • Draw the chain from feedstock to a final plastic and name each intermediate.
  • Heat, pressure and a catalyst decide how much product forms; each has a safe window.
  • Side products are not always waste: some are sold or burned for energy.
  • A change in feed quality changes everything downstream, so feed is tested on arrival.
Worked example. A feed lot arrives with more water than allowed. The lab flags it, the lot is held, and the plant uses stock from another tank instead of risking the catalyst.

Activity. Draw the chain for one plastic and mark where heat, pressure and a catalyst act.

Standard to meet. Every step labelled; each safe window written; feed test named.

Module 10 Runaway reactions and pressure safety 10 hApply

A reaction that makes its own heat can speed up faster than cooling can follow. Pressure relief and interlocks exist for that moment.

  • Know the signs: rising temperature with cooling at maximum, pressure rising faster than normal.
  • A relief valve opens at a set pressure and sends gas to a safe place; it must never be isolated without a permit.
  • An interlock stops the feed automatically if a limit is crossed; never bypass one without written approval.
  • Practise the shutdown order on paper: stop feed, keep cooling, vent safely, call for help.
Worked example. Reactor temperature climbs 3 degrees Celsius per minute with cooling full. At the alarm limit the interlock stops the feed. The operator confirms, keeps cooling and reports the time to the supervisor.

Activity. Write the shutdown order for a reactor and list which devices act at each step.

Standard to meet. Order is safe; no step relies on bypassing a protection.

Module 11 Quality of plastics: tests and records 9 hEvaluate

Customers buy by property: how easily the plastic melts, how dense it is, how strong. Each lot carries a test certificate.

  • Typical tests: melt flow, density, tensile strength, colour and moisture.
  • Sample the same way every time: from several places, labelled and sealed.
  • Compare results with the written specification; out-of-limit lots are held, retested and decided by the quality lead.
  • Keep records for each lot so a complaint can be traced to the exact shift.
Worked example. Lot 114 melt flow is 8.4 g per 10 minutes against a limit of 7.0 to 8.0 g per 10 minutes. It is held; a retest gives 8.3; the lot is sold as a lower grade with the customer told.

Activity. Fill a certificate for three sample lots and decide each as pass, hold or downgrade.

Standard to meet. Every result compared with a limit; decisions explained.

levels L1 to L7

Slide by slide, with notes

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: From gas to plastic

On the slide: L1 Basic · From gas to plastic

Notes: Name a feedstock, a plastic made from it and the three hazards of toxic release, fire and runaway reaction. Notes: draw the chain on one page; no plant entry, and ask a supervisor before any visit.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Safe windows and specification

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Safe windows and specification

Notes: Describe why heat, pressure and a catalyst each have a safe window and what a specification is. Notes: show a lot with a test result outside its limit and ask who may release it; the answer is the quality lead, never the operator alone.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Draw the reaction chain with safe windows

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Draw the reaction chain with safe windows

Notes: Draw the chain for one plastic and mark where heat, pressure and a catalyst act, with a test for feed on arrival. Notes: work only under a competent supervisor; check every step is labelled and each safe window has a written number and unit.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Shut down a reactor on paper

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Shut down a reactor on paper

Notes: Write the shutdown order for a reactor with stop feed, keep cooling, vent safely and call for help, and name the device acting at each step. Notes: use a rise of 3 degrees Celsius per minute with cooling at full; no step may rely on bypassing a protection.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Decide pass, hold or downgrade

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Decide pass, hold or downgrade

Notes: Evaluate three sample lots against written limits and decide pass, hold or downgrade with a reason. Notes: lot 114 reads 8.4 against a limit of 7.0 to 8.0 and retests at 8.3, so it is held and sold as a lower grade with the customer told; at AED 4,200 price and AED 3,600 cost a tonne gains AED 600, a margin of 14.3 per cent.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Coach operators on records and interlocks

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Coach operators on records and interlocks

Notes: Lead a team in keeping complete batch records and in never bypassing an interlock without written approval. Notes: sample five batch records a week against a checklist; any bypass without approval is a stop-and-report event.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Design the quality and safety system

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Design the quality and safety system

Notes: Design a feed test, batch record, relief and interlock check system for a plant, with an audit and a 12-month plan. Notes: trace three complaints to the exact shift to test the records; present one risk, one saving per tonne in AED and a review date for each protective device.
