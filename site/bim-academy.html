BIM academy

Learn to design, coordinate and hand over buildings with digital models, to our own campus standards. Seven levels, from first steps to leading a network of campuses.

Start at level 1See career pathwaysCampus standards

seven levels

L1 to L7, about 141 hours in all

  • L1 Basic · 6 h

    Buildings and models: the basics

    What a building is made of, why we draw it before we build it, and what a digital model adds to a drawing.

    Open L1 Basic
  • L2 Awareness · 9 h

    BIM awareness: read a model and a standard

    Open a shared model, move around it, read its data and understand what LOD means.

    Open L2 Awareness
  • L3 Foundation · 14 h

    Model a campus module to LOD 200

    Build a campus module and a small learning studio in a model, using our element library and naming rules.

    Open L3 Foundation
  • L4 Practitioner · 18 h

    Coordinate trades and take off quantities (LOD 300)

    Bring architecture, structure and services together, find clashes and produce quantities you can defend.

    Open L4 Practitioner
  • L5 Specialist · 24 h

    Fabrication models, programme and cost (LOD 350 to 400)

    Prepare factory-ready modules, link the model to a build programme and to cost, and plan site assembly.

    Open L5 Specialist
  • L6 Advanced · 30 h

    Lead a BIM project: plans, rules and audits

    Write the information rules for a campus project, run the shared model environment and audit the work of others.

    Open L6 Advanced
  • L7 Super expert · 40 h

    Campus network standards and digital twins

    Own a whole campus standard across many sites and countries, and link the as-built model to live operation.

    Open L7 Super expert

how it works

Learn by building our own campus

  • Every level uses the same campus module, so the skills build on each other.
  • Each module has a worked example, an activity and a clear standard to meet.
  • Every level ends with five checks and model answers.
  • We teach the method, not a software brand: open formats and open standards.
  • Supplier and maker names never appear in a course model. You learn to describe what a part must do.

levels of development

LOD in one picture

LOD says how much detail a model element carries. You learn all six levels, and when to stop adding detail.
