flagship course · GL-34

Online Tutoring Platform

Set up and run a small tutoring service online: safe tutor onboarding, good lessons, fair pricing, scheduling, learner progress and child safeguarding.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Set up and run a small tutoring service online: safe tutor onboarding, good lessons, fair pricing, scheduling, learner progress and child safeguarding.

  • Understand Explain how an online tutoring service works and its safeguarding duties.
  • Apply Design a lesson format and a tutor checklist.
  • Analyse Analyse learner progress and tutor quality.
  • Create Create onboarding, scheduling and feedback processes.
  • Evaluate Evaluate safety, learning gains and finance.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Pilot an online tutoring service with at least 3 tutors and 10 learners (or a full simulation) for 4 weeks, with safeguarding rules, lesson plans, progress measures and finances.

Condition

A video tool, scheduling tool, lesson templates, a spreadsheet, a mentor with education experience and a peer circle. About 12 weeks part-time.

Standard

Tutors checked and trained; safeguarding policy followed (no private contact, recorded sessions where agreed, parent consent); learning goals set; progress measured; feedback 4 of 5 or better; finances positive per hour.

Who it is for: Teachers, tutors, education entrepreneurs, students, community educators. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Children first: background checks where the law allows, consent from parents, sessions in view of agreed rules, no personal contact outside the platform. Follow child-protection law in your place.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 What tutoring is 4 hUnderstand

Support that fills a gap in learning. You will define the learners you serve and pick one launch format, giving the tutoring service a focused scope and a clear first-month measure of success.

  • Types: homework help, exam prep, skills, language.
  • One-to-one or small group.
  • What works: goals, practice, feedback.
  • Gaps and strengths first.
  • Define who you serve, such as ages 11 to 14 needing maths support, and write down what a successful first month looks like, for example two sessions a week with a short progress note.
  • Compare one-to-one and small-group formats on cost, attention and scheduling, and choose a single format to launch with rather than offering everything at once.
  • Visit a tutoring session as an observer, with permission, and note what the learner did.
  • Write the safeguarding policy in plain language that parents can read.
Worked example. A learner can add but struggles with fractions: start with a diagnostic.

Activity. List five learner needs and best formats.

Standard to meet. Build a table with reasons.

Common mistake. Starting with price instead of learner need.

Second worked example. A diagnostic shows a learner scores 4 of 10 on fractions and 9 of 10 on addition; the first six sessions focus on fractions.

Check yourself. What gap did you find?

Module 2 Safeguarding 6 hUnderstand

Protect learners before everything. You will write a safeguarding policy and communication rules for tutors and parents, so every session has protections in place before any child is taught.

  • Checks and references for tutors.
  • Parent consent and clear rules.
  • No private messages or meetings.
  • Reporting route and records.
  • Write a short safeguarding policy covering recorded or open sessions, who may contact children, and what a tutor does if a worry arises, and make every tutor read and sign it.
  • Require parents to be reachable during lessons for younger learners, keep communication inside the platform, and never allow tutors to share personal accounts or private numbers.
  • Check that only approved tools and accounts are used for lessons.
  • Record sessions only where parents have agreed in writing.
Worked example. A tutor asks for a learner's personal number: refuse, report, review.

Activity. Write a safeguarding policy.

Standard to meet. Confirm with the mentor that it matches local rules.

Common mistake. Skipping checks.

Second worked example. Of 10 tutor applicants, 4 pass a sample lesson, 3 pass reference checks and 3 begin training; the process stays strict by design.

Check yourself. What checks did you do?

Module 3 Recruit and train tutors 8 hApply

Quality tutors make the service. You will run a structured tutor application with a sample lesson, references and orientation, which helps you admit only tutors who are ready for learners.

  • Skills test and sample lesson.
  • Training on the platform and rules.
  • Code of conduct.
  • Ongoing observation.
  • Use a structured application with a short sample lesson of ten minutes, two references and identity and background checks required where local rules apply, before any tutor meets a learner.
  • Give new tutors a two-hour orientation with a checklist covering the platform, lesson structure, tone with learners and how to report a concern.
  • Give learners a way to report a worry that does not depend on the tutor.
  • Keep all tutor documents in a locked place with access logs.
Worked example. Of 10 applicants 4 pass a test lesson; those 4 complete training.

Activity. Design an onboarding path.

Standard to meet. Write a path with check, training, sign-off.

Common mistake. Lenient onboarding.

Second worked example. A 45-minute lesson: 5 warm-up, 10 teach, 20 practice, 5 check, 5 recap. The tutor speaks for about 15 minutes in total.

Check yourself. How do you select?

Module 4 Lesson design 8 hApply

Short, clear, active. You will plan a 45-minute lesson with one stated learning aim and a closing check question, so each session leaves the learner with a visible gain.

  • Goal, warm-up, teach, practice, check, recap.
  • Use shared screen and exercises.
  • Adapt to age and level.
  • Record homework and next step.
  • Plan each lesson in three parts: five minutes to warm up, ten to teach, twenty to practise, five to check and five to record homework and the next step.
  • Match each activity to a clear learning aim written as 'the learner can...', and end every session by checking that single aim with a short question.
  • Set standard lesson plans but allow tutors to adapt them.
  • Share a one-page guide for parents on how to prepare.
Worked example. 45 minutes: 5 warm-up, 10 teach, 20 practice, 5 check, 5 recap.

Activity. Plan three lessons.

Standard to meet. Give each a measurable goal.

Common mistake. Lessons that are all talking.

Second worked example. A reminder 24 hours and 1 hour before cuts no-shows from 15% to 5%, saving about 8 lost lessons in 80.

Check yourself. How much do learners do?

Module 5 Scheduling and tools 8 hAnalyse

Make booking simple. You will set booking blocks with time-zone display and test the video tools on a slow link, reducing missed sessions and technical stress for families.

  • Time zones and reminders.
  • Cancellation rules.
  • Tool checks before the lesson.
  • Backup for tech failures.
  • Offer booking slots in fixed blocks, such as 45 minutes with a 15-minute gap, and show times in the learner's own time zone to avoid missed sessions.
  • Test your video, shared whiteboard and chat tools on a slow connection before launch, and prepare a fallback such as a phone call for the first ten minutes.
  • Test a lesson on slow internet and write the fallback.
  • Agree a cancellation and make-up lesson policy.
Worked example. A reminder 24 hours before cuts no-shows from 15% to 5%.

Activity. Set up booking and reminders.

Standard to meet. Test with a family.

Common mistake. Complicated booking.

Second worked example. Price 20 an hour; tutor 14; fees 1.20; margin 4.80. At 50 hours a month that is 240. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. What is your no-show rate?

Module 6 Pricing and finance 10 hCreate

Fair to tutors and families. You will calculate cost per lesson, set a price with a sensible margin and publish a cancellation rule, keeping the service fair to families and tutors alike.

  • Hourly rate; package; discounts.
  • Platform fee; tutor pay.
  • Payment through a reputable, regulated provider.
  • Refund rules.
  • Work out your cost per lesson by adding tutor pay, tools and support time, then divide by lessons delivered, before you choose a price that leaves a sensible margin.
  • Set a cancellation rule in advance, such as notice at least 12 hours before, and state it clearly at booking so that both families and tutors know the terms.
  • Check payments are through a reputable, regulated provider.
  • Collect feedback from learners in a short, friendly way.
Worked example. Price 20 an hour (an estimate; check your market); tutor 14; fees 1.20; margin 4.80. Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Build a pricing sheet.

Standard to meet. Show a positive margin per hour.

Common mistake. Hidden fees.

Second worked example. Pre-quiz 40% and post-quiz 70% after 4 weeks give a gain of 30 percentage points; the report shows both.

Check yourself. What is the margin?

Module 7 Progress and feedback 6 hEvaluate

Show learning, not just attendance. You will write short session notes and a before-and-after quiz, giving parents real evidence of progress instead of simply reporting that lessons took place.

  • Pre and post checks.
  • Parent update after each month.
  • Tutor reviews.
  • Adjust goals.
  • After every session, write a three-line note with what was covered, what went well and the next step, and share a summary with parents each month.
  • Use a simple before-and-after check, such as a five-question quiz in week one and week six, to show progress with real numbers rather than impressions.
  • Compare progress for different groups of learners to spot gaps.
  • Thank tutors with regular, specific feedback.
Worked example. Quiz score from 40% to 70% in 4 weeks.

Activity. Measure progress for 10 learners.

Standard to meet. Calculate the gain.

Common mistake. Reporting attendance only.

Second worked example. A parent complains the tutor ran 10 minutes over. You reply within a day, set a timer rule and check the next two sessions.

Check yourself. What gain?

Module 8 Quality and growth 4 hCreate

Keep standards as you grow. You will gather brief feedback, spot patterns in low scores and add tutors in small steps, so growth does not cost you the quality families chose.

  • Observe lessons.
  • Handle complaints.
  • Collect testimonials with consent.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Collect a one-minute feedback form from learners and parents after each fifth session, and review the lowest scores within a week to find a pattern.
  • Grow only when quality holds: add new tutors in small groups of two or three, observe their first lessons, and compare their feedback with your existing average.
  • Review the safeguarding policy every term.
  • Write a 90-day plan with a number of learners, tutors and a quality target.
Worked example. Observations find tutors talk 80% of the time; coach to ask more.

Activity. Report on the pilot.

Standard to meet. Report lists three changes.

Common mistake. Ignoring complaints.

Second worked example. Three tutors observed: one talks 80% of the time. Coaching on open questions drops it to 45% in a month.

Check yourself. How many complaints?

case cards and glossary

Case 1. The private message

A tutor messages a learner outside the platform.

Question: What do you do?

Model answer: Stop, report per policy, inform the parent, review the tutor.

Case 2. The no-show

A learner misses three sessions.

Question: Response?

Model answer: Contact the parent, adjust times, offer a pause.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why safeguarding first?

Model answer: Children's safety comes before anything else. (Covered in module 2: Safeguarding. To pass that module: Confirm with the mentor that it matches local rules.)

Q2. No private contact because?

Model answer: It prevents harm and protects tutors. (Covered in module 2: Safeguarding. To pass that module: Confirm with the mentor that it matches local rules.)

Q3. Name lesson steps.

Model answer: Goal, warm-up, teach, practice, check, recap. (Covered in module 4: Lesson design. To pass that module: Give each a measurable goal.)

Q4. How measure progress?

Model answer: Pre and post checks. (Covered in module 7: Progress and feedback. To pass that module: Calculate the gain.)

Q5. Why reminders?

Model answer: They reduce no-shows. (Covered in module 5: Scheduling and tools. To pass that module: Test with a family.)

Q7. How to pay?

Model answer: Through a reputable, regulated payment provider. (Covered in module 6: Pricing and finance. To pass that module: Show a positive margin per hour.)

Q8. Why observe lessons?

Model answer: To keep quality up. (Covered in module 8: Quality and growth. To pass that module: Report lists three changes.)

Q9. What is a diagnostic?

Model answer: A short check to find gaps. (Covered in module 1: What tutoring is. To pass that module: Build a table with reasons.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Child-protection, tutor registration and privacy rules differ by place; check before you launch. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Partner to practise with: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Practitioner

Notes: Run three practice lessons with two learners, record attendance and progress, and apply the safeguarding rules. Notes: recordings only with written consent. Standard: each lesson has a log and a progress note.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Specialist

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Specialist

Notes: Pilot an online tutoring service with at least 3 tutors and 10 learners for four weeks, with safeguarding rules, lesson plans, progress measures and finances. Notes: include tutor vetting and a reporting route. Standard: vetting records complete and a progress measure reported per learner.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Advanced

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Advanced

Notes: Run a tutoring platform with quality review, tutor training and a safeguarding audit. Notes: add a complaints log and a monthly lesson-observation sample. Standard: every tutor observed each term and serious reports answered within a day.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Super expert

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Super expert

Notes: Set the lesson-quality and child-safeguarding method for online tutoring and teach the tutors who use it. Notes: hold a safeguarding review of incidents, reports and recordings each term.
