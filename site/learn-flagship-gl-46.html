flagship course

DNA Analysis (Awareness Level)

Understand what DNA tests can and cannot tell you, how samples are handled, and how to protect privacy and dignity. Awareness only.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L2 Awareness16:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain DNA, genes and the main kinds of tests in plain words.
  • Apply Read a sample report and say what it does and does not mean.
  • Analyse Spot overclaims in test marketing.
  • Evaluate Weigh benefits and risks of sharing genetic data.
  • Create Draft a plain-language consent explainer.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a two-page plain-language explainer on DNA testing with a risk list and a consent checklist.

Condition

A computer, public information, two peers and a mentor. You will not handle real samples or interpret anyone's results.

Standard

Facts are correct and sourced; limits are stated; no diagnosis is offered; privacy risks are named; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Lab assistants, health communicators, teachers, curious adults. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 DNA in simple words 2 hUnderstand

DNA is the instruction code in cells.

  • Genes are sections of DNA; we inherit them from parents.
  • Most traits come from many genes and the environment.
  • A test reads some parts, not everything.
Worked example. Height depends on many genes and on food and health in childhood.

Activity. Draw a simple DNA to trait picture.

Standard to meet. Picture has no false claims.

Module 2 Kinds of tests 2 hUnderstand

Tests differ in purpose.

  • Ancestry, health risk, carrier, paternity, forensic.
  • A test result is a probability, not destiny.
  • Medical tests belong with a clinician.
Worked example. A carrier test shows chance of passing on a condition, not that a child will have it.

Activity. Compare three test types in a table.

Standard to meet. Table states purpose and limit.

Module 3 From sample to result 2 hAnalyse

Many steps can go wrong.

  • Collect, label, store, extract, read, report.
  • Chain of custody records who held the sample.
  • Contamination and labelling errors cause false results.
Worked example. A mislabelled tube swaps two results; a barcode check prevents it.

Activity. Map the steps and risks.

Standard to meet. Every step has a quality check.

Module 4 Reading a result 2 hAnalyse

Results need context.

  • Percent and risk numbers explain chance, not certainty.
  • Variants of uncertain meaning are common.
  • Only a genetic counsellor or clinician explains health results.
Worked example. A report says a 1.5 times higher chance; absolute risk goes from 2 to 3 in 100.

Activity. Explain a sample number to a peer.

Standard to meet. Peer repeats it correctly.

Module 5 Marketing and overclaims 2 hEvaluate

Be careful with big promises.

  • Beware "find your perfect diet by DNA" or "know your talent".
  • Look for evidence, regulation and independent reviews.
  • Price does not prove quality.
Worked example. An ad claims a diet plan from DNA; no study is shown. The learner marks it as unproven.

Activity. Review two adverts.

Standard to meet. Overclaims named with reasons.

Module 6 Privacy and family impact 2 hEvaluate

Your DNA reveals things about relatives.

  • Data may be sold, hacked or used by insurers in some places.
  • Findings can surprise families, such as unknown relatives.
  • Consent must be informed and free.
Worked example. A family agrees before testing that no one will be told surprises without a counsellor.

Activity. Write a consent checklist.

Standard to meet. Eight clear questions.

Module 7 Ethics and fairness 2 hEvaluate

Genetic data must not be used to discriminate.

  • No test should be forced by employers or schools.
  • Respect culture and faith views.
  • Data from some groups is underrepresented, so results can be less accurate.
Worked example. A study notes fewer data from certain populations and urges wider sampling.

Activity. Discuss two cases with peers.

Standard to meet. Peers reach a reasoned view.

Module 8 Write the explainer 2 hCreate

Combine all parts.

  • Use short sentences.
  • Add a glossary and a where-to-get-help box.
  • Test with a non-expert.
Worked example. A reader says, "Now I know to ask a counsellor, not a website."

Activity. Finish and test.

Standard to meet. Reader can state three limits of DNA tests.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: explainer, risk list, consent checklist. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is DNA?

Model answer: The instruction code in cells.

Q2. Is a result destiny?

Model answer: No; it is a chance.

Q3. Name two test kinds.

Model answer: Ancestry and carrier (or health risk, paternity).

Q4. Why chain of custody?

Model answer: To show who held the sample.

Q5. Who explains health results?

Model answer: A genetic counsellor or clinician.

Q6. Name one marketing red flag.

Model answer: A big promise with no evidence.

Q7. Why does DNA affect relatives?

Model answer: Relatives share genes.

Q8. Name a data risk.

Model answer: Sale, hacking or misuse by insurers.

Q9. Can employers force tests?

Model answer: They should not.

Q10. Why is diverse data needed?

Model answer: Results are less accurate for under-represented groups.

ten locations and global

Your place

Genetic testing and data laws differ by place; some tests need a clinician.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
