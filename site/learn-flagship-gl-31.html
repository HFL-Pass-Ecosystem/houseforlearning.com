flagship course · GL-31

Affiliate Marketing

Earn by recommending products honestly: pick a niche, build a trustworthy page or channel, disclose links, and track clicks, sales and commission.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Earn by recommending products honestly: pick a niche, build a trustworthy page or channel, disclose links, and track clicks, sales and commission.

  • Understand Explain how affiliate programmes pay and what rules apply.
  • Apply Choose a niche and programmes with fair terms.
  • Analyse Analyse clicks, conversion and earnings per click.
  • Create Create useful content with clear disclosure.
  • Evaluate Evaluate whether the effort pays and the audience is served.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Build a small recommendation page or channel with at least 10 useful items, honest disclosure and a 4-week tracking sheet.

Condition

A free page or channel, programme sign-ups, a link tracker, a spreadsheet, a mentor with marketing experience and a peer circle. About 8 to 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

Disclosure visible next to every link; only products you have used or properly researched; each recommendation includes pros, cons and who it suits; tracking sheet complete; no false claims.

Who it is for: Creators, bloggers, small-business owners, students, returners. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Disclose affiliate links clearly. Follow advertising and programme rules. Never fake reviews or promise results. Do not recommend unsafe or misleading products.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 How affiliate works 4 hUnderstand

You recommend; a seller pays you if a buyer uses your link. You will be able to explain how commission links work and judge programme terms before joining, protecting your time and reputation.

  • Link, cookie (a small tracker that credits your click), commission percent or fixed amount.
  • Approval periods and the minimum amount before payout.
  • Programme rules on claims and coupons.
  • Trust is your asset.
  • Remember that you earn a commission only when a reader buys through your link, so a small trusted audience can outperform a large indifferent one.
  • Read each programme's terms for tracking-cookie length, payment threshold and banned methods before you join, and keep a copy of the terms.
  • Write down what you would pay for yourself; start with products you already know.
  • Read the terms for banned claims such as 'cure' or 'guaranteed'.
Worked example. Commission 8% on a 50.00 sale = 4.00, paid after a 30-day return window (sale amounts and rates are estimates; check your market).

Activity. Read the terms of three programmes.

Standard to meet. Summary compares rates, windows, thresholds.

Common mistake. Joining every programme.

Second worked example. A reader wants a pan for eggs. Your page compares three pans, 24 cm, with a test of 10 eggs each: pan A stuck twice, pan B once, pan C never. You link pan C and say pan A is cheaper but sticks.

Check yourself. Which programmes did you pick?

Module 2 Pick a niche 6 hUnderstand

Serve a specific audience. You will be able to test whether a niche has enough buyer questions and fair-paying products to sustain fifty articles.

  • Interest + knowledge + buyers.
  • Check competing pages and gaps.
  • Pick products with real quality.
  • Define who you help.
  • Choose a niche you could write about across 50 articles, check that people search for it, and confirm that related products pay a reasonable commission.
  • Test the idea by listing twenty questions buyers ask in this niche; if you cannot find twenty, the niche may be too narrow.
  • Use a spreadsheet to compare three programmes on rate, window, threshold and payment date.
  • Keep a list of products you tested and the dates, so claims can be proved.
Worked example. Niche: "Low-cost kitchen gear for first-time cooks".

Activity. Write niche statement and audience.

Standard to meet. Statement names the person and problem.

Common mistake. A niche too wide.

Second worked example. Niche test: 'budget camping gear' is too wide; 'first-time family camping, under 150 total' names the reader, the budget and the problem.

Check yourself. Who is your reader?

Module 3 Choose products and partners 8 hApply

Recommend only what you would use. You will be able to compare alternatives honestly and drop partners with poor refunds, weak support or late payments.

  • Test or research each item.
  • Check returns, support, reviews.
  • Prefer fair commissions and transparent terms.
  • Avoid anything unsafe or misleading.
  • Recommend only products you have tried or researched thoroughly, and compare at least three alternatives so your advice is fair to the reader.
  • Check each seller's refund rate, support quality and reputation, and drop partners who pay late or are hard to contact.
  • Set a rule: no product is listed without at least one real use or a documented second source.
  • Keep sponsored and affiliate content labelled differently so readers can tell them apart.
Worked example. A cheap knife set fails your test; do not list it even though commission is high.

Activity. List 10 products with notes.

Standard to meet. Each has evidence of use or research.

Common mistake. Recommending by commission.

Second worked example. You tested a kettle for 4 weeks, noting boil time (3 min 10 s), noise and a cracked lid in week 3. The honest review lists the lid fault even though the commission is 8%.

Check yourself. What did you reject?

Module 4 Create useful content 8 hApply

Help first; link second. You will be able to write single-question guides with a summary, balanced pros and cons, and dated updates readers can trust.

  • Comparison tables; how-to guides; checklists.
  • Pros, cons and who it suits.
  • Pictures of your own use.
  • Update when prices change.
  • Write guides that each answer one clear question, with a short summary at the top, honest pros and cons, and a plain recommendation.
  • Update each article every six months to check prices, links and facts, and show the update date so readers know it is current.
  • Re-check links monthly; dead links lose trust and income.
  • Disclose changes of programme in a short note when you move links.
Worked example. A guide titled "Which pan for eggs?" compares three pans with a test result.

Activity. Write three pieces.

Standard to meet. Each helps even without buying.

Common mistake. Thin pages.

Second worked example. Your page ranks four toasters in a table with price, slots, test result and who it suits; readers can pick without clicking any link.

Check yourself. What is your test?

Module 5 Disclosure and honesty 8 hAnalyse

Say it clearly. You will be able to place plain-language disclosures near every link and avoid claims or fake reviews that break advertising rules.

  • "I earn a commission if you buy through this link".
  • Place it near the link and at top.
  • No fake reviews or hidden claims.
  • Follow the advertising rules for your place.
  • Place a statement in plain words near each link saying you may earn a commission, visible before the reader clicks rather than buried in a footer.
  • Never claim results you have not seen, never write fake reviews, and follow the advertising rules of your country and of each platform you use.
  • Offer a free checklist for readers who sign up with consent, instead of pushing sales.
  • Respond to every comment within two days, including criticism.
Worked example. Disclosure is the first line of the page, not in the footer.

Activity. Add disclosures and check each page.

Standard to meet. Every link has nearby notice.

Common mistake. Burying disclosure.

Second worked example. The first line reads: 'I earn a small commission if you buy through links on this page, at no extra cost to you.' Every link sits within three lines of a repeat notice.

Check yourself. Where is yours?

Module 6 Traffic the honest way 10 hCreate

Earn attention without tricks. You will be able to grow readership through useful articles and an email list, while avoiding tricks that risk account bans.

  • Search: write what people ask.
  • Social: share useful posts.
  • Email: with consent.
  • No spam or bought traffic.
  • Build traffic with useful articles, an email list and honest social posts, aiming for steady growth such as 10 percent more visitors each month.
  • Avoid spam, hidden links and paid fake engagement, since these can get your account banned and destroy trust that took months to earn.
  • Use clear photos from your own testing rather than stock pictures.
  • Track clicks by page so you know which advice is read.
Worked example. A short video answering a common question brings 120 visits.

Activity. Share content in two places.

Standard to meet. Visits tracked.

Common mistake. Chasing quick traffic.

Second worked example. A short video answering 'which pan for eggs?' brings 120 visits in a week; a keyword-stuffed page brings 9.

Check yourself. Where do readers come from?

Module 7 Track and calculate 6 hEvaluate

Know what pays. You will be able to track clicks and sales per article and calculate real profit after hosting, tools and advertising costs.

  • Clicks, orders, commission.
  • Conversion = orders / clicks.
  • Earnings per click = commission / clicks.
  • Drop what does not work.
  • Use a unique tracking link for each article, record clicks, sales and earnings in a spreadsheet, and then work out your earnings per click.
  • Calculate profit by subtracting hosting, tools and advertising costs from commission earned, and choose the three articles that deserve more effort next month.
  • Delete pages that no one reads after three months.
  • Share one lesson from each month in your own words.
Worked example. 400 clicks, 12 orders = 3%; commission 48; earnings per click 0.12.

Activity. Track four weeks.

Standard to meet. Formulas correct.

Common mistake. Not tracking.

Second worked example. Out of 250 clicks, 5 became orders (2%) and paid 30; that is 0.12 a click. The post earning 0.02 a click is rewritten.

Check yourself. What are your clicks?

Module 8 Review and plan 4 hCreate

Decide whether to continue. You will be able to review page performance each quarter and decide, with a written plan, whether to continue, adjust or stop.

  • Hours spent vs money earned.
  • Audience feedback.
  • Fix weak pages.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Every quarter, review your best and worst pages, retire the weak ones, and set a target such as two new guides and one update each month.
  • Write a one-page plan with goals, schedule and budget, and review it with a trusted friend who will tell you honestly which parts are unclear.
  • Write the next month's plan with three pages to improve and one to build.
  • Ask two readers if the disclosure and the advice felt fair, and apply their comments.
Worked example. 20 hours for 48 earned is 2.40 per hour: improve content before adding more.

Activity. Write the review.

Standard to meet. Review uses time and money.

Common mistake. Counting only money.

Second worked example. 12 hours for 36 earned is 3 an hour; the plan is to update the two posts that bring most clicks before writing a new one.

Check yourself. What is your hourly return?

case cards and glossary

Case 1. The generous offer

A seller offers a high commission for a doubtful product.

Question: What do you do?

Model answer: Test it; if it fails, decline and say why.

Case 2. The missing disclosure

A reader notes you did not disclose a link.

Question: Response?

Model answer: Add it at once, apologise and review all pages.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is a cookie window?

Model answer: The time after a click during which a sale earns commission. (Covered in module 1: How affiliate works. To pass that module: Summary compares rates, windows, thresholds.)

Q2. Why disclose?

Model answer: It is honest and often required. (Covered in module 5: Disclosure and honesty. To pass that module: Every link has nearby notice.)

Q3. Where put disclosure?

Model answer: Near the link and at the top. (Covered in module 5: Disclosure and honesty. To pass that module: Every link has nearby notice.)

Q4. Conversion rate formula?

Model answer: Orders divided by clicks. (Covered in module 7: Track and calculate. To pass that module: Formulas correct.)

Q5. Earnings per click?

Model answer: Commission divided by clicks. (Covered in module 7: Track and calculate. To pass that module: Formulas correct.)

Q6. Can you recommend untested items?

Model answer: Only after proper research, and say so. (Covered in module 3: Choose products and partners. To pass that module: Each has evidence of use or research.)

Q7. What builds trust?

Model answer: Honest pros and cons and real tests. (Covered in module 4: Create useful content. To pass that module: Each helps even without buying.)

Q8. Why avoid fake reviews?

Model answer: They harm buyers and break rules. (Covered in module 5: Disclosure and honesty. To pass that module: Every link has nearby notice.)

Q9. Why compare terms?

Model answer: Rates and rules differ. (Covered in module 1: How affiliate works. To pass that module: Summary compares rates, windows, thresholds.)

Q10. What do you do with weak pages?

Model answer: Improve or remove them. (Covered in module 8: Review and plan. To pass that module: Review uses time and money.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Disclosure and advertising law vary: follow local rules and the programme terms. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Partner to practise with: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Specialist

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Advanced

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Advanced
