Artificial Intelligence and Data Science

Learn artificial intelligence and data science for the UAE: rules, workflow, costing in AED and a recorded 30-day practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 178 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Understand Explain the UAE market, rules and hazards for artificial intelligence and data science.
  • Apply Run the workflow: define the question, clean data, build and test, explain, monitor.
  • Analyse Measure model accuracy on unseen data and find the biggest cause of loss.
  • Evaluate Cost and price in AED and judge scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a record pack a supervisor can audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a real or simulated 30-day pilot for data analysis and small AI tools for a real question in the UAE and keep a full record of every step, cost and result.

Condition

About 178 hours over 3 to 6 months. A partner site or a simulated customer, a free spreadsheet, a phone, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Hazards named with controls; every number has a unit and AED; records complete; rules confirmed with the local authority; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better.

Process learning only. Main risk to manage: bias, privacy breaches and wrong confidence. Follow UAE federal and emirate rules and any licence needed before you charge a customer. Use only data you may lawfully use; explain results in plain words.

Requirements: none. Currency: AED on every number.

lesson by lesson

11 modules with worked examples (178 h guided)

Module 1 Map the market and the people 5 hUnderstand

Start with who needs data analysis and small AI tools for a real question and why. Around firms, public bodies and start-ups drive demand in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other emirates.

  • List 10 offers or suppliers in the UAE with price per analysis in AED and who buys.
  • Interview 5 people from firms, public bodies and start-ups: what do they buy now, how often, what do they pay, what annoys them?
  • Learn three terms: Training and test split (keeping some data hidden to test honestly); Bias (unfair error that falls harder on one group); Data minimisation (collecting only the data you truly need).
  • Write a one-page brief: user, need, hoped-for price, and the rule that matters most.
Worked example. Five interviews all say delay is the main pain. You write: 'Reliable data analysis and small AI tools for a real question, answer in one day, price near AED 9,000 per analysis.'

Activity. Complete the market table, five interview notes and the brief.

Standard to meet. Ten offers priced; five users heard; one need stated in one sentence.

Module 2 Rules, hazards and licences 6 hApply

Know the rule before the work. Main hazards here: bias, privacy breaches and wrong confidence. Hazard thinking means list each step, ask what could go wrong, and decide the control.

  • Ask your local authority or free zone which licence, permit or inspection applies. Write the answer and the date you asked.
  • For each step (define the question; clean data; build and test; explain; monitor) write the hazard and the control.
  • Use a hierarchy: remove the hazard, replace it, separate people from it, change the way of work, then protective equipment last.
  • Keep a one-page incident rule: who you call, what you stop, what you record.
Worked example. Step 'define the question': hazard = bias, privacy breaches; control = written check before start and a named supervisor; record = signed log line.

Activity. Draw the flow and mark hazard and control at each step.

Standard to meet. Every step has a hazard and a control; licence question answered in writing.

Module 3 Core workflow, step by step 12 hApply

The work is a short chain: define the question, clean data, build and test, explain, monitor. Learn each link, then link them with records.

  • Write a checklist for each of the 5 steps: input, action, check, output.
  • Time each step three times and note the slowest.
  • Record a defect or delay each time it happens: what, where, cause, fix.
  • Measure model accuracy on unseen data; this is your main number.
Worked example. Three timed runs of 'clean data' take 42, 38 and 51 minutes. Average 43.7; the 51-minute run had a missing tool. Fix: a tool tray checked at start.

Activity. Run the chain three times (simulated or real) and fill the log.

Standard to meet. Three complete logs; the slowest step has a cause and a fix.

Module 4 Quality and measurement 10 hAnalyse

What you do not measure you cannot improve. Your main measure is model accuracy on unseen data.

  • Define model accuracy on unseen data: what counts, what does not, and over what period.
  • Draw a simple chart of the last 10 results and mark the average.
  • Find the top two causes of loss with a tally sheet, then fix the biggest one first.
  • Check measuring tools: are they accurate, clean and in date?
Worked example. Tally of 40 faults: 22 from late inputs, 9 from wrong settings, 9 other. Fix late inputs first: that is 55% of faults.

Activity. Collect a tally for one week and make a Pareto chart.

Standard to meet. Tally complete; biggest cause named; one fix planned with a date.

Module 5 Costing and pricing in AED 12 hEvaluate

Know the cost of one analysis before you set the price. Include materials, labour, energy, transport, licence share, waste and a safety margin.

  • Cost per analysis = total cost divided by analyses sold (not made).
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs divided by gain per unit.
  • Test the price with two users and write down the answer.
Worked example. At AED 9,000 per analysis and cost AED 3,600, gain is AED 5,400 and margin 60.0%. At 12 analyses a month, gross gain is AED 64,800. If fixed costs are AED 8,640, break-even is 2 analyses.

Activity. Build the cost sheet with AED on every line and have your mentor check it.

Standard to meet. No missing cost found; margin calculated twice by different routes.

Module 6 Serve and sell responsibly 12 hApply

Customers return when you keep promises. Promise only what your workflow can deliver.

  • Write a one-page offer: what is included, what is not, price, time, how you fix mistakes.
  • Write a short script for the first call or visit.
  • Agree dates in writing; confirm each change.
  • Handle a complaint in four steps: listen, apologise for the impact, fix, record what changed.
Worked example. Offer: 'Data analysis and small ai tools for a real question delivered within one working day; price AED 9,000 per analysis; faults corrected free within 3 days.'

Activity. Present the offer to two users and note changes.

Standard to meet. Offer has scope, price, time and fix rule; two users commented.

Module 7 Run the 30-day pilot 55 hCreate

Small, real and recorded. Change one thing at a time and learn quickly.

  • Keep a daily log: date, units, cost, delays, defects, safety notes.
  • Hold a 15-minute review each week with your mentor.
  • Write down every complaint and what you changed.
  • Take photos only where allowed and never of people without consent.
Worked example. Week 2 shows model accuracy on unseen data at 71%; week 4 at 84% after the tool tray and an earlier input check.

Activity. Complete 30 days of the log and four weekly reviews.

Standard to meet. 30 days logged; each defect has a cause; weekly notes kept.

Module 8 Review, decide and plan 38 hEvaluate

A decision beats a feeling. Compare plan with result and choose to scale, change or stop.

  • Make a before and after table: cost per unit, defects, delays, repeat users.
  • Write three lessons.
  • Decide scale, change or stop, with the numbers behind it.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
Worked example. Result: cost per analysis fell from AED 3,600 to AED 3,384; repeat users 6 of 9. Decision: scale to a second site; keep price.

Activity. Present to a review room and collect one written comment from a user.

Standard to meet. Decision backed by numbers; two peer reviews given.

Module 9 Test a model honestly on unseen data 10 hApply

A model that scores well on the data it learned from may fail on new data. An honest test keeps some records hidden until the end.

  • Split the records before you start: for example 70% to train, 15% to tune and 15% to test. Never look at the test part while building.
  • Pick a measure that fits the question: accuracy for balanced groups, but precision (how many flagged cases were truly positive) and recall (how many true positives were found) when one outcome is rare.
  • Compare the model with a simple baseline, such as always predicting the most common answer. A model that cannot beat the baseline is not useful.
  • Write down the split, the measure and the result before you change anything, so you cannot fool yourself later.
Worked example. A set of 2,000 customer records is split 1,400 / 300 / 300. The model gets 288 of the 300 test cases right: 288 / 300 = 96.0%. But 95% of the 300 test customers (285 of them) never cancel, so the baseline of always saying 'stays' already scores 95.0%. The gain is only 1.0 percentage point, so the model adds little.

Activity. Take a sample table of at least 500 rows from your mentor, split it, run a simple model and a baseline, and report both scores.

Standard to meet. Split sizes written down; baseline score shown next to the model score; test part untouched until the final run.

Module 10 Bias, privacy and rules for using data 9 hEvaluate

Data about people can harm them if it is unfair, leaked or used for a purpose they never agreed to. Checking this is part of the job, not an extra.

  • Check error rates for each group, such as age band or nationality group. If one group is wrong far more often, the model is unfair even when the overall score is high.
  • Collect only the fields you need. Remove names and ID numbers before analysis where you can, and store the key to re-link them somewhere separate.
  • Know the purpose: data collected for one reason should not be reused for another without permission and a lawful basis.
  • Keep a short record of where the data came from, who may see it and when it will be deleted.
Worked example. A loan-screening model is wrong 6% of the time for group A (30 errors in 500 cases) but 15% for group B (75 errors in 500 cases). The gap is 9 percentage points, so the model must be reviewed before use, even though the overall error is 10.5% (105 in 1,000 cases).

Activity. Calculate error rate per group from a sample results table and write a half-page note: fair enough to use, or not, and why.

Standard to meet. Error rate shown for every group; data fields justified one by one; clear recommendation with reasons.

Module 11 Explain results and keep watching the model 9 hEvaluate

A good result is only useful if the decision-maker understands it and if it keeps working after launch. Data changes, and models slowly go stale.

  • Lead with the answer in one sentence, then give the number, then the limits. Avoid jargon; say 'wrong about 1 time in 10' instead of a technical score.
  • Show uncertainty: give a range, not a single figure, and say how many records it rests on.
  • Monitor after launch: track the live error rate each week against the test result and set a trigger, for example retrain if it drops more than 5 percentage points.
  • Keep a log of model versions, data dates and who approved each change.
Worked example. At launch the model was right 92% of the time. In week 6, a check of 400 live cases finds 352 right: 352 / 400 = 88.0%. The drop is 4 percentage points, close to the 5-point trigger, so the team schedules a review of the new data.

Activity. Write a one-page summary for a non-technical manager using the results from module 1, with one chart and one monitoring rule.

Standard to meet. Answer in the first sentence; range or limit stated; a numeric monitoring trigger set.

Slide by slide, with notes

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: What data analysis is for

On the slide: L1 Basic · What data analysis is for

Notes: Name the five steps of a data job (question, clean, build and test, explain, monitor) and give one real question a firm could ask. Notes: use a small table of ten rows; learners point to one row and one column.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Bias, privacy and lawful data

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Bias, privacy and lawful data

Notes: Explain what bias is, why data is minimised and why only lawful data is used. Notes: show one field that is not needed and remove it; teach that data collected for one purpose is not reused for another without permission.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Split data and test honestly

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Split data and test honestly

Notes: Apply a split of 70% train, 15% tune and 15% test and compare the model with a baseline. Notes: check the practice case of 2,000 records split 1,400, 300 and 300; 288 of 300 right is 96.0% but the baseline of 95.0% leaves a gain of only 1.0 point.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Run a full analysis and explain it

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Run a full analysis and explain it

Notes: Run the whole workflow on a sample table of at least 500 rows and write a summary for a manager. Notes: answer in the first sentence, give a range, state how many records it rests on and set a numeric monitoring trigger.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Test fairness across groups

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Test fairness across groups

Notes: Analyse error rates for each group and decide whether a model is fair enough to use. Notes: 30 errors in 500 for group A is 6% and 75 in 500 for group B is 15%, a gap of 9 points, so review before use; price check is AED 9,000 per analysis against AED 3,600 cost, a margin of 60.0%.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Coach a team on model checks

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Coach a team on model checks

Notes: Set a baseline rule, a fairness check and a weekly drift review for a small analyst team. Notes: retrain if live accuracy drops more than 5 points; sample five analyses a week for split sizes written down and the test part untouched.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Design the whole data and AI system

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Design the whole data and AI system

Notes: Design the data rules, testing, fairness, monitoring and approval log for several analyses, audit privacy risks and write a 12-month plan. Notes: plan for 12 analyses a month; present one risk, one saving and quarterly targets for accuracy on unseen data.

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.
