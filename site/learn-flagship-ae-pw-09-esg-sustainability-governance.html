ESG and Sustainability Governance

Learn esg and sustainability governance for the UAE: rules, workflow, costing in AED and a recorded 30-day practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 178 hPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Understand Explain the UAE market, rules and hazards for esg and sustainability governance.
  • Apply Run the workflow: set the baseline, choose measures, collect evidence, report, improve.
  • Analyse Measure reported measures backed by evidence and find the biggest cause of loss.
  • Evaluate Cost and price in AED and judge scale, change or stop.
  • Create Produce a record pack a supervisor can audit.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a real or simulated 30-day pilot for environmental, social and governance reporting and control in the UAE and keep a full record of every step, cost and result.

Condition

About 178 hours over 3 to 6 months. A partner site or a simulated customer, a free spreadsheet, a phone, a mentor and a peer circle.

Standard

Hazards named with controls; every number has a unit and AED; records complete; rules confirmed with the local authority; at least 70% on the check; rubric at Meets or better.

Process learning only. Main risk to manage: greenwashing and weak evidence. Follow UAE federal and emirate rules and any licence needed before you charge a customer. Never claim more than the evidence shows.

Requirements: none. Currency: AED on every number.

lesson by lesson

11 modules with worked examples (178 h guided)

Module 1 Map the market and the people 5 hUnderstand

Start with who needs environmental, social and governance reporting and control and why. Around companies, lenders and public bodies drive demand in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other emirates.

  • List 10 offers or suppliers in the UAE with price per report in AED and who buys.
  • Interview 5 people from companies, lenders and public bodies: what do they buy now, how often, what do they pay, what annoys them?
  • Learn three terms: Materiality (the issues that matter most to the firm and its stakeholders); Scope 1 and 2 (direct emissions and emissions from bought energy); Assurance (an independent check of a report).
  • Write a one-page brief: user, need, hoped-for price, and the rule that matters most.
Worked example. Five interviews all say delay is the main pain. You write: 'Reliable environmental, social and governance reporting and control, answer in one day, price near AED 14,000 per report.'

Activity. Complete the market table, five interview notes and the brief.

Standard to meet. Price ten offers, interview five users, and state one need in one sentence.

Module 2 Rules, hazards and licences 6 hApply

Know the rule before the work. Main hazards here: greenwashing and weak evidence. Hazard thinking means list each step, ask what could go wrong, and decide the control.

  • Ask your local authority or free zone which licence, permit or inspection applies. Write the answer and the date you asked.
  • For each step (set the baseline; choose measures; collect evidence; report; improve) write the hazard and the control.
  • Use a hierarchy: remove the hazard, replace it, separate people from it, change the way of work, then protective equipment last.
  • Keep a one-page incident rule: who you call, what you stop, what you record.
Worked example. Step 'set the baseline': hazard = greenwashing; control = written check before start and a named supervisor; record = signed log line.

Activity. Draw the flow and mark hazard and control at each step.

Standard to meet. Name a hazard and a control for every step, and answer the licence question in writing.

Module 3 Core workflow, step by step 12 hApply

The work is a short chain: set the baseline, choose measures, collect evidence, report, improve. Learn each link, then link them with records.

  • Write a checklist for each of the 5 steps: input, action, check, output.
  • Time each step three times and note the slowest.
  • Record a defect or delay each time it happens: what, where, cause, fix.
  • Measure reported measures backed by evidence; this is your main number.
Worked example. Three timed runs of 'choose measures' take 42, 38 and 51 minutes. Average 43.7; the 51-minute run had a missing tool. Fix: a tool tray checked at start.

Activity. Run the chain three times (simulated or real) and fill the log.

Standard to meet. Complete three logs, and give the slowest step a cause and a fix.

Module 4 Quality and measurement 10 hAnalyse

What you do not measure you cannot improve. Your main measure is reported measures backed by evidence.

  • Define reported measures backed by evidence: what counts, what does not, and over what period.
  • Draw a simple chart of the last 10 results and mark the average.
  • Find the top two causes of loss with a tally sheet, then fix the biggest one first.
  • Check measuring tools: are they accurate, clean and in date?
Worked example. Tally of 40 faults: 22 from late inputs, 9 from wrong settings, 9 other. Fix late inputs first: that is 55% of faults.

Activity. Collect a tally for one week and make a Pareto chart.

Standard to meet. Complete the tally, name the biggest cause, and plan one fix with a date.

Module 5 Costing and pricing in AED 12 hEvaluate

Know the cost of one report before you set the price. Include materials, labour, energy, transport, licence share, waste and a safety margin.

  • Cost per report = total cost divided by reports sold (not made).
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs divided by gain per unit.
  • Test the price with two users and write down the answer.
Worked example. At AED 14,000 per report and cost AED 6,000, gain is AED 8,000 and margin 57.1%. At 10 reports a month, gross gain is AED 80,000. If fixed costs are AED 12,000, break-even is 2 reports.

Activity. Build the cost sheet with AED on every line and have your mentor check it.

Standard to meet. Check that no cost is missing, and calculate the margin twice by different routes.

Module 6 Serve and sell responsibly 12 hApply

Customers return when you keep promises. Promise only what your workflow can deliver.

  • Write a one-page offer: what is included, what is not, price, time, how you fix mistakes.
  • Write a short script for the first call or visit.
  • Agree dates in writing; confirm each change.
  • Handle a complaint in four steps: listen, apologise for the impact, fix, record what changed.
Worked example. Offer: 'Environmental, social and governance reporting and control delivered within one working day; price AED 14,000 per report; faults corrected free within 3 days.'

Activity. Present the offer to two users and note changes.

Standard to meet. Write an offer with scope, price, time and a fix rule, and collect comments from two users.

Module 7 Run the 30-day pilot 55 hCreate

Small, real and recorded. Change one thing at a time and learn quickly.

  • Keep a daily log: date, units, cost, delays, defects, safety notes.
  • Hold a 15-minute review each week with your mentor.
  • Write down every complaint and what you changed.
  • Take photos only where allowed and never of people without consent.
Worked example. Week 2 shows reported measures backed by evidence at 71%; week 4 at 84% after the tool tray and an earlier input check.

Activity. Complete 30 days of the log and four weekly reviews.

Standard to meet. Log 30 days, give each defect a cause, and keep weekly notes.

Module 8 Review, decide and plan 38 hEvaluate

A decision beats a feeling. Compare plan with result and choose to scale, change or stop.

  • Make a before and after table: cost per unit, defects, delays, repeat users.
  • Write three lessons.
  • Decide scale, change or stop, with the numbers behind it.
  • Give two peers one strength and one fix each.
Worked example. Result: cost per report fell from AED 6,000 to AED 5,640; repeat users 6 of 9. Decision: scale to a second site; keep price.

Activity. Present to a review room and collect one written comment from a user.

Standard to meet. Justify the decision with numbers, and give two peer reviews.

Module 9 Measure emissions: baseline and calculation 10 hApply

You cannot cut what you have not counted. A baseline gives the starting number against which later progress is compared.

  • Scope 1 is direct emissions, such as fuel burned in company vehicles. Scope 2 is emissions from bought electricity.
  • Method: activity amount x emission factor = emissions. Use a published factor and write down its source and year. CO2e means carbon dioxide equivalent, one unit that covers all greenhouse gases; kWh means kilowatt-hour, the unit of electricity use.
  • Choose a baseline year with complete records and keep the method the same each year so results can be compared.
  • Record the data source for every figure: invoice, meter reading or fuel card. Estimates are marked as estimates.
Worked example. An office uses 120,000 kWh of electricity in a year. With a factor of 0.40 kg of CO2e per kWh: 120,000 x 0.40 = 48,000 kg = 48.0 tonnes CO2e. Fleet fuel is 9,000 litres at 2.3 kg per litre (a petrol-type factor; use the factor for your own fuel, diesel is higher) = 20,700 kg = 20.7 tonnes. Total = 68.7 tonnes CO2e.

Activity. Compute a baseline for a sample site from a table of utility and fuel data and list the source of each number.

Standard to meet. State the factor, source and year; give units on every line; mark estimates clearly.

Module 10 Materiality, greenwashing and assurance 9 hEvaluate

Reports lose trust when they exaggerate. Choosing the right issues and backing every claim with evidence keeps a report honest.

  • Materiality means focusing on issues that matter most to the firm and the people it affects. Ask staff, customers, lenders and neighbours, then rank the issues.
  • Greenwashing is making a claim the evidence does not support, such as calling a product green without data. Test each claim: what, how much, compared with what, and where is the proof?
  • Assurance is an independent check of the report. Limited assurance gives a lower level of comfort than reasonable assurance.
  • Report bad news too. Leaving out targets that were missed is itself misleading.
Worked example. A claim says 'we cut emissions by half'. The records show 80.0 tonnes in the baseline year and 66.0 tonnes now. Cut = 14.0 tonnes = 17.5%, not 50%. The wording must change to 'a 17.5% reduction against the baseline year'.

Activity. Check five sample claims from a draft report against the data table and rewrite each one so it matches the evidence.

Standard to meet. Support every claim with a figure, a comparison and a source; remove or reword unsupported claims.

Module 11 Targets, governance and progress review 9 hAnalyse

A target only works if someone owns it, a date is set and progress is checked on a schedule.

  • Set targets as a number, a unit, a baseline and a date, for example a 20% cut by year 5.
  • Name an owner for each target and a board or committee that reviews progress at least twice a year.
  • Compare actual with the straight-line path to the target, and explain any gap.
  • Update the plan when evidence shows a measure is not working; record what changed and why.
Worked example. Baseline 68.7 tonnes CO2e. Target: 20% cut in 4 years = 13.74 tonnes, so 54.96 tonnes. The straight-line path is 3.435 tonnes per year, so after year 2 the path value is 61.83 tonnes. Actual is 64.0 tonnes, which is 2.17 tonnes behind the path, so the committee asks for extra measures.

Activity. Draw a target path for a sample site, plot two years of actual figures and write a short note on the gap.

Standard to meet. State the target with baseline and date, calculate the gap in the same unit, and name the owner by role, not by person.

Q3. Gain per report if price is AED 14,000 and cost is AED 6,000?

Model answer: AED 8,000. (Covered in module 5: Costing and pricing in AED. To pass that module: Check that no cost is missing, and calculate the margin twice by different routes.)

Q4. Margin for the same figures?

Model answer: 57.1%. (Covered in module 5: Costing and pricing in AED. To pass that module: Check that no cost is missing, and calculate the margin twice by different routes.)

Q5. Name the steps of the workflow.

Model answer: Set the baseline; choose measures; collect evidence; report; improve. (Covered in module 3: Core workflow, step by step. To pass that module: Complete three logs, and give the slowest step a cause and a fix.)

Q6. What is Materiality?

Model answer: The issues that matter most to the firm and its stakeholders. (Covered in module 1: Map the market and the people. To pass that module: Price ten offers, interview five users, and state one need in one sentence.)

Q7. What is Scope 1 and 2?

Model answer: Direct emissions and emissions from bought energy. (Covered in module 1: Map the market and the people. To pass that module: Price ten offers, interview five users, and state one need in one sentence.)

Q8. What is Assurance?

Model answer: An independent check of a report. (Covered in module 1: Map the market and the people. To pass that module: Price ten offers, interview five users, and state one need in one sentence.)

Q9. Which control comes first in the hierarchy?

ten locations and global

Your place

Use your own currency, language and local rules. Check each rule with your local authority.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency INR; language English, Hindi, Kannada; set up legally: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; personal data: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; where to practise: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency EUR; language Dutch, English; set up legally: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK); personal data: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; where to practise: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency AED; language Arabic, English; set up legally: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence; personal data: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); where to practise: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency BRL; language Portuguese (Spanish and English support); set up legally: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs; personal data: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); where to practise: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency CAD; language English, French; set up legally: federal or Ontario business registration; personal data: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; where to practise: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency USD; language English, Spanish; set up legally: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID; personal data: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; where to practise: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency SGD; language English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; set up legally: registration with ACRA; personal data: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); where to practise: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency AUD; language English; set up legally: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration; personal data: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; where to practise: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency HKD; language Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; set up legally: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry; personal data: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; where to practise: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency GEL or ILS; language Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; set up legally: local business registration in the country where you work; personal data: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; where to practise: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency KYD; language English; set up legally: a trade and business licence or company registration; personal data: the Data Protection Act; where to practise: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency MXN; language Spanish, English; set up legally: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration; personal data: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; where to practise: a technical college or business chamber.
