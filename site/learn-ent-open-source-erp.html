enterprise software course

Open-Source All-in-One ERP: Setup to Daily Operations

Set up and run an open-source business suite for sales, stock, buying, accounting and websites.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Set up and run an open-source business suite for sales, stock, buying, accounting and websites. Finish all 8 modules on a practice set-up.

Condition

A free trial or practice account, practice data only (never real customer data), a peer circle and a mentor for the final review.

Standard

Every module standard is met; records are kept; a peer and a mentor rate the work 'Meets' or better on the rubric.

Who it is for: Small-business owners, accountants, operations staff, junior consultants. Requirements: a computer and internet. Materials: slides with notes, worked examples, activities, quiz, rubric.

How the ladder works: modules climb from L1 (remember) to L7 (create); the ladder section shows each level as a slide. Take them in order; the first three suit new learners, 4 and 5 suit practitioners, 6 and 7 suit advanced staff.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. A small shop wants to sell, track stock and keep accounts. Which apps would you install first and why?

Model answer: Sales, inventory and accounting, because they cover the daily work. Add other apps later only when there is a real need. (Covered in module 1: Apps and the dashboard. To pass that module: Three apps working; one user with limited rights.)

Q2. A shirt comes in three sizes and two colours. How should you set it up?

Model answer: Create one product with variants for size and colour, giving six combinations. This keeps one set of reports instead of six separate products. (Covered in module 2: Contacts, products and prices. To pass that module: No duplicates; tax set.)

Q3. You import 20 products and find 3 duplicates. What check should have been done before import?

Model answer: Compare names or codes with the existing list and clean the sheet first. Also check units and tax columns before loading. (Covered in module 2: Contacts, products and prices. To pass that module: No duplicates; tax set.)

Q4. A quote is confirmed. What usually follows in the sales flow?

Model answer: A delivery is made, an invoice is created from the order and then payment is registered. Each step is linked to the one before. (Covered in module 3: Sales to invoice. To pass that module: All invoiced and paid.)

Q5. An invoice has payment terms of Net 30. What does that mean?

Model answer: The customer should pay within 30 days of the invoice date. The due date on the invoice is set automatically from this. (Covered in module 3: Sales to invoice. To pass that module: All invoiced and paid.)

Q6. A product has a minimum of 10 and a maximum of 40 in its reorder rule. Stock falls to 8. What happens?

Model answer: A purchase request is created to bring stock up to the maximum, so about 32 units are suggested. The buyer reviews it before ordering. (Covered in module 4: Buying and stock. To pass that module: Counts match books.)

Q7. Twenty bank lines were imported and 18 matched. What do you do with the other two?

Model answer: Open each one and find the invoice or bill it belongs to, or create the missing entry. The target is zero unreconciled lines. (Covered in module 5: Accounting and bank matching. To pass that module: Zero unreconciled lines.)

Q8. Why should a web shop and a physical shop use the same stock?

Model answer: Both sales reduce the same quantity, so you do not sell items you no longer have. Reports also show true totals. (Covered in module 6: Website, shop and automation. To pass that module: Order flows to stock.)

Q9. Before upgrading the live system, what should you do?

Model answer: Take a backup and run the upgrade on a copy first. If the copy fails you still have the working live system and a way back. (Covered in module 7: Customisation, hosting and migration. To pass that module: Backup, test, rollback described.)

Q10. Compare the work in a capstone month with a go-live plan.

Model answer: A capstone month tests that you can run all daily work and close the books. A go-live plan prepares moving a real company, with backup, test, rollback and support. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: run a small company for a month. To pass that module: Month closed with zero unreconciled bank lines, stock matching the count, and a clear written explanation of each issue.)

levels L1 to L7

The ladder, slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: L1 Basic: know the apps

Remember the main apps and what each is for. Identify the company, the current user and the access rights. Recognise a customer, a product and an order on screen.

Speaker notes. Show the app list and ask learners which they need for a shop. Check they can find their own company name. A common mistake is installing every app at once.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: L2 Awareness: see the flow

Understand how a sale links to stock and to the invoice. Explain why clean product and customer data matters. Describe the idea of a chart of accounts and bank matching.

Speaker notes. Draw the flow with arrows from quote to payment. Ask where stock changes. Watch for learners who think each app keeps separate numbers.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: L3 Foundation: enter daily work

Apply the steps to create contacts, products, quotes, orders and invoices. Register a payment. Use filters and search to find a record.

Speaker notes. Let learners enter five orders. Check each is invoiced and paid. Common mistake: typing a new customer when one already exists.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: L4 Practitioner: run stock and buying

Apply reorder rules, purchase orders and stock counts. Analyse a stock difference and post an adjustment with a reason. Check the stock value against the books.

Speaker notes. Plant one missed receipt in the practice data. Ask learners to find it. Common mistake: forcing the count to agree without finding the cause.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: L5 Specialist: close the books

Analyse bank lines and match them to invoices and bills. Evaluate whether the tax report is correct. Fix wrong entries in a safe way.

Speaker notes. Give a bank file with two odd lines. Check learners explain each one. Common mistake: matching to the nearest amount instead of the true document.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: L6 Advanced: build and automate

Create a test shop page and simple automated actions that save time. Evaluate changes made with the studio tool against changes made with code. Keep every change in a test copy first.

Speaker notes. Ask learners to show a change on the copy and then on live. Check they can name the risk. Common mistake: editing the live system directly.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: L7 Super expert: lead the rollout

Create a go-live plan with migration, backups, rehearsal, rollback and support. Evaluate trade-offs for a real small company. Teach a team to run the system.

Speaker notes. Ask learners to present a plan to a peer who plays the owner. Check the backup and rollback steps. Common mistake: moving all data in one step with no rehearsal.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local law changes how you store customer data and issue invoices. Check it for your place first.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Use INR for amounts. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Use EUR for amounts. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Use AED for amounts. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Use BRL for amounts. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Use CAD for amounts. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Use USD for amounts. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Use SGD for amounts. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Use AUD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Use HKD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Use GEL or ILS for amounts. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Use KYD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Use MXN for amounts. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
