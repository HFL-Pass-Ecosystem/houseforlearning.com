Personal Wellness

Run a pilot of personal Wellness in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small personal Wellness offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: health, care, wellness and education. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the words of personal wellness 6 hUnderstand

Wellness means habits that support how you feel day to day. It is not medicine and it does not replace a doctor.

  • Learn the terms: habit, sleep, movement, nutrition, stress, goal, routine, coach.
  • Wellness has parts: body, mind, social and daily life.
  • A coach helps a person set and keep goals. A coach does not diagnose or treat illness.
  • Know where your role stops and a licensed health professional starts.
  • Give one example habit for each part of wellness: a walk, a quiet break, a call with a friend, a set meal time.
  • Compare two requests, 'help me keep a bedtime' and 'why do I get chest pain', and state which one you take and which you refer.
  • Explain in one plain sentence to a new client what you do and what you do not do.
Worked example. A client says she is tired. A coach asks about sleep, meals and routine, then says: 'If the tiredness lasts, please see your doctor.' If six of your ten words are coaching words and four are medical words, 6 + 4 = 10, so every word is sorted.

Activity. List ten wellness words with a plain meaning each and mark which ones are coaching and which are medical.

Standard to meet. Task: define ten wellness terms and mark the coaching line. Condition: from memory, then check. Standard: all meanings plain and correct, and the line between coaching and medical care stated.

Module 2 Explain how a wellness session and plan work 6 hApply

A session has a shape. A plan has small steps the person can repeat.

  • A session runs: welcome, check-in, one goal, one or two small steps, how to track, next time.
  • Start where the person is, not where you think they should be.
  • Small steps beat big promises: a ten-minute walk after lunch, a set bedtime, a glass of water with each meal.
  • Write the plan in the person's own words.
  • Open with a check-in question such as 'What went well since last time?' and listen before you advise.
  • Limit each plan to two steps so the person can succeed within a week.
  • End each session by writing the next date and one thing the person will do before it.
Worked example. Goal: 'feel less rushed in the morning.' Step one: prepare clothes three nights this week; step two: set out breakfast two nights. A 30-minute mock session can run 5 minutes welcome, 5 check-in, 10 goal, 5 steps and 5 tracking and next date: 5 + 5 + 10 + 5 + 5 = 30.

Activity. Run a mock 30-minute session with a friend and write a one-page plan.

Standard to meet. Task: run a mock session. Condition: 30 minutes with a friend. Standard: one goal, two small steps, a tracking method and a next date are written down.

Module 3 Coach habits to a clear standard 10 hApply

Now you apply what you know. Help a person pick a goal, track it and adjust.

  • Make goals specific: what, how often, by when.
  • Ask open questions: What would make this easier? What got in the way?
  • Track simply: a tick on a calendar is enough.
  • Praise effort and progress, not weight or looks. Never promise a result.
  • Apply the smallest-next-step test: if the person says 'maybe', shrink the step until the answer is 'yes'.
  • Describe what you do when a week goes badly: ask what got in the way, then change the step, not the person.
  • Evaluate progress every two weeks by counting the ticks on the calendar.
Worked example. Before: 'Get fit.' After: 'Walk 20 minutes on Monday, Wednesday and Friday for four weeks.' That is 3 walks of 20 minutes, 60 minutes a week, and 4 x 60 = 240 minutes in four weeks. If the calendar shows 9 ticks out of 12 planned walks, that is 9 / 12 = 75%, so you ask what got in the way of the other three.

Activity. Coach one volunteer for two weeks with a weekly check-in and a simple tracker.

Standard to meet. Task: coach one volunteer for two weeks. Condition: with consent and a written plan. Standard: goal is specific, tracker is used, no medical advice is given, and the volunteer says what helped.

Module 4 Follow Canadian rules and know your limits 8 hEvaluate

Health care is regulated in each province. Wellness coaching must stay on the right side of the line.

  • Titles such as doctor, nurse, dietitian, physiotherapist, psychotherapist and some others are protected in many provinces. Use only titles you hold; check the current rule with the local authority.
  • Do not diagnose, treat or promise to cure. Do not tell anyone to stop a medicine or treatment.
  • Advertising must be true. Do not claim miracle results.
  • Natural health products and supplements are regulated by the federal health agency; do not sell or advise on them unless you know the current rules and check them.
  • List five situations to refer on: chest pain, thoughts of self-harm, signs of an eating disorder, pregnancy questions and changes to medicine.
  • Describe your role with a plain title such as 'wellness coach' and check that no provincial rule protects any word you use.
  • Check the advertising rules before you post any claim about results; if you are unsure, do not post it.
Worked example. A client asks if a herb can replace her medicine. You say that is a question for her doctor or pharmacist and offer to help her write her questions. In a table of ten client questions, if four need a referral, you can coach on the other 10 - 4 = 6.

Activity. Build a table of what you may do, what you must refer, and who to refer to, for ten common client questions.

Standard to meet. Task: build a scope-of-practice table. Condition: using official public pages. Standard: each row shows a referral or a safe coaching reply and a source; no guesswork.

Module 5 Price your sessions and check it pays 8 hApply

You earn only what remains after costs and unbilled time.

  • Time includes prep, session, notes, travel and admin.
  • Hourly cost = yearly costs plus the pay you need, divided by hours you can really bill.
  • Compare single sessions, packages and group sessions.
  • Sales tax may apply to some services; check the small-supplier limit with the tax agency.
  • Calculate your real hours per session by adding prep, session, notes and travel.
  • Evaluate a package of four sessions against four single sessions and say who gains from each.
  • Check each month that income minus costs covers your pay target; if it does not, change the price or the hours.
Worked example. A 60-minute session takes 90 minutes with prep and notes. At $60 per session, that is $60 / 1.5 = $40 per hour of real time. A package of 4 sessions at $216 is $54 each, so you give up 4 x $60 - $216 = $24 in total.

Activity. Time three sessions end to end and set a price for each format.

Standard to meet. Task: price three formats. Condition: from your own time log. Standard: hours, price and result shown and a mentor can follow every number.

Module 6 Find clients and show proof 6 hCreate

People choose a coach they trust. Show your method and your care.

  • Write a one-page description of who you help and how, in plain words and without health claims.
  • Offer a free 20-minute chat so people can meet you.
  • Ask permission before using any client story, and remove names.
  • Keep a simple list: contact, date, reply, next step. Only contact people who agree to hear from you.
  • Practise saying in three plain sentences who you help and what a session looks like.
  • Prepare five questions for a free chat that begin with 'What would you like to change?'
  • Compare two places to meet clients, such as a community board and a local group, by cost and by how many people they reach.
Worked example. A post says: 'I help busy parents build a simple evening routine.' It makes no claim about health outcomes. If you hold 5 chats and 2 people book, your booking rate is 2 / 5 = 40%; to win 3 clients you would plan about 3 / 0.4 = 7.5, so 8 chats.

Activity. Hold five intro chats and log the results.

Standard to meet. Task: run five intro chats. Condition: with consent and no pressure. Standard: notes kept, no claims made, and one lesson written.

Module 7 Protect privacy, safety and quality 6 hEvaluate

Clients share personal things. Keep them safe.

  • Health and personal details are sensitive. Keep notes locked and share nothing without written consent.
  • Know when to refer: signs of serious illness, self-harm, abuse or an eating disorder. Have a list of local services ready.
  • Keep a short written agreement: services, fees, cancellation, privacy and your limits.
  • Consider insurance for your work; ask an adviser.
  • Store notes in a locked drawer or a password-protected file and list who may see them (only you).
  • Draft a referral list of five local services, such as a family doctor line, a crisis line, a community health centre, a pharmacist and a dietitian clinic; check the numbers are current.
  • Write a cancellation rule, such as 24 hours' notice, and put it in the agreement.
Worked example. Your agreement says: 'I am a coach, not a health care provider. I do not diagnose or treat. I will refer you when a matter is outside my role.' It also says: 'Sessions cancelled with less than 24 hours' notice are charged.' Anyone reading it can see your limits before the first session.

Activity. Write an agreement template and a referral list for your area.

Standard to meet. Task: produce an agreement and a referral list. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: scope, fees, privacy and limits are all in plain words.

Module 8 Run a coaching practice for 60 days 8 hCreate

Now you run it for real, measure and decide.

  • Set a goal such as three clients and ten sessions.
  • Each week record hours, income, client progress and what you learned.
  • Ask each client for feedback and one improvement.
  • Choose at the end: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Calculate weekly hours and income and compare them with your goal.
  • List three changes drawn from client feedback and rank them by effort.
  • Evaluate whether your income per real hour reached your target.
Worked example. After 60 days: 3 clients, 10 sessions, $600, two clients returned. That is $600 / 10 = $60 per session; if the 10 sessions took 15 real hours, you earned $600 / 15 = $40 per hour. You decide to offer a four-week package.

Activity. Run the pilot and present a 12-month plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a 60-day pilot. Condition: real clients and a signed agreement. Standard: log complete, no medical claims made, and the plan uses numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.
