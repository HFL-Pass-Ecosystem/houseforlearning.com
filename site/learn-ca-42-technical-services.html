Technical Services

Run a real technical Services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small technical Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: professional, scientific and community services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the words and fields of technical services 6 hUnderstand

Technical services support design, building, testing and upkeep of systems, such as drawings, inspections, equipment and software tools.

  • Terms: specification, drawing, tolerance, standard, test, fault, maintenance, documentation.
  • A specification says what a thing must do and how well.
  • Tolerance is the allowed difference from a target size.
  • Read one simple drawing or data sheet and list its key details.
  • Know the common units: millimetres, metres, volts, amps, kilograms, degrees Celsius.
  • Learn the difference between a drawing, a data sheet and a manual.
  • List the tools in your field and say what each measures.
Worked example. A part must be 50 mm long with a tolerance of plus or minus 0.2 mm. Anything from 49.8 to 50.2 passes, a range of 0.4 mm. A part of 50.3 mm is 0.1 mm over the top limit and fails.

Activity. Read one data sheet or drawing and write a list of ten key facts with units.

Standard to meet. Task: extract key facts. Condition: one simple drawing or sheet. Standard: ten facts with correct units and terms.

Module 2 Explain how a technical job is planned and done 6 hApply

Technical jobs need planning, method and records.

  • Steps: understand the need, plan, prepare, do, test, document, hand over.
  • Write the method step by step with safety checks.
  • Version your documents so everyone uses the latest one.
  • Record what was changed, when and by whom.
  • Write down the need in one sentence and confirm it with the client before you start.
  • List the tools, parts and safety gear before the job, not during it.
  • Hand over with a short summary of what was done and what to check next.
Worked example. Change log: 'v2, 12 March, replaced sensor type, retested, results attached, signed by JT.' If a project has versions v1 to v5, there are 5 - 1 = 4 changes recorded after the first issue.

Activity. Write a method for a simple task, such as testing a circuit or checking a measuring tool.

Standard to meet. Task: write a step-by-step method. Condition: for one simple task. Standard: another person can follow it, with safety steps and a record sheet.

Module 3 Measure, test and document to a standard 10 hApply

Reliable measures and clear records are the heart of technical work.

  • Use the right tool and check that it is calibrated.
  • Compare measured values with the specification and mark pass or fail.
  • Repeat tests if the result is close to the limit.
  • Write the result with units, date, tool used and your initials.
  • Take the reading at the same place and in the same way each time.
  • Mark each result pass, fail or recheck, and say why.
  • Keep the failed parts apart so no one uses them.
Worked example. Measured 50.3 mm against a limit of 50.2 mm: fail. Repeat gives 50.3 again. Report: part rejected, cause to be checked. If 2 of 10 parts fail, the fail rate is 2 / 10 = 20 percent.

Activity. Test ten items against a specification and write a test report.

Standard to meet. Task: complete a test report. Condition: with calibrated tools. Standard: all results with units, pass or fail marked correctly, and a clear conclusion.

Module 4 Know who may do what: licences, standards and safety 8 hEvaluate

Technical work is often regulated for public safety.

  • Engineering work that affects public safety is regulated by provincial engineering bodies. Do not offer work or titles you are not licensed for. Check with the provincial body.
  • Technologists and technicians can have their own certification routes. Check what employers accept.
  • Electrical, gas, pressure and building work follow codes and may need permits and licensed workers. Check the current rules.
  • Workplace safety rules apply to all sites and labs, including lockout and protective gear.
  • Ask the provincial body what work needs a licensed person and what a technician may do under supervision.
  • Check which codes and standards apply to your type of work and where to read them.
  • Write the steps of lockout for your equipment and practise them.
Worked example. Rules table row: 'Structural design sign-off | provincial engineering body | needs a licensed engineer | before drawings are issued | stamped drawing on file'. If sign-off takes 2 weeks and drawings are due on week 5, request it by week 3 at the latest.

Activity. Build a rules table for the type of technical work you plan to do.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: official pages only. Standard: each row has enforcer, action and date checked; scope limits are written clearly.

Module 5 Find faults and fix them 8 hApply

Diagnose before you fix. A guess costs time and money.

  • Describe the symptom clearly: what, when and how often.
  • List possible causes and test the easiest first.
  • Change one thing at a time and retest.
  • Record the cause, the fix and how you proved it worked.
  • Check power, connections and settings before you open a device.
  • Write down what was tried, in order, so you do not repeat a step.
  • Ask the user to show the fault, so you see it yourself.
Worked example. A sensor reads high. Check wiring, then calibration, then the sensor itself. Calibration was off by 3 percent. At a true reading of 200 units, the sensor showed 206 (200 x 1.03). Recalibrated, retested, recorded.

Activity. Diagnose and fix a staged fault in a practice kit and write a fault report.

Standard to meet. Task: complete a fault report. Condition: practice equipment, supervised. Standard: symptom, tests, cause, fix and retest result are all recorded.

Module 6 Price technical work and manage a job 6 hCreate

Technical work must be priced for time, tools, risk and follow-up.

  • List tasks, hours, parts, travel, tools, insurance and a buffer for rework.
  • Price per hour, per job or per test, and use the method that fits the work and the client.
  • Track actual hours against your estimate.
  • State the warranty and what it does not cover in writing before the job starts.
  • Add the time to travel, set up and write the report to your hours.
  • Set a rule for extra work: it is quoted and agreed before it is done.
  • Keep records of tools and calibration costs as part of your overhead.
Worked example. An example price of 150 CAD per hour for 20 hours is 3,000 CAD. Cost is 35 per hour, so 20 x 35 = 700. Margin is 3,000 - 700 = 2,300, which is 115 per hour. If rework adds 3 hours at your cost, the margin falls by 3 x 35 = 105. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build an estimate and cost sheet for one job, then compare to the actual hours.

Standard to meet. Task: estimate and track a job. Condition: for a real or practice job. Standard: units shown, formulas visible, difference between estimate and actual explained.

Module 7 Manage risk, quality and client data 6 hEvaluate

Mistakes can harm people and cost money. Build habits that catch them early.

  • Use checklists and second-person checks for critical steps.
  • Keep a risk register: wrong measure, missed standard, tool failure, data loss, injury.
  • Protect drawings and client data with backups and access limits.
  • Report incidents and near-misses so they can be fixed.
  • Name one person who checks each critical step.
  • Rate each risk for chance and harm, and start with the highest.
  • Review the register after each job and add anything new.
Worked example. A near-miss: a wrong drawing version was used. Fix: old versions moved to an archive folder and the latest marked on the cover. If the team had 12 drawings in use and 3 were out of date, 3 / 12 = 25 percent were wrong, so the archive step is worth the effort.

Activity. Write a risk register of eight rows and a one-page quality plan.

Standard to meet. Task: write a risk register and quality plan. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: each risk has control, owner and review date.

Module 8 Run a technical service for real and grow 8 hCreate

Run a small real service and decide how to grow.

  • Pilot for 30 to 60 days with clear scope and a stop rule.
  • Track hours, errors, rework, client feedback and income.
  • Write a handover pack so another person could maintain your work.
  • Plan the next 12 months: training, tools and licensing steps.
  • Pick one client type and one service for the pilot.
  • Ask the client to sign off the scope and results at the end.
  • List the tools and training you need to buy or learn next.
Worked example. Stop rule: 'If rework passes 15 percent of hours, pause new jobs and fix the method.' On 100 hours worked, 15 percent is 15 hours. If rework takes 18 hours, it is 18 percent and the rule is triggered.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and the plan, and present to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a technical service pilot. Condition: within your licence and safety rules. Standard: records complete, rework measured, decision supported by numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
