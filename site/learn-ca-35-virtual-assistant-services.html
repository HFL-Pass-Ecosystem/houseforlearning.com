Virtual Assistant Services

Run a real virtual Assistant Services pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small virtual Assistant Services offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: content, creative and marketing work. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know what a virtual assistant does 6 hUnderstand

A virtual assistant helps a client with tasks from a distance. Learn the common tasks and the words used.

  • Common tasks: email and calendar help, booking, data entry, simple bookkeeping entries, document edits, research and customer messages.
  • Learn the terms: scope, retainer, deliverable, turnaround, handover.
  • A VA is usually self-employed, not an employee.
  • List ten tasks you already do well and the tools you use.
  • Sort ten tasks into those you can do now and those you need to learn.
  • Describe what turnaround means for one task you offer.
  • Compare working as a VA with working as an employee.
Worked example. A therapist needs appointments booked and reminders sent. Task list: manage calendar, send reminders, file receipts weekly. If these take 2 + 1 + 1 = 4 hours a week, the client needs about 4 x 4 = 16 hours a month.

Activity. Interview one busy small-business owner and write down the five tasks they most want off their plate.

Standard to meet. Task: record a client task list. Condition: one 20-minute interview. Standard: five tasks written in plain words with how often each repeats.

Module 2 Explain how work flows from request to done 6 hApply

Good assistants follow a simple loop so nothing is lost.

  • Loop: receive request, confirm, do, check, report.
  • Confirm in writing what, by when, and in what format.
  • Keep a task list with owner, due date and status.
  • Tell the client early if a deadline will be missed.
  • Review the list at the end of each day.
  • Write a short status update template.
  • Agree with the client how and when updates are sent.
Worked example. Request: 'Book a meeting room for Tuesday.' Confirm: 'Tuesday, 10 a.m., six people, projector. Is that right?' Report: 'Booked, confirmation in your email.' If you finish 18 of 20 tasks on time, that is 18 / 20 = 90%.

Activity. Write a one-page process for how you take and track requests. Test it for one week with a friend as the pretend client.

Standard to meet. Task: run the request loop for five tasks. Condition: using a free spreadsheet or list. Standard: each task has a confirmed scope, due date, result and a short report.

Module 3 Do routine tasks accurately and on time 10 hApply

Apply your skills. Small errors cost trust, so work to a checked standard.

  • Use a checklist for every repeating task.
  • Check names, dates, times and time zones twice before sending.
  • Write emails in short clear sentences and keep the client's tone.
  • Save files with clear names and in the folder the client expects.
  • Use templates for repeated emails.
  • Confirm the meeting link works before you send.
  • Log each error you catch and the step that caused it.
Worked example. Calendar task checklist: right person, right time zone, link added, reminder set, client told. If you check 20 tasks and catch 3 errors before sending, that is 3 / 20 = 15% caught; the target is zero errors sent.

Activity. Complete twenty practice tasks (emails, bookings, data entry) and log errors found before sending.

Standard to meet. Task: complete twenty tasks. Condition: from written instructions in set time. Standard: no errors reach the client; every task is logged with time taken.

Module 4 Know the Canadian rules for self-employed work 8 hEvaluate

In Canada, whether you are a contractor or an employee depends on the real working relationship, not only the contract title.

  • Signs of a contractor: you choose how and when to work, use your own tools, and can have other clients.
  • Contractors usually register for tax, track income and expenses, and pay their own tax and pension contributions. Check the current rules with the federal tax agency.
  • Sales tax registration is required once taxable sales pass the small-supplier limit. Check the current limit.
  • Your province may require a business name registration or local licence. Check the local authority.
  • List the records you must keep: invoices, receipts and payments.
  • List what an invoice must show: your name, date, amount, tax and payment terms.
  • Check the rule before you hire a helper.
Worked example. A client wants you to work set hours with their equipment and no other clients. That is three signs of employment, so talk to an adviser before you accept.

Activity. Make a rules table: rule, who enforces it, what you do, date checked.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table for your province. Condition: using official public pages. Standard: every row has a dated source and an action; unclear points say to check with the authority.

Module 5 Price services and check you earn enough 8 hApply

Pricing must cover your time, tools, tax and unpaid hours.

  • Not all hours are billable; admin and finding clients take time too.
  • Choose a model: hourly, monthly retainer of agreed hours, or fixed price for a clear task.
  • Retainer example: 10 hours a month at a set rate, unused hours do not carry over unless agreed.
  • Send invoices on a fixed day and state payment terms.
  • Calculate billable hours: if you work 40 hours and bill 30, one quarter is unpaid.
  • Compare a 10-hour retainer with hourly billing.
  • Add a late payment rule to your terms.
Worked example. At $28 an hour with 30 billable hours a month you bill 30 x 28 = $840. Tools cost $40, so before tax you keep 840 - 40 = $800. If you worked 40 hours to bill those 30, $800 / 40 = $20 per hour worked.

Activity. Build a price sheet with three packages and a monthly cost sheet.

Standard to meet. Task: build a price and cost sheet. Condition: spreadsheet with visible formulas. Standard: break-even hours per month shown, three what-if cases, and a mentor checks the arithmetic.

Module 6 Find and keep clients 6 hCreate

Clients come from trust. Show them you are careful and easy to work with.

  • Choose one kind of client first, such as clinics, real estate agents or trades.
  • Write a one-page offer: who you help, what you do, result, price basis, how to start.
  • Run a short trial of one to two weeks with clear scope.
  • Ask for referrals after a good month.
  • Write a four-sentence message to a possible client.
  • List fifteen possible clients with where you found each.
  • Define what a successful trial looks like before it starts.
Worked example. Offer: 'For solo trades owners: I answer email, book jobs and send invoices. Five hours a week. First week is a trial.' Five hours a week for four weeks is 5 x 4 = 20 hours, which at $28 an hour is $560 a month.

Activity. Contact fifteen possible clients with consent-based messages and book three calls.

Standard to meet. Task: run a client search for two weeks. Condition: only people who may be contacted under the anti-spam rules. Standard: messages logged, calls booked, one lesson written.

Module 7 Protect client data and set limits 6 hEvaluate

You will see private information. Treat it with great care.

  • Use a password manager and two-step sign-in. Never share passwords in plain email.
  • Ask for the least access needed and remove it when the job ends.
  • Sign a confidentiality agreement and say what you will not do, such as legal or medical advice.
  • If you make a mistake or lose data, tell the client at once and follow your breach steps.
  • Set up a password vault and list who can open it.
  • Write the steps you take after a data mistake.
  • List each client account you can open and the date you will remove access.
Worked example. A client sends a password in a text message. You reply asking them to use a shared vault with limited access, then delete the message. You note the date you will remove your access when the job ends.

Activity. Write a one-page privacy note and a risk list of eight items with controls.

Standard to meet. Task: write privacy note and risk list. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: each risk has a control, an owner and a review date, in plain words.

Module 8 Run your VA business and grow 8 hCreate

Run it for real and decide from numbers.

  • Pilot for 60 days with at least two paying clients.
  • Track hours, income, errors, late payments and client feedback each week.
  • Write a handover guide so a client could carry on without you.
  • Decide: raise price, add a service, hire a helper or stop; write a 12-month plan.
  • Calculate your monthly income from hours and rate.
  • List the tools and logins the handover guide needs.
  • Plan a price rise for new clients.
Worked example. At day 60: two clients, 38 hours a month, errors 2 (both caught), one referral. At $28 an hour, 38 hours bills 38 x 28 = $1,064 a month. A 10 percent rise gives 28 x 1.10 = $30.80 an hour for new clients.

Activity. Run the pilot, keep the log and present the plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: operate a 60-day pilot. Condition: real clients with written scope. Standard: log complete, privacy rules followed, decision backed by numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
