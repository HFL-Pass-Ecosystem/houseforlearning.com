CPD · Telecommunications and IT

Documenting a change

Writing a change request that others can approve and reverse.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 2 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playTelecommunications and IT

Writing a change request that others can approve and reverse.

  • Describe a change, given a scenario, with reason, scope and impact.
  • Plan a back-out, given the change, with clear steps and a time limit.
  • Record approval, given a template, with name, date and decision.
  • Review a change after the fact, given outcome notes, stating what to keep or fix.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write a change record with reason, risk and rollback, test the change with a checklist, record the approval and result, and review a risky change to choose a safer time

Condition

Given change scenarios such as a mail server update and a firewall rule change, a record template and a test checklist, in a 2-hour practical session

Standard

Complete a record with reason, risk, rollback, test plan, named approver and result, stop and record when a test fails, and choose a safer time for a risky change

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Why changes need records 15 min
  • A record lets others approve, reverse and learn from a change.
  • Emergency changes are recorded afterwards with a reason.
  • No record means no proof of what changed.
Worked example. Change C-014: a fault appears 2 weeks after a mail update. The record shows who changed what and when, so the cause is found in minutes and not days.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "why changes need records": describe a change, given a scenario, with reason, scope and impact.

Standard to meet. Explain why a change needs a record, so that others can approve it, reverse it and learn from it, including emergency changes recorded afterwards with a reason.

Module 2 Reason, risk, rollback 25 min
  • Reason: why the change is needed.
  • Risk: what could go wrong and for how long.
  • Rollback: clear steps to undo the change.
Worked example. Mail server update. Reason: security fix. Risk: 30-minute outage. Rollback: restore the snapshot, about 20 minutes. Start it if a test fails.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "reason, risk, rollback": plan a back-out, given the change, with clear steps and a time limit.

Standard to meet. Write the reason, risk and rollback for a change, given a scenario such as a mail server update, with the rollback as clear steps that undo the change.

Module 3 Testing with a checklist 25 min
  • Write the test list before the change.
  • Test that the service works and nothing else broke.
  • If a test fails, stop and record it.
Worked example. Checklist: 1 send an internal message, 2 receive an external message, 3 open the shared calendar. Result 3 of 3 pass. If test 2 fails: stop, start rollback, record the result.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "testing with a checklist": record approval, given a template, with name, date and decision.

Standard to meet. Test a change with a checklist, confirming that the service works and nothing else broke, and stop and record the result if a test fails.

Module 4 Approval and result 20 min
  • Record the named approver, date, time and decision.
  • Record the outcome after the change.
  • Keep the record for later review.
Worked example. Approval line: approver named, 2026-11-05 21:45, decision approved. Result line: 22:35 change done, 3 of 3 tests passed, no rollback.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "approval and result": review a change after the fact, given outcome notes, stating what to keep or fix.

Standard to meet. Record approval and the result, given a template, with the named approver, date, time and decision, and the outcome noted for later review.

Module 5 Practice 10 min
  • Fill every field of the template.
  • Keep the wording short and exact.
  • Check the rollback is possible.
Worked example. Firewall rule change. Reason: allow staff to reach the supplier portal on port 443. Risk: low, one new rule. Rollback: delete the rule, 1 minute. Test: open the portal from 1 office PC. Approver named.

Practise: Run one full practice round for "documenting a change" on a case card or template: work through each outcome in turn (describe a change; plan a back-out; record approval; review a change after the fact).

Standard to meet. Complete a change record for a firewall rule change, given a scenario, with reason, risk, rollback, test plan and approver all filled in.

Module 6 Check 5 min
  • Check the record for six fields.
  • Judge whether the timing is safe.
  • Choose a better time if the risk is high.
Worked example. Record check: reason, risk, rollback, test plan, approver, result: 6 of 6 present. A record with the rollback blank scores 5 of 6 and goes back to its author.

Practise: Take the end check for "documenting a change": show that you can review a change after the fact, given outcome notes, stating what to keep or fix, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Review a risky change, such as a mail server upgrade on a Friday evening, and choose a safer time, with the record complete and consistent.

Module 7 Second worked case: a Friday evening upgrade 10 min
  • Add the time for the change, the tests, the rollback and a margin.
  • Choose a window when few people are affected.
  • Rewrite the record with the new time.
Worked example. Case card 2: mail upgrade planned for Friday at 18:00, 30-minute change plus 15 minutes of tests plus 20-minute rollback = 65 minutes. A Sunday 06:00 to 08:00 window gives 120 minutes, about 1.8 times the need. Choose Sunday.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second worked case: a Friday evening upgrade": review a change after the fact, given outcome notes, stating what to keep or fix. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Review a risky upgrade planned for a busy time, calculate the time needed including rollback, and choose a safer window with the record rewritten.

Module 8 Teach-back and peer check 10 min
  • Swap records with a partner.
  • Check the six fields one by one.
  • Name any field that is blank or unclear.
Worked example. Pairs of 2: partner checks a record in 5 minutes against the six fields. Target: all 6 fields found, the one missing rollback named, and a clear approve or return decision.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "teach-back and peer check": review a change after the fact, given outcome notes, stating what to keep or fix. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate a partner's change record against the six required fields, find any blank or unclear field, and decide whether to approve or return it.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Why a rollback plan?

Model answer: To recover if it fails. (Covered in module 2: Reason, risk, rollback. To pass that module: Write the reason, risk and rollback for a change, given a scenario such as a mail server update, with the rollback as clear steps that undo the change.)

Who approves?

Model answer: The named approver, per policy. (Covered in module 4: Approval and result. To pass that module: Record approval and the result, given a template, with the named approver, date, time and decision, and the outcome noted for later review.)

What is tested?

Model answer: That the service works and nothing else broke. (Covered in module 3: Testing with a checklist. To pass that module: Test a change with a checklist, confirming that the service works and nothing else broke, and stop and record the result if a test fails.)

Record the result?

Model answer: Yes, with time. (Covered in module 4: Approval and result. To pass that module: Record approval and the result, given a template, with the named approver, date, time and decision, and the outcome noted for later review.)

What is rollback?

Model answer: The steps to undo the change. (Covered in module 2: Reason, risk, rollback. To pass that module: Write the reason, risk and rollback for a change, given a scenario such as a mail server update, with the rollback as clear steps that undo the change.)

Who approves?

Model answer: The named approver. (Covered in module 4: Approval and result. To pass that module: Record approval and the result, given a template, with the named approver, date, time and decision, and the outcome noted for later review.)

What if the test fails?

Model answer: Do not proceed; record the result. (Covered in module 3: Testing with a checklist. To pass that module: Test a change with a checklist, confirming that the service works and nothing else broke, and stop and record the result if a test fails.)

Why record the result?

Model answer: For later learning and audit. (Covered in module 4: Approval and result. To pass that module: Record approval and the result, given a template, with the named approver, date, time and decision, and the outcome noted for later review.)

Emergency change?

Model answer: Same record, written after, with reason. (Covered in module 1: Why changes need records. To pass that module: Explain why a change needs a record, so that others can approve it, reverse it and learn from it, including emergency changes recorded afterwards with a reason.)

A change takes 30 minutes, testing 15 and rollback 20, in a 60-minute window. Does it fit?

Model answer: No: 30 + 15 + 20 = 65 minutes, which is over 60; ask for a longer window or choose another time. (Covered in module 7: Second worked case: a Friday evening upgrade. To pass that module: Review a risky upgrade planned for a busy time, calculate the time needed including rollback, and choose a safer window with the record rewritten.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What a change is. Speaker note: A change is any alteration to a system, such as an update or a new firewall rule. At this first step the learner can say what a change is and why a written record helps.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Why record changes. Speaker note: The learner can explain that a record lets others approve a change, undo it and learn from it. They know the main parts: reason, risk, rollback, test, approver and result.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Fill in the record. Speaker note: The learner can fill in a simple record with help, using the mail server update as a model. They write a rollback as clear steps and name the test that shows the service still works.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Plan, test and approve. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner completes a change record, tests with a checklist, stops and records a failed test, records approval and result, and chooses a safer time for a risky change.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Linked and emergency changes. Speaker note: The specialist handles changes that depend on each other, tight windows and emergency changes recorded afterwards. They check timing for change, test and rollback, and keep records consistent across systems.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Review and learn. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner reviews finished changes, compares plan with result, and finds patterns such as missing rollbacks or rushed windows. They give feedback that improves the next record.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Own the process. Speaker note: The super expert designs the change process, templates and approval rules for an organisation. They set windows and risk levels, audit records, and train teams so changes are safe and reversible.
