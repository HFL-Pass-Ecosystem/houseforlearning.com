Checking a protection setting

Reviewing a protection setting against the fault level and the load.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5 SpecialistAbout 4 h of CPDSelf-study plus role-playEngineering

Reviewing a protection setting against the fault level and the load.

  • Interpret a time-current curve, given a sample, identifying pick-up and time for two faults.
  • Check a setting, given a fault level and load, that it trips for faults and not for normal load.
  • Identify a coordination problem, given two curves, with the point of overlap named.
  • Record the finding, given a template, with a clear pass or fail.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Read a one-line diagram and coordination curves, check a protection setting against the fault level and load, and report any mismatch with a clear pass or fail

Condition

Given a sample one-line diagram, two time-current curves, a fault level and load, and a report template, in a 4-hour practical session

Standard

Name every device from supply to motor, identify pick-up and trip time on two curves, show where a downstream device does or does not trip first, and report a mismatch with setting, expected value, difference and location

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 One-line diagram basics 30 min
  • A one-line diagram shows the path of power in single lines.
  • Each symbol is one device with one job.
  • Read from the supply to the motor.
Worked example. Diagram: 400 V supply, main breaker 400 A, feeder breaker 100 A, motor starter, 37 kW motor. Four devices, each named with its purpose: isolate the supply, protect the board, protect the feeder, protect the motor.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "one-line diagram basics": interpret a time-current curve, given a sample, identifying pick-up and time for two faults.

Standard to meet. Read a one-line diagram and name each device from supply to motor, stating the purpose of every device shown on the diagram.

Module 2 Device order from source to load 30 min
  • List devices from source to load.
  • For a fault at the load, the device nearest the load should act first.
  • That order is called selectivity.
Worked example. Order for a motor fault: main 400 A, then feeder 100 A, then motor starter. Expected first to act: the motor starter. The feeder is the backup and the main is the last resort.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "device order from source to load": check a setting, given a fault level and load, that it trips for faults and not for normal load.

Standard to meet. List the devices in order from source to load, given a one-line diagram, and state which device should act first for a fault at the load.

Module 3 Coordination curves 45 min
  • Pick-up is the current at which a device starts to act.
  • Trip time is read on the curve at the fault current.
  • Two curves must not cross where faults can occur.
Worked example. At a 1,000 A fault: feeder (pick-up 100 A) trips in 0.10 s; main (pick-up 400 A) trips in 0.40 s. The 0.30 s gap means the downstream feeder acts first, so the curves do not cross there.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "coordination curves": identify a coordination problem, given two curves, with the point of overlap named.

Standard to meet. Interpret two coordination curves, given a sample, identifying pick-up and time for a fault and showing where the downstream device does or does not trip first.

Module 4 Checking a setting 35 min
  • A setting must not trip on normal load.
  • It must trip on the fault level given.
  • Compare the setting with both numbers.
Worked example. Normal load 80 A, fault level 3,000 A. Setting: pick-up 100 A, fast trip at 1,000 A. 80 A is under 100 A, so no trip on load. 3,000 A is over 1,000 A, so it trips fast. Result: pass.

Practise: Take the end check for "checking a protection setting": show that you can record the finding, given a template, with a clear pass or fail, and meet every outcome standard above.

Standard to meet. Check a given setting against the coordination curve, given a fault level and load, and state whether it trips for faults but not for normal load.

Module 5 Reporting a mismatch and next check 25 min
  • Name the device, the setting and the location.
  • Give the expected value, the actual value and the difference.
  • Say the next check and who owns it.
Worked example. Report: feeder F2 at panel P1, trip time 0.45 s at 1,500 A, expected 0.10 s, difference 0.35 s. Next check: ask the responsible engineer to review the setting. Do not change it yourself.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "reporting a mismatch and next check": record the finding, given a template, with a clear pass or fail. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Report a mismatch, given two curves that cross, naming the setting, expected value, difference and location, and state the next check for the responsible engineer.

Module 6 Assessment 15 min
  • Give a clear pass or fail with evidence.
  • Say plainly what you could not verify.
  • Do not guess a missing value.
Worked example. Sample case: fault 1,500 A, feeder trips in 0.08 s, main in 0.35 s. Pass, with the curve readings as evidence. The motor starter curve was not supplied, so it is reported as not verified.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "assessment": record the finding, given a template, with a clear pass or fail. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Check a sample case, given curves and a one-line diagram, with a clear pass or fail and evidence, and say plainly when something cannot be verified.

Module 7 Second worked case: crossed curves 30 min
  • Mark both curves at the same fault current.
  • The downstream device must be faster, not slower.
  • State the mismatch and the next step.
Worked example. Case card 2: at a 2,000 A fault the downstream feeder trips in 0.50 s and the upstream main in 0.30 s. The feeder is 0.20 s slower, so the main trips first. Report the mismatch and ask the responsible engineer.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second worked case: crossed curves": record the finding, given a template, with a clear pass or fail. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Mark two crossing curves at a given fault current, state the time difference, and report the mismatch with the next step for the responsible engineer.

Module 8 Teach-back and peer check 30 min
  • Explain your reading to a partner in plain words.
  • The partner checks three things: order, trip times and wording.
  • Swap roles and agree one improvement each.
Worked example. Pairs of 2: reader 3 minutes, partner checks the device order, two trip times and the report wording. Target: 4 of 4 devices named in order and both trip times read within 0.05 s.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "teach-back and peer check": record the finding, given a template, with a clear pass or fail. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Evaluate a partner's curve reading and mismatch report against a checklist, give one strength and one improvement, and confirm the device order and trip times are correct.

practice

Worked example and case cards

Worked example

One-line: supply, main breaker, feeder breaker, motor. Rule of thumb for order: devices nearer the load must trip faster than devices nearer the supply for the same fault (selectivity). Compare given settings with the coordination curve; if the feeder curve crosses above the main's, report the mismatch and ask the responsible engineer.

Case card

two curves and a one-line diagram.

Case card 2

A downstream breaker trips later than the upstream. Learners mark the curves, state the mismatch and next step.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

What is selectivity?

Model answer: Only the device nearest the fault trips. (Covered in module 2: Device order from source to load. To pass that module: List the devices in order from source to load, given a one-line diagram, and state which device should act first for a fault at the load.)

Who changes a setting?

Model answer: Only the authorised person. (Covered in module 4: Checking a setting. To pass that module: Check a given setting against the coordination curve, given a fault level and load, and state whether it trips for faults but not for normal load.)

What do you report?

Model answer: The device, the setting, the mismatch, the evidence. (Covered in module 5: Reporting a mismatch and next check. To pass that module: Report a mismatch, given two curves that cross, naming the setting, expected value, difference and location, and state the next check for the responsible engineer.)

Is a pass on paper enough?

Model answer: No; tests on site confirm. (Covered in module 4: Checking a setting. To pass that module: Check a given setting against the coordination curve, given a fault level and load, and state whether it trips for faults but not for normal load.)

What is coordination?

Model answer: Only the device nearest the fault trips. (Covered in module 3: Coordination curves. To pass that module: Interpret two coordination curves, given a sample, identifying pick-up and time for a fault and showing where the downstream device does or does not trip first.)

Who may change a setting?

Model answer: An authorized person with a change record. (Covered in module 4: Checking a setting. To pass that module: Check a given setting against the coordination curve, given a fault level and load, and state whether it trips for faults but not for normal load.)

What do you report?

Model answer: Setting, expected value, difference, location. (Covered in module 5: Reporting a mismatch and next check. To pass that module: Report a mismatch, given two curves that cross, naming the setting, expected value, difference and location, and state the next check for the responsible engineer.)

Why list devices in order?

Model answer: To trace which one should act first. (Covered in module 2: Device order from source to load. To pass that module: List the devices in order from source to load, given a one-line diagram, and state which device should act first for a fault at the load.)

What if you cannot verify?

Model answer: Say so; do not guess. (Covered in module 5: Reporting a mismatch and next check. To pass that module: Report a mismatch, given two curves that cross, naming the setting, expected value, difference and location, and state the next check for the responsible engineer.)

At a 2,000 A fault the feeder trips in 0.50 s and the main in 0.30 s. What is the gap and what do you report?

Model answer: The downstream feeder is 0.20 s slower, the wrong way round; report the device, setting, difference and location, and ask the responsible engineer. (Covered in module 7: Second worked case: crossed curves. To pass that module: Mark two crossing curves at a given fault current, state the time difference, and report the mismatch with the next step for the responsible engineer.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What protection does. Speaker note: A protective device cuts off power when a fault occurs. At this first step the learner can say why a fault is dangerous and why a breaker must open fast enough to protect people and equipment.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Reading a one-line diagram. Speaker note: The learner can follow a one-line diagram from the supply to a motor and name each device and its purpose. They know that only an authorised person may change a setting, with a change record.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Order and curves. Speaker note: The learner knows the device order from source to load and can find pick-up and trip time on a simple curve with help. They follow the worked example where the feeder must act before the main.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Check a setting and report. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner checks a setting against fault level and normal load, spots where two curves cross, and reports a mismatch with a clear pass or fail and the evidence.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Several devices in a chain. Speaker note: The specialist checks chains of three or more devices, with motor starting current and different fault levels along the line. They find the weakest gap and propose what the responsible engineer should review.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Review and verify on site. Speaker note: The advanced practitioner reviews other people's findings, asks for site tests that confirm a paper pass, and records what could not be verified rather than guessing. They keep change records complete.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Set the checking standard. Speaker note: The super expert writes the checking method and report template for a team. They audit past findings for missed mismatches, train others in selectivity, and advise on when a study needs a specialist.

This course sits at L5 Specialist. Credits stack; aligned with, not accredited by, any framework.
