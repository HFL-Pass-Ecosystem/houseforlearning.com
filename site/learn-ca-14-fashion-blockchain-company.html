Fashion blockchain company

Run a pilot of fashion blockchain company in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small fashion blockchain company offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: software, data and digital services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Find the authenticity and resale problem 6 hUnderstand

Fashion has problems with fakes, hidden labour and waste. A shared digital record can tie a garment to its origin, owner history and repair record.

  • List buyer worries: fake goods, unknown origin, resale value, repair history.
  • Choose one product: a bag, a jacket or a pair of shoes, and a first brand partner.
  • Decide the record: material source, maker, date, size, care and each change of owner.
  • Test the need with ten shoppers: would proof of origin change what you pay?
  • Interview one repair shop and one resale seller to learn what proof of origin they need most and what they check today.
  • Write the buyer's questions as a list (Is it real? Where was it made? Who owned it? Was it repaired?) and match each to a field in the record.
  • Choose the first line by picking an item with a high price, frequent copying or a resale market, and count how many units exist.
Worked example. Assume (teaching figures, not market facts) that a resale buyer pays 15% more for a bag with a verified history and a repair record, and that fakes cost the brand 2% of sales. If the bag normally resells for 400, 15% more is 400 x 0.15 = 60, so 460. If the brand sells 500,000 a year, 2% lost to fakes is 10,000.

Activity. Survey ten shoppers and one brand and write the problem in one page.

Standard to meet. Survey shows a real willingness to pay.

Module 2 Know consumer, textile and data rules 6 hApply

Fashion claims and labels are regulated, so check each rule before you launch.

  • Federal textile labelling rules require fibre content to be shown in English and French; care labels are voluntary but any care claim must be true. Check the current rule for your product.
  • Claims such as 'sustainable' or 'ethical' must be provable under competition law.
  • Customer personal data is protected by privacy law and should not sit on a record that cannot be erased.
  • Cross-border sales add customs, duty and product-safety rules.
  • Check which country-of-origin wording you may use on a label, and keep supplier records that back it up.
  • Ask a privacy adviser whether a tag linked to an owner needs consent and how a person can delete their data; collect only what is needed.
  • List which claims go on the tag, the website and ads, and who approves each claim before it is published.
Worked example. Row: 'Claim recycled content | competition law and labelling rules | hold supplier certificate | before launch | certificate on file'. Second row: 'Label a jacket for sale in Canada | federal textile labelling rules | check the fibre content label in both languages and the care label if you add one | before launch | label proof approved'. Check the provincial rule for any extra requirements.

Activity. Build a rules table for one product line.

Standard to meet. Rows sourced and dated.

Module 3 Design the digital product passport 10 hApply

Keep the record small and useful.

  • Attach a tag: QR code, NFC chip or hidden label that cannot be copied easily.
  • Record business facts only: lot, maker, date, material, certificate numbers.
  • Let owners add care and repair entries; a transfer is a new entry.
  • Test the tag after washing, rubbing and sunlight.
  • Test ten tags each for rubbing, folding, rain and sunlight, and tabulate scan success for each test.
  • Choose where the tag sits: hidden, protected and easy to scan, and photograph the spot for the guide.
  • Build a scan page that shows only business facts and a plain message if the tag is unknown or already used.
Worked example. After 20 washes, 18 of 20 QR labels still scanned and 2 failed. Survival is 18 / 20 = 90%. That passes the 50% stop rule, but a 10% failure rate is too high for a lasting record, so test a woven label next.

Activity. Prototype a passport for one garment and test the tag.

Standard to meet. Test results recorded.

Module 4 Price the service and prove the gain 8 hEvaluate

Brands pay when the passport raises sales or cuts fakes.

  • Charge the brand a fee per item tagged; the worked example below uses 1.80 per item.
  • List the brand's gains: price premium, resale royalty, fewer fakes and better returns data.
  • List your own costs: tags, hosting, support and onboarding.
  • Work out break-even volume as fixed cost divided by margin per item.
  • Estimate the price premium the brand can charge, and the share of it the passport must earn to be worth the fee.
  • Compare tag types (QR label, woven label, NFC chip) and check that your choice fits within the 0.60 variable cost.
  • Quote a pilot package: setup fee, per-item fee and a review at day 90.
Worked example. Fee 1.80 per item; variable cost 0.60; margin 1.20; fixed cost 8,000 a month needs about 6,700 items a month. Teaching figures in CAD. Check: 6,700 x 1.20 = 8,040, just above 8,000. At 50% volume, 3,350 items give 3,350 x 1.20 = 4,020, leaving a monthly gap of 8,000 - 4,020 = 3,980.

Activity. Build the model with a 50% volume case.

Standard to meet. Cases shown.

Module 5 Run supplier onboarding and data quality 8 hApply

Trust depends on honest inputs.

  • Train suppliers to scan at each step.
  • Verify supplier entries with audits and photos.
  • Tell the supplier what went wrong as soon as an audit finds an error.
  • Track the share of items with a complete record each week.
  • Give each supplier a one-page checklist: what to scan, when, and whom to call if the tag does not work.
  • Audit a sample of 20 records a week against photos and paper documents, and record the error rate.
  • Never delete an entry; add a correction with date, reason and who made it.
Worked example. Pilot: 85% of items had a complete record after 6 weeks. Of 2,000 items tagged, 85% complete means 1,700 full records and 300 incomplete. A target of 95% is 1,900, so 200 more records must be completed.

Activity. Run a 4-week capture test.

Standard to meet. Completion tracked.

Module 6 Sell to brands and marketplaces 6 hCreate

Start with a brand that has a fake or resale problem.

  • Offer a 90-day pilot on one line.
  • Show a live scan demo to every brand you meet.
  • Partner with repair shops and resale sites.
  • Track the funnel from brands contacted to demos to pilots.
  • Prepare a three-minute live demo: scan a tag, show the history, and add a repair entry.
  • Meet a repair shop and a resale seller, agree how they will add entries, and record their needs.
  • Write a one-page pilot offer: line, units, fee, dates, measures and exit terms.
Worked example. Funnel: 20 brands, 6 demos, 2 pilots. Brand to demo is 6 / 20 = 30%, demo to pilot is 2 / 6 = 33%, and overall 2 / 20 = 10%. At 10%, winning 5 pilots needs about 50 brands.

Activity. Pitch three brands.

Standard to meet. Pitch has numbers.

Module 7 Manage greenwashing, privacy and tech risks 6 hEvaluate

A record can mislead if inputs are wrong.

  • Claim only what you can prove.
  • Keep personal data off the shared record.
  • Plan for tag failure and re-tagging.
  • Prepare for the brand leaving the service.
  • Keep a claims log: each public claim, its evidence, the date checked and the person who signed it off.
  • Design the tag to carry only an item number, with all other data held in a record you can correct and delete.
  • Write a brand-exit plan: how owners keep access to history if the brand stops using the service, and how data is exported.
Worked example. Risk: 'Wrong origin entered | 3 x 5 | audit and photo proof | quality lead | monthly'. The score is 3 x 5 = 15 out of 25, so it is high priority. After audits with photo proof, likelihood falls to 2 and the score is 2 x 5 = 10.

Activity. Write a risk register.

Standard to meet. Owners named.

Module 8 Pilot and scale 8 hCreate

Prove one line.

  • Run the pilot for 90 days on one product line.
  • Measure scan rate, resale uplift, fakes found.
  • Stop rule: under 50% tag survival means change tag.
  • Decide at day 90 whether to proceed, change the tag or stop.
  • Record three numbers each week: scan rate, tags lost and fakes found, and plot each.
  • Collect resale prices for tagged and untagged items of the same type and compare the averages.
  • Plan a second line only after the review, and list what must be fixed first.
Worked example. Tag survival 94%; resale uplift 12%. Of 500 tagged items, 94% is 470 tags still scanning at day 90, far above the 50% stop rule. A 12% resale uplift on a 400 bag is 400 x 0.12 = 48; decision: proceed.

Activity. Run pilot review.

Standard to meet. Review has decision.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
