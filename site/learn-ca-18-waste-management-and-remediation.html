Waste management and remediation

Run a real waste management and remediation pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small waste management and remediation offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: energy, environment and resource work. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Understand waste streams and remediation 6 hUnderstand

Waste comes as household, business, construction and hazardous streams. Remediation cleans contaminated land or water.

  • Know the hierarchy: reduce, reuse, recycle, recover, dispose.
  • Measure a stream: tonnes, composition, sources.
  • Choose materials that have a market where you are; a material nobody buys is a cost, not a product.
  • Pick a niche: construction waste, organics, e-waste, site clean-up.
  • Sort a sample into wood, metal, drywall, plastics and organics, and weigh each.
  • Find who buys each recovered material and what contamination limit they set.
  • Estimate haul distance, because long distances can erase the value of a material.
Worked example. A building site produces 60 t of waste; sorting could divert 70%, which is 42 t, leaving 18 t for landfill. If disposal costs 100 per tonne, diverting 42 t avoids 4,200, before the cost of sorting.

Activity. Sample and weigh a waste stream.

Standard to meet. Composition table made.

Module 2 Know environmental and handling rules 6 hApply

Waste is heavily regulated.

  • Provincial approvals are required for waste handling, transport and processing.
  • Hazardous waste needs manifests and licensed carriers.
  • Contaminated sites follow assessment and clean-up standards with qualified professionals.
  • Worker safety training and protective gear are mandatory.
  • Learn that the definition of hazardous waste varies by province; check the provincial rule.
  • Keep a training record for each worker, with date and topic.
  • For contaminated sites, require an assessment report from a qualified professional before work starts.
Worked example. Row: 'Haul hazardous waste | provincial environment ministry | licence and manifest | each load | manifest copies'. A second row: 'Operate a transfer site | provincial environment ministry | approval | before opening | approval letter'. Date each source.

Activity. Build a rules table.

Standard to meet. Rows sourced and dated.

Module 3 Plan collection, sorting and treatment 10 hApply

Flow design decides cost.

  • Route collection; sort at source if possible.
  • Design the sorting line and storage.
  • Test contamination levels by customer to see who needs help sorting.
  • Record every load with date, source, weight and condition.
  • Place labelled bins where waste is made, using pictures as well as words.
  • Size storage for a week of peak flow and keep fire separation between piles.
  • Weigh the contaminants in ten sample loads.
Worked example. Contamination is 18% in mixed loads and 6% with source sorting. In a 50 t load that is 9 t versus 3 t, so source sorting removes 6 t of contamination.

Activity. Design a sorting flow.

Standard to meet. Flow diagram with limits.

Module 4 Cost per tonne and revenue 8 hEvaluate

Gate fees plus material sales.

  • List costs: collection, labour, equipment and disposal.
  • Add revenue per tonne from collection fees, gate fees and material sales.
  • Work out margin per tonne as price minus cost.
  • Work out break-even tonnes as fixed cost divided by margin per tonne.
  • Work out cost per tonne from trucks, fuel, labour and tipping fees divided by tonnes handled.
  • Check how much revenue depends on recovered-material prices, since those can fall.
  • Test a price fall, since recovered-material prices change.
Worked example. Price 180 per tonne; cost 120; margin 60; fixed cost 540,000 a year gives 540,000 / 60 = 9,000 t. If the price falls 10% to 162, margin is 42 and break-even rises to 540,000 / 42 = about 12,857 t. Teaching figures in CAD.

Activity. Build a cost sheet with price fall.

Standard to meet. Cases shown.

Module 5 Operate safely and measure 8 hApply

Keep records.

  • Weigh loads in and recovered material out so the tonnes balance.
  • Keep protective gear checked and in good condition for every worker.
  • Keep spill kits stocked and run spill and fire drills.
  • Report the diversion rate to management each month.
  • Weigh every load and keep a scale ticket with date, vehicle and weight.
  • Hold a daily briefing on hazards, protective gear, who is on site and exits.
  • Compute diversion rate as diverted tonnes divided by total tonnes.
Worked example. This month 400 t came in and 272 t were diverted, so the rate is 272 / 400 = 68%. Last month's rate was 61%. The change is 7 percentage points; write the cause.

Activity. Keep a 4-week operations log.

Standard to meet. Rates computed.

Module 6 Win contracts and partners 6 hCreate

Win with compliance and price.

  • Look for municipal and commercial tenders that match your permits.
  • Collect references from past clients.
  • Offer certified reporting of tonnes diverted.
  • Track bids, shortlists and wins.
  • Read each tender for scoring weights, mandatory forms and reporting duties.
  • Prepare references that show outcomes such as tonnes diverted.
  • Price with a margin and a fuel-escalation clause.
Worked example. Funnel: 15 bids, 4 shortlists, 2 wins. Shortlist rate is 4 / 15 = 27%, win rate from shortlist is 2 / 4 = 50%, and overall 2 / 15 = 13%.

Activity. Prepare a tender response.

Standard to meet. Response complete.

Module 7 Manage contamination, community and liability risk 6 hEvaluate

Harm lasts.

  • Expect odour, noise and traffic complaints and plan controls for each.
  • Plan for fire at storage areas.
  • Understand financial assurance: security a regulator may require to cover clean-up if you fail.
  • Keep insurance current for pollution, property and liability.
  • Keep a complaint log with date, type, response and closing date.
  • Reduce fire risk with separate piles, temperature checks, extinguishers and fire-truck access.
  • Check that insurance covers pollution and check the provincial financial-assurance rule.
Worked example. Risk: 'Storage fire | 2 x 5 | spacing, detection | site lead | monthly'. The score is 2 x 5 = 10; low likelihood but severe impact, so controls are checked monthly.

Activity. Write a risk register.

Standard to meet. Owners named.

Module 8 Pilot a stream 8 hCreate

Prove one stream.

  • Run the pilot for 60 days.
  • Measure diversion rate and cost per tonne.
  • Stop rule: contamination over 15% means fix.
  • Plan to scale only if the stream passes the stop rule.
  • Choose one stream, such as clean wood from three sites.
  • Weigh in, sort and weigh out, and keep the tickets.
  • Report results to the buyer and ask what they want improved.
Worked example. Contamination 8%: in 25 t that is 2 t. The stop line of 15% would be 3.75 t (25 x 0.15), so the pilot passes and you proceed to plan scale.

Activity. Run pilot review.

Standard to meet. Review has decision.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: value statement, rules table, core task record, cost sheet, 4-week log, funnel, risk register and privacy note, pilot review and 12-month plan. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet".

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. List the waste hierarchy.

Model answer: Reduce, reuse, recycle, recover, dispose. (Covered in module 1: Understand waste streams and remediation. To pass that module: Composition table made.)

Q2. What is a manifest?

Model answer: A document tracking hazardous waste. (Covered in module 2: Know environmental and handling rules. To pass that module: Rows sourced and dated.)

Q3. What is diversion rate?

Model answer: Share kept from landfill. (Covered in module 5: Operate safely and measure. To pass that module: Rates computed.)

Q4. Why sort at source?

Model answer: Lower contamination. (Covered in module 3: Plan collection, sorting and treatment. To pass that module: Flow diagram with limits.)

Q5. Who approves waste operations?

Model answer: The provincial environment authority, under the provincial rule. (Covered in module 2: Know environmental and handling rules. To pass that module: Rows sourced and dated.)

Q6. Why financial assurance?

Model answer: Clean-up cost if the operator fails. (Covered in module 7: Manage contamination, community and liability risk. To pass that module: Owners named.)

Q7. What is a gate fee?

Model answer: Fee for accepting waste. (Covered in module 4: Cost per tonne and revenue. To pass that module: Cases shown.)

Q8. What triggers a fix?

Model answer: Contamination over 15%. (Covered in module 8: Pilot a stream. To pass that module: Review has decision.)

Q9. Why drills?

Model answer: Spills and fires need quick response. (Covered in module 5: Operate safely and measure. To pass that module: Rates computed.)

Q10. Who assesses contaminated sites?

Model answer: Qualified professionals following standards. (Covered in module 2: Know environmental and handling rules. To pass that module: Rows sourced and dated.)

levels

Seven levels, L1 to L7

Use this ladder to see where you are and what to show next. Each level builds on the one below.

Seven levels, L1 to L7
LevelRoleYou canNotes and evidence
L1Sustainability aware learnerDefine: name the main terms, people and rules of waste management and remediationStart here if new. Evidence: a one-page glossary and a visit or interview note.
L2Field or plant assistantDescribe: explain how waste management and remediation works from first contact to resultEvidence: a flow chart and a two-minute spoken explanation.
L3TechnicianApply: carry out a routine task in waste management and remediation to a written standardEvidence: a supervised task record that meets the standard once.
L4SpecialistAnalyse: diagnose why a waste management and remediation result missed its target and fix itEvidence: a fault or gap analysis with a fix and a repeat test. This course targets this level.
L5Senior specialist or leadEvaluate: judge options for waste management and remediation against cost, risk and qualityEvidence: an options paper with numbers and a recommendation defended to a panel.
L6Programme managerCreate: design and pilot a new offer or process in waste management and remediationEvidence: a pilot with measured results over at least 30 days.
L7Director or founderLead: run a team or business in waste management and remediation and develop othersEvidence: a record of results over a year and of people you have coached to a higher level.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
