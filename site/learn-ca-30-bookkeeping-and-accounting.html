Bookkeeping and accounting

Run a real bookkeeping and accounting pilot in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small bookkeeping and accounting offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: finance and accounting. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the words of bookkeeping 6 hUnderstand

Bookkeeping is recording money in and out so that a business can see where it stands.

  • Learn the terms: asset, liability, equity, income, expense, debit, credit, ledger, invoice, receipt.
  • Assets are what a business has; liabilities are what it owes; equity is what is left.
  • Every entry has two sides: a debit and a credit that are equal.
  • Bookkeepers record; accountants and tax advisers give advice. Know the difference.
  • Sort fifteen transactions into asset, liability, equity, income or expense.
  • Describe the debit and credit for a sale paid in cash and for a bill paid later.
  • Explain why a bookkeeper does not give tax advice and who does.
Worked example. The business buys a printer for $300 cash. Debit equipment $300, credit cash $300, so both sides equal 300. A sale of $150 paid in cash is debit cash $150, credit sales $150.

Activity. Write ten terms with an example each and sort 15 transactions into income or expense.

Standard to meet. Task: sort and define. Condition: from a list, no help. Standard: all terms plain and correct and 15 of 15 transactions sorted.

Module 2 Explain the books from receipt to report 6 hApply

The books follow a path. If you can name it, you can keep it tidy.

  • The path: collect documents, record, categorise, reconcile, review, report.
  • A chart of accounts is the list of categories used for all entries.
  • Reconcile means compare your books to the bank statement and explain each difference.
  • Keep a file for every document: receipts, invoices, bank statements.
  • List the documents for one month: bank statement, sales invoices, bills and receipts.
  • Draw the path from document to report as a one-page flow chart.
  • Check that every bank line has a document behind it.
Worked example. Bank balance $1,200; books $1,050. A cheque for $150 has not cleared, so 1,200 - 150 = 1,050 and the books match. Write the $150 on the reconciliation as outstanding and clear it next month.

Activity. Set up a chart of accounts for a small shop and trace five transactions through it.

Standard to meet. Task: trace five transactions. Condition: using a sheet or free practice file. Standard: each entry is balanced and has a document.

Module 3 Record and reconcile to a standard 10 hApply

Now you do the work. Enter a month of transactions and prove the books agree with the bank.

  • Enter income and expenses with the date, who, what, amount and tax.
  • Categorise the same way each time.
  • Reconcile the bank each month; list every difference and fix it.
  • Produce a simple profit and loss and a list of who owes the business and whom it owes.
  • Compare total income and expenses in the books with bank deposits and withdrawals.
  • Flag any entry without a document and ask the client for it.
  • Check the tax amount on each entry against the invoice.
Worked example. A month: income $4,200, expenses $3,100, profit 4,200 - 3,100 = $1,100. The bank is reconciled with zero unexplained difference. If expenses had been $3,250, profit would be $950.

Activity. Complete one month of practice books and a reconciliation.

Standard to meet. Task: complete one month of books. Condition: from provided documents in three hours. Standard: debits equal credits, bank reconciled, and reports show a correct profit.

Module 4 Follow Canadian tax and record rules 8 hEvaluate

Canadian businesses have sales tax, payroll and record rules. Learn where they touch the books.

  • GST/HST and some provincial sales taxes may apply; registration depends on sales and rules. Check the current limits and rates with the tax agency.
  • If a business pays staff, it must withhold and send deductions to the tax agency by their due dates. Check the current rules.
  • Business records are generally kept for six years; check the current rule.
  • Keep client information private and give tax or legal advice only if you are qualified. Professional titles for accountants are protected; use only titles you hold.
  • List which clients collect sales tax and note each filing date.
  • Ask the tax agency for the due date of payroll remittances and write it down.
  • Design a folder system that holds six years of records and check the current retention rule.
Worked example. A client asks if a car expense is fully deductible. You write the question, the amount and the date in a questions list and refer it to a tax professional. If the car costs were $2,400 for the year, you record $2,400 in the car expense account and let the professional decide how much is deductible.

Activity. Make a table of tax or record item, who sets it, due date and where you checked, for ten items.

Standard to meet. Task: build a tax and records table. Condition: using official public pages. Standard: each row has a dated source and unclear items say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.

Module 5 Price your bookkeeping and check it pays 8 hApply

You are paid for accuracy and time. Work out what each client really costs you.

  • Time includes collecting documents, chasing missing items, entering, reconciling and questions.
  • Compare hourly, monthly fixed fee and per-transaction pricing.
  • A fixed fee needs a clear scope: number of transactions, accounts and reports.
  • Track hours per client to see if the fee is fair.
  • Calculate your hourly break-even from yearly costs and your pay target.
  • Compare a fixed fee with hourly time on the same client.
  • Add a rule for extra work: transactions above the agreed number cost extra.
Worked example. A client has 80 transactions a month. It takes 3 hours at $40 an hour: 3 x 40 = $120 a month. If the client sends 100 transactions and it takes 3.75 hours, the cost is 3.75 x 40 = $150, so an extra fee of $1.50 per extra transaction (20 x 1.50 = $30) covers the gap.

Activity. Time three practice clients and set a monthly fee for each.

Standard to meet. Task: price three clients. Condition: from your time log. Standard: hours, rate and fee are shown and a mentor can follow every number.

Module 6 Use software and find clients 6 hCreate

Software saves time. Clients come from trust.

  • Use accounting software with a bank feed and a clear audit trail; practise on a demo file.
  • Check each automated entry. The software suggests, you decide.
  • Reach local small businesses, with consent, and offer a free 20-minute review of their record keeping.
  • Ask permission before using any client example, and remove names.
  • Set up a demo file with a chart of accounts and import a sample bank file.
  • Create a software rule and check it files five test items correctly.
  • Draft a short message offering a free 20-minute review of record keeping.
Worked example. The bank feed suggests 'office' for a grocery bill. You change it to 'meals' after checking the receipt. If the feed brings 60 lines and you find 6 wrong, that is 6 / 60 = 10%, a good reason to review every line.

Activity. Practise software on a demo file and contact ten small businesses.

Standard to meet. Task: practise and reach out. Condition: demo data and consent-based contact. Standard: ten entries checked, ten contacts made and replies logged.

Module 7 Protect clients, data and accuracy 6 hEvaluate

Bookkeepers hold private financial information.

  • Keep client files in a secure place with strong passwords and two-step sign-in.
  • Back up data and test that you can restore it.
  • Use a second check on large or unusual payments.
  • Write an engagement letter that lists scope, fees, deadlines, who is responsible for what and how you keep information private.
  • List who has access to each file and remove access when a client leaves.
  • Practise a restore from backup each quarter and record the result.
  • Describe what you do on finding a mistake in a filed return: tell the client and a tax professional.
Worked example. Your letter says: 'The client provides all documents by the 10th. The bookkeeper reconciles by the 20th.' That gives 20 - 10 = 10 days for the work. You also set a limit you choose, such as a second check on any payment above $5,000.

Activity. Write an engagement letter and a monthly close checklist.

Standard to meet. Task: produce a letter and checklist. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: scope, fees, deadlines and privacy are stated in plain words.

Module 8 Run a real bookkeeping practice for 60 days 8 hCreate

Now you run it, measure and decide.

  • Set a goal such as two clients and two monthly closes.
  • Each week record hours, income, errors found and late items.
  • Ask each client for feedback.
  • Choose at the end: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Calculate your effective hourly rate as income divided by hours.
  • List the errors found and fix the step that caused each.
  • Evaluate whether a third client fits your hours.
Worked example. After 60 days: 2 clients, 14 hours, $560, no unexplained bank differences. Your rate is 560 / 14 = $40 an hour, equal to your target. You plan to add a third client.

Activity. Run the pilot and present a 12-month plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a 60-day pilot. Condition: real or practice clients with a signed letter. Standard: log complete, reconciliations clean and the decision uses numbers.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
