Ghost Kitchens: Cost a delivery-only menu with a partner kitchen.

Ghost Kitchens: Cost a delivery-only menu with a partner kitchen.

  • Understand Explain who needs Ghost Kitchens and name one real first user, with evidence from your own market scan.
  • Apply Build and run the practice task: Cost a delivery-only menu with a partner kitchen.
  • Analyse Record every step, cost and problem, then compare the plan with what really happened.
  • Evaluate Judge your result against your goal using numbers, and write three lessons learned.
  • Create Present your evidence to a review room and give two peers written feedback.
  • Create Plan your next 90 days and contribute one template or worked example back to learners.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Cost a delivery-only menu with a partner kitchen.

Condition

About 60 hours over 1-3 months. Own tools, free spreadsheet and form tools, and a real practice partner. Mentor and peer circle available. Currency written on every number.

Standard

Evidence reviewed by a mentor; at least 70% on the 10-question check; two written peer reviews given; every number carries its currency and unit.

Who it is for, requirements and materials

  • Who it is for: learners aged 16 and over, small-business starters, workers moving to a new field, and teams (see B2B, B2C, D2C).
  • Requirements: finish P2P Lending Platform Concepts first, or show equivalent work.
  • Materials included: lesson text, worked examples, case exercise, 10-question check, templates in My page.
  • Teaching team: a trainer from our network, a mentor who reviews your evidence, and a peer circle. Names are given at enrolment.

lesson by lesson

9 modules, in the order you do them

Module 1 Who needs this, and who is your first user IdeaStudy · 2 hUnderstand

Goal: name one real first user: a delivery-only meal buyer such as an office or a block of flats.

  1. Market scan: List the delivery-only food offers in your area. Write the menu size, average order value with currency, delivery time, and the most common complaint in public reviews.
  2. Talk to 3 to 5 possible users or experts. Ask: what do you do now, what is hard, what would make you trust a new service?
  3. Write a half-page idea: who, what you will do, what success looks like in numbers.

Evidence you keep: the scan notes and the half-page idea.

Module 2 Kick-off circle IdeaShare and mentoring · 3 hUnderstand
  1. Each learner reads their idea aloud (3 minutes).
  2. Peers ask three questions: who is it for, why you, what could go wrong.
  3. The mentor agrees the evidence you will produce in this course (listed at the end of each lesson).

Evidence you keep: your revised idea page and the agreed evidence list.

Module 3 Start the practice task ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Task steps:

  1. Choose one cuisine and a menu of 6 to 8 dishes that travel well. Say why each dish survives 30 minutes in a bag.
  2. Agree with a partner kitchen (a licensed shared kitchen or a quiet restaurant) when you may use it.
  3. Cost every dish: ingredients by weight, packaging, labour minutes, energy, share of kitchen rent. Currency on every line.
  4. Cook each dish twice. Time it, weigh it, taste it after 20 minutes in its box.

Evidence you keep: Dish costing sheet and travel-test notes.

Module 4 Short modules ActionStudy · 2 hApply
  • Food-safety licensing: a kitchen that sells food needs to be registered and inspected under local rules. Ask your local authority which licence applies and write down who told you.
  • Delivery options: your own rider, a local courier, or a collection point. Compare cost per order and control over quality.
  • Costing: food cost percent = ingredient cost divided by price. Add packaging, labour and rent. A dish that looks cheap can lose money.

Check: 10 questions at the end of the course.

Module 5 Continue and log ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Keep a record of every step, cost (with currency) and problem.

  1. Run a 2-week test with 20 real orders from a small group such as an office floor.
  2. Log order time, ready time, delivered time, waste, and complaints.
  3. Remove the 2 weakest dishes and re-cost the menu.

Evidence you keep: Order log and revised menu.

Module 6 Review circle ValueShare and mentoring · 6 hEvaluate
  1. Show your costing sheet.
  2. Peers challenge the one dish with the thinnest margin.
  3. Agree what to change: price, portion, ingredients, or drop the dish.

Evidence you keep: Costing sheet with peer notes.

Module 7 Finish and measure ValuePractice · 14 hEvaluate
  1. Finish the task and fix the biggest problem found in the circle.
  2. Measure the result against your goal: food cost percent, average delivery time, waste percent, orders per day.
  3. Write three lessons learned and one decision: scale, change or stop.

Evidence you keep: a before and after table and the decision note.

Module 8 Review room EmpowermentShare and mentoring · 3 hCreate
  1. Present to a review room: idea, task record, numbers, risks, decision.
  2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
  3. Get a written note from one real user or expert where possible.

Evidence you keep: slides or notes, two peer reviews, one outside note.

Module 9 Check and next step RegenerationStudy · 2 hCreate
  1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
  2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what to do, where, how much, what to learn next. Pick a follow-on course from the catalogue.
  3. Contribute back: share a template, checklist or worked example so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates are placeholders until the owner fixes the revenue plan).

Evidence you keep: check result and the 90-day plan.

practice material and assessment

Worked examples, case, check and local law pointers

Worked examples
  1. Idea: delivery-only rice bowls for an office block. Menu of 6.
  2. Dish costing: rice 0.6, protein 3.0, vegetables 1.0, sauce 0.4, box and bag 0.9 = 5.9. Price 14. Food cost percent (ingredients only 5.0) = 36%.
  3. Margin after labour (6 minutes at 20 per hour = 2), energy and rent share 1.5: 14 - 5.9 - 2 - 1.5 = 4.6 (same currency).
  4. Travel test: after 20 minutes the fried item went soft; replaced by a baked one.
Case exercise

A dish sells at 14 with ingredient cost 5.0 and total cost 9.4. A delivery partner takes 25% of price. Questions: Profit per order with and without the partner? Which price makes it worthwhile? Which dish would you drop? Write a one-page decision.

Check questions (draft set, 10)
  1. What is food cost percent? 2. Why must dishes survive in a box? 3. Which licence applies to you, and who told you? 4. Name three costs beginners forget. 5. Compute margin: price 18, cost 13 (same currency). 6. Why log delivery time? 7. How would you handle a complaint about cold food? 8. What allergen information must be clear? 9. Why is a shared kitchen useful at the start? 10. Scale, change or stop: what is your decision?
Law check (DRAFT, not legal advice)

Points to check for this course: Food-business registration and inspection, allergen information, labelling, delivery-platform terms.

Personal data: in the EU the data-protection regulation applies. India's data-protection Rules are phased in: consent managers around Nov 2026 and the main duties around May 2027. UAE executive regulations are still uncertain: check the official portal. Collect little, ask consent clearly, protect it, delete it.

Rules differ by country and change. Write down the rule you rely on, the official source (use the official portal where the rules are unclear) and the date you checked. Ask an adviser before acting for real. Everything in the course stays test, made-up or consented material.

Rules for this course

No health claims. Allergens always shown. Only use a licensed kitchen to cook for paying customers. No brand names or logos of other companies on any page or slide. No personal data in shared files. No medical, legal or investment advice.
