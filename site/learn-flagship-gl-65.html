flagship course

Greenhouse Gas Auditing

Measure, report and reduce greenhouse gas emissions for an organisation using recognised accounting scopes and data checks.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5 Specialist34:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain scopes 1, 2 and 3 and emission factors.
  • Apply Collect activity data and calculate emissions.
  • Analyse Find hotspots and data gaps.
  • Evaluate Judge data quality and boundaries.
  • Create Produce an inventory report and reduction plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Build a greenhouse gas inventory for one small organisation (practice case), with boundary, data, factors, hotspot analysis and reduction plan.

Condition

A computer, a spreadsheet, public emission factors, two peers and a mentor. A practice dataset may be used.

Standard

Boundary is stated; each number has unit and source; calculations reproduce; data quality is rated; plan has measurable targets; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Sustainability officers, finance staff, engineers, consultants in training. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Why measure emissions 3 hUnderstand

You cannot reduce what you do not count.

  • Greenhouse gases trap heat; carbon dioxide is the main one, followed by methane and nitrous oxide.
  • CO2e converts other gases to CO2 equivalent using warming factors.
  • Reporting supports saving money, customer requests and regulation.
  • Use recognised methods and keep records.
Worked example. Methane has a larger warming effect than CO2 per tonne, so a leaking gas line is expressed as tonnes CO2e. Replacing old lights with LEDs saves 2 tonnes CO2e and 20,000 INR a year.

Activity. Explain CO2e and a baseline year to a peer.

Standard to meet. Peer can explain both terms.

Module 2 Boundaries and scopes 4 hUnderstand

Decide what is in and what is out, and write it down.

  • Organisational boundary by control or ownership.
  • Scope 1: direct emissions from fuel, vehicles and leaks. Scope 2: purchased electricity, heat and cooling. Scope 3: everything else in the chain.
  • Record exclusions and reasons.
  • Keep the boundary the same each year.
Worked example. Company vehicles are scope 1; electricity is scope 2; business flights and purchased goods are scope 3. A rented warehouse is excluded and the reason is recorded.

Activity. Set a boundary for a practice organisation with two inclusions and two exclusions.

Standard to meet. Boundary is written and justified.

Module 3 Collecting activity data 5 hApply

Good data comes before good calculations.

  • Sources: bills, meters, fuel logs, travel records and purchase records.
  • Units must match: litres, kWh, km, kg.
  • Record source and date; keep invoices.
  • Fill gaps with clearly labelled estimates.
Worked example. Fuel invoices give 1,200 litres of diesel. Missing March data is estimated from February and April and flagged as an estimate.

Activity. Collect and list data for a practice case with sources.

Standard to meet. Every line has unit, source and date.

Module 4 Emission factors and calculation 5 hApply

Emissions equal activity data multiplied by an emission factor.

  • Use recent factors for your country and year and keep the version.
  • Show each calculation line by line.
  • Check units and decimal places.
  • Separate fuels, electricity and refrigerants.
Worked example. 1,200 L x 2.68 kg CO2 per litre = 3,216 kg CO2. 40,000 kWh x 0.82 = 32,800 kg CO2e.

Activity. Calculate five lines with a factor table.

Standard to meet. Maths and units are correct.

Module 5 Scope 3 estimates 5 hAnalyse

The chain is often the largest part, and the hardest to measure.

  • Start with the largest categories: purchased goods, travel, commuting, waste.
  • Use spend-based factors where activity data is unavailable and say so.
  • Ask the top suppliers for their data.
  • Improve estimates yearly.
Worked example. Purchased goods account for 35 per cent of the total using spend factors; commuting 11 per cent and travel 22 per cent.

Activity. Estimate two scope 3 categories and rank all categories.

Standard to meet. Method stated and ranking shown.

Module 6 Data quality and checks 4 hEvaluate

Numbers must be trusted.

  • Reconcile totals with bills and look for outliers.
  • Check for double counting.
  • Rate data quality high, medium or low.
  • Have a second person reproduce one number.
Worked example. A sample check finds a unit error (MWh entered as kWh) that changes the total by 1,000 times. A duplicated invoice is also removed.

Activity. Review a data set and log issues.

Standard to meet. At least three checks done and logged.

Module 7 Hotspots and reduction targets 4 hAnalyse

Decide where action pays most.

  • Rank sources by tonnes.
  • Estimate tonnes saved per rupee spent for each action.
  • Set targets with dates and owners.
  • Track progress quarterly.
Worked example. Fixing refrigerant leaks saves 6 tonnes CO2e for 15,000 INR; efficient lighting saves 8 per cent for a two-year payback.

Activity. Draw a hotspot chart and rank five actions.

Standard to meet. Ranking uses tonnes and cost.

Module 8 Reporting and verification 4 hCreate

Share results honestly.

  • Report summary, method, boundary, exclusions and limits.
  • Have an independent review where possible.
  • Update yearly and compare to the baseline.
  • Publish what you can and keep the evidence.
Worked example. The report states excluded sources, data quality ratings and the baseline. A peer repeats the diesel calculation and gets the same result.

Activity. Write the report and ask a peer to reproduce one number.

Standard to meet. Report is reproducible and peer confirmed one number.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: boundary note, data sheet, inventory, hotspot chart, plan. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Boundary and methodUnclearClearClear and verified
CalculationsErrorsCorrectReproducible
Reduction planNoneTargetsCosted targets
Source of factorsFactors unsourcedEach emission factor names its source, year and unitFactors from one consistent set, with any change of set explained
Reporting rulesReporting rules not checkedThe reporting rule and any threshold for the place are written downRule checked on the regulator's page and the report format used

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What are scope 1, 2 and 3?

Model answer: Direct, purchased energy, other chain emissions. (Covered in module 2: Boundaries and scopes. To pass that module: Boundary is written and justified.)

Q2. What is CO2e?

Model answer: CO2 equivalent of other gases. (Covered in module 1: Why measure emissions. To pass that module: Peer can explain both terms.)

Q3. Emissions formula?

Model answer: Activity data x emission factor. (Covered in module 4: Emission factors and calculation. To pass that module: Maths and units are correct.)

Q4. Why record sources?

Model answer: To audit. (Covered in module 3: Collecting activity data. To pass that module: Every line has unit, source and date.)

Q5. Name a scope 3 category.

Model answer: Purchased goods, travel, commuting or waste. (Covered in module 5: Scope 3 estimates. To pass that module: Method stated and ranking shown.)

Q6. Double counting?

Model answer: Counting the same emission twice. (Covered in module 6: Data quality and checks. To pass that module: At least three checks done and logged.)

Q7. Why local factors?

Model answer: Grids and fuels differ. (Covered in module 4: Emission factors and calculation. To pass that module: Maths and units are correct.)

Q8. Why state exclusions?

Model answer: Honesty. (Covered in module 8: Reporting and verification. To pass that module: Report is reproducible and peer confirmed one number.)

Q9. How rate data quality?

Model answer: High, medium, low. (Covered in module 6: Data quality and checks. To pass that module: At least three checks done and logged.)

Q10. How often update?

Model answer: Yearly. (Covered in module 8: Reporting and verification. To pass that module: Report is reproducible and peer confirmed one number.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Reporting rules and emission factors differ by place and year.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
