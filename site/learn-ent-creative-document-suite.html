Creative and Document Suite Practice: Design, PDF and Brand Control

Produce, protect and manage brand-safe designs and documents with a professional creative suite.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L1 to L7 ladder25:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Remember Remember : the tools and the file types.
  • Understand Understand : layout, type and colour basics.
  • Apply Apply : photo editing and export.
  • Apply Apply : documents, pdf, forms and signatures.
  • Analyse Analyse : brand systems and shared libraries.
  • Evaluate Evaluate : campaign production at scale.
  • Create Create : creative operations and governance.
  • Create Create : capstone: a full brand and document project.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce, protect and manage brand-safe designs and documents with a professional creative suite. Finish all 8 modules on a practice set-up.

Condition

A free trial or practice account, practice data only (never real customer data), a peer circle and a mentor for the final review.

Standard

Every module standard is met; records are kept; a peer and a mentor rate the work 'Meets' or better on the rubric.

Who it is for: Designers, marketers, office staff, teachers, small-business owners. Requirements: a computer and internet. Materials: slides with notes, worked examples, activities, quiz, rubric.

How the ladder works: modules climb from L1 (remember) to L7 (create); the ladder section shows each level as a slide. Take them in order; the first three suit new learners, 4 and 5 suit practitioners, 6 and 7 suit advanced staff.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The tools and the file types 2 hRememberL1 Basic

Know which tool does which job and which file to save.

  • Raster (photos) versus vector (logos and shapes).
  • Layout tool, photo tool, drawing tool, PDF tool.
  • Save an editable source and an export; name files with date and version.
Worked example. A logo is drawn as a vector so it prints sharp on a card and on a banner.

Activity. List five jobs and choose the tool and file type for each.

Standard to meet. Five correct pairs; file names follow the naming rule.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Photo or drawing?. Speaker note: Show a zoomed photo and a zoomed logo. Note: pixels blur, vectors stay sharp.
  2. Slide 2: Source and export. Speaker note: Why keep both.
Module 2 Layout, type and colour basics 2 hUnderstandL2 Awareness

Good design is clear before it is beautiful: one message, strong contrast, readable type.

  • Grid and spacing: align, repeat, leave room.
  • Two fonts at most; size shows importance.
  • Colour: brand palette, contrast checked for reading.
Worked example. A flyer with one headline, one picture and one action reads in 5 seconds.

Activity. Design one A4 flyer from a text brief.

Standard to meet. One message; contrast passes; two fonts; peers recall the action.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: One message. Speaker note: Remove everything that does not serve the message.
  2. Slide 2: Contrast. Speaker note: Show a pass and a fail.
Module 3 Photo editing and export 3 hApplyL3 Foundation

Edit without damaging the original and export for the right place.

  • Use layers and non-destructive edits.
  • Crop, straighten, correct light and colour.
  • Export sizes: print, web, social; check file size.
Worked example. A 12 MB photo becomes a 300 KB web image that still looks clean.

Activity. Edit three photos and export each in two sizes.

Standard to meet. Originals untouched; web files under 500 KB; no visible damage.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Layers. Speaker note: Show a layer stack; note that the original stays safe.
  2. Slide 2: Right size, right place. Speaker note: A table of sizes.
Module 4 Documents, PDF, forms and signatures 3 hApplyL4 Practitioner

Documents are evidence. Make them readable, fillable and signed correctly.

  • Create, combine, compress and protect a PDF.
  • Make a fillable form with required fields.
  • Request an electronic signature; keep the audit trail.
  • Make it accessible: headings, alt text, reading order.
Worked example. A three-page agreement is signed by two people in one day with a full audit record.

Activity. Create a fillable form and send it for signature to a practice partner.

Standard to meet. Form works; signature trail kept; accessibility check passes.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Document journey. Speaker note: Create, fill, sign, store.
  2. Slide 2: Accessible by design. Speaker note: Headings and alt text.
Module 5 Brand systems and shared libraries 3 hAnalyseL5 Specialist

A brand is a set of rules people can find and follow.

  • Library: colours, fonts, logos, templates.
  • Templates with locked and open parts.
  • Review flow: draft, comment, approve, publish.
Worked example. Ten staff make posters from one template; only the text and picture change.

Activity. Build a small brand kit and two templates; test with a peer.

Standard to meet. Peer produces a correct piece in 15 minutes without help.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Rules people can find. Speaker note: Where the kit lives.
  2. Slide 2: Locked and open. Speaker note: What users may change.
Module 6 Campaign production at scale 4 hEvaluateL6 Advanced

Make many pieces fast and keep quality.

  • Batch and automate repeat edits.
  • Version for each language and market.
  • Check list before publish: facts, spelling, rights, accessibility.
  • Track results and learn.
Worked example. One campaign becomes 24 pieces in 3 languages with one check list.

Activity. Plan and produce a 12-piece campaign with a check list and results sheet.

Standard to meet. All pieces pass the check list; results sheet compares two versions.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: One idea, many pieces. Speaker note: Show the family of pieces.
  2. Slide 2: Check list. Speaker note: Facts, rights, access.
Module 7 Creative operations and governance 4 hCreateL7 Super expert

At the top you manage people, licences, rights and cost.

  • Licence and seat plan; remove unused seats.
  • Rights register for pictures, fonts and music.
  • Archive and retention rules.
  • Training and onboarding plan.
Worked example. A rights register stops a picture being used after its licence ended.

Activity. Write an operations pack: seats, rights register, archive rule, onboarding.

Standard to meet. Pack has owners, dates and a yearly review.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Rights register. Speaker note: Columns and examples.
  2. Slide 2: Seat review. Speaker note: Cost saved.
Module 8 Capstone: a full brand and document project 4 hCreateL7 Super expert

Real work joins design, documents and rules in one job. In this capstone you plan, build, check and hand over a small brand pack and a signed document, then show others how to reuse it.

  • Plan first: audience, one message, list of pieces, deadline and who approves.
  • Build the set: a brand kit, two templates, a fillable form and a final export for print and web.
  • Run the check list before handover: facts, spelling, contrast, rights and accessibility.
  • Hand over with a short guide so another person can reuse the templates without help.
Worked example. A small shop needs a poster, a social post and a booking form. One kit gives all three the same colours and fonts, and the form is signed and filed with an audit record on the first day.

Activity. Produce a brand kit, two templates, one fillable form and one export set for a practice business, then let a peer make a new piece from your guide.

Standard to meet. All items pass the check list; the peer makes a correct piece in 15 minutes; files are named with date and version.

Slides with notes

  1. Slide 1: Capstone: a full brand and document project: the idea. Speaker note: Real work joins design, documents and rules in one job. In this capstone you plan, build, check and hand over a small brand pack and a signed document, then show others how to reuse it.
  2. Slide 2: Capstone: a full brand and document project: your turn. Speaker note: Produce a brand kit, two templates, one fillable form and one export set for a practice business, then let a peer make a new piece from your guide.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. A logo must print on a business card and on a large banner. Should it be a photo file or a vector file, and why?

Model answer: A vector file, because it stays sharp at any size. A photo is made of pixels and blurs when enlarged. (Covered in module 1: The tools and the file types. To pass that module: Five correct pairs; file names follow the naming rule.)

Q2. A campaign needs 24 pieces in 3 languages. How do you keep quality high and fast?

Model answer: Batch the repeat edits, make a version for each language and market, and run one check list before publishing covering facts, spelling, rights and accessibility. (Covered in module 6: Campaign production at scale. To pass that module: All pieces pass the check list; results sheet compares two versions.)

Q3. A flyer has three fonts, five colours and four messages. What would you change?

Model answer: Cut it to two fonts, the brand colours and one message with one clear action. Then check that the contrast is readable. (Covered in module 2: Layout, type and colour basics. To pass that module: One message; contrast passes; two fonts; peers recall the action.)

Q4. At the capstone handover another person must reuse your templates. What two things do you give them and how do you test it?

Model answer: Give a project folder with a brand kit and templates plus a short guide. Test it by letting a peer make a correct new piece in about 15 minutes without help. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: a full brand and document project. To pass that module: All items pass the check list; the peer makes a correct piece in 15 minutes; files are named with date and version.)

Q5. A 12 MB photo must go on a web page. What do you do?

Model answer: Edit a copy and export a web size, for example about 300 KB, keeping the original untouched. Check it still looks clean. (Covered in module 3: Photo editing and export. To pass that module: Originals untouched; web files under 500 KB; no visible damage.)

Q6. What does a non-destructive edit mean, and why use layers?

Model answer: The original pixels are never changed, so you can undo or adjust later. Layers hold each edit separately. (Covered in module 3: Photo editing and export. To pass that module: Originals untouched; web files under 500 KB; no visible damage.)

Q7. Name three steps to make a PDF agreement accessible.

Model answer: Add proper headings, add alt text to pictures and set a correct reading order. Then run the accessibility check. (Covered in module 4: Documents, PDF, forms and signatures. To pass that module: Form works; signature trail kept; accessibility check passes.)

Q8. A form is signed electronically by two people. What record should you keep and why?

Model answer: Keep the audit trail showing who signed and when. It is the evidence that the signature is genuine. (Covered in module 4: Documents, PDF, forms and signatures. To pass that module: Form works; signature trail kept; accessibility check passes.)

Q9. Ten staff make posters from one template. What should be locked and what should be open?

Model answer: Lock logo, colours and fonts; leave text and picture open. This keeps the brand safe while allowing quick work. (Covered in module 5: Brand systems and shared libraries. To pass that module: Peer produces a correct piece in 15 minutes without help.)

Q10. A picture licence ended last month. How does a rights register help?

Model answer: It records the licence end date, so the picture is removed before it is used again. It also shows who owns the check. (Covered in module 7: Creative operations and governance. To pass that module: Pack has owners, dates and a yearly review.)

levels L1 to L7

The ladder, slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: L1 Basic

Remember the difference between photo files and vector files, and name the tool for each job. Save a source file and an export with a clear name.

Speaker notes. Show a zoomed photo and a zoomed logo side by side. Common mistake: using a small photo logo on a large banner.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: L2 Awareness

Understand how grid, spacing, two fonts and strong contrast make one message clear. Explain why less is often better.

Speaker notes. Ask learners to find the one message in a flyer. Common mistake: adding extra pictures and colours.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: L3 Foundation

Apply layers, crop and light correction to edit photos without harming the original. Export the right size for print, web and social.

Speaker notes. Check file sizes and that originals are untouched. Common mistake: saving over the only copy.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: L4 Practitioner

Apply PDF tools to combine, protect and fill documents and request a signature. Make the file accessible with headings and alt text.

Speaker notes. Run the accessibility check together. Common mistake: scanning a page as a picture so the text cannot be read by screen readers.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: L5 Specialist

Analyse how a shared kit and locked templates keep a brand consistent. Compare a template that works with one that people break.

Speaker notes. Test the template with a peer who has not seen it. Common mistake: leaving too many parts open to change.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: L6 Advanced

Evaluate a batch of campaign pieces against a check list and a results sheet. Decide which version worked better and why.

Speaker notes. Ask for facts, rights and access to be checked for every piece. Common mistake: judging results on taste, not numbers.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: L7 Super expert

Create a full project and an operations pack with seats, rights register, archive rules and onboarding. Teach others to reuse it.

Speaker notes. Check that every rule has an owner and a date. Common mistake: no yearly review, so unused seats and expired rights go unnoticed.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local law changes how you store customer data and issue invoices. Check it for your place first.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Use INR for amounts. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Use EUR for amounts. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Use AED for amounts. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Use BRL for amounts. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Use CAD for amounts. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Use USD for amounts. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Use SGD for amounts. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Use AUD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Use HKD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Use GEL or ILS for amounts. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Use KYD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Use MXN for amounts. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
