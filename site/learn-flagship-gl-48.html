Nanotechnology in Medicine (Awareness Level)

Understand how very small materials are used in medicine, from imaging to drug delivery, and how safety, testing and ethics apply. Awareness only.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5 Specialist21:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain what nanoscale means and give three medical uses.
  • Apply Use size comparisons and units correctly.
  • Analyse Compare benefits and risks of nanomedicines.
  • Evaluate Judge claims in news and adverts.
  • Create Write a balanced one-page briefing.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write a two-page briefing on three medical uses of nanotechnology, with benefits, risks, evidence stage and ethical questions.

Condition

A computer, public information, two peers and a mentor. No laboratory work is done in this course.

Standard

Units and sizes are correct; each use has an evidence stage; risks are stated; no overclaims; sources dated; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Science students, biomedical trainees, health writers, regulators' assistants. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The nanoscale 2 hUnderstand

A nanometre is one billionth of a metre. At this size, materials can behave differently from the same material at ordinary size.

  • 1 metre = 1,000,000,000 nanometres. A human hair is about 80,000 nm wide, a red blood cell about 7,000 nm, a virus about 100 nm, a DNA strand about 2 nm.
  • Small particles have a large surface compared with their volume, so they react faster and interact with cells differently.
  • Gold particles look red or purple in liquid, not yellow, because of how light interacts with them.
  • Nano does not mean magic; it means a size range with new properties to test and control.
Worked example. A gold nanoparticle 20 nm wide is about 4,000 times narrower than a hair. A rapid test strip uses such particles to show a coloured line when a marker is present.

Activity. Make a size chart from a metre to an atom with at least eight objects and their sizes in nanometres.

Standard to meet. All sizes are within the correct order of magnitude and the chart is labelled with units.

Module 2 Imaging and diagnostics 2 hUnderstand

Nanoparticles can make signals clearer or detect small amounts of a marker in a sample.

  • Contrast agents improve scan images in some tests.
  • Test strips use nanoparticles to make a line visible, and sensors can detect very small amounts of a substance.
  • False positives and false negatives still occur, so results must be confirmed when they matter.
  • Always follow instructions and storage conditions of a test.
Worked example. A pregnancy test strip uses coloured nanoparticles attached to antibodies. They gather at the test line only if the hormone is present, giving a visible line.

Activity. Explain a test strip to a peer in your own words using a drawing.

Standard to meet. Peer can repeat how the line forms and one limit of the test.

Module 3 Drug delivery 3 hUnderstand

Carriers can protect a drug and help it reach where it is needed.

  • Lipid (fat-based) particles protect fragile genetic medicines during delivery.
  • Targeting aims to put drug where it is needed and reduce side effects elsewhere.
  • Particles can be cleared by the liver and kidneys or trigger immune reactions.
  • Each carrier is tested for size, stability, safety and release.
Worked example. Some vaccines use fat-based particles around genetic instructions so that they survive until they reach cells, then release their cargo.

Activity. Draw a delivery diagram labelling carrier, drug, target and clearance route.

Standard to meet. Diagram labels all four and has no false claims.

Module 4 Particles in the body 3 hAnalyse

Size, shape and surface decide where particles go and how long they stay.

  • Very small particles may pass through kidneys; larger ones are caught by the liver and spleen.
  • Surface coatings change how the immune system sees them.
  • Long-term build-up is a concern for some materials.
  • Animal-free and human-cell methods help early safety testing where possible.
Worked example. Studies often report particles around 100 nm circulating longer than particles of 500 nm, which are filtered quickly.

Activity. Make a table of size, likely route and a concern for three particle types.

Standard to meet. Entries agree with the sources you cite.

Module 5 Testing and regulation 3 hEvaluate

Nanomedicines go through extra checks because size and surface strongly affect behaviour.

  • Path: lab studies, safety testing, human trials, regulator review, approval, monitoring after sale.
  • Regulators ask for particle size distribution, stability, purity and batch consistency.
  • Manufacturing changes can change behaviour, so they are controlled.
  • Report side effects to the national system.
Worked example. A batch is rejected because particle size varied beyond the allowed range. The factory fixes mixing time and re-tests before release.

Activity. Map the path from lab to patient with at least six stages and one check at each.

Standard to meet. All stages present; checks are specific.

Module 6 Risks and unknowns 3 hEvaluate

An honest briefing lists uncertainty as clearly as benefit.

  • Possible concerns: inflammation, accumulation, allergy, effects over many years.
  • Environmental release during making and disposal needs control.
  • Workers handling powders need closed systems and protective equipment.
  • State what is not yet known.
Worked example. A plant uses closed handling and extraction, masks for sampling, and a waste route for nano-containing material.

Activity. List five risks, a control for each and one unknown.

Standard to meet. Each risk has a control and the unknown is stated honestly.

Module 7 Hype, claims and headlines 2 hAnalyse

News often reports lab results as if they were cures.

  • Stage check: idea, lab or cell study, animal study, human trial, approved.
  • Be wary of "miracle" words and of results in a single small study.
  • Look for independent reviews and trial registry entries.
  • Ask who funded the work.
Worked example. A headline says tiny particles "destroy cancer". The study is in cells in a dish. The learner labels it "lab stage, not a treatment".

Activity. Review three real headlines and assign each an evidence stage.

Standard to meet. Each stage is justified with a quote.

Module 8 Write the balanced briefing 3 hCreate

Combine facts, benefits, risks and fairness.

  • Cover three uses with benefits, risks and stage.
  • Add access and cost questions.
  • State what is unknown.
  • Test with a non-expert.
Worked example. The reader can explain why approval takes years and why one lab result is not enough.

Activity. Write and present a two-page briefing.

Standard to meet. Reader repeats three limits correctly.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: briefing, size chart, claim review. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Science accuracyErrorsCorrectCorrect with sources
Risks and unknownsMissingListedListed with controls
Claim reviewNoneDoneDone with evidence stage
Claim sourcesUses are described with no study citedEach of the three uses cites a study stage (lab, animal, human)Stage cited with year, and one conflicting finding included
Ethics and public concernEthical questions not raisedConsent, access and unknown long-term risk each raised as a questionEach question matched to a public body's guidance, with the date

Model answer: Imaging, test strips or drug delivery. (Covered in module 2: Imaging and diagnostics. To pass that module: Peer can repeat how the line forms and one limit of the test.)

Q3. Why does size matter in the body?

Model answer: It decides where particles go and how they clear. (Covered in module 4: Particles in the body. To pass that module: Entries agree with the sources you cite.)

Q4. What do regulators check?

Model answer: Size distribution, stability and safety. (Covered in module 5: Testing and regulation. To pass that module: All stages present; checks are specific.)

Q5. Name a risk.

Model answer: Inflammation, accumulation or allergy. (Covered in module 6: Risks and unknowns. To pass that module: Each risk has a control and the unknown is stated honestly.)

Q6. Name a worker control.

Model answer: Closed systems or masks. (Covered in module 6: Risks and unknowns. To pass that module: Each risk has a control and the unknown is stated honestly.)

Q7. How judge a headline?

Model answer: Find the evidence stage. (Covered in module 7: Hype, claims and headlines. To pass that module: Each stage is justified with a quote.)

Q8. Who gets access?

Model answer: Access and cost are fairness questions that a balanced briefing should raise. (Covered in module 8: Write the balanced briefing. To pass that module: Reader repeats three limits correctly.)

Q9. Is lab result a cure?

Model answer: No. (Covered in module 7: Hype, claims and headlines. To pass that module: Each stage is justified with a quote.)

Q10. Why monitor after approval?

Model answer: Long-term effects can appear. (Covered in module 5: Testing and regulation. To pass that module: All stages present; checks are specific.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Medicine regulation differs by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text.
