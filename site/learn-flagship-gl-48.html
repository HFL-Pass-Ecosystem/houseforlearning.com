Nanotechnology in Medicine (Awareness Level)

Understand how very small materials are used in medicine, from imaging to drug delivery, and how safety, testing and ethics apply. Awareness only.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L5 Specialist21:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain what nanoscale means and give three medical uses.
  • Apply Use size comparisons and units correctly.
  • Analyse Compare benefits and risks of nanomedicines.
  • Evaluate Judge claims in news and adverts.
  • Create Write a balanced one-page briefing.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Write a two-page briefing on three medical uses of nanotechnology, with benefits, risks, evidence stage and ethical questions.

Condition

A computer, public information, two peers and a mentor. No laboratory work is done in this course.

Standard

Units and sizes are correct; each use has an evidence stage; risks are stated; no overclaims; sources dated; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Science students, biomedical trainees, health writers, regulators' assistants. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The nanoscale 2 hUnderstand

A nanometre is one billionth of a metre.

  • A hair is about 80,000 nm wide; a virus about 100 nm.
  • Materials behave differently at this size.
  • Units: 1 m = 1,000,000,000 nm.
Worked example. Gold looks red when particles are tiny, not yellow.

Activity. Make a size chart from hair to atom.

Standard to meet. Orders of magnitude correct.

Module 2 Imaging and diagnostics 2 hUnderstand

Tiny particles can make signals clearer.

  • Contrast agents and rapid test strips use nanoparticles.
  • Sensors can detect small amounts of a marker.
  • False results still occur; follow-up tests are needed.
Worked example. A rapid test shows a coloured line using gold particles.

Activity. Explain a test strip in your own words.

Standard to meet. Peer understands.

Module 3 Drug delivery 3 hUnderstand

Carriers can protect a drug and aim it.

  • Lipid particles carry some vaccines and drugs.
  • Targeting may reduce side effects.
  • The body can clear or react to carriers.
Worked example. Fat-based particles shield fragile genetic material during delivery.

Activity. Draw a delivery diagram.

Standard to meet. Diagram labels carrier, drug, target.

Module 4 Materials in the body 3 hAnalyse

Where particles go matters.

  • Size, shape and surface decide where they travel.
  • Liver and kidneys clear many particles.
  • Long-term effects need study.
Worked example. Particles above a certain size stay in the liver longer.

Activity. Make a table of size and body route.

Standard to meet. Table is consistent with sources.

Module 5 Testing and regulation 3 hEvaluate

Nanomedicines go through extra checks.

  • Lab, animal-free methods where possible, human trials, approval.
  • Regulators ask for size distribution, stability and safety.
  • Post-approval monitoring continues.
Worked example. A batch is rejected because particle size varied.

Activity. Map the path from lab to patient.

Standard to meet. All stages shown.

Module 6 Risks and unknowns 3 hEvaluate

Honest briefings include uncertainty.

  • Possible inflammation, accumulation, allergy.
  • Environmental release during manufacture and disposal.
  • Workers need protection from dust.
Worked example. A plant uses closed systems and masks for powders.

Activity. List risks and controls.

Standard to meet. Each risk has a control.

Module 7 Hype and claims 2 hAnalyse

Sort proven from promised.

  • Check evidence stage: idea, lab, trial, approved.
  • Be careful with "miracle" words.
  • Look for independent reviews.
Worked example. A news story calls a lab result a cure; the learner marks it "lab stage".

Activity. Review three headlines.

Standard to meet. Stage is named for each.

Module 8 Write the briefing 3 hCreate

Balance benefit, risk and fairness.

  • Cover access and cost.
  • State what is unknown.
  • Test with a non-expert.
Worked example. The reader can explain why approval takes years.

Activity. Finish and test.

Standard to meet. Reader repeats three limits.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: briefing, size chart, claim review. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Science accuracyErrorsCorrectCorrect with sources
Risks and unknownsMissingListedListed with controls
Claim reviewNoneDoneDone with evidence stage
Honesty and evidenceClaims without proofEvery claim has a recordEvery claim linked to a dated record and checked by a peer
Safety, ethics and local rulesNot consideredChecked and written downChecked, dated and explained to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. How many nanometres in a metre?

Model answer: 1,000,000,000.

Q2. Name a medical use.

Model answer: Imaging, test strips or drug delivery.

Q3. Why does size matter in the body?

Model answer: It decides where particles go and how they clear.

Q4. What do regulators check?

Model answer: Size distribution, stability and safety.

Q5. Name a risk.

Model answer: Inflammation, accumulation or allergy.

Q6. Name a worker control.

Model answer: Closed systems or masks.

Q7. How judge a headline?

Model answer: Find the evidence stage.

Q8. Who gets access?

Model answer: A fairness question to state.

Q9. Is lab result a cure?

Model answer: No.

Q10. Why monitor after approval?

Model answer: Long-term effects can appear.

ten locations and global

Your place

Medicine regulation differs by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
