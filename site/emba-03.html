executive MBA subject 03

Team Skills

Pre-programme, open term. 3.5 ECTS, about 88 hours, 4 chapters. Free for everyone.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task, condition and standard

Task

Agree a team charter and run a fair peer review for a four-week team task.

Condition

Given a team of four to six people, at least two of them in other time zones.

Standard

The charter has roles, meeting rules and a conflict step, and the peer review scores are explained with examples.

Who it is for: Anyone who will work in study groups, project teams or remote teams during the programme.

how this subject works

Free entry, voice and your learning style

Free for everyone. This pre-programme subject is open to all, with no payment and no sign-in needed. It is a good first step if you are new to study or to computers.

  • Listen. Every part has a Listen button that reads it aloud.
  • Speak or type. Answer each part by talking (press Speak) or by typing. Your answers stay on this device only.
  • Learn your way. Every part has a See, Hear, Do and Read-and-write route. Not sure which suits you? Take the eight-question learning-style check.

4 chapters

The chapters, with worked examples

Chapter 1 Team formation and roles 22 hUnderstand: explain

New teams go through forming, testing each other, settling and performing. Clear roles shorten the testing stage.

  • Name a leader for each task, not one boss for everything
  • Give every role a clear output
  • Agree how decisions are made: vote, consensus or owner decides

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. A team of 5 has 10 possible pairs to talk to each other (5 x 4 / 2 = 10). A team of 8 has 28 pairs. That is why bigger teams need clearer roles and fewer meetings.

Activity: Write the roles for your study team with one output each.

Standard: Every member has a role and a clear output, and the decision rule is written.

Chapter 2 Conflict and negotiation in teams 22 hApply: resolve

Conflict about tasks can improve work; conflict about people damages it. Move arguments back to facts and shared goals.

  • Separate the person from the problem
  • Find the interest behind each position
  • Look for an option that meets both interests

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Two members argue: one wants to start writing now, one wants more research. Interests: the first fears the deadline, the second fears weak evidence. Option: split 3 days research, 4 days writing within the 7-day deadline, with a mid-point check.

Activity: Describe a past team disagreement and write the interests behind each side.

Standard: Both interests stated fairly and one option that serves both.

Chapter 3 Virtual and cross-cultural teams 22 hApply: organise

Remote teams lose the quick chat in the corridor. Replace it with written updates, clear time zones and respect for different working styles.

  • Publish a shared time-zone table and a core overlap hour
  • Write decisions down in one shared place
  • Agree how direct feedback should be

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Members are in Dubai (UTC+4), London (UTC+0) and Singapore (UTC+8). 12:00 in Dubai is 08:00 in London and 16:00 in Singapore: a fair overlap hour for all three.

Activity: Build a time-zone table for your team and pick one overlap hour.

Standard: All members listed with correct offsets and an overlap hour inside 08:00 to 18:00 for everyone.

Chapter 4 Team charter and peer review 22 hCreate: agree and review

A charter is a one-page agreement written at the start. Peer review at the end is fairer when it uses the charter as the yardstick.

  • Charter: goal, roles, meeting rules, conflict step, quality bar
  • Peer review: score each person on agreed criteria with examples
  • Discuss the scores, do not just send them

Worked example: Worked-example figures, in your currency. Four criteria scored 1 to 5: on time 4, quality 5, helping others 3, communication 4. Total 16 of 20, or 80%. The example under "helping others" shows what to improve.

Activity: Write your team charter on one page and agree it with the team.

Standard: One page, all five parts included and agreed by every member.

ten checks

Check yourself

Say or write your answer first, then read the model answer.

  1. Q1. How many pairs are there in a team of 5?
    Model answer: 10. (Covered in chapter 1: Team formation and roles.)
  2. Q2. Why do larger teams need clearer roles?
    Model answer: The number of links grows fast, so confusion grows too. (Covered in chapter 1: Team formation and roles.)
  3. Q3. Name three ways a team can decide.
    Model answer: Vote, consensus, or the owner decides. (Covered in chapter 1: Team formation and roles.)
  4. Q4. Which kind of conflict helps work?
    Model answer: Conflict about the task, not about people. (Covered in chapter 2: Conflict and negotiation in teams.)
  5. Q5. What is an interest?
    Model answer: The need or fear behind a stated position. (Covered in chapter 2: Conflict and negotiation in teams.)
  6. Q6. 12:00 in Dubai (UTC+4) is what time in London (UTC+0)?
    Model answer: 08:00. (Covered in chapter 3: Virtual and cross-cultural teams.)
  7. Q7. Where should remote teams record decisions?
    Model answer: In one shared written place. (Covered in chapter 3: Virtual and cross-cultural teams.)
  8. Q8. Name the five parts of a charter.
    Model answer: Goal, roles, meeting rules, conflict step, quality bar. (Covered in chapter 4: Team charter and peer review.)
  9. Q9. Scores of 4, 5, 3 and 4 out of 5: what percentage?
    Model answer: 16 of 20, 80%. (Covered in chapter 4: Team charter and peer review.)
  10. Q10. Why use the charter in peer review?
    Model answer: It gives an agreed, fair yardstick. (Covered in chapter 4: Team charter and peer review.)
