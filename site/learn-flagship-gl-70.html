flagship course

Podcasting

Plan, record, edit, publish and grow a podcast with good audio, honest rights, and a simple way to earn.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L3 Foundation29:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain formats, audiences and the production chain.
  • Apply Record clean audio and edit with free tools.
  • Analyse Read listener data.
  • Evaluate Judge rights, ethics and sponsors.
  • Create Publish a three-episode season.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and publish a three-episode podcast season with show plan, scripts, clean audio, show notes, rights check and listener review.

Condition

A phone or mic, free editing software, a quiet room, two peers and a mentor.

Standard

Audio is clear with steady levels; every episode has notes and a rights check; episodes publish on a free host; listener data reviewed; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Creators, teachers, small businesses, community groups. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Concept, audience and promise 3 hUnderstand

A podcast succeeds when one kind of listener knows exactly what they will get. Start with that promise, not with equipment.

  • Write the promise in one sentence: "For [listener], this show gives [benefit] every [time] in [length]".
  • Listen to five shows in your topic. Note length, format, opening, and what you would do differently.
  • Choose a format that suits you: solo talk, interview, story, panel or news. Interviews need guest management; solo shows need discipline.
  • Choose a schedule you can keep for 20 episodes, not 5.
Worked example. "For small farmers, this show gives one tested tip every Monday in 12 minutes." The host can now say no to topics that do not fit and a listener can decide in ten seconds.

Activity. Write your one-sentence promise, a 5-line show plan (format, length, schedule, first six episode ideas) and a listener profile.

Standard to meet. Promise names listener, benefit, rhythm and length; six episode ideas fit the promise.

Module 2 Scripting and structure 4 hCreate

A script keeps you short and clear. It need not be word for word, but the opening and closing should be written out.

  • Structure: hook (under 30 seconds), context, body in 2 or 3 points, one action, sign-off.
  • Write for the ear: short sentences, everyday words, one idea per sentence.
  • Use signposts such as "first", "second" and "last" so listeners can follow.
  • Time yourself reading aloud; speaking runs about 140 words per minute.
Worked example. A 12-minute episode needs about 1,700 words (12 x 140). The host writes 250 words for the opening and first point, bullet notes for the middle, and a written closing of 100 words.

Activity. Script your first episode and time it by reading aloud.

Standard to meet. Script runs within 10 per cent of the target length and has a hook and an action.

Module 3 Recording clean audio 5 hApply

Clean sound matters more than expensive microphones.

  • Record in a small room with soft surfaces: curtains, rugs, a wardrobe full of clothes or blankets.
  • Keep the mic 10 to 15 cm from your mouth and speak slightly across it to reduce harsh sounds.
  • Use headphones to monitor; keep peaks below the red zone and record a few seconds of room tone.
  • Switch off fans, phones and fridge noise; record a backup copy.
Worked example. Recording moves from a bare kitchen to a bedroom with a mattress against one wall. Echo falls so much that the editor needs no special filter.

Activity. Record a 2-minute test in two rooms and compare.

Standard to meet. Chosen recording has steady level, little noise and no clipping.

Module 4 Editing and sound quality 5 hApply

Editing is mostly deleting. Free software is enough.

  • Remove long pauses, false starts, repeated words and loud breaths; keep natural rhythm.
  • Apply gentle noise reduction and a limiter (which stops loud peaks); avoid heavy effects.
  • Match loudness between episodes and check on phone speakers and earbuds.
  • Export in a standard format and keep the project file as backup.
Worked example. A 20-minute raw recording becomes a 14-minute episode after cutting two tangents and four false starts. Loudness is set to the same level as the last episode.

Activity. Edit your first episode and keep before and after files.

Standard to meet. Edited episode is within target length and sounds equal in level start to finish.

Module 5 Rights, consent and ethics 3 hEvaluate

Respecting rights keeps your show alive and your guests safe.

  • Do not use popular music without a licence. Use music you made or music with a licence that allows podcast use, and credit as required.
  • Short quotes may be allowed in some places; check the rule and give credit.
  • Get a guest's spoken and written consent to record and publish.
  • Do not publish private information, defamatory claims or misleading advice.
Worked example. A host records a one-line consent at the start ("Do you agree that this interview can be published?") and stores a signed note with the files.

Activity. Complete a rights sheet for one episode: music, quotes, guest consent and images.

Standard to meet. Every item has a source or permission and a date.

Module 6 Publishing and promoting 3 hApply

Publishing is a repeatable routine.

  • Write a clear title, a three-sentence description and time stamps.
  • Upload audio to a podcast host and submit the feed link to listening apps once.
  • Make a square cover image that is readable at small size.
  • Share short clips, quotes or questions on a platform where your listeners are.
Worked example. Episodes are released on Monday at 6 am so they are ready for commuters. A 30-second clip with captions brings new listeners.

Activity. Publish a trailer or first episode and a checklist for each release.

Standard to meet. Episode is live in at least one app and the checklist is complete.

Module 7 Listener data and improvement 3 hAnalyse

Numbers show where listeners lose interest.

  • Look at downloads, listen-through rate and drop-off points.
  • Ask for feedback with one question: what would you change?
  • Change one thing per episode so you can see the effect.
  • Keep a simple results table.
Worked example. Retention falls at minute 8 in four episodes. The host moves the long interview later and shortens the intro; retention improves by 10 per cent.

Activity. Write three findings and one test for the next three episodes.

Standard to meet. Findings are based on numbers and the test is measurable.

Module 8 Earning, sustaining and growing 3 hEvaluate

A podcast earns slowly and honestly.

  • Income sources: sponsors, listener support, courses, services and events.
  • Disclose sponsors at the start of the episode.
  • Plan a calendar and batch-record to avoid burn-out.
  • Review quarterly: keep, change or stop.
Worked example. A show with 800 listeners per episode earns from a local sponsor at a flat monthly fee and a paid mini-course. The host records four episodes in one day each month.

Activity. Write a six-month plan with release calendar, income goal and review date.

Standard to meet. Plan shows realistic numbers and a review date.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: show plan, 3 episodes, show notes, rights sheet, review. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
AudioNoisyClearClear and balanced
RightsMissingCheckedChecked and credited
GrowthNo reviewReviewReview with changes
Rights recordMusic or clips used with no permissionEvery sound and clip has a licence or source notedLicence terms read and the credit line checked
Copyright and consentRights not checkedCopyright, guest consent and data rule are written downRelease signed by every guest and the law checked for the place

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Hook purpose?

Model answer: Capture attention. (Covered in module 2: Scripting and structure. To pass that module: Script runs within 10 per cent of the target length and has a hook and an action.)

Q2. Clipping?

Model answer: Distorted loud peaks. (Covered in module 3: Recording clean audio. To pass that module: Chosen recording has steady level, little noise and no clipping.)

Q3. Mic distance?

Model answer: About 10 to 15 cm (a hand width). (Covered in module 3: Recording clean audio. To pass that module: Chosen recording has steady level, little noise and no clipping.)

Q4. Rights rule?

Model answer: Use only what you may. (Covered in module 5: Rights, consent and ethics. To pass that module: Every item has a source or permission and a date.)

Q5. Guest consent?

Model answer: Required. (Covered in module 5: Rights, consent and ethics. To pass that module: Every item has a source or permission and a date.)

Q6. Drop-off?

Model answer: Where listeners leave. (Covered in module 7: Listener data and improvement. To pass that module: Findings are based on numbers and the test is measurable.)

Q7. Sponsor rule?

Model answer: Disclose. (Covered in module 8: Earning, sustaining and growing. To pass that module: Plan shows realistic numbers and a review date.)

Q8. Why room tone?

Model answer: Fix gaps. (Covered in module 3: Recording clean audio. To pass that module: Chosen recording has steady level, little noise and no clipping.)

Q9. Show notes use?

Model answer: Search and clarity. (Covered in module 6: Publishing and promoting. To pass that module: Episode is live in at least one app and the checklist is complete.)

Q10. Free host?

Model answer: Distributes feed. (Covered in module 6: Publishing and promoting. To pass that module: Episode is live in at least one app and the checklist is complete.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Music and privacy laws differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.
