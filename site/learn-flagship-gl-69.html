Boat and Helicopter Tourism

Plan a small boat or helicopter tour offering with safe operations, licences, weather rules, guest care and pricing.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner31:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain operator duties and passenger safety.
  • Apply Run briefings and weather checks.
  • Analyse Compute cost per hour and per guest.
  • Evaluate Judge risk and environment.
  • Create Write an operations plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan a tour product with route, safety briefing, weather rules, licences, price and incident plan, working with licensed operators.

Condition

A computer, a map, a spreadsheet, two peers and a licensed operator mentor. Learners do not operate vessels or aircraft.

Standard

Licences and insurance are checked with sources; weather go/no-go rules are written; briefing covers equipment and emergencies; cost per guest is correct; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Tour planners, guides, hotel desks, port staff. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Designing the tour product 3 hUnderstand

A tour is a product: a route, a time, a promise and a safety plan. Design all four before selling a single seat.

  • Types: scenic loops, sunset trips, wildlife viewing, island transfers and special-occasion flights.
  • Fix duration, number of guests, boarding point, landing or mooring point and toilet or rest access.
  • Plan accessibility: step-free boarding, help for older guests and children, seat limits.
  • Add a weather back-up: a shorter route, a new date or an indoor alternative.
Worked example. A 45-minute coastal route has a 20-minute boarding window, 8 guests, a sunset slot in season and a harbour museum visit as the rainy-day alternative.

Activity. Design a route with a map, timings, capacity, accessibility notes and a weather alternative.

Standard to meet. Route has times, capacity, access notes and a backup plan.

Module 2 Licences, certificates and insurance 5 hApply

Only licensed operators, qualified crews and certified vessels or aircraft carry paying guests.

  • Check vessel or aircraft certificates, crew licences and operator permits with the authority.
  • Confirm passenger insurance and its limits in writing.
  • Write down expiry dates and set reminders one month ahead.
  • If the operator is a partner, you still check; you cannot hand off your duty to guests.
Worked example. A tour desk finds that a boat certificate expires on 30 November. It stops selling trips after that date until the new certificate is shown.

Activity. Build a licence and insurance checklist with source and expiry date for each item.

Standard to meet. Every item has an authority, document number or practice placeholder and a date.

Module 3 Weather rules and go/no-go decisions 5 hApply

Safety beats schedule. Rules must be written before the day, with numbers.

  • Choose limits: wind speed, wave height, visibility, cloud ceiling and temperature.
  • Use two forecast sources and a rule for when they disagree: follow the cautious one.
  • Name one decision maker with authority to cancel and record each decision.
  • Plan the guest message: reason, new date or refund, and a thank-you.
Worked example. The rule says: no trip if wind exceeds 20 knots or visibility is under 5 km. At 07:30 the forecast shows 24 knots; the decision maker cancels and sends the message by 08:00.

Activity. Write go/no-go rules with numbers and a message template.

Standard to meet. Rules have limits, decision maker and guest message.

Module 4 Briefing and equipment 4 hApply

Guests who understand the rules stay safe and enjoy the trip more.

  • Briefing items: equipment, exits, seat belts or life jackets, behaviour and what to do in an emergency.
  • Use plain words, pictures and the guests' language; ask guests to repeat key steps.
  • Inspect equipment before each trip and log it.
  • Special briefing for children, older guests and guests with disabilities.
Worked example. Guests repeat how to fasten a life jacket before boarding. A picture card on every seat shows the steps.

Activity. Write a briefing script under five minutes and make a picture card.

Standard to meet. Script covers equipment, exits, behaviour and emergency, and a peer can repeat it.

Module 5 Emergency planning and drills 4 hEvaluate

Emergencies are rare, so practice is the only preparation.

  • Plan for man overboard or passenger emergency, fire, engine or equipment failure and medical events.
  • Assign roles, contacts and radio channels; keep emergency numbers visible.
  • Practise a drill every month and record the time to complete it.
  • Review each drill and change the plan.
Worked example. A drill finds the radio channel on the sheet was wrong. The sheet is corrected and the next drill recovers a dummy in 90 seconds.

Activity. Write an emergency plan and run a table-top drill.

Standard to meet. Plan has roles, contacts and drill results.

Module 6 Costs, price and break-even 4 hAnalyse

Tours are expensive to run; price must cover real costs.

  • Costs: crew, fuel, maintenance, landing or mooring fees, insurance, marketing and booking fees.
  • Cost per guest = hourly cost x hours / guests.
  • Break-even load factor = cost per trip / (price x seats).
  • Seasonal pricing and group offers must stay above cost.
Worked example. Hourly cost 40,000 INR for a 1-hour trip with 8 seats is 5,000 INR per seat. At 6,000 INR a seat, break-even is 83 per cent full.

Activity. Build a cost sheet and break-even load factor.

Standard to meet. Maths is correct and the currency is shown.

Module 7 Environment and community 3 hEvaluate

Tourism lasts only if neighbours and nature are protected.

  • Noise, wildlife disturbance, emissions and waste.
  • Fly or sail around nesting and breeding areas and keep minimum heights or distances.
  • Use local guides and suppliers; share some benefit with the community.
  • Publish your commitments.
Worked example. The route avoids a nesting beach in spring and keeps above 300 m over houses. Neighbours receive a flight schedule.

Activity. List five environmental and community commitments with measures.

Standard to meet. Each commitment has a number or rule.

Module 8 Operations plan and guest care 3 hCreate

Bring it all together into a plan staff can follow.

  • Roles, schedule, records, training and spare parts.
  • Guest journey: booking, reminders, arrival, briefing, trip, thanks and feedback.
  • Complaints and refunds within a stated time.
  • Review each season.
Worked example. The plan reaches an average guest score of 4.7 out of 5 in the first 20 trips and cuts late arrivals with a reminder message.

Activity. Write the operations plan and present it.

Standard to meet. Plan includes roles, records, guest journey and a review date.

Model answer: Equipment, exits, behaviour. (Covered in module 4: Briefing and equipment. To pass that module: Script covers equipment, exits, behaviour and emergency, and a peer can repeat it.)

Model answer: Hourly cost x hours / guests. (Covered in module 6: Costs, price and break-even. To pass that module: Maths is correct and the currency is shown.)

Model answer: Passengers. (Covered in module 2: Licences, certificates and insurance. To pass that module: Every item has an authority, document number or practice placeholder and a date.)

Q7. Why check expiry?

Model answer: Valid permits. (Covered in module 2: Licences, certificates and insurance. To pass that module: Every item has an authority, document number or practice placeholder and a date.)

Q8. Environment control?

Model answer: Avoid nesting sites. (Covered in module 7: Environment and community. To pass that module: Each commitment has a number or rule.)

Q9. Cancel policy?

Model answer: Refund. (Covered in module 3: Weather rules and go/no-go decisions. To pass that module: Rules have limits, decision maker and guest message.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Maritime and aviation law differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
