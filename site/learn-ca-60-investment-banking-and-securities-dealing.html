Investment Banking and Securities Dealing

Run a pilot of investment Banking and Securities Dealing in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small investment Banking and Securities Dealing offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: finance and accounting. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know what investment banking and dealing are 6 hUnderstand

This course explains how the work is done. It is education only and not advice. Consult a licensed professional about your own money.

  • Explain that a bank can help a company or government raise money by selling shares or bonds.
  • Explain that a securities dealer buys and sells securities for clients or for itself.
  • Explain a market in plain words: buyers and sellers who meet at a price.
  • Name the main players: issuers, investors, dealers, regulators and exchanges.
  • Explain primary market (new sales) and secondary market (resale).
  • Describe one way a Canadian company can raise money, such as a first share sale or a bond, in your own words.
  • Sort five example businesses into issuer, investor or dealer by what each one does.
Worked example. A company sells 2,000,000 new shares at 15 dollars each in the primary market. It raises 2,000,000 x 15 = 30,000,000 dollars before costs. Later, investors resell those shares among themselves in the secondary market, and the company receives none of that money.

Activity. Draw a map of the players in one example share sale and explain each role in one sentence.

Standard to meet. Task: map the players. Condition: one worked example. Standard: every role named correctly with its job and who pays whom.

Module 2 Know the rules and who must be registered 6 hApply

Dealing in securities is closely regulated in Canada. Know the rules before you do any work.

  • Explain that dealers and advisers need registration with the provincial securities regulators; check the current rule with your regulator.
  • Explain that a self-regulatory body also sets conduct rules for dealers; look up the current one with the regulator.
  • Explain insider trading: using or sharing private facts that could move a price is illegal.
  • Explain a prospectus: the legal document a company gives investors; there are exemptions, so check the rule before you rely on one.
  • Explain anti-money-laundering duties: know your client, keep records and report suspicious activity.
  • Write a one-page do and do-not list for a new trainee: what to share, what to keep private and who to ask.
  • Name the compliance contact in a made-up firm and write when you would go to that person.
Worked example. An analyst learns of a coming takeover before it is public. The correct steps are to not trade, not tell anyone and tell the compliance team. She also writes down the time she reported it, so the record shows she acted at once.

Activity. Build a rules table with rule, who enforces it, what you must do and a dated official source.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table. Condition: official regulator pages only. Standard: each row has an enforcer, action and dated source and no rule is guessed.

Module 3 Read company numbers and judge value 10 hApply

Bankers and dealers read financial statements to judge what a company is worth.

  • Name the three statements: income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement.
  • Compute profit margin = profit / sales.
  • Compute debt ratio = total debt / total assets.
  • Explain that value comes from future cash, and that estimates vary.
  • Compare a company to a few similar ones, not to a single one.
  • Compute return on equity = profit / owners' equity from a made-up balance sheet.
  • Compute price-to-earnings ratio = share price / earnings per share and compare it with similar firms.
Worked example. Sales 2,000,000; profit 160,000. Profit margin = 160,000 / 2,000,000 = 8%. Three similar firms show 6%, 7% and 10%, so 8% is in the middle. If total debt is 300,000 and total assets are 1,000,000, the debt ratio = 300,000 / 1,000,000 = 30%.

Activity. Read a made-up set of statements and write a half-page summary with five ratios.

Standard to meet. Task: summarise a company. Condition: made-up statements. Standard: ratios are correct, similar firms are compared and limits of the estimate are stated.

Module 4 Follow how a deal is prepared 8 hEvaluate

A deal runs in steps with many checks. Know the steps and who does each.

  • List the steps: goal, advisers, due diligence, documents, regulator filing, marketing, pricing, closing.
  • Explain due diligence: checking the facts about a company before a deal.
  • Explain how a price range is set from comparisons and demand.
  • Explain confidentiality: deal facts are private until announced.
  • Keep a deal checklist with owners and dates.
  • Write a due diligence question list that covers sales, debts, contracts, lawsuits and owners.
  • Explain a demand book: investors say how many shares they would buy at each price, and the price is set where demand meets the shares on offer.
Worked example. A deal timeline shows 12 weeks: 4 for checks, 3 for documents, 2 for regulator review, 2 for marketing and 1 for closing, and 4 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 12. If the sale raises 30,000,000 dollars and the made-up bank charge is 5%, the charge is 30,000,000 x 0.05 = 1,500,000 dollars.

Activity. Write a deal timeline and checklist for a made-up share sale.

Standard to meet. Task: plan a deal. Condition: made-up company. Standard: every step has an owner and a date, and the confidential steps are marked.

Module 5 Handle client orders and trading fairly 8 hApply

Dealing means handling orders. Fairness and records matter most.

  • Explain order types in plain words: market order and limit order.
  • Explain best execution: trying to get the client a fair result on price, cost and speed.
  • Explain that client orders come before your own trades.
  • Record the time, price and client for every order.
  • Explain settlement: the later day when cash and securities change hands.
  • Explain the bid and the ask: the best price a buyer will pay and the best price a seller will accept at a moment.
  • Explain that settlement timing is set by market rules; check the current settlement cycle with the regulator.
Worked example. A client places a limit order to buy 500 shares at 20 or less. The price stays at 20.40, so the order waits and does not fill at a higher price. If the price later falls to 20.00 and the order fills, the cost is 500 x 20 = 10,000 dollars.

Activity. Walk through five made-up orders and record the details and outcomes.

Standard to meet. Task: process practice orders. Condition: made-up market data. Standard: each record has time, type, price, outcome and reason.

Module 6 Manage risk and keep records 6 hCreate

Firms limit losses with rules and checks. Records prove you followed them.

  • Explain market risk, credit risk and operational risk.
  • Explain limits: a cap on how much risk one person or desk can take.
  • Explain separation of duties: one person trades, another checks.
  • Keep records of calls, messages and orders for the time the regulator requires.
  • Report errors and breaches quickly to compliance.
  • Explain liquidity risk: not being able to sell a holding quickly without a large drop in price.
  • Explain a stress test: asking how much a desk would lose if prices fell sharply.
Worked example. A trader enters 10,000 shares instead of 1,000. A system limit blocks the order and the check person confirms the error. The incident is logged with the time and the cause, and the extra 9,000 shares are never bought.

Activity. Write a one-page risk register with eight risks, a control for each and an owner.

Standard to meet. Task: write a risk register. Condition: one page. Standard: each risk has likelihood, harm, control, owner and review date.

Module 7 Communicate honestly and manage conflicts 6 hEvaluate

Trust is the core asset. Honest words and open conflicts keep it.

  • Never promise or guarantee results.
  • Tell clients about conflicts in writing; examples include a firm that advises a company and also sells its shares.
  • Explain information barriers between teams that hold private facts and teams that trade.
  • Write research and notes in plain words, with sources.
  • Treat clients fairly whatever their size.
  • Explain that gifts and perks from clients or issuers must be disclosed and approved; check the firm's policy and the provincial rule.
  • Rewrite a promise such as 'this will double' into an honest sentence that states the risk.
Worked example. A firm advises a company on a sale and its trading team wants to trade that company's shares. An information barrier blocks the sharing and the trading team is told to stay out. A research note that says 'this stock will double' is changed to 'growth is expected, and the price could also fall', with sources added.

Activity. Write a half-page note on a made-up conflict with the steps you would take.

Standard to meet. Task: process a conflict. Condition: one made-up case. Standard: the conflict is named, disclosed and handled by steps that protect the client.

Module 8 Run a supervised practice deal for real 8 hCreate

Run the full cycle in a safe setting: made-up or supervised work with a registered professional.

  • Work with a registered supervisor on any real matter.
  • Run the cycle: prepare, check, price, execute, settle, record.
  • Review the results and lessons within a week.
  • Keep all records dated and complete.
  • Plan your next training step and check the current exam and registration route with the regulator.
  • Hold a short review meeting where each person names one thing that went well and one thing to change.
  • Compare the planned timeline with the real one in weeks and explain each gap.
Worked example. A learner runs a practice share sale on paper. The plan was 12 weeks, but the due diligence list was 2 weeks late, so the real time is 12 + 2 = 14 weeks. She adds an earlier start to the next plan.

Activity. Run a practice deal end to end and present the review to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a practice deal. Condition: made-up data or a registered supervisor. Standard: the cycle is complete, records are dated and the review reasons from evidence.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
