flagship course · GL-26

Box Subscription Services

Design and run a small subscription-box service: pick a theme, source and cost the box, set a fair price, ship on time and keep subscribers happy.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Design and run a small subscription-box service: pick a theme, source and cost the box, set a fair price, ship on time and keep subscribers happy.

  • Understand Explain the subscription model, churn and lifetime value.
  • Apply Source and cost the contents of a box.
  • Analyse Analyse price, margin and break-even.
  • Create Create the box, packing and shipping plan, and a fair cancellation process.
  • Evaluate Evaluate retention and satisfaction after the first cycles.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and deliver two cycles of a small box (at least 10 subscribers or practice buyers) with costings, packing, delivery and feedback.

Condition

A theme, suppliers, packaging, a sign-up form, a spreadsheet, a mentor with retail experience and a peer circle. About 12 weeks part-time.

Standard

Landed cost per box and margin calculated; delivery on the promised day for 95% of boxes; cancellation possible in one click; consent kept; ratings of 4 of 5 or better; churn measured.

Who it is for: Small makers, shop owners, hobby experts, students. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Follow consumer rules on clear pricing, renewals and cancellation. Sell only safe, labelled goods. Use practice payments if you have no licensed partner.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The subscription idea 4 hUnderstand

People subscribe for convenience, discovery or habit. You will be able to define a target subscriber and test willingness to pay, so you commit to a box people truly want.

  • Pick a theme with repeat need: tea, craft, snacks, books, plant care.
  • Define the subscriber and the monthly promise.
  • Frequency and size.
  • Name and short story.
  • Define your customer in one sentence, such as busy parents who want a monthly craft kit, and test the idea by asking ten of them what they would pay.
  • Choose a theme narrow enough to source reliably yet broad enough for at least twelve distinct monthly variations, so subscribers never receive repeated boxes.
  • Test the box idea with a one-off box first.
  • Ask two suppliers for samples and quotes.
Worked example. Theme "Weekend Baker": 3 mixes, 1 tool, 1 recipe card each month.

Activity. Write theme, subscriber and promise.

Standard to meet. Fit the promise into one sentence.

Common mistake. Choosing a theme you like instead of one that repeats.

Second worked example. Theme 'Desk Garden': 2 seed packs, 1 small pot and 1 care card each month. The promise: something green on your desk by the end of the week.

Check yourself. Why will your subscriber want a second box?

Module 2 Source and cost 6 hUnderstand

Know the true cost of every item. You will be able to compare supplier quotes at several volumes and calculate a true cost per box including delivery and packaging.

  • Quotes from at least two suppliers.
  • Landed cost = item + delivery in + tax + packaging.
  • Quality checks and samples.
  • Back-up suppliers.
  • Get quotes from three suppliers for each item and compare unit prices at 50, 100 and 250 units, adding delivery and packaging to find the true cost per box.
  • Order one sample of every item and check its quality, size and arrival time, and reject any supplier that misses its promised delivery date.
  • Write the cut-off and ship dates on the page.
  • Show the contents list or a teaser.
Worked example. Item 2.00 + delivery in 0.30 + packaging 0.20 = 2.50 each (an estimate; check your market); five items = 12.50. Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Cost one full box.

Standard to meet. Calculate landed cost per item and per box correctly.

Common mistake. Pricing from the supplier quote only.

Second worked example. A plant set: pot 3.50, seeds 1.00, care card 0.30, tissue 0.20, plus 0.40 to receive them: 5.40 per set. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. What is your landed cost?

Module 3 Price and margin 8 hApply

Price must cover everything and leave profit. You will be able to set a box price with a healthy margin and design plan lengths that bring cash in sooner.

  • Box cost + packing + shipping + fees + marketing.
  • Margin % = (price - cost) / price.
  • Break-even subscribers = fixed costs / margin per box.
  • Test willingness to pay.
  • Work out your box cost first, for example 9.00 for items, 2.50 for packaging and 4.00 for postage, then set a price that gives a margin of at least 35 percent. Amounts are in your currency.
  • Compare monthly, quarterly and prepaid plans, and offer a small saving on the longer plans so that your cash arrives earlier.
  • Keep a reserve of the key item.
  • Plan a pause option for customers.
Worked example. Price 25 a box; cost 12.50 + pack 1.50 + ship 4.00 + fee 0.90 = 18.90; margin 6.10 (24%); fixed 300 → 50 boxes to break even. Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Build the pricing sheet.

Standard to meet. Compute the break-even.

Common mistake. Forgetting free shipping in the margin.

Second worked example. A box that sells at 32 costs 22 to make and send, so each leaves 10; 400 of fixed cost needs 40 boxes a month. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. Is shipping in your margin?

Module 4 Sign-up and rules 8 hApply

Be clear and fair. You will be able to write fair sign-up terms and set billing and address cut-off dates that keep your monthly orders predictable.

  • Show price, renewal date and how to cancel before payment.
  • Consent for contact.
  • Pause and skip options.
  • Refund policy in plain words.
  • Write clear terms covering the billing date, skip and cancel rules, the address cut-off day and refund conditions, and show them to the customer before payment.
  • Set a sign-up cut-off, such as the 20th of each month, so you know the exact number of boxes to buy and pack.
  • Track return and damage causes.
  • Photograph every packed box.
Worked example. The sign-up page says "25 per month, renews on the 5th, cancel any time in one click". Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Draft sign-up page and policy.

Standard to meet. Use plain words and a one-click cancel.

Common mistake. Hiding cancel links.

Second worked example. The page says 'Pay 19 on the first of each month. Pause for a month or cancel with one click. Refund within 14 days.' Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. Where is cancel?

Module 5 Packing and surprise 8 hAnalyse

Unboxing is the product. You will be able to design a box around one main item, avoid repeat contents, and pack it so it survives the journey.

  • Layout, protection, card, branding.
  • Weight and size for cheap shipping.
  • Test drop and shake.
  • Keep a few spares.
  • Plan each box around one main item plus supporting pieces, and keep a spreadsheet of what each subscriber has already received so nobody gets a repeat.
  • Write a packing checklist with a photo, weigh a finished box, and drop it once from waist height to confirm that the contents survive.
  • Add a thank-you card with the recipe.
  • Send a tracking link on dispatch day.
Worked example. Switching to a flat box saves 1.20 postage per box. Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Pack and test five boxes.

Standard to meet. Test that nothing breaks.

Common mistake. Over-packing.

Second worked example. Packing tissue by hand adds 2 minutes a box; a printed sleeve replaces it and saves 40 minutes on a batch of 20 boxes.

Check yourself. What is your packed weight?

Module 6 Ship and track 10 hCreate

Reliability keeps subscribers. You will be able to choose box sizes and carriers by price band and keep a log that exposes late or damaged deliveries.

  • Cut-off dates and ship day.
  • Labels and tracking.
  • Handle failed deliveries and replacements.
  • Message before and after dispatch.
  • Compare carrier prices for your packed weight and choose a box size that falls in the cheaper price band, then label each box with its order number.
  • Send tracking details to each subscriber on dispatch day, and keep a log of late or damaged parcels to raise with the carrier.
  • Collect reasons when someone cancels.
  • Offer a gift option with a clear price.
Worked example. 48 of 50 boxes arrive on the promised day = 96%.

Activity. Deliver cycle one.

Standard to meet. Calculate the delivery rate.

Common mistake. Promising a ship date you cannot meet.

Second worked example. 55 of 60 boxes arrive within 3 days: 92%. Tracking shows 4 delayed at one depot, so the next batch ships a day earlier.

Check yourself. What is your ship date?

Module 7 Retention and churn 6 hEvaluate

Keeping a subscriber costs less than finding one. You will be able to calculate monthly churn, find why subscribers leave, and test small changes that encourage them to stay.

  • Churn = cancellations / subscribers at start.
  • Lifetime value = margin per box x months kept.
  • Ask why they cancel.
  • Improve box, perks, community.
  • Track monthly churn as cancellations divided by active subscribers; if it stays above 10 percent, survey those who left and fix the most common reason.
  • Add a small surprise, a thank-you note or a skip option, and measure over the next three months whether cancellations fall.
  • Check labelling rules for the goods inside.
  • Review profit after every cycle.
Worked example. 10 of 100 cancel: churn 10%; average life 10 months; lifetime margin 61. Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Measure churn and survey leavers.

Standard to meet. Record churn and reasons.

Common mistake. Ignoring leavers.

Second worked example. 15 of 150 cancel in a month: churn 10%. Asking the 15 shows 9 wanted fewer items, so a smaller plan is added.

Check yourself. Why did subscribers leave?

Module 8 Review and plan 4 hCreate

Decide how to grow safely. You will be able to read a monthly results table and set one realistic growth target before spending on new subscribers.

  • Cycle two improved by feedback.
  • Add a gift option or bundle.
  • Plan stock for growth.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Each month, review revenue, box cost, margin, and new and lost subscribers, and record the figures in one table that you update the same day.
  • Set a growth target such as 20 more subscribers in 90 days, choose one channel to reach them, and review the result before changing your plan.
  • Plan the next three themes.
  • Write a 90-day plan with subscribers and margin targets.
Worked example. Feedback says too many snacks: swap one for a tool; rating rises to 4.6.

Activity. Run cycle two and report.

Standard to meet. Tie changes to feedback.

Common mistake. Skipping cycle-two changes.

Second worked example. Feedback says the cards are too long; they are cut to half a page and the rating moves from 4.0 to 4.4.

Check yourself. What changed in cycle two?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. The late supplier

A key item arrives a week late.

Question: What do you do?

Model answer: Tell subscribers early, swap in an equal item or ship in two parts, and record the supplier failure.

Case 2. The cancellation complaint

A customer says they could not find cancel.

Question: What now?

Model answer: Refund the last charge if law requires, fix the cancel path and log the change.

Glossary

Churn
Share of subscribers who cancel.
Landed cost
Full cost to get an item ready to ship.
Break-even
Point where income equals costs.
Lifetime value
Margin from one subscriber over their stay.
Cut-off date
Last day to join for the next box.
Unboxing
The experience of opening the box.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is churn?

Model answer: The share of subscribers who cancel in a period. (Covered in module 7: Retention and churn. To pass that module: Record churn and reasons.)

Q2. Landed cost?

Model answer: Item plus inbound delivery, tax and packaging. (Covered in module 2: Source and cost. To pass that module: Calculate landed cost per item and per box correctly.)

Q3. Margin percent formula?

Model answer: (Price - cost) divided by price. (Covered in module 3: Price and margin. To pass that module: Compute the break-even.)

Q4. Break-even subscribers?

Model answer: Fixed costs divided by margin per box. (Covered in module 3: Price and margin. To pass that module: Compute the break-even.)

Q5. What must sign-up show?

Model answer: Price, renewal date and how to cancel. (Covered in module 4: Sign-up and rules. To pass that module: Use plain words and a one-click cancel.)

Q6. Why offer pause?

Model answer: It keeps people who need a break. (Covered in module 4: Sign-up and rules. To pass that module: Use plain words and a one-click cancel.)

Q7. How to cut postage?

Model answer: Lighter, flatter packs. (Covered in module 5: Packing and surprise. To pass that module: Test that nothing breaks.)

Q8. What is lifetime value?

Model answer: Margin per box times months kept. (Covered in module 7: Retention and churn. To pass that module: Record churn and reasons.)

Q9. Why back-up suppliers?

Model answer: To avoid missing a cycle. (Covered in module 2: Source and cost. To pass that module: Calculate landed cost per item and per box correctly.)

Q10. How measure happiness?

Model answer: Ratings, comments and retention. (Covered in module 7: Retention and churn. To pass that module: Record churn and reasons.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Renewal and cancellation rules and import duties vary by place; check consumer law. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Partner to practise with: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Basics of subscriptions

On the slide: L1 Basic · Basics of subscriptions

Notes: Describe a subscription box and what a subscriber expects each month. Notes: look at one box you or a friend receive and list what is in it. Standard: five items listed with a guess of cost each.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Awareness

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Awareness

Notes: Describe the box cycle (choose, buy, pack, ship, renew) and the reasons subscribers cancel. Notes: read public reviews of two boxes and list the top three complaints. Standard: each complaint matched to a cycle step.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Foundation

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Foundation

Notes: Cost one box: items, packing, postage and loss, and set a price with margin. Notes: practice figures with currency. Standard: margin per box correct and a break-even number of subscribers.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Practitioner

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Practitioner

Notes: Pack and send five practice boxes, time each step and collect feedback. Notes: include one late and one damaged case. Standard: all five delivered or explained, with feedback recorded.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Specialist

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Specialist

Notes: Plan and deliver two cycles of a small box for at least 10 subscribers or practice buyers, with costings, packing, delivery and feedback. Notes: report churn between cycles. Standard: cost per box checked against the plan and one change made from feedback.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced: Advanced

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Advanced

Notes: Run several boxes with supplier contracts, a forecast and a cancellation policy. Notes: add a monthly churn and stock review. Standard: forecast within 10% and cancellation terms clear before sign-up.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert: Super expert

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Super expert

Notes: Set the plan, churn and fulfilment method for subscription boxes and teach the managers who use it. Notes: re-read the consumer-subscription and cancellation rules for the place each year.
