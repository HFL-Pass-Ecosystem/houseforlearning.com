Online marketplace selling partner

Run a pilot of online marketplace selling partner in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small online marketplace selling partner offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: commerce, retail and online selling. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know words, buyers and how an online marketplace works 6 hUnderstand

An online marketplace is a website where many sellers list goods and buyers pick from them. Start with the basic words.

  • Learn the terms: listing, SKU, stock, fee, shipping, return, review, seller account.
  • A listing has a title, photos, a description, a price and a delivery cost.
  • The marketplace is the shop floor; you still own the product, the promise and the customer care.
  • Browse ten listings and note what makes one easy to trust.
  • Read the seller rules and write the three that matter most for your goods.
  • Compare two marketplaces on fees, buyers and rules.
  • List what a buyer sees first: photo, price, rating and delivery cost.
Worked example. A listing for a used bike helmet shows size, condition, four clear photos, a price and the delivery cost in the first screen. Of ten listings you browse, 7 show the delivery cost up front, so 7 / 10 = 70 percent, and you decide to do the same.

Activity. Pick ten listings in one product group and mark title, photo quality, price, delivery and reviews on each.

Standard to meet. Task: label the parts of ten listings. Condition: from saved pages and no help. Standard: every part labelled and one trust sign named per listing.

Module 2 Explain the order flow from listing to delivery 6 hApply

Every sale follows the same steps. If you can name them, you can plan your day.

  • The steps are list, order received, pick, pack, ship, deliver, handle questions, handle returns, get paid.
  • Payment from the marketplace often arrives after a delay and after fees are taken out; read the payout rules.
  • Write down who does each step and how long it takes.
  • Keep one order sheet: date, item, price, fees, shipping, status.
  • Set the latest time of day for an order to ship that day.
  • Learn the payout date and the fee taken before payout.
  • Write what you do if an item sells but is out of stock.
Worked example. An order arrives on Monday. You pack it Tuesday, ship Wednesday, record the tracking number and message the buyer the same day. That is 2 days from order to shipping, so a promise of 'ships within 3 days' is safe.

Activity. Draw the flow on one page and time a mock order from start to finish.

Standard to meet. Task: explain and time the order flow. Condition: with a pretend order and a timer. Standard: all nine steps named in order and the time for each written down.

Module 3 Create listings and pack orders to a standard 10 hApply

Now you do the work. A good listing is clear, honest and complete.

  • Write a plain title: what it is, size or model, colour, condition.
  • Take photos in daylight on a plain background: front, back, label, flaws.
  • Say what is true. Show every flaw. Never claim a result you cannot prove.
  • Pack so the item survives a drop; weigh and measure the parcel before you price shipping.
  • Use the same photo background for all listings.
  • Add measurements in centimetres and weight in grams.
  • Compare two carrier prices for the packed weight.
Worked example. Before: 'Great item, must see.' After: 'Steel water bottle, 750 ml, blue, small dent on base (see photo 4). Ships in 2 days.' The bottle is 350 g and the box 150 g, so you price shipping for 350 + 150 = 500 g.

Activity. Create five listings and pack one mock parcel. Ask a friend to find faults.

Standard to meet. Task: write five listings and pack one parcel. Condition: within three hours. Standard: every listing states condition and delivery cost, flaws are shown, and the parcel passes a gentle drop test.

Module 4 Follow Canadian rules for selling online 8 hEvaluate

Selling to the public has rules. Learn which ones touch your listings and sales.

  • Advertising and prices must be true and clear. Do not hide fees or make false claims.
  • Consumer protection rules about returns, refunds and warranties are set by each province; check the current rule with the local authority.
  • Sales tax: you may need to register for GST/HST once your sales pass the small-supplier limit. Check the current limit with the tax agency.
  • Some goods have extra rules, such as child products, electrical items, food and goods with labels. Check before you list them. Do not sell fakes or recalled goods.
  • Write a return policy that follows your province's rule and the marketplace's rule.
  • Check whether your goods need bilingual or content labels; check the current rule.
  • Keep sales and tax records for the period the tax agency requires.
Worked example. A buyer asks for a refund on a damaged item. You check the marketplace policy and the provincial rule, then reply in writing with the choice you will offer. You keep the reply with the order record.

Activity. Build a table of rule, who sets it, what you must do and where you checked, for ten things you will sell.

Standard to meet. Task: build a rules table for your product group. Condition: using official public pages. Standard: each row has a source and date; unclear cases say 'check the current rule with the local authority'.

Module 5 Price, fees and profit 8 hApply

You earn only what is left after every cost. Work it out before you list.

  • Costs: item cost, marketplace fee, payment fee, packing, shipping, returns, your time.
  • Profit per sale = price minus all costs. Margin = profit divided by price.
  • Compare your price with ten similar listings, but do not copy a price that loses money.
  • Track tax collected separately; it is not your income.
  • Recompute profit when fees or shipping prices change.
  • Include a share of returns in your cost.
  • Set a floor price below which you do not sell.
Worked example. Sale price 40. Item cost 18, fees 6, packing 2, shipping paid by you 7, so total cost = 18 + 6 + 2 + 7 = 33. Profit = 40 - 33 = 7, and margin = 7 / 40 = about 17.5 percent. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Cost out ten items on a sheet and mark any item that earns under 10 dollars.

Standard to meet. Task: compute profit on ten items. Condition: with a simple spreadsheet. Standard: every cost shown, results checked by a mentor, and losing items identified.

Module 6 Find stock, serve buyers and build reviews 6 hCreate

Good sellers find reliable supply and answer people well.

  • Ask three suppliers for price, minimum order, lead time and a sample.
  • Keep stock counts and set a reorder point so you do not oversell.
  • Reply to buyer messages within one working day, politely and in short sentences.
  • Ask happy buyers for an honest review; never pay for or write fake reviews.
  • Compare suppliers on total delivered cost, not item price alone.
  • Reply to questions with a photo when it helps.
  • Count orders and quality complaints per supplier.
Worked example. A buyer asks if the jacket is lined. You answer with a photo of the inside and the fabric label. With 30 in stock and 6 sold a week, stock lasts 30 / 6 = 5 weeks, so you reorder before then.

Activity. Contact three suppliers, compare them in a table and log ten mock buyer questions with replies.

Standard to meet. Task: choose a supplier and answer ten questions. Condition: using a comparison table. Standard: choice is backed by numbers and every reply is polite, true and under 60 words.

Module 7 Protect buyers, data and money 6 hEvaluate

A trusted seller keeps promises and keeps information safe.

  • Keep buyer names, addresses and messages private and use them only to fulfil the order. Privacy law applies.
  • Use strong passwords and two-step sign-in on your seller account and email.
  • Watch for scams: fake payment emails, overpayment requests, requests to move the chat off the marketplace.
  • Keep records of sales, fees and tax for the period the tax agency requires; check the current rule with the tax agency.
  • Keep the chat and the payment on the marketplace.
  • Record tax collected for each month.
  • Delete buyer details you no longer need.
Worked example. A buyer sends a cheque for 140 for a 40 dollar item and asks you to refund the difference. The extra is 140 - 40 = 100. You refuse and report it to the marketplace.

Activity. Write a one-page safety checklist for account, scams, records and returns.

Standard to meet. Task: produce a safety and records checklist. Condition: reviewed by a mentor. Standard: covers passwords, scams, privacy, records and returns in plain words.

Module 8 Run a real shop for 60 days and decide 8 hCreate

Now you run it: list, sell, measure and choose what to do next.

  • Set a pilot goal, such as 20 listings and 10 sales with a profit on each.
  • Each week record orders, profit, returns, late shipments and buyer feedback.
  • Fix the biggest cause of lost money first.
  • At the end choose: stop, change or grow, and write a 12-month plan.
  • Mark which listings give a profit and which lose money.
  • Compare planned and actual numbers of listings and sales.
  • Choose one change to test next month.
Worked example. After 60 days: 18 orders, profit 126 dollars, 2 returns. 126 / 18 = 7 profit per order. You drop two losing items and raise two prices.

Activity. Run the pilot, keep the weekly log and present a 12-month plan to a panel of three.

Standard to meet. Task: run a 60-day shop pilot. Condition: real or agreed sales with rules followed. Standard: log complete, decision uses numbers, a panel can repeat your plan.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
