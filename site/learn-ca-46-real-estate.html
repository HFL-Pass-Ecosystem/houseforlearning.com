Real Estate

Run a pilot of real Estate in Canada: eight modules from user need to review, with rules, costs, risks and a seven-level ladder.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small real Estate offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: property, construction and smart buildings. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know the real estate world 6 hUnderstand

Real estate means land and what is built on it. Learn the main types and the people who work with them.

  • Name the types: house, condo, rental, land, shop and office.
  • Describe the roles: owner, buyer, tenant, agent, lawyer or notary, lender, inspector.
  • Explain the difference between buying, renting and managing a property.
  • List the words: deed or title, mortgage, deposit, closing, lease.
  • Explain freehold, condo and rental in one sentence each.
  • Identify who is paid for what in a sale: agent, lawyer or notary, inspector, lender.
  • Compare buying and renting for one family using monthly cost and freedom to move.
Worked example. A family buys a condo. The buyer, the seller, two agents, a lender and a lawyer or notary all have a job. Each job is written next to the person, with a note on who pays whom.

Activity. Walk one street. Note five properties, their type and who might own or rent each.

Standard to meet. Task: name types and roles. Condition: from one real street. Standard: five properties and six roles correctly matched.

Module 2 Read the local market 6 hApply

Prices and rents change by town and street. Learn to look at real listings before you decide anything.

  • Compare ten listings: price, size, age, price per square metre.
  • Describe what moves a local market: jobs, schools, transport, interest rates.
  • Explain days on market and what a long time may mean.
  • Check public sources for sold prices and rents; do not guess.
  • Calculate price per square metre as price divided by area for each listing.
  • Find the median of ten values by sorting them and averaging the middle two.
  • Compare asking price with sold price for five sales from public sources and write the gap in percent.
Worked example. Ten condos list at these prices per square metre: 4,200, 4,500, 4,600, 4,800, 4,900, 5,000, 5,100, 5,300, 5,600 and 6,900. The median is (4,900 + 5,000) / 2 = 4,950. The 6,900 unit is about 39% above the median (6,900 / 4,950 = 1.39), so it needs a reason such as a new building or a view.

Activity. Build a table of ten listings and mark the median price per square metre.

Standard to meet. Task: read a local market. Condition: ten real listings. Standard: median and range computed correctly with units.

Module 3 Follow the rules in Canada 10 hApply

Property law is mostly provincial. Licence, tax and contract rules differ, so check with the local authority each time.

  • Explain that agents and brokers need a provincial licence; check the regulator in your province.
  • Describe land transfer tax and title registration; check the current rule with the province or city.
  • Apply the rules on disclosure: sellers must not hide known serious faults; check your province for what must be disclosed.
  • Check tenant law: notice, deposits and rent increases are set by the province.
  • Ask the provincial regulator what training and supervision a new agent needs and write the steps.
  • Identify whether your province uses a lawyer or a notary to close a sale.
  • Ask the regulator how deposit money must be held and who may hold it.
Worked example. Rules table row: Land transfer tax | province or city | pay at closing | before keys are handed over | receipt kept in the deal file. If closing is on day 60 and the receipt takes 3 days to arrive, plan to pay by day 57.

Activity. Build a rules table for buying, selling and renting in your province, with an official page for each row.

Standard to meet. Task: list property rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: every row has the enforcer, the action and the date checked.

Module 4 Value a property 8 hEvaluate

Value is what a careful buyer would pay. Use facts, not hopes.

  • Apply the comparison method: find three recent sales of similar homes and adjust for size and condition.
  • Compute income value for a rental: yearly rent minus yearly costs.
  • Describe how an agent's opinion of value differs from a written appraisal, and check how your province regulates appraisers.
  • Explain why a lender may ask for a written appraisal from a qualified appraiser instead of an agent's opinion.
  • Calculate each adjustment with a stated reason, such as a bedroom or a newer roof.
  • Compare income value with the asking price by dividing net yearly rent by the price.
  • List repair items from an inspection report and estimate each from a local quote.
Worked example. Three similar homes sold for 400, 410 and 425 thousand CAD, an average of 411.7 thousand. Yours has one fewer bedroom, so adjust down by 15 thousand to about 397 thousand, and write the reason beside the number. Teaching numbers only.

Activity. Value one real listing using three sold comparisons and write each adjustment with a reason.

Standard to meet. Task: estimate a value range. Condition: three recent sales. Standard: adjustments explained and a range, not one number, is given.

Module 5 Money, loans and costs 8 hApply

Most property deals depend on money. Count every cost before you promise anything.

  • Compute monthly mortgage cost with a free calculator and test two interest rates.
  • List buying costs: deposit, transfer tax, legal fees, inspection, moving, repairs.
  • List owning costs: property tax, insurance, upkeep, utilities, condo fees.
  • Evaluate a rental: rent minus all costs and a gap for empty months.
  • Calculate the loan size as price minus deposit.
  • Test how a one-point rise in the interest rate changes the monthly payment with a free calculator.
  • Ask a lender how they test whether a buyer can still pay if rates rise, and write the answer.
Worked example. Rent 2,000 a month; costs for tax, insurance, upkeep and fees are 900 a month; one empty month a year. Net = 11 x 2,000 - 12 x 900 = 22,000 - 10,800 = 11,200 before loan payments. If rent drops 15% to 1,700: 11 x 1,700 - 10,800 = 7,900. Teaching numbers.

Activity. Build a one-property cash sheet with a normal case and a bad case where rent drops 15%.

Standard to meet. Task: check if a property pays. Condition: all costs counted. Standard: normal and bad case shown and the decision explained.

Module 6 Find clients and manage a deal 6 hCreate

A deal moves in steps. Keep a file and keep every promise in writing.

  • Build a deal checklist: listing, viewing, offer, conditions, inspection, financing, closing.
  • Explain what a condition in an offer is and why it has a date.
  • Apply honest marketing: true photos, true size, no hidden faults.
  • Track leads: contacts, viewings, offers, closings.
  • Write a plain-word reply for a buyer who asks about a fault.
  • Calculate the days from first call to closing for three practice deals.
  • Keep a written record of every offer, counter-offer and date in the deal file.
Worked example. An offer has an inspection condition on day 7, a financing condition on day 10 and closing on day 45. If the lender says no on day 8, the buyer can step away under the financing condition; the inspection date has already passed. The lawyer or notary checks the wording.

Activity. Create a deal file template and run a pretend deal from first call to closing with a partner.

Standard to meet. Task: manage a deal file. Condition: a practice deal. Standard: every step is dated and every promise is written.

Module 7 Manage a property well 6 hEvaluate

Good management keeps tenants, protects the building and keeps records clean.

  • Write a lease in plain words: rent, date, deposit, rules, repairs; check your provincial form.
  • Apply a repair process: report, urgent or not, fix, record the cost.
  • Check fire and safety items: smoke alarms, exits, heating; follow local rules.
  • Handle a late rent case: call, write, follow the provincial steps, never lock out a tenant.
  • Calculate yearly repair cost by setting aside a share of rent; state the share as an assumption.
  • Inspect a unit at move-in and move-out with photos and a signed list.
  • Ask the province where tenant and owner disputes are filed and write the steps.
Worked example. A water leak at night is urgent: the tenant calls, the owner sends a plumber, photos are taken, and the cost and date are recorded. A month of five repair-log entries costs 120 + 85 + 380 + 60 + 45 = 690.

Activity. Build a monthly checklist for one rental and a repair log with five practice entries.

Standard to meet. Task: manage a rental month. Condition: practice file. Standard: rent, repairs and safety checks recorded and dated.

Module 8 Run a real estate practice 8 hCreate

Now plan and run the work for real, under the rules and with supervision where the law needs it.

  • Evaluate your path: licensed agent, property manager, investor, or builder helper.
  • Run a 90-day pilot such as managing one unit or helping with five viewings under a licensed person.
  • Create a 12-month plan with money, licences and learning goals.
  • Lead with ethics: tell the truth, avoid conflict of interest, keep client money separate.
  • Compare fees earned with practice costs after the pilot.
  • Identify the licence or training steps for your chosen path from the official source.
  • Write a conflict-of-interest rule: tell all parties in writing when you or a relative has an interest.
Worked example. Stop rule: if after 90 days the practice cost is more than the fees earned, change the plan. Nine pilot jobs at 2,420 each earn 9 x 2,420 = 21,780; costs of 9 x 1,750 = 15,750 leave 6,030, so the plan continues. Result and decision are written with the numbers. Teaching numbers only.

Activity. Run the pilot, write the review and present a 12-month plan to three people.

Standard to meet. Task: run a supervised practice pilot. Condition: 90 days. Standard: results compared with the stop rule and licence needs checked with the regulator.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Name three roles in a property sale.

Model answer: Buyer, seller, agent, lender, lawyer or notary, inspector (any three). (Covered in module 1: Know the real estate world. To pass that module: Task: name types and roles. Condition: from one real street. Standard: five properties and six roles correctly matched.)

Q2. Who makes most property laws in Canada?

Model answer: The provinces; check your own province. (Covered in module 3: Follow the rules in Canada. To pass that module: Task: list property rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: every row has the enforcer, the action and the date checked.)

Q3. What is price per square metre used for?

Model answer: To compare properties of different sizes. (Covered in module 2: Read the local market. To pass that module: Task: read a local market. Condition: ten real listings. Standard: median and range computed correctly with units.)

Q4. Who gives an official appraisal?

Model answer: A qualified, regulated appraiser; check how your province regulates the title. (Covered in module 4: Value a property. To pass that module: Task: estimate a value range. Condition: three recent sales. Standard: adjustments explained and a range, not one number, is given.)

Q5. How do you find the income value of a rental?

Model answer: Yearly rent minus yearly costs. (Covered in module 4: Value a property. To pass that module: Task: estimate a value range. Condition: three recent sales. Standard: adjustments explained and a range, not one number, is given.)

Q6. Name two costs of owning a rental.

Model answer: Property tax, insurance, upkeep, fees (any two). (Covered in module 5: Money, loans and costs. To pass that module: Task: check if a property pays. Condition: all costs counted. Standard: normal and bad case shown and the decision explained.)

Q7. What does a condition in an offer do?

Model answer: It lets a party step away if a stated thing is not met by a stated date. (Covered in module 6: Find clients and manage a deal. To pass that module: Task: manage a deal file. Condition: a practice deal. Standard: every step is dated and every promise is written.)

Q8. What do you do with a known serious fault?

Model answer: Disclose it; do not hide it. (Covered in module 3: Follow the rules in Canada. To pass that module: Task: list property rules. Condition: official pages only. Standard: every row has the enforcer, the action and the date checked.)

Q9. Why test a bad case?

Model answer: To see if the property still works when rent drops or costs rise. (Covered in module 5: Money, loans and costs. To pass that module: Task: check if a property pays. Condition: all costs counted. Standard: normal and bad case shown and the decision explained.)

Q10. What should you do if you are unsure of a rule?

Model answer: Ask the regulator or a licensed adviser and keep the answer in writing. (Covered in module 8: Run a real estate practice. To pass that module: Task: run a supervised practice pilot. Condition: 90 days. Standard: results compared with the stop rule and licence needs checked with the regulator.)

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
