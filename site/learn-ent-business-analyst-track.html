Business Analyst Track: From Business Need to Working Platform

Turn a business need into requirements, process maps, configuration, tests and training.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L1 to L7 ladder27:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Remember Remember : understanding the business need.
  • Understand Understand : mapping the current process.
  • Apply Apply : writing requirements and stories.
  • Analyse Analyse : fit and gap against the platform.
  • Evaluate Evaluate : configuring, testing and accepting.
  • Create Create : data migration and reporting.
  • Create Create : change, training and benefits.
  • Evaluate Evaluate : capstone: run an end-to-end change and audit the result.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Turn a business need into requirements, process maps, configuration, tests and training. Finish all 8 modules on a practice set-up.

Condition

A free trial or practice account, practice data only (never real customer data), a peer circle and a mentor for the final review.

Standard

Every module standard is met; records are kept; a peer and a mentor rate the work 'Meets' or better on the rubric.

Who it is for: Analysts, project coordinators, product owners, graduates. Requirements: a computer and internet. Materials: slides with notes, worked examples, activities, quiz, rubric.

How the ladder works: modules climb from L1 (remember) to L7 (create); the ladder section shows each level as a slide. Take them in order; the first three suit new learners, 4 and 5 suit practitioners, 6 and 7 suit advanced staff.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. A manager asks you to add a field for 'reason'. How do you find the real need?

Model answer: Ask who needs it, what decision it supports and how often, and use the five whys. The real need may be a missing approval or report, not a field. (Covered in module 1: Understanding the business need. To pass that module: Write the need in one clear sentence.)

Q2. Your process map shows an order waiting 3 days in a queue and 2 hours being worked. What do you report?

Model answer: That the wait time is the main problem, not the work time. Most of the delay is waiting between hand-offs, so improve those first. (Covered in module 2: Mapping the current process. To pass that module: Map checked by process owner.)

Q3. Write one user story with an acceptance rule for a sales rep who wants reminders.

Model answer: As a sales rep, I want a reminder 2 days before a quote expires so I can follow up. Accepted when a reminder appears exactly 2 days before expiry. (Covered in module 3: Writing requirements and stories. To pass that module: Write each story so that it is testable.)

Q4. What do Must, Should and Could mean in a priority list?

Model answer: Must items are needed for go-live, Should items are important but have a workaround, and Could items are nice to have if time allows. (Covered in module 3: Writing requirements and stories. To pass that module: Write each story so that it is testable.)

Q5. Out of 50 requirements, 40 fit the standard features and 10 are gaps. What do you do next?

Model answer: Use standard for the 40, then list options for each of the 10 with cost and risk. Choose to change the process, use a simple setting or build custom work. (Covered in module 4: Fit and gap against the platform. To pass that module: Give every gap options and cost.)

Q6. Users run 10 test scripts and 3 fail. Can you ask for sign-off?

Model answer: No. Log the 3 defects, fix them, and rerun those scripts (and any related ones) until they pass. Sign-off is given only on evidence that the tests pass. (Covered in module 5: Configuring, testing and accepting. To pass that module: Log and close defects.)

Q7. The old system has 12,400 customers and the new one shows 12,150 after loading. What do you do?

Model answer: Do not sign off. Reconcile the 250 missing records, find the reason such as duplicates or bad formats, and fix and reload until the counts match or differences are explained. (Covered in module 6: Data migration and reporting. To pass that module: Complete the reconciliation table.)

Q8. Name two parts of a good training plan.

Model answer: Training matched to each role and champions in each team who help colleagues. Go-live support and follow-up checks also help. (Covered in module 7: Change, training and benefits. To pass that module: Plan has measures and dates.)

Q9. How would you measure benefits 90 days after go-live?

Model answer: Compare numbers set before the project, such as approval time or support calls, with the numbers now. Also check how many people actually use the new feature. (Covered in module 7: Change, training and benefits. To pass that module: Plan has measures and dates.)

Q10. In your audit, the approval time did not improve. What do you do?

Model answer: Find out why, such as users working around the new step or poor training, and report it honestly. Then agree a fix and record the lesson for future projects. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: run an end-to-end change and audit the result. To pass that module: Audit compares results with the original need and lists at least three lessons.)

levels L1 to L7

The ladder, slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: L1 Basic: Know the words

Remember key terms: stakeholder, requirement, user story, process map and acceptance test. Say what each one means in plain words.

Speaker notes. Use a simple shop example for each term. Check the learner can give one example back. Common mistake: using requirement and solution as the same word.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: L2 Awareness: See the flow of work

Understand the path from need to benefit and where an analyst helps. Explain why asking questions comes before building.

Speaker notes. Draw the path on one page. Ask what happens if a step is skipped. Common mistake: jumping to a solution on day one.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: L3 Foundation: Ask and draw

Apply interview questions and swim lane maps to one real process. Write a need in one clear sentence.

Speaker notes. Run a short mock interview. Check the map shows waits and hand-offs. Common mistake: drawing how it should work instead of how it works.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: L4 Practitioner: Write and fit

Apply stories with acceptance rules and build a fit-gap table. Analyse each gap for cost and risk.

Speaker notes. Give a messy request and ask for ten stories. Check each is testable. Common mistake: stories that are too large to test.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: L5 Specialist: Prove and migrate

Analyse test results and data loads. Evaluate whether a solution is ready for users to sign off.

Speaker notes. Ask for the reconciliation table. Check defects are logged and closed. Common mistake: signing off on feelings, not evidence.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: L6 Advanced: Lead adoption

Evaluate training and change plans against measures. Create an adoption plan with owners and dates.

Speaker notes. Review the plan for measures. Check each team has a champion. Common mistake: ending the project at go-live.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: L7 Super expert: Audit and coach

Create an end-to-end approach and audit the results against the first need. Evaluate lessons and teach others to use them.

Speaker notes. Ask for a 90-day audit and a lessons list. Check the learner can coach a new analyst. Common mistake: celebrating delivery without checking benefit.

jobs this leads to

  • Product owner. Prioritises the backlog.
  • Process analyst. Maps and improves processes.
  • Test coordinator. Plans acceptance tests.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local law changes how you store customer data and issue invoices. Check it for your place first.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Use INR for amounts. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Use EUR for amounts. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Use AED for amounts. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Use BRL for amounts. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Use CAD for amounts. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Use USD for amounts. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Use SGD for amounts. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Use AUD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Use HKD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Use GEL or ILS for amounts. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Use KYD for amounts. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Use MXN for amounts. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
