Industrial Activity: Light Manufacturing

Plan, run and measure a small safe production batch, from drawing to checked product.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 150 h guidedPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Plan, run and measure a small safe production batch, from drawing to checked product.

  • Understand Explain flow from material to checked product.
  • Apply Run a small batch safely.
  • Analyse Analyse defects and time.
  • Create Design a better layout or step.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan and run one small production batch with a safety plan, quality checks and a cost record.

Condition

About 150 hours over 3 to 6 months. Access to a supervised workshop, basic tools, free spreadsheet, a qualified supervisor, mentor and peer circle.

Standard

Safety plan approved; batch records; quality results; cost sheet; improvement plan; at least 70% on the check.

Learning only. Follow your local law and get written permission before you work with real customers, premises, data or money.

Requirements: none. Who it is for: learners 16 and over, workers changing field, small-business starters, teams. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (150 h guided)

Module 1 Flow of work 10 hUnderstand

See the whole path.

  • Draw steps from material in to product out
  • Mark waits and carry steps
  • Name the slowest step
Worked example. Cutting waits 40 minutes for a tool.

Activity. Draw a flow map. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Draw steps from material in to product out; Mark waits and carry steps; Name the slowest step), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. All steps timed. Meets the standard when your record shows: draw steps from material in to product out; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 2 Safety first 16 hApply

No batch without a safety plan.

  • List hazards and controls
  • Wear required protection
  • Supervisor sign-off
Worked example. Guard on saw; sleeves tied back; sign-off.

Activity. Write and get sign-off. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (List hazards and controls; Wear required protection; Supervisor sign-off), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Meets the standard when your record shows: list hazards and controls; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 3 Standards and drawings 16 hApply

Everyone must build the same thing.

  • Read a simple drawing
  • Tolerance: allowed variation
  • Check with the right gauge
Worked example. Part 100 mm plus or minus 0.5.

Activity. Measure five parts. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Read a simple drawing; Tolerance: allowed variation; Check with the right gauge), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Records within tolerance. Meets the standard when your record shows: read a simple drawing; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 4 Plan the batch 16 hCreate

Material, time, people.

  • Bill of materials
  • Time per step
  • Order and check stock
Worked example. 50 parts need 12 kg material plus 10% scrap.

Activity. Plan the batch. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Bill of materials; Time per step; Order and check stock), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Module 5 Run the batch 40 hApply

Measure while you build.

  • Log time and defects

Worked example. Defect rate climbs after tool wear; tool replaced.

Find causes, not blame.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: your records, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Evidence qualityClaims without recordsRecords for most claimsEvery claim linked to a dated record
Numbers and unitsMissing units or currencyCorrect, units shownCorrect, with a check calculation
Rules and safetyRules ignoredRules named and followedRules checked with the local authority, note kept
User focusNo real userOne real userSeveral users, changes made from feedback
ReflectionNo lessons writtenThree lessons writtenLessons turned into a changed plan with dates

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What is a waiting step?

Model answer: Time when no value is added.

Q2. Who signs off safety?

Model answer: A qualified supervisor.

Q3. What is tolerance?

Model answer: The allowed difference from the target size.

Q4. Why add scrap allowance?

Model answer: Some material is always lost.

Q5. When do you stop?

Model answer: When a defect trend appears.

Q6. What is ask why five times?

Model answer: Repeat why until root cause is found.

Q7. Why use good parts?

Model answer: Bad parts add cost without value.

Q8. Why one change at a time?

Model answer: To know which change worked.

Q9. What is the task of this course, in one sentence?

Model answer: Plan and run one small production batch with a safety plan, quality checks and a cost record.

Q10. What standard must your final evidence meet?

Model answer: Safety plan approved; batch records; quality results; cost sheet; improvement plan; at least 70% on the check.

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, with notes

  1. L1 Basic: First contact: words, tools and safety. Short units, about 2 to 6 hours, no record needed.
  2. L2 Awareness: Explain what the field is and who it serves. Shared with schools and community groups.
  3. L3 Foundation: Do guided tasks with a checklist. Evidence is a small set of records.
  4. L4 Practitioner: Run the full task alone with a mentor. This course sits here.
  5. L5 Specialist: Take one narrow area to depth and teach a peer circle.
  6. L6 Advanced: Lead a team, design a service or a study, and review other people's evidence.
  7. L7 Super expert: Set the standard: write the method, audit others, publish worked examples back to learners.

Notes: credits stack and you can step in and out. This course is L4. After it, L5 takes one narrow area of Industrial Activity: Light Manufacturing to depth; L6 leads a team; L7 sets and audits the standard. Aligned with, not accredited by, national credit frameworks.

Local custom changes rules, language, currency and who you practise with. Check each rule with your local authority before you act.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

United States

Singapore

Australia
