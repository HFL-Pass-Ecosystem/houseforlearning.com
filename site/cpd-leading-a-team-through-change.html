CPD · Business professionals

Leading a team through change

Helping a team understand, try and keep a change.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 PractitionerAbout 4 hours (240 minutes) of CPDSelf-study plus role-playBusiness professionals

Helping a team understand, try and keep a change.

  • Describe a change, given a scenario, in one page with reason, date and impact.
  • Assess team concerns, given a short survey, naming the top three.
  • Plan communication, given a calendar, with who hears what and when.
  • Measure adoption, given a sample dashboard, with two measures and a target.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Describe a change on one page, map who is affected, plan three timed messages with named senders, respond to resistance and choose three simple measures of adoption.

Condition

Given a change scenario, a calendar and a short survey, working in a four-hour supervised session with role-play and case cards.

Standard

Produce a one-page description with reason, date and impact, a message plan naming who hears what and when, the top three concerns, and three measures with a target.

These courses teach process, communication and recording. They give no clinical, legal, tax, financial or engineering sign-off. A named subject expert must review every line, and you must check your own professional body's current CPD rules, before you rely on a certificate.

sessions

8 modules

Module 1 Why change fails 25 min
  • Change fails when people do not know why
  • Give the reason first
  • Do not leave a gap for rumour
Worked example. Change: new ordering system in 6 weeks. Staff first hear in week 5 by rumour, so they distrust it. Fix: week 0 message from the manager gives the reason first, then the date.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "why change fails": describe a change, given a scenario, in one page with reason, date and impact.

Standard to meet. Explain why change often fails, including people not knowing the reason, and show why the reason should be given first.

Module 2 Mapping who is affected 35 min
  • List staff, customers, suppliers
  • Say how each is touched
  • Put the most affected first
Worked example. Affected: order team (12 people) enters orders in the new system; finance (5) sees new reports; customers (many) get a new order form. Each group is touched differently, so each needs its own message.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "mapping who is affected": assess team concerns, given a short survey, naming the top three.

Standard to meet. Identify the groups affected by a change, such as staff, customers and suppliers, and say how each group is touched.

Module 3 Three messages: time and sender 40 min
  • Set a time for each
  • Name a sender
  • Most affected hear first, from their own manager
Worked example. Week 0: "why and when", from the manager. Week 3: "training dates", from the team lead. Week 6: "go-live help desk", from the project lead. Three messages, three senders, set times.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "three messages: time and sender": plan communication, given a calendar, with who hears what and when.

Standard to meet. Plan three messages for a change, each with a set time and a named sender, so the most affected group hears first from their own manager.

Module 4 Handling worry and resistance 35 min
  • Listen first
  • Find the cause
  • Adjust the support
Worked example. Staff says: "I don't have time to learn this." Listen: training clashes with month-end. Cause found. Adjust: move training to the quiet week. Do not repeat the same message louder.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "handling worry and resistance": measure adoption, given a sample dashboard, with two measures and a target.

Standard to meet. Respond to worry and resistance by listening, finding the cause and adjusting the support offered, rather than repeating the message.

Module 5 Three simple measures 30 min
  • Pick three simple measures
  • Set a target
  • State how each is read
  • Stop measuring when the change is routine.
Worked example. Measures: orders entered per day (80 before), errors per 100 items, confidence score 1 to 5. Week 8: 12 errors in 400 items = 3 per 100. Target 3 or lower: met.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "three simple measures": measure adoption, given a sample dashboard, with two measures and a target. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Choose three simple measures for a change, such as errors per 100 items or briefing attendance, and say how each will be read.

Module 6 Case practice 25 min
  • Map groups
  • Plan three messages
  • Pick three measures
Worked example. Case practice: paper forms move to an online form. Groups: office staff, front desk, customers. Messages: week 0 reason, week 2 training, week 4 help line. Measures: forms completed online, errors per 100, staff confidence.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "case practice": measure adoption, given a sample dashboard, with two measures and a target. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Apply the whole method to a case such as moving from paper forms to an online form, mapping groups, planning messages and picking measures.

Module 7 Assessment 10 min
  • Timed
  • Use the checklist
  • Reason first in the message
Worked example. Assessment sample: a new change case. Map the groups, write a first message with reason and date, choose measures. Pass needs every checklist point and the reason stated first.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "assessment": measure adoption, given a sample dashboard, with two measures and a target. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. Complete a timed assessment on a new change case by mapping groups, writing a first message and choosing measures that meet the checklist.

Module 8 Second case: a shift change for 40 staff 40 min
  • Work case card 2
  • Write the first message
  • Choose measures
Worked example. Case card 2: a shift pattern changes for 40 staff in 3 weeks. Groups: 40 staff (4 teams of 10), team leaders, customers at peak times. First message, week 0, from the line manager: "Your shift changes in 3 weeks. Why: cover at peak hours. I will meet each team in week 1." Measures: briefing attendance (40 of 40), questions answered, rota swaps per week.

Practise: Practise with a case card or template for "second case: a shift change for 40 staff": measure adoption, given a sample dashboard, with two measures and a target. Use a new case this time (round 2).

Standard to meet. List the groups affected by the shift change, write the first message with reason, date and sender, and choose three measures with a target.

checks

10 check questions with model answers

Why explain the reason first?

Model answer: People accept change better when they know why. (Covered in module 1: Why change fails. To pass that module: Explain why change often fails, including people not knowing the reason, and show why the reason should be given first.)

Name 3 groups affected by a change.

Model answer: Staff, customers, suppliers. (Covered in module 2: Mapping who is affected. To pass that module: Identify the groups affected by a change, such as staff, customers and suppliers, and say how each group is touched.)

One good simple measure?

Model answer: Errors per 100 items. (Covered in module 5: Three simple measures. To pass that module: Choose three simple measures for a change, such as errors per 100 items or briefing attendance, and say how each will be read.)

What if resistance stays high?

Model answer: Listen, find the cause, adjust support. (Covered in module 4: Handling worry and resistance. To pass that module: Respond to worry and resistance by listening, finding the cause and adjusting the support offered, rather than repeating the message.)

Who hears bad news first?

Model answer: The most affected, from their own manager. (Covered in module 3: Three messages: time and sender. To pass that module: Plan three messages for a change, each with a set time and a named sender, so the most affected group hears first from their own manager.)

Why a sender for each message?

Model answer: People trust a known voice. (Covered in module 3: Three messages: time and sender. To pass that module: Plan three messages for a change, each with a set time and a named sender, so the most affected group hears first from their own manager.)

Name two measures.

Model answer: Attendance at briefings, questions answered, tasks done on time. (Covered in module 5: Three simple measures. To pass that module: Choose three simple measures for a change, such as errors per 100 items or briefing attendance, and say how each will be read.)

What if resistance stays high?

Model answer: Ask what is behind it; adjust the plan. (Covered in module 4: Handling worry and resistance. To pass that module: Respond to worry and resistance by listening, finding the cause and adjusting the support offered, rather than repeating the message.)

When to stop measuring?

Model answer: When the change is routine. (Covered in module 5: Three simple measures. To pass that module: Choose three simple measures for a change, such as errors per 100 items or briefing attendance, and say how each will be read.)

A shift pattern changes for 40 staff in 3 weeks. When should the first message go out, and from whom?

Model answer: At once, in week 0, from the line manager, with the reason first. (Covered in module 8: Second case: a shift change for 40 staff. To pass that module: List the groups affected by the shift change, write the first message with reason, date and sender, and choose three measures with a target.)

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, slide by slide with notes

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: What is a change. Speaker note: A change is anything that alters how people work: a new system, form or shift. Learners at this level can name one change they have seen and say that people feel it differently.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Awareness: Why people worry. Speaker note: Show how a change feels from the staff side: lost routines, fear of mistakes, not knowing why. Learners know that worry is normal and that explaining the reason early helps.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Foundation: Who is affected. Speaker note: Learners list the groups touched by a simple change, such as the ordering system move, and say what each group needs to hear. They work with a mentor and a one-page template.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Practitioner: Lead a team through the change. Speaker note: This course sits here. The learner maps groups, plans three timed messages with named senders, handles resistance by listening, picks three measures and describes the change on one page.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Specialist: Many teams at once. Speaker note: The specialist coordinates changes touching several teams, handles different reactions, builds a plan with dates and champions, and adjusts the plan as survey results and early measures come in.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Advanced: Programmes of change. Speaker note: The advanced leader runs a programme with several linked changes, sets targets for adoption, reports to sponsors, and decides when to extend support or stop measuring because the change is routine.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Super expert: Making change normal. Speaker note: The super expert builds an organisation that handles change well: shared method, trained sponsors, honest measures and lessons kept from each change. They teach leaders to explain the reason first.
