Artificial intelligence & machine learning investment firm

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30 to 90 day pilot of a small artificial intelligence & machine learning investment firm offer for real users in Canada and keep a full record.

Condition

About 58 hours over 3 to 6 months. A free spreadsheet, a phone or computer, one mentor, a peer circle and at least five real users. Practice partners are agreed locally.

Standard

Rules table complete with dated sources; cost sheet checked by a mentor; risk register and privacy note written; pilot reviewed with evidence; at least 70% on the ten-question check.

Field: software, data and digital services. Who it is for: students, career changers, returners, newcomers to Canada, small-business starters. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, rules table, cost sheet, risk register, rubric, ten-question check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Understand how investment in AI works 6 hUnderstand

An investment firm puts money into companies; an AI focus means judging data, models and teams.

  • The main stages are seed, early and growth, and each has a different level of risk and information.
  • Money comes from limited partners, and managers owe them honest, regular reports.
  • AI companies differ: tools, applications, data, services. Judge whether the data or the model is the real asset.
  • Write a one-page thesis: what we back, why now, what we avoid.
  • Read one real company's public filings or website and list five claims you can check and five you cannot.
  • Ask what a customer would do if the company vanished tomorrow. A product people could not easily replace is a stronger sign than a clever model.
  • Define the sectors you know in Canada, such as health, logistics, energy or agriculture, and say what you would need to learn before you judge a company in each.
Worked example. Thesis: 'We back applied AI for Canadian clinics and logistics at seed stage; we avoid pure model-building without data access.' A test on 3 companies might find 2 that fit and 1 that fails the data rule, and the reason for the failure is written beside it.

Activity. Write a thesis and test it against three real companies' public information.

Standard to meet. Thesis has reasons and exclusions.

Module 2 Know the securities rules 6 hApply

Raising and investing money is regulated.

  • Provincial securities regulators govern who may raise funds, give advice and sell investments; exemptions exist for some investors.
  • Managers may need registration; do not act before checking.
  • Anti-money-laundering rules require identifying investors and sources of funds.
  • Marketing must be balanced; never promise returns.
  • Check that you can lawfully see the information you use; do not trade on private information.
  • Keep every message to investors accurate and dated; if a figure is an estimate, label it as an estimate.
  • Rules differ by province and by type of investor. Check the provincial rule and ask a licensed adviser before any pitch.
Worked example. Row: 'Raise a fund from friends | provincial securities regulator | check exemption and registration | before any pitch | lawyer's written opinion'. A fund table with 8 rows, each with an owner and a date, shows at a glance which of the 8 are still open before a first pitch.

Activity. Build a rules table for a small fund and ask a licensed adviser to review.

Standard to meet. No row guessed; opinion kept.

Module 3 Evaluate an AI company 10 hApply

Due diligence means checking claims.

  • Check the data: source, rights to use, quality and bias.
  • Check the model: accuracy on held-out data, cost to run, drift over time.
  • Check the team and customers: paid pilots count more than letters of intent.
  • Write findings in a short memo with a rating and open questions.
  • Ask what the company would do with money, by when, and how you would know it worked.
  • Compare accuracy on a fair test: examples the model has never seen, drawn the way real customers' data is drawn.
  • Ask for cost to serve one customer per month and the price they pay, and compute the gross margin.
Worked example. Claim '95% accuracy' tested on the company's own test set; independent sample shows 84%, a gap of 11 points. Memo rating drops from strong to watch. If a customer pays 1,500 a month and serving them costs 400, gross margin is 1,100 (about 73%).

Activity. Review one public AI company and write a two-page memo.

Standard to meet. Each claim has evidence or is marked unverified.

Module 4 Model returns and fees 8 hEvaluate

Understand how money flows.

  • A fund of 10,000,000 with a 2% yearly fee pays its managers 200,000 a year.
  • Returns come from a few big winners while most investments return little, so model that spread.
  • Model three cases for the fund: poor, expected and strong.
  • Be clear about fees and carried interest, and show investors how each is calculated.
  • Compare fund costs over ten years with a fee table, not a feeling.
  • Multiple of money = money back divided by money invested. A result of 1.0 means investors only got their money back.
  • Note that a multiple before fees is higher than what investors keep; show both before and after fees.
Worked example. Invest 500,000 across 10 companies at 50,000 each. Outcomes: 6 return nothing, 3 return 50,000 each, 1 returns 400,000 (8x). Total back = 150,000 + 400,000 = 550,000, which is 1.1x before fees. If the 400,000 winner is missed, the total is 150,000, only 0.3x. Fees on a 10,000,000 fund at 2% for 10 years are 2,000,000. Teaching figures in CAD.

Activity. Build a three-case return table.

Standard to meet. Fees and assumptions shown.

Module 5 Manage the portfolio 8 hApply

After investing, help and monitor.

  • Hold a quarterly review of each company's revenue, cash, hires and risks.
  • Reserve money for follow-on rounds in the companies that are working.
  • Set clear triggers for extra support or for exit.
  • Report to investors in a fixed format every quarter.
  • Record why you made each decision on the day, so you can learn later.
  • Give each company a status: on plan, behind or needs help, with a reason and a date to look again.
  • Count months of cash left (cash divided by monthly spending) and flag any company under 9 months.
Worked example. Quarter page: 10 companies, 2 behind plan (20%), 1 needing a bridge; action: reserve 200,000. A company with 240,000 in cash spending 30,000 a month has 8 months left, under the 9-month line, so it is on the watch list.

Activity. Build a quarterly dashboard for a fictional portfolio.

Standard to meet. Every company has a status and next step.

Module 6 Source and win deals 6 hCreate

Good deals come from trust.

  • Network with universities, accelerators and founders.
  • Give founders fast, clear answers.
  • Offer help beyond money: introductions and advice.
  • Track deal flow through five stages: seen, met, diligence, offer and closed.
  • Keep a list of founders you met and notes for follow-up, with their consent.
  • Say no kindly and quickly, with one honest reason. Founders remember how you treated them.
  • Ask each founder how they found you and track which source brings the best deals.
Worked example. Funnel: 120 seen, 30 met, 8 diligence, 3 offers, 2 closed. Rates: met 30/120 = 25%, diligence 8/30 = 27%, offers 3/8 = 38%, closed 2/3 = 67%. Overall 2/120 = 1.7%, so about 60 deals seen per closing.

Activity. Run a deal funnel for 4 weeks (public companies or case studies).

Standard to meet. Funnel recorded.

Module 7 Handle AI ethics and conflicts 6 hEvaluate

Invest responsibly.

  • Screen each company for harmful uses and biased data.
  • Declare every conflict of interest in writing.
  • Respect federal and provincial private-sector privacy rules in portfolio companies; check the rule that applies in the province.
  • Publish an investment policy that investors and founders can read.
  • Write the policy for what you do if a founder behaves badly.
  • Ask each company how it tests for unfair results across groups of people, and ask to see the test.
  • Keep a conflicts register: who owns or advises what, and who left the room when a decision was made.
Worked example. Policy line: 'We do not back face-recognition used without consent.' A conflicts register with 5 entries, each with a date and an action, lets an investor see that every conflict was handled.

Activity. Write a one-page responsible investing policy.

Standard to meet. Policy has screens and a review step.

Module 8 Pilot a mock fund 8 hCreate

Practise with a paper portfolio.

  • Pick five public companies and write a thesis for the group.
  • Track them for 60 days with dated notes.
  • Compare the result with a simple benchmark.
  • Write a review of what the thesis got right and wrong.
  • Keep your paper fund simple: no more than five names.
  • Write the entry reason and the price for each name on day one, before the results arrive.
  • State clearly that a paper fund is practice; it is not advice and nothing is bought.
Worked example. Paper portfolio +3% vs benchmark +1%, a gap of 2 points over 60 days; reason noted: luck vs thesis. If one of five names rose 12% and the other four were flat, 12 / 5 = 2.4 points came from a single name, which suggests luck.

Activity. Run the paper fund and present the review.

Standard to meet. Review separates luck from skill.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. What are the stages of company investing?

Model answer: Seed, early and growth.

Q2. Why test accuracy independently?

Model answer: Company figures may come from its own favourable test set.

Q3. Who regulates raising funds?

Model answer: Provincial securities regulators.

Q4. What is due diligence?

Model answer: Checking a company's claims and risks before investing.

Q5. Why reserve follow-on money?

Model answer: Good companies need later rounds.

Q6. Why do fees matter?

Model answer: They reduce investor returns every year.

Q7. Which kind of data does federal private-sector privacy law cover?

Model answer: Personal information held by private organisations.

Q8. What belongs in an investment policy?

Model answer: Screens, conflicts and review steps.

Q9. Why never promise returns?

Model answer: Rules forbid it and results are uncertain.

Q10. What separates luck from skill?

Model answer: A written thesis tested against results.

ten locations and global

Your place

The course is written for Canada. The same method works elsewhere; local rules, currency and levels change. Pick your place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registering: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Levels frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registering: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Levels frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registering: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Levels frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registering: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Levels frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registering: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Levels frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registering: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Levels frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registering: registration with ACRA. Data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Levels frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registering: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Levels frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Registering: a business registration and, for companies, the Companies Registry. Data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Levels frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Registering: local business registration in the country where you work. Data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Levels frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Registering: a trade and business licence or company registration. Data rule: the Data Protection Act. Levels frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Registering: tax registration (RFC) and company or sole-trader registration. Data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Levels frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
