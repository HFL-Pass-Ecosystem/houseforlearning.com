Business Coach: Georgia and Israel deep edition

Business Coach planned in detail for Georgia and Israel: sector lessons plus local rules, jobs and GEL examples. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real. All amounts are estimates for planning, not quotes. The module lines use rounded sample numbers; the Local example in module 6 uses separate local estimates.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Produce a coaching programme outline with a pilot plan

Condition

Within 2 hours, using a sample founder profile, checked by a mentor and a peer

Standard

One result with a number, a fair promise with no income guarantee, a price and break-even client count, and a week-8 review.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Read your local market 4 hUnderstand

Every plan starts with the place. Read Georgia and Israel as your customer does before you choose a sector.

  • Coach one type of founder through one result, such as first 10 customers.
  • Two different countries, two sets of rules. Pick the country first, then use that country’s list in every module. Check with a local expert.
  • Both have strong technology and start-up activity; many small firms sell abroad from day one.
  • Languages differ by customer: write the page in the local language plus English.
  • Public holidays and the working week differ; put the working days on every delivery promise.
Worked example. Programme: 8 weeks, weekly 45 minutes, one task each week. | Local example: Market page for Georgia and Israel: size in one line, three languages or fewer, main payment methods, three public holidays that affect delivery, and one risk. Each line has a source you can open and a date.

Activity. Write a one-page market page with sources and dates. Ask two local people to correct it.

Standard to meet. Cite a source and a date for every line, have two local readers confirm the facts, and name one risk with a response.

Module 2 Choose your sector 4 hAnalyse

The global list has 75 sectors in 8 clusters. Pick by local fit, not by fashion.

  • Choose group, one-to-one or both; compare income and time.
  • Cluster A, Online selling and marketing: Strong fit in both: small firms sell online to the region. Check delivery rules and returns for each country. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster B, Technology and digital services: Both countries have a technology culture: security, privacy, cloud and testing services sell to small firms. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster C, Health, care and wellbeing (regulated): Regulated in both. Practise only under the right licence. This course teaches planning, not practice. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster D, Mobility, aerial and space (regulated): Regulated. Airspace and port rules are strict in the region; plan on paper and with a licensed partner. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster E, Energy, building and environment: Sunny and windy sites make energy services a fit; hold permits and grid rules before any build. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster F, Food and farming: Strong farming and food tradition: ghost kitchens, vertical farms and food science fit well; check food-safety rules. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster G, Money services (regulated): Regulated money services; plan as a partner of a licensed firm. Check with a local expert.
  • Cluster H, People and business support: Coaching, coworking and outsourcing fit; many founders need flexible support in more than one language.
Worked example. 8 group members at GEL 150 per month = GEL 1,200; one-to-one 4 hours at GEL 120 = GEL 480. | Local example: Scoring example: pick three sectors, score each 1 to 5 on local demand, skills you hold, start-up cost, rules and partner access. A sector that scores 5 on demand but 1 on rules is a later project, not a first one. Pick the sector with the highest total and a rules score of 3 or more.

Activity. Rank six sectors from at least three clusters. Choose one and write the reason in three lines.

Standard to meet. Score six sectors on five criteria, state the reason for your choice, and mark any regulated sector as planning only.

Module 3 Register and license 4 hApply

Rules differ by place. Check current rules with a licensed local adviser before you act for real.

  • Check what you may claim and promise; no guarantees of income.
  • Name the country first. Each has its own registration route: an individual-entrepreneur or small-business type in one, a dealer type or a company in the other. Check with a local expert.
  • Compare the types by tax, liability and filing work before you choose; write the table. Check with a local expert.
  • Licences vary by activity (food, health, money, education centres); list yours before the first order. Check with a local expert.
  • Keep a local address and a named contact in the country where you trade.
Worked example. Claims list: allowed statements and banned statements. | Local example: Licence list for a small Business Coach business: registration type, tax registration, address, any activity licence, insurance. Next to each, write who issues it, what it costs in GEL (from their public page), how long it takes, and the date you checked.

Activity. Build a licence table for your chosen sector with issuer, cost, time and the date checked. Do not apply; this is a plan.

Standard to meet. Complete the table with a public source for every row, put the steps in a logical order, and write one question for an adviser.

Module 4 Protect data and customers 3 hApply

Trust is the real product. Protect customer data and keep your promises in writing.

  • Keep client notes private; agree confidentiality in writing.
  • Both countries have a personal-data law; read the one for the country where the customer lives. Check with a local expert.
  • Say in plain words what you collect and why; give a way to see and correct it.
  • Keep marketing consent records; customers must be able to say no.
  • Moving data between the two countries or to a third place may need a safeguard; check with a local expert.
Worked example. Notes locked; shared only with written consent. | Local example: Privacy notice in five lines: who we are; what we collect; why; who sees it; how to ask for a copy, correction or removal. Plus a data map listing each tool and where it stores data.

Activity. Write the five-line notice and a data map for your plan. Ask a peer to find any gap.

Standard to meet. Write the notice in plain words, list every tool in the data map, and write and date the refund and complaint steps.

Module 5 Find and serve customers 4 hCreate

Go where your customers already are, in the language they use. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, Russian and English depending on the country and customer.

  • Prove results with client numbers (with permission).
  • Channels: messaging apps and social media are strong in both; add a local marketplace.
  • Language: one page in the local language, one in English; never mix currencies on one price.
  • Payments: bank transfer and local payment methods; show the currency code next to every price.
  • Trust: registration number, address, delivery days and a refund note on every page. Check with a local expert.
Worked example. Client moved from 2 to 9 monthly orders in 8 weeks. | Local example: Test plan: show the offer to 20 real people through two channels over two weeks; count enquiries, replies and first orders. Example result: 20 shown, 6 enquiries, 2 orders: a 10% order rate. Change one thing (price or wording) and test again.

Activity. Test two channels with 20 people. Record results in a table and write the next change.

Standard to meet. Test has a start and end date; results are counted, not guessed; one change is chosen and justified.

Module 6 Price, tax and money 4 hEvaluate

Know your numbers in GEL. Tax rule in one line: Local sales tax and income tax differ in each country; a small-business regime may apply in one and a dealer type in the other.

  • Price per programme; offer one trial session at a fair rate.
  • Write GEL next to every number.
  • Contribution per unit = price minus the cost of each unit.
  • Break-even units = fixed monthly cost divided by contribution per unit; round up.
  • Add tax and duty explicitly; never bury them in a price. Check with a local expert.
  • Check against a worse case: if sales are 25% lower, do you still cover costs?
Worked example. Programme GEL 2,700; your time 8 weeks x 3 hours at GEL 75 = GEL 1,800; margin GEL 900. | Local example: For a small Business Coach business (all amounts are estimates): price GEL 120 per unit, cost GEL 25 per unit, fixed cost GEL 1,200 per month. Contribution = 120 - 25 = 95. Break-even = 1,200 / 95 = 12.6, so 13 units per month (one unit = coaching session hour). If sales fall 25% from 26, you sell 19 and the margin is 605.

Activity. Build your own sheet in GEL: price, unit cost, fixed cost, contribution, break-even and a 25% worse case. Put the tax and duty rules in a separate line with a source.

Standard to meet. Complete all six lines of the sheet in GEL, have a peer check the arithmetic, and show the worse case.

Module 7 People and partners 3 hApply

Plans become real through people. Hire, partner and write every promise down.

  • Use a simple tracker and templates; partner with a referral source.
  • Employment rules, notice and social contributions differ; read the one for the country of the worker. Check with a local expert.
  • Written terms and signed copies for every helper, even part-time.
  • Freelancers are common: write scope, price, language and date in one page.
  • Partner with a local college, chamber or training body to find helpers and to practise.
Worked example. Tracker: goal, task, due date, result. | Local example: Role card for a first helper: tasks, hours, pay in GEL, trial period, review date, and one measure of success. Signed by both sides.

Activity. Write one role card and one partner letter (what each side gives). Role-play a first meeting for 10 minutes.

Standard to meet. Write a role card with pay in local currency, hours and a review date, balance the partner letter, and keep signed copies of both.

Module 8 Launch plan and review 4 hCreate

Finish with a one-page launch plan and a plan to review it every month.

  • Launch with 3 pilots and a review at week 8.
  • Dates: first test, first order, first review.
  • One measure per week: enquiries, orders or revenue; pick one.
  • A monthly review: what we planned, what happened, what we change.
  • Keep a risk list with a response for each; include a rules change. Check with a local expert.
  • Local caution: the final country choice will be confirmed before opening.
Worked example. Go rule: 2 of 3 reach their goal. | Local example: Launch page for Georgia and Israel: Week 1 licence table done; Week 2 test with 20 people; Week 3 numbers sheet checked; Week 4 launch decision with a go or no-go rule written in advance.

Activity. Write the launch page and present it to two people for 10 minutes. Record their questions and your answers.

Standard to meet. Plan has dates, one weekly measure, a go or no-go rule and a risk list; presenter answers three questions with evidence.

jobs

Where this takes you

  • Business Coach assistant. supports a small Business Coach business in Georgia and Israel.
  • Subscription shop operator. runs boxes, orders and delivery for a small seller.
  • Export assistant. prepares documents and delivery notes for cross-border sales.
  • Security and privacy assistant. keeps customer data safe in small firms.
  • Food business assistant. plans menus, safety logs and orders.
  • Solar and energy assistant. prepares site data and quotes.

Six steps from entry to senior

  1. Business Coach assistant (L2, Entry). helps with set tasks, keeps records and follows the checklist.
  2. Junior Business Coach coordinator (L3, Junior). runs small jobs end to end and reports each week.
  3. Business Coach practitioner (L4, Practitioner). plans and delivers the work alone, with a mentor check.
  4. Business Coach specialist (L5, Specialist). goes deep in one area, such as rules, numbers or customers.
  5. Business Coach team lead or manager (L6, Advanced). leads people and partners, owns the plan and the data.
  6. Senior Business Coach adviser or mentor (L7, Senior). designs the whole system, reviews others and publishes cases.

See how these steps connect to study and work in Career pathways and Pathways.
