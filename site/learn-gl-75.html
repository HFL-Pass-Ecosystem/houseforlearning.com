Smart Apparel: Prototype a simple sensor garment concept.

  • Understand Explain who needs Smart Apparel and name one real first user, with evidence from your own market scan.
  • Apply Build and run the practice task: Prototype a simple sensor garment concept.
  • Analyse Record every step, cost and problem, then compare the plan with what really happened.
  • Evaluate Judge your result against your goal using numbers, and write three lessons learned.
  • Create Present your evidence to a review room and give two peers written feedback.
  • Create Plan your next 90 days and contribute one template or worked example back to learners.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Prototype a simple sensor garment concept.

Condition

About 60 hours over 1-3 months. Own tools, free spreadsheet and form tools, and a real practice partner. Mentor and peer circle available. Currency written on every number.

Standard

Evidence reviewed by a mentor; at least 70% on the 10-question check; two written peer reviews given; every number carries its currency and unit.

Who it is for, requirements and materials

  • Who it is for: learners aged 16 and over, small-business starters, workers moving to a new field, and teams (see B2B, B2C, D2C).
  • Requirements: finish Space Tourism Awareness first, or show equivalent work.
  • Materials included: lesson text, worked examples, case exercise, 10-question check, templates in My page.
  • Teaching team: a trainer from our network, a mentor who reviews your evidence, and a peer circle. Names are given at enrolment.

lesson by lesson

9 modules, in the order you do them

Module 1 What smart apparel is and what problem it solves IdeaStudy · 2 hUnderstand

Goal: choose a concept and a wearer.

  1. Describe smart apparel: clothes with sensors or lights that measure or show something, such as light for safety, temperature, posture or movement.
  2. List problems: visibility for night walkers, a warning for heat, a reminder to move.
  3. Interview 5 possible wearers about what they would actually wear daily, comfort, washing and cost.
  4. Choose one concept and write what the garment must do, without medical claims.

Evidence you keep: notes, five wearer interviews and a concept statement.

Module 2 Kick-off circle IdeaShare and mentoring · 3 hUnderstand

Goal: say your goal aloud and agree the evidence you will produce.

  1. Each learner reads their one-page plan aloud (3 minutes).
  2. Peers ask three questions: what does the wearer notice, how is it washed, what if the battery fails.
  3. The mentor agrees the evidence list for this module: a concept sheet, a sketch with parts list, a prototype test record, a washing note and a safety list.

Evidence you keep: your revised plan and the agreed evidence list.

Module 3 Design the concept ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Goal: turn the idea into a drawing and a parts list.

  1. Draw the garment front and back, mark where each part sits: sensor or light, small controller, battery holder, wires or conductive thread.
  2. Write the parts list with function, voltage (low voltage only) and a removable connection for washing.
  3. Write how the wearer switches it on and off and how they know the battery state.
  4. Mark weak points: bends, sweat, rubbing, washing.

Evidence you keep: the sketch and parts list.

Module 4 Textiles, sensors and washability ActionStudy · 2 hApply

Short modules:

  • Textiles: choose fabric for comfort, stretch and breathability; seams and stitches must carry wiring without pinching.
  • Sensors and lights: simple low-voltage parts can measure light, temperature, or movement, or show lights; keep wires insulated and strain-relieved.
  • Washability: remove electronics before washing; test how the textile and any conductive thread behave when washed and dried; moisture and metal corrode.
  • Safety: low voltage only, no hot parts against skin, a removable battery in a protective pouch, no tight wires around the body.

Check: 10 questions at the end of the module.

Module 5 Build and test the prototype ActionPractice · 14 hApply

Goal: make a working simple version.

  1. Make a prototype on a spare piece of cloth or an old garment: attach a small battery-powered light or sensor module using stitches, snaps or a pocket.
  2. Test comfort: wear for 30 minutes and note rubbing, weight, heat.
  3. Test function: does it do what the wearer needs, in light and dark, moving and still?
  4. Test one wash of the textile with electronics removed. Record changes.

Evidence you keep: prototype photos (no faces), test record and wash note.

Module 6 Design-critique circle ValueShare and mentoring · 6 hEvaluate

Goal: get feedback on use and design.

  1. Present the prototype in 6 minutes.
  2. Peers wear it or look at it and say what they would change.
  3. A textile or electronics mentor points out safety issues.
  4. List changes.

Evidence you keep: critique notes and change list.

Module 7 Cost it and decide ValuePractice · 14 hEvaluate

Goal: see if the concept is worth making.

  1. Cost per garment: fabric, parts, labour, packaging, testing. Currency shown.
  2. Decide a price and compare with similar clothes without electronics.
  3. List the rules to check before selling: electrical safety, textile labelling, battery rules, claims.
  4. Write three lessons learned and one decision.

Evidence you keep: cost sheet, rules list and decision note.

Module 8 Review room EmpowermentShare and mentoring · 3 hCreate

Goal: show your evidence and judge two peers fairly.

  1. Present to a review room in 8 minutes: the concept, the prototype, the tests and the decision.
  2. Give two peers written feedback: one thing that is strong, one thing to fix.
  3. Get a written note from a wearer.

Evidence you keep: slides or notes, two peer reviews, and the note.

Module 9 Check and next step RegenerationStudy · 2 hCreate

Goal: close the module and plan what comes next.

  1. Take the 10-question check. Pass mark is 70% (draft).
  2. Write a 90-day next-step plan: what you will do, where, with whom, and what you will learn next. Good follow-on courses: 3D Printing, Fitness Technology, Portable and Wireless Charging Solutions.
  3. Contribute back: share your parts list template or wash-test sheet so the next learner starts higher. Accepted items earn credits (rates will be confirmed before opening).

Evidence you keep: check result and the 90-day plan.

practice material and assessment

Worked examples, case, check and local law pointers

Worked examples
  1. Parts: light module 5 + battery holder 3 + controller 4 + stitching and fabric 8 + packaging 2 = 22 (currency shown).
  2. Price 40: margin = 40 - 22 = 18, i.e. 45% of price.
  3. Battery 3.7 V; light draws 0.05 A; capacity 0.5 Ah gives 10 hours.
  4. Wash test: 1 wash; 2 of 4 stitches loosened = 50%. Fix: reinforce seams.
Case exercise

A night walker wants a jacket with lights. Questions: How is it washed? What if the battery fails? Which claims must not be made? Which rules apply before selling? Write a half-page concept sheet.

Check questions (draft set, 10)
  1. What is smart apparel? 2. Why use low voltage only? 3. Why remove electronics before washing? 4. Why does moisture harm electronics? 5. Name two weak points on a garment. 6. Why interview wearers? 7. What do you test for comfort? 8. Which rules apply before selling? 9. Why avoid health claims? 10. What is your decision?

Rules for this course

No brand names or logos of other companies on any page, file or slide. No personal data in shared files. Low voltage only. No mains power. No medical claims. Remove batteries before washing and storage. Photographs must not show faces.
