Financing Start-ups

Eight modules take you from the first customer to a running, measured service in financing start-ups.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Plan, start and run financing start-ups in São Paulo: serve a founder and an investor, deliver funding and support for a new firm, keep records, and show a working month of numbers.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. Own phone or computer, real customers or a real practice site, a peer and a mentor for the final review. Local rules and licences checked against official sources.

Standard

Every module standard is met; all claims are true and backed by records; peers and a mentor mark the evidence 'Meets' or better.

Who it is for: students, graduates, career changers, owners, workers and returners. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, templates, tracker, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Know your customer and market 4 hAnalyse

Every sound business in financing start-ups begins with one clear customer and one clear problem. In São Paulo and the wider state, demand is real but crowded, so a small, well-described customer group wins before a broad one.

  • In financing start-ups: Founders want money and help; investors want evidence; write both profiles.
  • Write the customer in one sentence: a founder and an investor. Add the place, the moment of need and what they use today.
  • List five rivals or substitutes within reach. For each, record price, strength, weakness and how people reach it.
  • Interview at least eight real customers with open questions. Record exact words, not your summary.
  • Count the market in units: how many advisory packages could one area buy in a month? Use two public sources and your own count, then take the lower figure.
  • Choose one first segment and say why the others wait.
Worked example. Weak: 'Everyone needs funding and support for a new firm.' Strong: 'Our first customer is a founder and an investor within 8 km of the centre, who today pays R$ 3.300 per advisory package and complains about delay. We target 5 advisory packages a month by month three.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: ten customers are interviewed for financing start-ups. Seven say price, two say speed, one says trust. Question: which first message do you write, and which interview record backs it? Model answer: lead with the advisory package price and speed promise, keep trust proof as the second line, and cite the seven price answers by date.

Activity. Write the one-sentence customer, a five-row rival table and eight interview records. Share them with a peer for a challenge round.

Standard to meet. Customer is specific; rival table has prices; every claim traces to an interview or a source.

Module 2 Rules, registration and data duties in Brazil 4 hUnderstand

Brazil asks every trader to be visible and accountable. Know which register, tax regime and sector rules apply before the first sale, and check each against the current official text.

  • In financing start-ups: Raising money may bring securities rules; confirm what is allowed before offering to the public.
  • Choose a legal form: micro-entrepreneur (MEI) only if your activity appears on the official MEI list, or a company registered with a CNPJ. Regulated activities need their sector authorisation first. Check the current limits and lists on the official portal.
  • Learn the tax regime that fits your size and write down which taxes you will collect and pay, and when.
  • Find the sector rules for financing start-ups: licences, inspections, professional registers. List each with the office that issues it and the renewal date.
  • Personal data of customers falls under Brazil's data protection law (LGPD). Collect only what you need, say why, keep it safe, and name who answers questions.
  • Consumer law gives buyers clear rights on information, returns and complaints. Put your terms in plain Portuguese.
Worked example. Checklist for a new financing start-ups trader: 1) legal form (CNPJ, or MEI only if your activity is on the official list); 2) city operating licence; 3) sector licence or register; 4) tax set-up; 5) privacy notice; 6) written terms. Each has an owner and a date.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a neighbour says registration can wait until the first R$ 15.000 of sales. Question: what do you do? Model answer: do not delay; write the compliance table first, confirm the register, tax and sector licence with the official office, and start only where the table shows no open blocker.

Activity. Build your own six-line compliance table for your first location. Mark what is done, what is pending and who confirms it.

Standard to meet. Every line cites an official source; nothing is assumed; pending items have dates. Local law always wins over this course.

Module 3 Design the offer 4 hCreate

An offer states what the customer gets, how well, by when and at what price. For funding and support for a new firm, the offer must make the quality visible and the next step easy.

  • In financing start-ups: Offer stages: small grant or loan first, larger funding after milestones.
  • Describe the core offer in three lines: what is delivered (funding and support for a new firm), the standard it meets, and the time it takes.
  • Add one proof for each promise: a sample, a test result, a guarantee or a customer record.
  • Price from cost upward and from customer value downward, then pick a price inside both.
  • Write a simple one-page terms sheet: what is included, what is extra, how to cancel.
  • Create two options at most, so choice stays easy.
Worked example. Offer: 'Funding and support for a new firm, delivered within agreed time, checked against a written standard, price R$ 3.000 per advisory package. Proof: a dated record the customer can check and sign. Changes or cancellation by written agreement before work begins.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a customer asks for a discount from R$ 3.000 to R$ 2.700 per advisory package. Question: decide with numbers. Model answer: contribution falls from R$ 2.400 to R$ 2.100 per advisory package; offer an extra of value (for example faster service) before cutting price, and accept only if the lower contribution still clears break-even.

Longer notes. State the one problem you solve, for example helping a founder who needs a clear cash plan before asking anyone for money. List what you deliver: a monthly cash sheet, a comparison of funding paths and a checklist of documents. Compare the paths in plain words: own savings, a partner, bank credit, public programmes and grants, giving the cost and the risk of each. Calculate the hours you need to deliver each service. Never promise approval or returns. Check the current official text of any programme before you describe its rules.

Extra worked example. A founder needs R$ 25.000 and compares two credit offers over 10 months. Offer A has total repayments of R$ 29.500 and offer B has R$ 28.750. The extra cost of A is R$ 29.500 - R$ 25.000 = R$ 4.500 and of B is R$ 28.750 - R$ 25.000 = R$ 3.750. Offer B costs R$ 750 less in total. Monthly payment of B is R$ 28.750 / 10 = R$ 2.875, against R$ 2.950 for A. Check each contract before signing.

Activity. Write the offer card, the proof list and the terms sheet. Test them with three customers and note every point where they hesitated.

Standard to meet. Promise, proof and price are consistent; terms fit one page; three customer reactions are recorded.

Module 4 Run the work step by step 5 hApply

Good operations make results repeatable. For financing start-ups, write each step so a new person could follow it on their first day, and measure each step.

  • In financing start-ups: Process: screen, interview, due diligence, terms, funding, milestone review.
  • Map the process from first contact to paid and closed. Typical stages: enquiry, quote, preparation, delivery of the advisory package, check, payment, follow-up.
  • Name the key resource at each stage: a milestone plan. Note what happens if it is missing.
  • Write a one-page standard procedure per stage: who, what, tool, time, record.
  • Choose three measures: speed, quality (errors per 100) and customer rating. Review them every week.
  • Fix the cause of a defect, not the symptom. Ask 'why' five times and record the answer.
Worked example. Stage record: 'Prepare. Owner: lead. Time: 30 min. Tool: checklist. Record: signed sheet. Defect found on 1 of 2 advisory packages last week; cause: missing check on a milestone plan; fix: add check at start, review next Friday.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: of 8 advisory packages last month, 1 had a defect traced to a milestone plan. Question: what is the defect rate and what is your first action? Model answer: about 13 defects per 100; stop the cause (add a check at the start), correct affected customers, log the cause, and re-measure next week.

Activity. Draw the process map and write procedures for the two riskiest stages. Run each once and time it.

Standard to meet. Procedures are followable by a stranger; measures have weekly numbers; one defect is traced to a root cause.

Module 5 Safety, quality and risk 4 hEvaluate

The most serious risk in this field is money given without a plan or tracking. A calm, written risk routine protects people first and the business second.

  • In financing start-ups: Risk of funding weak plans; score team, market, product and traction.
  • List ten things that could go wrong. Score each for how likely (1 to 5) and how serious (1 to 5). Multiply and sort.
  • For the top five, choose a control: remove the hazard, replace it, guard it, train people, or use protective gear, in that order of preference.
  • Write who does what in an emergency, and rehearse it once a quarter.
  • Keep an incident and near-miss log. Near-misses are free lessons.
  • Check that insurance and legal duties match the risk you actually carry.
Worked example. Risk row: 'Hazard: money given without a plan or tracking. Likelihood 3, severity 5, score 15. Control: written check before each advisory package, trained staff, log sheet. Owner: site lead. Review monthly.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a near-miss occurs: money given without a plan or tracking nearly happened but no one was hurt. Question: what do you do in the next 24 hours? Model answer: make safe, record the event, ask the five whys, add or tighten one control, brief the team, and set a review date; near-misses count as lessons, not blame.

Activity. Build a ten-row risk register and a one-page emergency plan. Run a ten-minute drill with a peer and note two improvements.

Standard to meet. Top risks have controls ranked by preference; drill done; log started. Where a regulation applies, follow the official rule.

Module 6 People, skills and service 4 hApply

People deliver the promise. Define roles, train to a standard and give feedback in the open.

  • In financing start-ups: Train the review panel on fair scoring and conflicts of interest.
  • Write a role card for each person: purpose, five tasks, the standard for each, and who to ask for help.
  • Train by showing, doing with support, then doing alone with a check. Record sign-off per task.
  • Set service rules for answering customers: reply times, tone, how to apologise and fix.
  • Hold a ten-minute team check each day: yesterday's problem, today's plan, one risk.
  • Build a growth path: what a new person can do in week 1, month 3 and month 12.
Worked example. Training record: 'Task: handle a complaint. Shown 2 May; done with support 4 May; done alone 8 May, checked: apology, fix, follow-up call. Signed by lead.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: a new hire completes training but a customer complaint shows a skipped step. Question: whose gap is it? Model answer: first the system: check the role card, the training sign-off and the checklist; then coach the person and fix the document so the step cannot be skipped.

Longer notes. Prepare the founder for the questions a lender or partner will ask: what the money buys, who the customers are, what the monthly fixed costs are and how repayment will work. Practise a three-minute pitch aloud and record it on a phone. Create an index card with the five key numbers and rehearse stating each without reading. Explain risks openly, since trust weighs more than polish. Assess each rehearsal on clarity, numbers and calm. Identify who can review the plan, such as an accountant or a mentor, and book that conversation before the meeting.

Extra worked example. Before the pitch, the founder lists monthly fixed costs: rent R$ 1.800, utilities R$ 400, one salary R$ 2.500 and internet R$ 150. The total is R$ 1.800 + R$ 400 + R$ 2.500 + R$ 150 = R$ 4.850. With a gross margin of 40 percent on each sale, break-even sales are R$ 4.850 / 0.40 = R$ 12.125 per month. This is the number the founder must state without reading. If rent rises by R$ 200, break-even becomes R$ 12.625.

Activity. Write two role cards and a training plan for one new hire. Role-play a hard customer conversation twice and review.

Standard to meet. Role cards have measurable standards; training shows sign-off steps; service rules are written in plain words.

Module 7 Money: cost, price and break-even 5 hAnalyse

A business lives or dies on a few numbers. Know your contribution per unit, your fixed costs and the volume you need.

  • In financing start-ups: Track funds out, follow-on rate and returns; expect most to fail.
  • Contribution per advisory package = price minus variable cost. Fixed costs are paid whether you sell or not.
  • Break-even units = fixed cost divided by contribution. Plan to pass it by a safe margin.
  • Make a 12-month cash plan. Cash timing matters as much as profit: customers may pay late, suppliers may want money first.
  • Keep money for business and money for the owner apart.
  • Test the plan: what if sales fall by a quarter, or cost rises by a tenth?
Worked example. Worked example, all figures in reais: price R$ 3.000, variable cost R$ 600, contribution R$ 2.400 per advisory package. Fixed cost R$ 12.000 a month. Break-even = R$ 12.000 / R$ 2.400 = 5 advisory packages a month. At 8 advisory packages a month, profit = R$ 2.400 x 8 - R$ 12.000 = R$ 7.200. These are teaching figures that belong to this example only; later examples and the case study use their own figures. Use your own.
Second worked scenario. Scenario: sales fall to 6 advisory packages a month. Question: what is the monthly result? Model answer: R$ 2.400 x 6 - R$ 12.000 = R$ 2.400; compare with break-even of 5 advisory packages and decide whether to cut fixed cost or raise volume first. Teaching figures only.

Activity. Build your own table: price, variable cost, contribution, fixed cost, break-even and a downside case at 75% of sales. Show it to a peer or mentor.

Standard to meet. Arithmetic is correct; every figure has a source or is marked as an assumption; a downside case is shown.

Module 8 Grow, measure and renew 4 hEvaluate

Growth should follow proof. Measure, learn, then add; and keep returning to the customer.

  • In financing start-ups: Grow with a portfolio and visible founder success stories.
  • Pick one growth lever at a time: new customers, a higher repeat rate, a better price, or a new offer.
  • Set one measurable target for 90 days, such as 'raise repeat customers from 20% to 30%'.
  • Ask each customer how they found you, and record it. Spend more where the record shows results.
  • Review monthly: numbers, defects, complaints, cash. Write three decisions each time.
  • Plan the renewal of licences, equipment and skills a year ahead.
Worked example. 90-day plan: 'Lever: repeat customers. Action: follow-up call after every advisory package. Target: repeat rate from 20% to 30%. Check: weekly count. Review date: day 45 and day 90.'
Second worked scenario. Scenario: you can add 2 advisory packages a month by one of two levers: a follow-up call to past customers or a price rise of R$ 300 to R$ 3.300. Question: how do you choose? Model answer: test the cheaper reversible lever first (the call), measure for 30 days, and only then try the price change, so you can tell which one worked.

Longer notes. After funds arrive, record every month the cash in, cash out, closing balance and the date of each repayment. Calculate runway by dividing cash in the bank by average net monthly spending. Compare actual results with the plan and write one line explaining each gap. Test every new expense with one question: does it bring a customer or save money soon? Send updated numbers to lenders and partners before a renewal talk, not after. Keep receipts and contracts in labelled folders. Never count on future sales to cover today's payments.

Extra worked example. A start-up has R$ 36.000 in the bank. Monthly sales are R$ 11.000 and monthly spending is R$ 17.000, so net cash use is R$ 17.000 - R$ 11.000 = R$ 6.000. Runway is R$ 36.000 / R$ 6.000 = 6 months. If you cut spending by R$ 1.500, net use becomes R$ 4.500 and runway becomes R$ 36.000 / R$ 4.500 = 8 months, which gives 2 extra months to win customers.

Activity. Write a 90-day plan with one target, three actions and a review date. Present it in five minutes and take questions.

Standard to meet. Target is measurable and dated; actions are small enough to start this week; review date is in the calendar.

case study

A case to work through

Situation. Juliana is starting a small cafe-bakery in Sao Paulo and needs money for ovens, a counter and the first stock. Her list shows R$ 38,000 for equipment, R$ 7,000 for renovation, and R$ 9,000 for the first three months of rent and supplies, so R$ 54,000 in total. She has R$ 14,000 saved and her uncle offered R$ 10,000 with no paper. A bank offered a business loan for the rest, with monthly payments of R$ 1,650 for 30 months. She expects monthly sales of R$ 18,000 and costs of R$ 14,500 before the loan payment. Juliana wants to sign quickly because the shop lease is available only for two weeks, but she has not tested whether the business can carry the payment in a slow month.

Task. Say what you would do first, next and last, and what record you would keep.

Model answer. First, compute the gap: R$ 54,000 minus R$ 14,000 savings minus R$ 10,000 from her uncle leaves R$ 30,000 to borrow. Next, test the loan: R$ 1,650 x 30 = R$ 49,500 in total payments, which is R$ 19,500 more than the R$ 30,000 borrowed. Ask the lender for the full written cost, including all charges, and compare it with one other offer. Then test the monthly result: R$ 18,000 sales minus R$ 14,500 costs leaves R$ 3,500; after R$ 1,650 the monthly balance is R$ 1,850. Run a slow month at 20 percent lower sales: R$ 14,400 minus R$ 14,500 is minus R$ 100, so the loan payment makes it minus R$ 1,750. Hold a cash reserve for at least two such months, about R$ 3,500. Last, put the uncle's loan in a short signed note with amount and repayment dates. Records to keep: budget, offers, contract copy, repayment table, and monthly cash sheet. Standard to meet: sign only when the slow-month test is covered by a reserve.

Evidence is a case study with numbers, results and lessons. The standard is work that a client or employer would trust without close checking. Slide 6 of 7: L6 Advanced

This level suits experienced people leading teams or larger work in financing start-ups. Plan about 100 to 150 hours of study and applied projects. Evidence is a leadership project with measured results and a review by a senior peer. The standard is steady results under pressure. Where law applies, check the current official text. It prepares you for Super expert, where you shape the field.

Slide 7 of 7: L7 Super expert

  • Design a funding strategy across a company's life
  • Mentor founders and finance teams
  • Advise boards on risk and capital choices
Notes for slide 7

This level suits seasoned leaders in financing start-ups who teach others and shape standards. Plan 150 or more hours across mentoring, writing and advice. Evidence is a body of work: trained people, documented methods and results over time. The standard is sound judgment on hard cases and honest limits. It is the top of the ladder; continue by teaching and learning from peers.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, taxes, data duties and customer rights. This course is written for São Paulo. Check the current official text for your own place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Registration: Udyam (small business) or company registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Registration: registration with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK). Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Registration: a trade licence from the emirate economic department or a free-zone licence. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Registration: CNPJ registration, or MEI for micro-entrepreneurs. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Registration: federal or Ontario business registration. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Registration: state registration (Secretary of State) and a federal tax ID. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Registration: registration with ACRA. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Registration: an ABN and, for companies, ASIC registration. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)
