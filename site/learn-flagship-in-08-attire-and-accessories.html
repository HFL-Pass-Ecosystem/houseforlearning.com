flagship course

Attire and Accessories Business

Plan, source, make or sell clothing and accessories for a local market, with correct costing, written quality checks, honest descriptions and fair practice.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 40 h lessons plus practice

Plan, source, make or sell clothing and accessories for a local market, with correct costing, written quality checks, honest descriptions and fair practice.

  • Define target customers and product lines with size and price range.
  • Apply costing and pricing to each product.
  • Analyse fabric, fit and finish against a quality checklist.
  • Evaluate suppliers, returns and margins.
  • Create a five-product collection with a selling plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Develop a small collection of five products with cost sheets, quality checks, honest listings and a selling plan.

Condition

About 10 weeks part-time. Fabric swatches, a measuring tape, a kitchen scale, a phone camera, a sewing or sourcing contact, a mentor.

Standard

Each product has a full cost sheet where price covers cost plus at least 30% margin, passes a written quality checklist, and has a listing that matches the real item.

Who it is for: Designers, tailors, shop owners, online sellers, students. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Choose customers and product lines 4 hDefine
  • Describe one customer in detail: age, work, budget, occasions and where she or he shops.
  • Pick a line of 5 related products, for example cotton kurtas, stoles, tote bags, belts and hair accessories.
  • Check demand by visiting 3 shops and 3 online listings and noting price, size range and colour.
  • Choose a season: festive, summer, wedding or school, and plan stock for it.
Worked example. Customer: working woman aged 25 to 35, spends Rs 800 to Rs 1,500 on a kurta, wears them 4 days a week. Line: 3 cotton kurtas, 1 stole, 1 tote. Three shops sell similar kurtas at Rs 899, Rs 1,099 and Rs 1,299.

Activity. Write a one-page customer profile and line plan with price bands.

Standard to meet. Profile has budget, occasion and a price band for every product.

Module 2 Know fabrics and trims 5 hAnalyse
  • Identify common fabrics: cotton, linen, rayon, polyester, silk blends and leather or faux leather, by feel, burn-free tests and labels.
  • Test a swatch for shrinkage by marking 10 cm squares, washing and measuring; more than 3% shrinkage needs pre-washing.
  • Test colour run by soaking a swatch in warm water on white cloth for 10 minutes.
  • Match fabric to use: breathable cotton for daily wear, stiff fabric for bags, soft lining for stoles.
Worked example. A 10 cm cotton swatch measures 9.5 cm after washing: shrinkage 5%. A 3 m kurta length will lose about 15 cm, so the pattern adds 5% allowance or the fabric is pre-washed.

Activity. Test three swatches for shrinkage and colour run and record the results.

Standard to meet. Results are in a table with percent shrinkage and a pass or fail for colour.

Module 3 Measure and fit 5 hApply
  • Take body measures with the tape snug but not tight: bust, waist, hip, shoulder, sleeve and length.
  • Build a size chart with S, M, L, XL based on measures plus ease of 4 to 6 cm for comfort.
  • Make or buy a sample, try it on two people of different shapes and note pulling or gaping.
  • Print the size chart in centimetres on every listing and tag.
Worked example. Medium kurta: bust 92 cm body plus 6 cm ease gives a garment bust of 98 cm; length 100 cm; sleeve 20 cm. Fit test on two people shows tightness under the arm, so armhole depth increases by 1.5 cm.

Activity. Build a four-size chart and fit-test one sample on two people.

Standard to meet. Chart lists body and garment measures, and the fit notes lead to one change.

Module 4 Cost each product 6 hApply
  • List fabric, lining, trims, thread, labels, tailoring labour, packing and a share of rent, power and transport.
  • Add wastage of 5 to 10% on fabric and rejects, and a share of unsold stock.
  • Divide monthly overheads by the number of pieces made to get overhead per piece.
  • Round the final cost up and keep the sheet in a file for every product.
Worked example. Kurta: fabric 2.5 m at Rs 120 = Rs 300; wastage 8% = Rs 24; trims Rs 30; tailoring Rs 180; packing Rs 20; overhead Rs 4,000 / 100 pieces = Rs 40. Total cost = Rs 594.

Activity. Make cost sheets for all five products.

Standard to meet. Sheets list every cost line, wastage and overhead, and totals are correct.

Module 5 Set prices and margin 5 hEvaluate
  • Margin is profit divided by selling price; markup is profit divided by cost; do not mix them.
  • Target at least 30% margin after discounts, marketplace fees and returns.
  • Check the price against three competitors and against your customer budget.
  • Plan discount rules in advance, such as 10% for festive sale, and recheck the margin.
Worked example. Cost Rs 594. Price Rs 999. Profit Rs 405, margin 405 / 999 = 40.5%. After a 10% festival discount: price Rs 899, profit Rs 305, margin 33.9%, still above 30%.

Activity. Set a price for each product and calculate margin with and without a discount.

Standard to meet. Each product meets 30% margin after the largest planned discount.

Module 6 Control quality 5 hEvaluate
  • Write a checklist: straight seams, 8 to 10 stitches per inch, no loose threads, matching pattern, working zips and snaps, correct size tag.
  • Inspect each piece under good light and use a measure to check key dimensions within 1 cm.
  • Record defects in a log with type, cause and fix; investigate any defect seen in 3 or more pieces.
  • Pack in clean bags and keep damp pieces away from stock.
Worked example. Batch of 40 kurtas: 3 show uneven hems, 2 have loose buttons. Defect rate 5 of 40 = 12.5%. Cause: tailor fatigue near end of day. Fix: hem check at mid-batch; next batch has 1 defect (2.5%).

Activity. Inspect five pieces with your checklist and log every defect.

Standard to meet. Log shows defect type, count, cause and a fix.

Module 7 Source and select suppliers 4 hEvaluate
  • Collect quotes from at least three suppliers for the same fabric with price, minimum order and delivery time.
  • Ask for a swatch and test it before ordering the full quantity.
  • Check fair practice: no child labour, fair wages, and safe workshop conditions.
  • Pay on written terms and keep invoices and bills for tax and returns.
Worked example. Supplier A: Rs 110 per metre, 50 m minimum, 10 days. Supplier B: Rs 118, 20 m minimum, 5 days. For a 40 m first batch, B costs Rs 4,720 and arrives sooner; A needs a 50 m order costing Rs 5,500. B is chosen.

Activity. Compare three suppliers for one material in a table and choose one with reasons.

Standard to meet. Table shows price, minimum, time and the test result for each.

Module 8 Capstone: collection and selling plan 6 hCreate
  • Finish five products with cost sheets, size charts and a quality log.
  • Photograph each in daylight, front, back and detail, with a true colour.
  • Write honest listings with fabric, size, care, price and return rules.
  • Plan channels, a launch month and a target for units and profit.
Worked example. Collection of 5 products, average price Rs 899, average margin 36%. Launch plan: 100 pieces in month 1 through a social page and two stalls, target profit Rs 30,000, returns kept under 5%.

Activity. Present the collection, sample pieces and the selling plan.

Standard to meet. Every product has cost sheet, passed checklist and honest listing; plan has numbers.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: the work product from each module, kept in one folder with dates. A mentor and two peers mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why choose one customer first?

Model answer: Designs, prices and sales channels all follow from knowing who will buy. (Covered in module 1: Choose customers and product lines. To pass that module: Profile has budget, occasion and a price band for every product.)

Q2. What does 5% shrinkage mean for a 100 cm length?

Model answer: It will shrink about 5 cm. (Covered in module 2: Know fabrics and trims. To pass that module: Results are in a table with percent shrinkage and a pass or fail for colour.)

Q3. Why add ease to body measures?

Model answer: So the wearer can move and breathe comfortably. (Covered in module 3: Measure and fit. To pass that module: Chart lists body and garment measures, and the fit notes lead to one change.)

Q4. Why include wastage in the cost?

Model answer: Cut-offs and rejects are real costs you must recover. (Covered in module 4: Cost each product. To pass that module: Sheets list every cost line, wastage and overhead, and totals are correct.)

Q5. If cost is Rs 600 and price Rs 1,000, what is the margin?

Model answer: Profit Rs 400 divided by price Rs 1,000 is 40%. (Covered in module 5: Set prices and margin. To pass that module: Each product meets 30% margin after the largest planned discount.)

Q6. What do you do when the same defect appears three times?

Model answer: Find the cause and fix the process, not just the piece. (Covered in module 6: Control quality. To pass that module: Log shows defect type, count, cause and a fix.)

Q7. Why test a swatch before a bulk order?

Model answer: To avoid shrinking, colour run or poor quality across the whole batch. (Covered in module 7: Source and select suppliers. To pass that module: Table shows price, minimum, time and the test result for each.)

Q8. What makes a listing honest?

Model answer: It shows true colour, real measures, fabric and care, and clear return rules. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: collection and selling plan. To pass that module: Every product has cost sheet, passed checklist and honest listing; plan has numbers.)

Q9. What is margin?

Model answer: Profit as a share of the selling price. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. Why test fabric before sewing?

Model answer: To avoid shrinking and colour run after the garment is made. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Know garment parts and fabric types.
  2. L2 Awareness. Explain fabric, fit and care.
  3. L3 Foundation. Make and sell simple items.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Run a small line with costing and checks.
  5. L5 Specialist. Lead collections and supplier systems.
  6. L6 Advanced. Lead a brand team across channels.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set standards across a regional brand.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Garment parts and fabrics

Name 10 garment parts and tell 6 fabric types apart by touch and look.

Speaker notes. Teacher gives fabric swatches to sort. Covered in module 2, Know fabrics and trims.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Fabric, fit and care

Explain how fabric, fit and care labels affect a garment, with 3 examples.

Speaker notes. Learner reads a care label aloud and explains each symbol. Covered in module 3.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: Make and sell simple items

Make 3 simple items and sell them, recording cost and price for each.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the costing sheet for each item. Covered in module 8, Capstone: collection and selling plan.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Run a small line

Run a line of 5 products with costing, 10 quality checks and a margin you can explain.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the margin sheet and inspects two items. Covered in module 5.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Lead collections

Lead a collection of 12 pieces and score 3 suppliers on a 5-point sheet before choosing one.

Speaker notes. Teacher reviews the score sheet and sample orders.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Lead a brand team

Lead a team across 3 selling channels with a monthly sales and returns report.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the report for returns by channel.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Set regional standards

Set a standard for sizing, labelling and quality across a regional brand, with 8 rules. Check local rules with an expert for labelling law.

Speaker notes. Teacher audits one product against the 8 rules.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
