flagship course

Social Media Management

Plan, publish and measure content for a small organisation across social channels, with care for people, consent and privacy.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 40 h lessons plus practice

Plan, publish and measure content for a small organisation across social channels, with care for people, consent and privacy.

  • Define a goal with a number and an audience for each channel.
  • Apply a content calendar, post formats and a reply routine.
  • Analyse reach, engagement and conversion from real numbers.
  • Evaluate risks such as false claims, copyright and unhappy comments.
  • Create a 30-day plan and a results report.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Run a 30-day content plan for a small organisation and report the results with real numbers.

Condition

About 8 weeks part-time. A practice or real organisation account, free design and scheduling tools, a phone, a mentor.

Standard

Every post is accurate, credited and scheduled; replies are polite and answered within 24 hours; the report compares results with the goal using real numbers.

Who it is for: Marketers, small business owners, students, community volunteers. Requirements: Comfort using a smartphone and social apps. Materials: lesson text, templates, rubric, check with answers.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Set goals and know the audience 4 hDefine
  • Write one goal with a number and a date, such as 200 new local followers in 30 days.
  • Describe the audience by age range, city, language and the hours they are online.
  • Choose two channels where that audience already spends time.
  • List what success looks like beyond followers: enquiries, visits, orders.
Worked example. A bakery wants 40 cake enquiries a month. Its buyers are women aged 25 to 40 in one city who use Hindi and English, so it picks a photo channel and a messaging channel.

Activity. Write a goal, an audience profile and a two-channel choice for one organisation.

Standard to meet. Give the goal a number and date, and put at least four facts in the audience profile.

Module 2 Plan content pillars and voice 4 hCreate
  • Choose three pillars: teach, show, ask.
  • Mix formats: photo, short video, carousel, text.
  • Write a voice guide of five do and don't rules, including language mix.
  • Check each pillar against the goal.
Worked example. Pillars for the bakery: baking tips (teach), order-day behind the scenes (show), flavour polls (ask); voice is warm, short, Hinglish allowed, no slang for customers over 50.

Activity. Create a pillar plan and a voice guide for your organisation.

Standard to meet. Link each pillar to the goal and write five rules in the guide.

Module 3 Build a calendar and schedule 5 hApply
  • Set a weekly rhythm, such as three posts and two stories per week.
  • Batch create: make one week of posts in one sitting.
  • Mark festivals, exam seasons and local events on the calendar.
  • Use a free scheduling tool and keep a backup copy of every file.
Worked example. Three posts a week for 30 days is 13 posts. Batching 4 posts takes 2 hours instead of 4 separate 1-hour sessions.

Activity. Fill a 30-day calendar with dates, pillars, formats and times.

Standard to meet. Plan all 30 days and give each post an owner and a posting time.

Module 4 Write and design posts 6 hApply
  • Open with a hook in the first line, give one useful point, end with one clear action.
  • Write captions in the language the audience uses; add alt text for images.
  • Use only images and music you own or have rights to, and credit sources.
  • Get written or recorded consent before showing any person, especially a child.
Worked example. Hook: "Why does your cake sink in the middle?" Value: oven temperature 10 degrees Celsius too low. Action: "Message us the word TIPS for a free checklist."

Activity. Create ten posts with captions, images and a credit or consent note for each.

Standard to meet. Give all ten posts a hook, a single action, and recorded consent or credit.

Module 5 Short video and visual basics 5 hCreate
  • Shoot vertical video in daylight with the phone held steady or on a stand.
  • Keep clips under 30 seconds and put the key message in the first 3 seconds.
  • Add captions because many viewers watch without sound.
  • Keep text inside the safe area so buttons do not cover it.
Worked example. A 20-second video with 5 clips of 4 seconds each, captions on, and the offer shown in the first 3 seconds.

Activity. Shoot and edit three short videos with captions.

Standard to meet. Check that each video is under 30 seconds, captioned and readable on a small screen.

Module 6 Community, replies and crises 5 hEvaluate
  • Answer comments and messages within 24 hours using a short reply guide.
  • Never share personal details such as phone numbers or addresses in public replies.
  • Handle a complaint in three steps: thank, move to private, solve and report back.
  • Hide abuse, report threats, and log every serious case.
Worked example. A customer posts that a cake arrived late. Reply in 2 hours, ask for the order number by private message, offer a refund or redo, and log the case.

Activity. Write a reply guide with five sample replies and a crisis checklist.

Standard to meet. Write replies that are polite, safe and fit the checklist.

Module 7 Measure and improve 5 hAnalyse
  • Record reach, engagement rate, link clicks and enquiries every week.
  • Work out engagement rate: interactions divided by reach, times 100.
  • Find the best and worst three posts and ask why.
  • Change one thing for the next week and test it.
Worked example. A post reached 2,000 people with 120 likes, comments and shares: 6% engagement. A text post reached 1,500 with 15: 1%. Make more of the first kind.

Activity. Fill a weekly results sheet for four weeks and write three findings.

Standard to meet. Calculate the numbers correctly and link each finding to one action.

Module 8 Capstone: 30-day plan and report 6 hCreate
  • Run the full 30-day plan with scheduling and replies.
  • Compare results with the goal using real numbers.
  • Report what worked, what failed and what you will change.
  • Present the report to the organisation and agree the next month.
Worked example. Goal 200 followers: result 164 followers, 31 enquiries, best content was a how-to video at 7% engagement. Next month: two videos a week.

Activity. Present a 30-day report with the calendar, results and next plan.

Standard to meet. Show goal against result in the report, with evidence from at least three posts, and next steps with dates.

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why choose only two channels at first?

Model answer: A small team can post well on two; spreading thin lowers quality and results. (Covered in module 1: Set goals and know the audience. To pass that module: Give the goal a number and date, and put at least four facts in the audience profile.)

Q2. What is a content pillar?

Model answer: A repeating theme that every post belongs to. (Covered in module 2: Plan content pillars and voice. To pass that module: Link each pillar to the goal and write five rules in the guide.)

Q3. Why batch the creation of posts?

Model answer: It saves set-up time and keeps the calendar full. (Covered in module 3: Build a calendar and schedule. To pass that module: Plan all 30 days and give each post an owner and a posting time.)

Q4. What belongs in every caption?

Model answer: A hook, a useful point and one clear action. (Covered in module 4: Write and design posts. To pass that module: Give all ten posts a hook, a single action, and recorded consent or credit.)

Q5. Why add captions to video?

Model answer: Many people watch with the sound off. (Covered in module 5: Short video and visual basics. To pass that module: Check that each video is under 30 seconds, captioned and readable on a small screen.)

Q6. Why move a complaint to a private message?

Model answer: It protects personal details and lets you solve it without a public argument. (Covered in module 6: Community, replies and crises. To pass that module: Write replies that are polite, safe and fit the checklist.)

Q7. How is engagement rate worked out?

Model answer: Interactions divided by reach, times 100. (Covered in module 7: Measure and improve. To pass that module: Calculate the numbers correctly and link each finding to one action.)

Q8. What makes a social report useful?

Model answer: It compares real results with the goal and leads to clear next steps. (Covered in module 8: Capstone: 30-day plan and report. To pass that module: Show goal against result in the report, with evidence from at least three posts, and next steps with dates.)

Q9. When should you ask permission to post a photo?

Model answer: Before posting, always for people and always in writing for children. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

Q10. What should you do if you find a post with a wrong fact?

Model answer: Correct it quickly, say what changed, and log it. (Covered in: Task, condition and standard, near the top of this page.)

seven levels

Your ladder, L1 to L7

This course sits at L4 Practitioner. Each level adds to the one below.

  1. L1 Basic. Use social media safely and kindly.
  2. L2 Awareness. Post with a hook and a clear action.
  3. L3 Foundation. Plan and schedule a month for one channel.
  4. L4 Practitioner. Run several channels and report with numbers.
  5. L5 Specialist. Lead a content team and set the monthly strategy.
  6. L6 Advanced. Direct social strategy and risk policy for an organisation.
  7. L7 Super expert. Set professional standards for social media practice.

Slide by slide

Seven slides, one per level. Open each for the slide text and the speaker notes.

Slide 1 L1 Basic: Safe and kind posting

Identify 5 safe-posting rules (privacy, consent, kindness, facts, reporting) and apply them to 3 sample posts, spotting every risky detail.

Speaker notes. Teacher hands over 3 sample posts and checks the learner flags the shared phone number in one. Covered in module 6, Community, replies and crises.

Slide 2 L2 Awareness: Hooks and actions

Explain how a hook and one clear action work, and rewrite 3 weak posts so each opens with a hook and ends with a single action.

Speaker notes. Teacher shows a dull post about a tea stall and checks the rewrite names one action, such as visit today. Covered in module 4, Write and design posts.

Slide 3 L3 Foundation: One-channel monthly plan

Build a 30-day calendar for one channel with 12 scheduled posts across 3 content pillars, each with date, time and caption.

Speaker notes. Teacher counts the posts and pillars on the calendar and checks 2 posts were actually scheduled in the tool. Covered in module 3.

Slide 4 L4 Practitioner: Multi-channel reporting

Run 3 channels for 4 weeks and report reach, clicks and replies in a table, naming the 2 best posts and why they worked.

Speaker notes. Teacher asks the learner to explain one number in the table and checks it matches the screenshots. Covered in module 7, Measure and improve.

Slide 5 L5 Specialist: Content team strategy

Lead a team of 3 creators through a monthly strategy with set goals, roles and a weekly review, and cut one weak post type using data.

Speaker notes. Teacher reads the strategy sheet and checks each goal has a number and an owner.

Slide 6 L6 Advanced: Risk policy direction

Write a 2-page social policy covering approvals, crisis steps and reply times, and test it on 2 mock crisis cases within 24 hours.

Speaker notes. Teacher plays a false rumour about the brand and times how fast the learner follows the policy steps.

Slide 7 L7 Super expert: Practice standards setting

Define a 10-point professional standard for social media work, run a review of 3 accounts against it and train a group on the gaps found.

Speaker notes. Teacher checks the standard is measurable, such as every reply within 4 hours, and that the review scores are shown.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local rules change licences, data handling, pricing words and names of bodies. Check them for your place before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies.

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.
