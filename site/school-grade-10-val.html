Grade 10: Values and life skills

4 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 10 Values and life skills, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 4 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

unit by unit

4 units with worked examples

Unit 1 Ethics and leadership Weeks 1 to 9Apply

In this unit you work on 'Ethics and leadership', taught in a way that is with notes, a worked line and a self-mark. The target is clear: take a role in a team and review it. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen and talk: hear a short story, text or example linked to 'Ethics and leadership' and say what it means in your own words.
  • Connect: name one place in your own day where it applies.
  • Use it: take a role in a team and review it. Practise with care and respect, in class and at home.
  • Reflect: what went well, what was hard, and one thing you will do next time.
Worked example. Solved: Ethical decision steps: facts, who is affected, options, which value each option meets, choose, review later. Method: Model run for 'Ethics and leadership'. A short text or story is read aloud. Learners say the main lesson in one sentence. In pairs they find one moment in school or home where this lesson would help. Each learner plans one small action for the week and tells it to a partner. At the end of the week they report back. What a good answer looks like: the lesson is in the learner's own words, the example is real and local, and the action is small enough to do. Content follows the approved local syllabus and is checked by a qualified teacher.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Choose the role: A team of 5 builds a school recycling campaign. Mariam volunteers as coordinator, with roles: Mariam leads, Hamad does posters, Sara tracks data, Ali talks to staff, Dana writes the newsletter. Clear roles come first.
  2. Step 2, Set ethical rules: The team agrees: credit everyone's work, tell the truth in data, listen to every idea, and ask permission before using photos of students. Four rules go on the first page of the plan.
  3. Step 3, Make a fair plan: There are 3 weeks and 15 tasks. 15 divided by 5 members is 3 tasks each. Mariam assigns 3 tasks to every member, including herself, so the leader does not take the easy ones.
  4. Step 4, Handle a conflict: Sara finds the recycling bins were only 60 percent full, not the 80 percent that the poster says. Hamad wants to keep 80 because it looks better. Mariam says the poster must show the true 60.
  5. Step 5, Decision: The team uses 60 percent and writes 'Bins are 60 percent full; let's make it 80.' Honest data makes a stronger, trusted campaign. Hamad agrees after seeing that exaggerated numbers could cost the team their credibility.
  6. Step 6, Review the role: Mariam asks each member to rate teamwork out of 5 on fairness, communication and honesty. Ratings 4, 5, 4, 4, 5 total 22, giving an average of 4.4. She writes one thing to improve: share deadlines earlier.
  7. Your turn: Team of 4 scores 3, 4, 5, 4 for teamwork. Average and one lesson? Model answer: total 16, average 4.0; a lesson might be to check in halfway through, because the lowest score was 3.

Activity. About 30 to 40 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a worked task and a corrected copy.

Standard to meet. Take a role in a team and review it: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer.

Unit 2 Career awareness Weeks 10 to 18Remember

In this unit you work on 'Career awareness', taught in a way that is with notes, a worked line and a self-mark. The target is clear: match strengths to career paths. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen and talk: hear a short story, text or example linked to 'Career awareness' and say what it means in your own words.
  • Connect: name one place in your own day where it applies.
  • Use it: match strengths to career paths. Practise with care and respect, in class and at home.
  • Reflect: what went well, what was hard, and one thing you will do next time.
Worked example. Solved: List 3 strengths, 3 interests and 3 job families; match one to a course and write a next step with a date. Method: Model run for 'Career awareness'. A short text or story is read aloud. Learners say the main lesson in one sentence. In pairs they find one moment in school or home where this lesson would help. Each learner plans one small action for the week and tells it to a partner. At the end of the week they report back. What a good answer looks like: the lesson is in the learner's own words, the example is real and local, and the action is small enough to do. Content follows the approved local syllabus and is checked by a qualified teacher.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, List strengths: Khalid rates himself from 1 to 5: Numbers 5, Making things 4, Helping people 3, Writing 2. These come from real activities, such as maths club, building a robot kit, class mentoring and essay marks, not just wishes.
  2. Step 2, Define career profiles: He lists what each career uses, as weights: Engineering 0.4 numbers, 0.4 making, 0.1 helping, 0.1 writing. Nursing 0.2, 0.1, 0.5, 0.2. Journalism 0.1, 0.1, 0.2, 0.6. Each set of weights adds up to 1.
  3. Step 3, Score Engineering: 5 times 0.4 is 2.0; 4 times 0.4 is 1.6; 3 times 0.1 is 0.3; 2 times 0.1 is 0.2. Total is 2.0 plus 1.6 plus 0.3 plus 0.2, which equals 4.1 out of 5.
  4. Step 4, Score the others: Nursing is 5 times 0.2 (1.0) plus 4 times 0.1 (0.4) plus 3 times 0.5 (1.5) plus 2 times 0.2 (0.4), which is 3.3. Journalism is 0.5 plus 0.4 plus 0.6 plus 1.2, which is 2.7.
  5. Step 5, Read the results: Engineering 4.1 is highest, then Nursing 3.3 and Journalism 2.7. The gap suggests exploring engineering fields first. A score is a guide, not a verdict, because interests grow and the weights are rough.
  6. Step 6, Next actions: He talks to an engineer, joins a design club, and checks the subjects needed for engineering courses. He keeps nursing as a second option and revisits the scores in a year. Real career choices include more than interest.
  7. Your turn: Strengths: Numbers 3, Making 2, Helping 5, Writing 4. Use Nursing weights 0.2, 0.1, 0.5, 0.2. Model answer: 0.6 plus 0.2 plus 2.5 plus 0.8 is 4.1 out of 5, a strong match for helping careers.

Activity. About 30 to 40 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a worked task and a corrected copy.

Standard to meet. Match strengths to career paths: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer.

Unit 3 Well-being and balance Weeks 19 to 27Create

In this unit you work on 'Well-being and balance', taught in a way that is with notes, a worked line and a self-mark. The target is clear: build a weekly routine for study and rest. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen and talk: hear a short story, text or example linked to 'Well-being and balance' and say what it means in your own words.
  • Connect: name one place in your own day where it applies.
  • Use it: build a weekly routine for study and rest. Practise with care and respect, in class and at home.
  • Reflect: what went well, what was hard, and one thing you will do next time.
Worked example. Solved: Calm routine: name the feeling, breathe in for 4 counts and out for 6, talk to someone, do one small helpful thing. Plan screen time: 1 hour, then a 10-minute break. Method: Model run for 'Well-being and balance'. A short text or story is read aloud. Learners say the main lesson in one sentence. In pairs they find one moment in school or home where this lesson would help. Each learner plans one small action for the week and tells it to a partner. At the end of the week they report back. What a good answer looks like: the lesson is in the learner's own words, the example is real and local, and the action is small enough to do. Content follows the approved local syllabus and is checked by a qualified teacher.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Count the hours. A week has 168 hours. Write the fixed items first: school and travel 35 hours, sleep 9 hours a night which is 63, and meals and chores about 2 hours a day, which is 14. Fixed time is 35 plus 63 plus 14, which is 112.
  2. Step 2, Add study and exercise. Plan 2 hours of study on 5 weekdays and 1 hour on each weekend day, giving 10 plus 2, which is 12 hours. Add 5 hours of exercise. Study and exercise take 17 hours in total.
  3. Step 3, Find rest time. 168 minus 35, 63, 14, 12 and 5 leaves 39 hours for friends, family, hobbies and unplanned rest. That is about 5.6 hours a day. Rest is not wasted time; it helps memory and mood.
  4. Step 4, Build the timetable. Put study in 25 to 50 minute blocks with 5 to 10 minute breaks. Do hard subjects when you are alert. Keep a regular bedtime, and keep one evening a week free from study.
  5. Step 5, Review and adjust. After a week, mark what worked, what ran over and when you were tired. Move or shorten tasks. A routine is a plan you test, not a rule you must keep perfectly.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: if sleep rises to 8 hours a night, how many hours does a week of sleep take, and what is left from 168 after school 35, sleep and 14 meals? Answer: 56 hours of sleep; 168 minus 35, 56 and 14 leaves 63.

Activity. About 30 to 40 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a worked task and a corrected copy.

Standard to meet. Build a weekly routine for study and rest: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer.

Unit 4 Civic responsibility Weeks 28 to 36Understand

In this unit you work on 'Civic responsibility', taught in a way that is with notes, a worked line and a self-mark. The target is clear: describe a citizen's duties in the UAE and globally. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen and talk: hear a short story, text or example linked to 'Civic responsibility' and say what it means in your own words.
  • Connect: name one place in your own day where it applies.
  • Use it: describe a citizen's duties in the UAE and globally. Practise with care and respect, in class and at home.
  • Reflect: what went well, what was hard, and one thing you will do next time.
Worked example. Solved: Class jobs: Monday Asha waters plants, Tuesday Ben tidies books. A fair rule: everyone takes a turn, and everyone knows it in advance. Method: Model run for 'Civic responsibility'. A short text or story is read aloud. Learners say the main lesson in one sentence. In pairs they find one moment in school or home where this lesson would help. Each learner plans one small action for the week and tells it to a partner. At the end of the week they report back. What a good answer looks like: the lesson is in the learner's own words, the example is real and local, and the action is small enough to do. Content follows the approved local syllabus and is checked by a qualified teacher.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Define the duties: Hind lists what citizens do: follow the law, respect other people and cultures, take part in the community, protect shared places and resources, and care about people beyond their own country. Rights go with these duties.
  2. Step 2, Local case: In the school street, litter is common after lunch. Following the rules and keeping shared space clean is a duty. Hind and 5 friends collect litter for 10 minutes each day, which is 5 days times 10 minutes, so 50 minutes a week.
  3. Step 3, Respect and law: A foreign visitor asks Hind for directions. She answers politely, in simple words, and she follows traffic rules when crossing. Respecting the law and treating guests kindly are both civic duties in her country.
  4. Step 4, Participation: The school council asks for ideas on the canteen waste. Hind submits one clear idea, a sorting bin for paper and plastic, rather than only complaining. Taking part means contributing and accepting majority decisions.
  5. Step 5, Global duty: Hind learns that wasted water is a shared global problem. She counts how long she runs the tap: 2 minutes twice a day, so 4 minutes. By turning it off while brushing she saves about 2 of those 4 minutes each day.
  6. Step 6, Plan and review: She makes a week table with 3 local actions (litter, kindness, school council idea) and 2 global ones (water, waste). That makes 5 actions, and she ticks those she completed, honestly reporting 4 out of 5.
  7. Your turn: List 2 local and 1 global duty you could do this week, with one measure. Model answer: keep the class tidy daily, greet a visitor politely, and turn the tap off while brushing; count days done out of 7.

Activity. About 30 to 40 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: a worked task and a corrected copy.

Standard to meet. Describe a citizen's duties in the UAE and globally: at least 8 of 10 marks on a short task, with one line of reasoning for each answer.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Kind and safe

On the slide: L1 Basic · Kind and safe

Notes: Show one kind action and state one safe rule, such as telling a trusted adult when worried. Notes: role-play sharing a toy; learners name three trusted adults and practise saying clearly, stop, I do not like that.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: Feelings and honesty

On the slide: L2 Awareness · Feelings and honesty

Notes: Name four feelings and say why telling the truth matters. Notes: use a feelings chart for happy, sad, angry and scared; read a short story of a broken cup and let learners practise saying what happened and offering to help fix it.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Friends and small savings

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Friends and small savings

Notes: Settle a small disagreement by listening and save a small amount regularly. Notes: practise the sentence I feel ... when ...; for money, save 10 from a weekly 50, which reaches 80 after 8 weeks, and learners say what they would plan to buy.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Choices, budget and online safety (this course)

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Choices, budget and online safety

Notes: Weigh choices, keep a simple budget and protect personal information online. Notes: use stop, think, choose; split an income of 200 into needs 120, wants 50 and savings 30, which adds back to 200. Learners list four things never to share online.

This course: Grade 10 Values and life skills is at this level. Its units include Ethics and leadership; Career awareness; Well-being and balance; Civic responsibility.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Wellbeing and responsibility

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Wellbeing and responsibility

Notes: Plan study, rest and sleep, and give a respectful no under peer pressure. Notes: teenagers usually need about 8 to 10 hours of sleep, so waking at 6:30 means bed by 21:30 for 9 hours. Learners plan one week and rehearse a calm refusal in pairs.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Ethics and behaviour evidence

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Ethics and behaviour evidence

Notes: Evaluate ethical frameworks and psychology evidence on habits, stress and decision making. Notes: compare consequence, duty and character approaches on one dilemma. Practice: 1,000 saved at 5 per cent a year for 10 years compounds to about 1,628.89; learners say why early habits matter.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Design a wellbeing programme

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Design a wellbeing programme

Notes: Design and critique an evidence-based wellbeing or ethics programme with safeguarding and evaluation. Notes: survey 120 pupils before and after; 78 positive (65 per cent) rising to 96 (80 per cent) is a 15-point rise. Learners state limits, such as no comparison group, and how consent is protected.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

India (Bengaluru)

Use INR in money problems; deliver in English, Hindi, Kannada; personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Use AED in money problems; deliver in Arabic, English; personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Use BRL in money problems; deliver in Portuguese (Spanish and English support); personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Canada (Toronto)

Use CAD in money problems; deliver in English, French; personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

United States

Use USD in money problems; deliver in English, Spanish; personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Singapore

Use SGD in money problems; deliver in English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Australia

Use AUD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Use HKD in money problems; deliver in Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Use GEL or ILS in money problems; deliver in Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Use KYD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Data Protection Act; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Mexico

Use MXN in money problems; deliver in Spanish, English; personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; guardian contact for every learner under 18.
