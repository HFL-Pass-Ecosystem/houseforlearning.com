NL-02 · full course

Handmade Goods and Craft Business

Turn a craft skill into a small product line that sells online and in person, with honest costs and a story buyers believe.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner8 modules · 120 h guidedPractice 70% · Share 20% · Study 10%

Turn a craft skill into a small product line that sells online and in person, with honest costs and a story buyers believe.

  • Understand Explain how design, craft and a buyer fit together.
  • Create Make a small range with consistent quality.
  • Analyse Work out true cost per piece including time.
  • Evaluate Judge which product to repeat or drop.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design, make, photograph, price and sell a small range of handmade goods and record 30 days of sales.

Condition

About 120 hours over 3 to 5 months. Basic tools, a phone camera, a free shop page or marketplace listing, a mentor and peer circle.

Standard

Three finished products; photos and descriptions; full cost sheet; 30-day sales log; at least 70% on the check.

Learning only. Follow your local law and get written permission before you work with real customers, premises, data or money.

Requirements: none. Who it is for: learners 16 and over, workers changing field, small-business starters, teams. Currency is shown on every number.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples (120 h guided)

Module 1 Choose a craft and a buyer 6 hUnderstand

Start with who will buy and why.

  • Pick one craft you can make well and repeat
  • Describe the buyer: age, need, price level
  • Visit five sellers and note price and quality
Worked example. A leather-card holder maker finds buyers want slim, EUR 25 to 35, in three colours.

Activity. Write a one-page product idea. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Pick one craft you can make well and repeat; Describe the buyer: age, need, price level; Visit five sellers and note price and quality), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Buyer, price band and three products named. Meets the standard when your record shows: pick one craft you can make well and repeat; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 2 Design and prototype 14 hCreate

Make it, test it, change it.

  • Sketch three options and pick one
  • Build a prototype and test with five people
  • Record changes in a design log
Worked example. Prototype strap too stiff; softer leather chosen after feedback.

Activity. Make two prototypes and log changes. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Sketch three options and pick one; Build a prototype and test with five people; Record changes in a design log), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Design log with at least two changes. Meets the standard when your record shows: sketch three options and pick one; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 3 Materials and quality 12 hApply

Quality is repeatable, not lucky.

  • Write a one-page standard for size, finish and fault limits
  • Buy materials in batches and keep receipts
  • Check each piece against the standard
Worked example. A candle maker rejects any piece with cracks over 2 mm and logs the rate.

Activity. Make five pieces and inspect each. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Write a one-page standard for size, finish and fault limits; Buy materials in batches and keep receipts; Check each piece against the standard), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Inspection record for each piece. Meets the standard when your record shows: write a one-page standard for size, finish and fault limits; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 4 True cost and price 12 hEvaluate

Most makers forget their time.

  • Cost = materials + packing + fees + time at a fair rate
  • Price = cost / (1 - target margin)
  • Test price with five buyers
Worked example. Material EUR 6, pack 1, fees 2, time 1.5 h at EUR 12 = EUR 27; price at 40% margin = EUR 45.

Activity. Build the cost sheet for three products. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Cost = materials + packing + fees + time at a fair rate; Price = cost / (1 - target margin); Test price with five buyers), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Time included; margin calculated twice. Meets the standard when your record shows: cost = materials + packing + fees + time at a fair rate; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 5 Photos, words and story 10 hCreate

Buyers cannot touch it, so show it.

  • Natural light, plain background, three angles
  • Describe size, material, care and who made it
  • Tell one true story of the product
Worked example. A pot photographed beside a hand shows size; description lists glaze care.

Activity. Photograph and write listings for three products.

Standard to meet. Photos clear; sizes and care given; story true.

Module 6 Selling online and in person 20 hApply

Use two channels and learn from both.

  • Open a shop page or listing and a market or friend sale
  • Track views, questions, sales
  • Reply to each message within a day
Worked example. Online: 120 views, 4 sales; stall: 18 sales; learn the stall works better for gifts.

Activity. Run both channels for two weeks. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Open a shop page or listing and a market or friend sale; Track views, questions, sales; Reply to each message within a day), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Sales log shows both channels. Meets the standard when your record shows: open a shop page or listing and a market or friend sale; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 7 Packing, post and returns 8 hApply

The parcel is part of the product.

  • Pack to survive a drop
  • Weigh and price postage before listing
  • Write a simple returns note
Worked example. A glass item uses double wrap; postage EUR 6.50 shown at checkout.

Activity. Pack and send one test parcel. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Pack to survive a drop; Weigh and price postage before listing; Write a simple returns note), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Parcel arrives undamaged. Meets the standard when your record shows: pack to survive a drop; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

Module 8 Review and grow 38 hEvaluate

Keep, change or stop each product.

  • Compare sales, margin and effort
  • Ask three buyers for feedback
  • Plan the next batch
Worked example. Product A margin 42% sells 12; product B margin 15% sells 2; drop B.

Activity. Present results and plan. Apply this module to your own project. Work through each point in order (Compare sales, margin and effort; Ask three buyers for feedback; Plan the next batch), and keep one dated record per point (photo, table or short note). Share it with a peer and ask for one improvement.

Standard to meet. Decision for each product with numbers. Meets the standard when your record shows: compare sales, margin and effort; every number has its currency or unit; a peer has read it and a mentor can follow your reasoning.

assessment

How you are checked

Evidence: your records, numbers and reflections. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at "Not yet" and at least 70% on the check.

Marking rubric
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Evidence qualityClaims without recordsRecords for most claimsEvery claim linked to a dated record
Numbers and unitsMissing units or currencyCorrect, units shownCorrect, with a check calculation
Rules and safetyRules ignoredRules named and followedRules checked with the local authority, note kept
User focusNo real userOne real userSeveral users, changes made from feedback
ReflectionNo lessons writtenThree lessons writtenLessons turned into a changed plan with dates

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why describe the buyer first?

Model answer: Because price, style and channel follow from the buyer.

Q2. What does a design log record?

Model answer: Each change and the reason for it.

Q3. Why write a quality standard?

Model answer: So each piece is judged the same way.

Q4. Price for cost EUR 27 at 40% margin?

Model answer: 27 / 0.6 = EUR 45.

Q5. Why add size and care to a listing?

Model answer: Fewer returns and fewer questions.

Q6. Why use two channels?

Model answer: To compare and learn which suits the product.

Q7. Why price postage before listing?

Model answer: So it does not wipe out the margin.

Q8. How do you decide to drop a product?

Model answer: Low margin and low sales against effort.

Q9. What is the task of this course, in one sentence?

Model answer: Design, make, photograph, price and sell a small range of handmade goods and record 30 days of sales.

Q10. What standard must your final evidence meet?

Model answer: Three finished products; photos and descriptions; full cost sheet; 30-day sales log; at least 70% on the check.

where next

Ladder L1 to L7, with notes

  1. L1 Basic: First contact: words, tools and safety. Short units, about 2 to 6 hours, no record needed.
  2. L2 Awareness: Explain what the field is and who it serves. Shared with schools and community groups.
  3. L3 Foundation: Do guided tasks with a checklist. Evidence is a small set of records.
  4. L4 Practitioner: Run the full task alone with a mentor. This course sits here.
  5. L5 Specialist: Take one narrow area to depth and teach a peer circle.
  6. L6 Advanced: Lead a team, design a service or a study, and review other people's evidence.
  7. L7 Super expert: Set the standard: write the method, audit others, publish worked examples back to learners.

Notes: credits stack and you can step in and out. This course is L4. After it, L5 takes one narrow area of Handmade Goods and Craft Business to depth; L6 leads a team; L7 sets and audits the standard. Aligned with, not accredited by, national credit frameworks.

ten locations and global

Your place

Local custom changes rules, language, currency and who you practise with. Check each rule with your local authority before you act.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Currency: INR. Data rule for what you publish: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to cite: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Currency: EUR. Data rule for what you publish: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to cite: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Currency: AED. Data rule for what you publish: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to cite: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Currency: BRL. Data rule for what you publish: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to cite: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Currency: CAD. Data rule for what you publish: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to cite: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Currency: USD. Data rule for what you publish: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to cite: state and community-college credit systems.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Currency: SGD. Data rule for what you publish: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to cite: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system.

Australia

Language: English. Currency: AUD. Data rule for what you publish: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to cite: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Currency: HKD. Data rule for what you publish: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to cite: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Currency: GEL or ILS. Data rule for what you publish: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to cite: the national qualifications framework of that country.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Currency: KYD. Data rule for what you publish: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to cite: the local education and training framework.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Currency: MXN. Data rule for what you publish: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to cite: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.
