flagship course · GL-39

Virtual Interior Design Consulting

Offer remote interior design: take a client brief, measure and plan a room from photos and sketches, build a mood board and layout, cost it, and deliver a clear package.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner54 h of lessons plus practiceAbout 8 to 12 weeks part-time

Offer remote interior design: take a client brief, measure and plan a room from photos and sketches, build a mood board and layout, cost it, and deliver a clear package.

  • Understand Explain the design process and how remote work differs.
  • Apply Take accurate measurements and a clear brief from afar.
  • Analyse Analyse a room for light, flow, use and budget.
  • Create Create a layout, mood board and shopping list.
  • Evaluate Evaluate the design with the client against the brief.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Design one room remotely for a real or practice client and deliver a package: brief, plan, mood board, shopping list with costs, and a feedback round.

Condition

A free floor-plan or drawing tool, photo and measurement instructions for the client, a spreadsheet, a mentor with design experience and a peer circle. About 10 weeks part-time.

Standard

Measurements checked by two methods; layout keeps walkways and exits clear; budget within 5% of the client's limit; products from open sources with prices and links; accessibility needs considered; client rates 4 of 5 or better.

Who it is for: Designers, decorators, furniture-shop staff, property stagers, students. Requirements: None. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, 10-question check.

Safety and limits: Keep safe walkways, exits and fire rules. Do not move load-bearing walls or alter electrics or gas without a qualified professional. Protect client photos and address.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 The remote process 4 hUnderstand

Same steps, more clarity needed. You will lay out the remote consulting process in dated steps, so a client knows what happens each week and what you need from them.

  • Brief, survey, concept, plan, selection, review, handover.
  • Tools: calls, shared folders, drawing tools.
  • What you cannot judge from photos.
  • Agree scope and fees.
  • Map the remote process into steps with dates, such as brief call, measurements, concept board, revision and final plan, so the client knows what happens in week one.
  • Use one shared folder for photos and plans, and send a written summary after every call, which avoids confusion over decisions that were only made in conversation.
  • Ask the client to send photos from each corner in daylight.
  • Agree what the client will do and what you will do, in writing.
Worked example. You cannot judge colour exactly from photos; ask for swatches in daylight.

Activity. Write the process and scope for one project.

Standard to meet. List deliverables and dates in the scope.

Common mistake. Starting with products.

Second worked example. Client: home office that also works for guests. Needs: desk, sofa bed, storage; budget 1,500; style words: calm, warm, light. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. How is it used?

Module 2 The brief 6 hUnderstand

Listen for needs, not just looks. You will run a brief conversation covering budget, daily routines, keepers and annoyances, helping you design for how the room is used and not only how it should look.

  • Who uses the room and how.
  • Budget, timing, must-keep items.
  • Style words and examples.
  • Accessibility and safety needs.
  • Ask the client for their budget range, who uses the room, daily routines, favourite items to keep and a list of three things that currently annoy them.
  • Request photos of the room taken at morning and evening, since light changes colour and mood considerably across the day.
  • Mark sockets, radiators and windows on the plan.
  • Check door swings and clearances for every piece.
Worked example. Client: "Home office that also works for guests." Needs: desk, sofa bed, storage.

Activity. Hold a brief call and write it up.

Standard to meet. Confirm the summary with the client.

Common mistake. Skipping the brief call.

Second worked example. Room 4.0 by 3.2 m: the diagonal should be 5.12 m; the client measures 5.10, so the plan is trusted within 2 cm.

Check yourself. What did you learn?

Module 3 Measure and photograph 8 hApply

Good data in, good design out. You will give clients precise measuring and photo instructions and cross-check the numbers, so your plans are accurate without ever visiting the room.

  • Client measures walls, doors, windows, heights.
  • Photos from corners.
  • Check by diagonals.
  • Mark sockets and radiators.
  • Ask the client to measure each wall, window and door to the nearest centimetre, and to sketch a simple plan with the room's shape, then check that opposite walls add up sensibly.
  • Give clear photo instructions: stand in each corner at chest height, keep the camera level, and include the ceiling, floor and any radiators or sockets.
  • Collect samples of fabrics and finishes before final choice.
  • Consider noise, light and storage as well as looks.
Worked example. Room 4.0 m by 3.2 m: diagonal 5.12 m should match; if not, remeasure.

Activity. Create instructions and check a sample.

Standard to meet. Pass the two-method check.

Common mistake. Trusting one measurement.

Second worked example. Layout A puts the desk under the window and the sofa bed on the long wall with a 1.0 m path; layout B swaps them. The client picks A for light.

Check yourself. Do yours match?

Module 4 Space planning 8 hApply

Layout first, decoration later. You will draw furniture to scale, respect walkway distances and offer two layouts with reasons, letting the client make an informed choice.

  • Zones, circulation (clear paths; about 0.9 m is a common rule of thumb, so check the local rule).
  • Furniture sizes and clearances.
  • Light and views.
  • Sketch two options.
  • Leave at least 90 centimetres for main walkways and about 45 centimetres between a sofa and coffee table, and draw each piece to scale before suggesting a layout.
  • Offer two layout options with a short reason for each, so the client can choose rather than simply accept or reject a single design.
  • Plan for people with different needs, including mobility.
  • Offer lower-cost options for each major item.
Worked example. Desk beneath window, sofa bed on the long wall; walkway 1.0 m kept.

Activity. Draw two layouts.

Standard to meet. Label the clearances.

Common mistake. Crowded layouts.

Second worked example. Palette: 60% soft neutral walls, 30% sofa and rug in warm grey, 10% accent cushions in terracotta, each with a reason.

Check yourself. What are your paths?

Module 5 Colour, light and materials 8 hAnalyse

Create mood with reasons. You will build a three-colour, two-texture concept board and suggest sample testing at home, so the chosen mood still works in the room's real light.

  • Colour palette (60-30-10).
  • Light layers: ceiling, task, accent.
  • Materials and durability.
  • Sample boards.
  • Build each concept board around a palette of three colours, one pattern and two textures, and note how each will look in the room's light.
  • Remind clients that colours on screens and in photos vary, and suggest ordering small samples and viewing them in the room for a full day.
  • Keep a list of suppliers with delivery times.
  • Warn the client about any item that needs a professional.
Worked example. 60% soft neutral walls, 30% sofa and rug, 10% accent cushions.

Activity. Build a mood board.

Standard to meet. Give reasons for the palette.

Common mistake. Colour without reasons.

Second worked example. Budget 1,500: furniture 900, lighting 250, textiles 200, contingency 150; total within 3% of the client's limit. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. What is your palette?

Module 6 Budget and sourcing 10 hCreate

Cost every item. You will assemble a sourcing sheet with totals, contingency and delivery checks, preventing purchases that exceed the budget or cannot fit through a door.

  • List with price, size, link, delivery.
  • Mix new, second-hand, make.
  • Contingency 10%.
  • Compare alternatives.
  • Prepare a sourcing sheet with item, supplier link, price, size, delivery time and an alternative, and total it against the budget, keeping a 10 percent contingency.
  • Check dimensions of each item against doors, stairs and lifts before recommending it, as a piece that cannot be delivered wastes money.
  • Ask for feedback after the first presentation.
  • Set a limit on rounds of change, with a fair price after.
Worked example. Budget 1,500: furniture 900, lighting 250, textiles 200, contingency 150 (an estimate; check your market). Amounts are in your currency.

Activity. Build a shopping list.

Standard to meet. Total within 5% of budget.

Common mistake. Overspending.

Second worked example. The client prefers a smaller sofa; layout and list are updated and cost drops by 120. Amounts are in your currency.

Check yourself. What is your total?

Module 7 Present and revise 6 hEvaluate

Make decisions easy. You will present options in a short call with a one-page summary and limit revision rounds, keeping decisions clear and the project moving forward.

  • Short deck: brief, plan, board, list.
  • Round of feedback.
  • Revise once or twice.
  • Final package.
  • Present designs in a short video call with a one-page summary, and invite comments on each element, writing the answers down so you can act on them.
  • Limit revisions to a stated number, such as two rounds, and explain what counts as a change of direction compared with a small adjustment.
  • Ask for permission to use photos for your portfolio.
  • Keep the client's address and photos private.
Worked example. Client prefers a smaller sofa; layout updated and cost reduced.

Activity. Present and revise.

Standard to meet. Obtain the client's sign-off.

Common mistake. One option only.

Second worked example. The deck has four slides: brief, plan, mood board, list. The client decides in one call.

Check yourself. What did the client choose?

Module 8 Handover and growth 4 hCreate

Help them finish. You will deliver a final pack with layout, shopping list and installation order, then record what ran late, so clients finish confidently and your next quote improves.

  • Installation order and checklist.
  • Safety reminders.
  • Photo after for portfolio with consent.
  • Plan 90 days.
  • Hand over a final pack with the layout, shopping list, colour codes, care notes and an installation order, so the client can finish the project with confidence.
  • Ask for a testimonial and before-and-after photos with permission, and record what took longer than planned to improve your next quote.
  • Write a short summary for the client to keep.
  • Write what you will change in your next project.
Worked example. Checklist: paint first, floor, furniture, lights, textiles.

Activity. Write handover and plan.

Standard to meet. Confirm clarity with the client.

Common mistake. No handover.

Second worked example. A guest-room handover pack lists the Wi-Fi code, rubbish days, a spare-key plan, and an emergency number on the fridge. The owner walks the host through it once and the host signs the list.

Check yourself. What is the order?

case cards and glossary

Practise on cases; learn the words

Case 1. The small room

A 3 m by 3 m room must be an office and guest room.

Question: Solution?

Model answer: Use a wall bed or sofa bed, wall desk, vertical storage, keep a path.

Case 2. The over-budget list

The list exceeds budget by 20%.

Question: Fix?

Model answer: Swap items, use second-hand, phase purchases.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: scope, brief, measurement check, two layouts, mood board, shopping list, feedback round and handover. A mentor and one peer mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet', and at least 8 of 10 check answers right.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why check two ways?

Model answer: To catch measuring errors. (Covered in module 3: Measure and photograph. To pass that module: Pass the two-method check.)

Q2. Minimum walkway?

Model answer: About 0.9 m is a common rule of thumb; check the local rule and allow more for access needs. (Covered in module 4: Space planning. To pass that module: Label the clearances.)

Q3. What is 60-30-10?

Model answer: A colour balance rule. (Covered in module 5: Colour, light and materials. To pass that module: Give reasons for the palette.)

Q4. What cannot be judged from photos?

Model answer: True colour and texture. (Covered in module 1: The remote process. To pass that module: List deliverables and dates in the scope.)

Q5. What is in a brief?

Model answer: Users, use, budget, timing, style and needs. (Covered in module 2: The brief. To pass that module: Confirm the summary with the client.)

Q6. Why contingency?

Model answer: Prices and delivery change. (Covered in module 6: Budget and sourcing. To pass that module: Total within 5% of budget.)

Q7. Who must do electrical changes?

Model answer: A qualified professional. (Covered in module 8: Handover and growth. To pass that module: Confirm clarity with the client.)

Q8. What goes in a package?

Model answer: Brief, plan, mood board, shopping list and handover. (Covered in module 7: Present and revise. To pass that module: Obtain the client's sign-off.)

Q9. Why get consent for photos?

Model answer: Privacy. (Covered in module 8: Handover and growth. To pass that module: Confirm clarity with the client.)

Q10. How many layouts first?

Model answer: Two options. (Covered in module 4: Space planning. To pass that module: Label the clearances.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Building, safety and tenancy rules differ by place; never advise changes that need approval without telling the client. Check your local rules with an adviser before you act for real.

India (Bengaluru)

Currency: write INR next to every number. Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Personal data law to check: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Level frame: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Partner to practise with: an industrial-training institute, a polytechnic, a small-business cluster or a Bengaluru incubator.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Currency: write EUR next to every number. Language: Dutch, English. Personal data law to check: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Level frame: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a regional college (MBO), a maker space or a small firm in the region.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Currency: write AED next to every number. Language: Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Level frame: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Partner to practise with: a free-zone business centre, a training institute or a municipality-licensed operator.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Currency: write BRL next to every number. Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Personal data law to check: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Level frame: the national technical-education catalogue and the national S-system training bodies. Partner to practise with: a small-business support agency, a technical school or a local cooperative.

Canada (Toronto)

Currency: write CAD next to every number. Language: English, French. Personal data law to check: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Level frame: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Partner to practise with: a community college, a settlement agency or a Toronto business hub.

United States

Currency: write USD next to every number. Language: English, Spanish. Personal data law to check: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Level frame: state and community-college credit systems. Partner to practise with: a community college, a workforce board or a small-business development centre.

Singapore

Currency: write SGD next to every number. Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Personal data law to check: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Level frame: the Singapore national skills frameworks and the national credit system. Partner to practise with: a channel partner, a polytechnic or an approved training provider.

Australia

Currency: write AUD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Level frame: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Partner to practise with: a vocational college or registered training organisation, or a regional business chamber.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Currency: write HKD next to every number. Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Personal data law to check: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Level frame: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Partner to practise with: a college, training body or business chamber.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Currency: write GEL or ILS next to every number. Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Personal data law to check: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Level frame: the national qualifications framework of that country. Partner to practise with: a local college, chamber or training body.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Currency: write KYD next to every number. Language: English. Personal data law to check: the Data Protection Act. Level frame: the local education and training framework. Partner to practise with: a local training body or business chamber.

Mexico

Currency: write MXN next to every number. Language: Spanish, English. Personal data law to check: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Level frame: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Partner to practise with: a technical college or business chamber.
