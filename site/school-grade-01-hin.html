school course

Grade 01: Hindi

4 units across the school year. Each has a worked example, an activity and a standard to meet.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L1Primary4 units36 teaching weeks36-week school yearBloom levels: Remember
  • Remember Say and write Hindi vowels and consonants.
  • Remember Read words with matras.
  • Remember Read and write simple words.
  • Remember Recite short rhymes.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Meet the outcome of every unit in Grade 01 Hindi, and keep one piece of evidence for each.

Condition

A 36-week school year, 4 units, a teacher or guide, a learning book or device, and local examples in the local language first with English support.

Standard

Each unit: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school. Course pass: no unit at 'Not yet' after one retry, and a pass in SA1 and SA2.

Assessment plan

Formative checks FA1 to FA4 around weeks 6, 12, 24 and 30; summative checks SA1 in week 18 and SA2 in week 36.

unit by unit

4 units with worked examples

Unit 1 Letters and sounds Weeks 1 to 9Remember

In this unit you work on 'Letters and sounds', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: say and write Hindi vowels and consonants. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Letters and sounds' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: say and write Hindi vowels and consonants, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Echo: sound at 340 m/s returns after 2 s, so it travelled 680 m there and back, and the wall is 340 m away. Method: Model run for 'Letters and sounds'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Say the first vowels: अ आ इ ई उ ऊ ए ऐ ओ औ. Say each sound slowly: a, aa, i, ee, u, oo, e, ai, o, au. Trace each letter in the air with your finger, and draw the top line (shirorekha) last, after the letter is written.
  2. Step 2, Link vowels to words. अ से अनार [A for pomegranate], आ से आम [Aa for mango], इ से इमली [I for tamarind], उ से उल्लू [U for owl]. Say the full line each time, like a chant: अ से अनार।
  3. Step 3, Say the first consonants: क ख ग घ. क से कमल [K for lotus], ख से खरगोश [Kh for rabbit], ग से गमला [G for flower pot], घ से घर [Gh for house]. Notice that each consonant carries a short a sound.
  4. Step 4, Write with a headline. Every letter in क, ख, ग, घ hangs from a line drawn on top, called शिरोरेखा [the headline]. Write each letter three times: क क क, ख ख ख. Then read your line aloud: कमल, खरगोश, घर.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. A learner writes the letters of कमल without the top line and reads क as k with no vowel. Correction: draw the line on top of every letter, and say each consonant with its short a: ka-ma-la, then read the whole word as कमल [lotus].
  6. Step 6, Your turn: say and write च छ ज with one word each. Model answer: च से चम्मच [spoon], छ से छाता [umbrella], ज से जहाज़ [ship]. Write each letter three times with its headline.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Say and write Hindi vowels and consonants: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 2 Matras: short and long vowel signs Weeks 10 to 18Remember

In this unit you work on 'Matras: short and long vowel signs', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: read words with matras. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Matras: short and long vowel signs' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: read words with matras, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: Short and long vowel signs change the sound: ka, kaa, ki, kee. Read: kamal, kaam, kitaab. Method: Model run for 'Matras: short and long vowel signs'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Meet the vowel signs on क. क (ka), का (kaa), कि (ki), की (kee), कु (ku), कू (koo), के (ke), कै (kai), को (ko), कौ (kau). The signs are matras: ा ि ी ु ू े ै ो ौ. Say each syllable aloud.
  2. Step 2, Read words with long aa. काम [work], नाम [name], आम [mango], घास [grass]. The sign ा is a straight line after the letter. Compare कल [tomorrow] with काल [time]; the matra makes the sound longer.
  3. Step 3, Short and long i, u. किताब [book] has the short ि, दीदी [elder sister] has the long ी, पुल [bridge] has the short ु, फूल [flower] has the long ू. The long sounds are held a little longer.
  4. Step 4, Read words with e, ai, o, au. मेला [fair], तैरना [to swim], मोर [peacock], चौका [kitchen platform]. Sentence: मोर नाचता है। [The peacock dances.] Point at each matra as you read.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. A learner reads कि as ik, because the short i sign is written before the letter. Correction: ि is written before but read after the consonant, so कि is ki. Write किताब and read ki-taab.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: read and write the words for mango, flower and book with their matras. Model answer: आम, फूल, किताब. Then add the correct matra: प_नी (water) should be पानी, using the long aa sign.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Read words with matras: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 3 Simple words and objects Weeks 19 to 27Remember

In this unit you work on 'Simple words and objects', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: read and write simple words. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Simple words and objects' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: read and write simple words, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: मेरा नाम अमन है। मैं स्कूल जाता हूँ। यह मेरी किताब है। शब्द: कलम, किताब, गेंद। Method: Model run for 'Simple words and objects'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Read two-letter words with no matras: जल [water], कल [tomorrow or yesterday], नल [tap], फल [fruit], घर [house], पल [a moment]. Sound out each consonant with its short a, then say the whole word smoothly.
  2. Step 2, Add objects found in the classroom: कलम [pen], मेज़ [table], कुर्सी [chair], किताब [book], बस्ता [school bag]. Touch each object as you say its name, and read the label card aloud.
  3. Step 3, Make sentences with है [is]. यह कलम है। [This is a pen.] वह घर है। [That is a house.] यह मेज़ है। [This is a table.] Replace the object with other words from your list.
  4. Step 4, Short question and answer. यह क्या है? [What is this?] यह फल है। [This is a fruit.] वह क्या है? [What is that?] वह नल है। [That is a tap.] Ask your partner five times.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. A learner writes कमल when they mean pen. Correction: कलम is pen and कमल is lotus; the letters म and ल have swapped places. Read the word aloud to check meaning: क-ल-म is pen.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: write one sentence about a chair and one about a school bag. Model answer: यह कुर्सी है। यह बस्ता है। [This is a chair. This is a school bag.] Check the headline over every letter.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Read and write simple words: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

Unit 4 Rhymes Weeks 28 to 36Remember

In this unit you work on 'Rhymes', taught in a way that is short, playful, with objects, pictures and talk. The target is clear: recite short rhymes. You move from seeing it, to saying it, to doing it alone.

  • Listen first: your teacher or a recording says the new words or sentences from 'Rhymes' twice. You repeat them aloud.
  • Notice the pattern: find where the new sound, word form or sentence rule appears in a short text and mark it.
  • Use it: recite short rhymes, first with help, then alone.
  • Check and fix: read your work aloud once more; fix one thing you hear is wrong.
Worked example. Solved: कविता की पंक्तियों में तुक खोजें (जैसे आम और शाम), कवि का भाव बताएँ और भावार्थ अपने शब्दों में लिखें। Method: Model run for 'Rhymes'. Teacher text (3 lines about a daily activity). Step 1, read it aloud together. Step 2, mark every word or sentence that uses the new pattern. Step 3, a learner writes or says two new lines of their own that use the same pattern. Step 4, the learner reads them aloud and corrects one slip. What a good answer looks like: the pattern is used correctly in both lines, the lines make sense, and the learner can say which part is the pattern.

Step by step.

  1. Step 1, Listen to a short rhyme I made: नन्ही चींटी, चली कतार, दाना लेकर, घर के द्वार। [The little ant walked in a line, carrying a grain to the door of home.] Clap once on each stressed word.
  2. Step 2, Find the rhyming words. कतार and द्वार end with the same sound आर. Other rhyming pairs: आम and शाम, बाग and आग, पल and जल. Say each pair twice and clap the rhythm.
  3. Step 3, Learn the rhyme line by line. First say नन्ही चींटी, then चली कतार. Next say दाना लेकर, then घर के द्वार। Repeat the lines slowly, then at a steady walking speed, with hands marking the beat.
  4. Step 4, Add actions. For नन्ही चींटी hold up a tiny finger. For चली कतार march on the spot. For दाना लेकर pretend to lift a grain. For घर के द्वार point to a door. Recite together as a group.
  5. Step 5, Fix an error. A child says दाना लेकर, कतार चली, changing the line order and losing the rhyme. Correction: keep the order नन्ही चींटी, चली कतार so that कतार and द्वार end the pair of lines.
  6. Step 6, Your turn: make a two-line rhyme using आम and शाम. Model answer: मीठा आम, खाऊँ सुबह-शाम। [Sweet mango, I eat it morning and evening.] Recite it three times with a clap.

Activity. About 20 to 30 minutes of class work, then one short practice task for home. Keep evidence: one page in your learning book.

Standard to meet. Recite short rhymes: at least 4 of 5 attempts correct, said or written, with one real example from home or school.

levels L1 to L7

Where this course sits on the ladder

Seven slides, one for each level. Step in at your level and move up; credits stack. A level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age.

Slide 1. L1 Basic: Greetings and first words (this course)

On the slide: L1 Basic · Greetings and first words

Notes: Greet people politely, say your name and name five family members in Hindi. Notes: teach नमस्ते (namaste, hello) and मेरा नाम ... है (mera naam ... hai, my name is ...) by ear; learners greet a partner and point to family photos while saying each word.

This course: Grade 01 Hindi is at this level. Its units include Letters and sounds; Matras: short and long vowel signs; Simple words and objects; Rhymes.

Slide 2. L2 Awareness: The Devanagari script

On the slide: L2 Awareness · The Devanagari script

Notes: Recognise the vowels and first consonants of Devanagari and copy them neatly under the top line. Notes: show how letters hang from a horizontal line, then write क (ka) with correct stroke order; learners find it in three printed words and read each one aloud.

Slide 3. L3 Foundation: Simple sentences and postpositions

On the slide: L3 Foundation · Simple sentences and postpositions

Notes: Read and write short sentences with verb at the end and use postpositions such as में (in) and पर (on). Notes: model किताब मेज़ पर है (kitaab mez par hai, the book is on the table); learners make five sentences about a classroom and draw each one.

Slide 4. L4 Practitioner: Daily conversation and tenses

On the slide: L4 Practitioner · Daily conversation and tenses

Notes: Hold a short conversation about daily life using present, past and future tenses with correct gender agreement. Notes: contrast वह जाता है (he goes) with वह जाती है (she goes); learners ask a partner what they did yesterday, then write six sentences marking each verb ending.

Slide 5. L5 Specialist: Reading, writing and register

On the slide: L5 Specialist · Reading, writing and register

Notes: Read a news article and write a formal letter of about 200 words, choosing between polite and casual forms. Notes: compare आप, तुम and तू (you, three levels of respect) in one dialogue; learners rewrite it for a teacher, a friend and a child.

Slide 6. L6 Advanced (degree level): Literature and linguistics

On the slide: L6 Advanced · Literature and linguistics

Notes: Analyse a Hindi poem or story for theme, form and vocabulary origins, and write a critical essay in Hindi. Notes: use a short poem; learners sort ten words into Sanskrit-derived, Persian-derived and English-derived, then argue in 500 words how word choice shapes tone.

Slide 7. L7 Super expert (postgraduate/expert): Research, translation and teaching

On the slide: L7 Super expert · Research, translation and teaching

Notes: Design a research or translation project on Hindi language or literature, justify the method and present findings to peers. Notes: pick a topic such as code-switching in advertising; collect forty examples, classify them, discuss limits of the sample, and have a fluent reviewer check every claim.

Notes: a level is reached when you can meet its standard, not by age. Learners may move up or down a level by subject.

ten locations and global

Localised for your place

Teach in the local language first, with English support. Use local currency, units and examples in every worksheet. Local curriculum and ministry rules are checked before use in a school; Arabic, Islamic, Social and Moral content needs licensed educators and ministry review. Child-safety rules apply for every learner under 18.

India (Bengaluru)

Use INR in money problems; deliver in English, Hindi, Kannada; personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Use EUR in money problems; deliver in Dutch, English; personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Use AED in money problems; deliver in Arabic, English; personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Use BRL in money problems; deliver in Portuguese (Spanish and English support); personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Canada (Toronto)

Use CAD in money problems; deliver in English, French; personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

United States

Use USD in money problems; deliver in English, Spanish; personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Singapore

Use SGD in money problems; deliver in English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil; personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Australia

Use AUD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Use HKD in money problems; deliver in Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English; personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Use GEL or ILS in money problems; deliver in Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English; personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Use KYD in money problems; deliver in English; personal data rule: the Data Protection Act; guardian contact for every learner under 18.

Mexico

Use MXN in money problems; deliver in Spanish, English; personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties; guardian contact for every learner under 18.
