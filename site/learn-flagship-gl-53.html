flagship course

Alternative Protein Food Products

Plan a small food product built on plant, fungal or insect protein: recipe, safety, labelling, costing and honest claims.

about this course

What you will be able to do

L4 Practitioner39:00 h of lessons plus practice and sharingSelf-paced with peer circle
  • Understand Explain protein sources and the nutrition and allergy basics.
  • Apply Scale a recipe and plan a safe process.
  • Analyse Compare taste, cost and nutrition against a normal product.
  • Evaluate Judge labels and claims.
  • Create Run a tasting test and write a launch plan.

Task, condition and standard

Task

Develop one alternative-protein product to a tested recipe with hygiene plan, label draft, cost sheet and tasting results.

Condition

A clean kitchen or partner food unit, scales, a spreadsheet, 10 tasters, two peers and a food-trade mentor. Local food rules are checked.

Standard

Recipe is repeatable within 5% weight; allergen list is complete; hygiene plan passes; label meets local rules; cost per pack is shown; 10 tasters' scores recorded; peers rate Meets or better.

Who it is for: Food entrepreneurs, cooks, product developers, hospitality staff. Requirements: none. Materials: lesson text, worked examples, templates, rubric, quiz, tracker.

lesson by lesson

8 modules with worked examples

Module 1 Protein sources 4 hUnderstand

Many foods can provide protein. A good alternative-protein product uses sources well.

  • Pulses, grains, soy, nuts and seeds, mushroom-based protein, algae and insects are all used.
  • Combining sources, such as rice with lentils, supplies a more complete amino acid profile.
  • Protein per 100 g varies widely; read labels and use recognised data.
  • Some sources need special processing to remove bitter or harmful compounds.
Worked example. Rice and lentils together give a more complete protein than either alone. A batch of lentil patties provides about 9 g protein per 100 g.

Activity. Make a table of six sources with protein per 100 g from labels, with the date.

Standard to meet. Values come from labels or recognised tables.

Module 2 Nutrition and allergy 5 hAnalyse

A product must be nutritious and safe for people with allergies.

  • Check iron, B12, calcium, fibre, salt and fat balance.
  • Major allergens: soy, nuts, gluten, milk, eggs, fish and shellfish; insect protein can cross-react with shellfish allergy.
  • Declare allergens in bold on the label.
  • Prevent cross-contact in the kitchen.
Worked example. An insect-flour product warns on the label that people with shellfish allergy should avoid it and that it is made in a unit that also handles nuts.

Activity. Check your recipe against an allergen list and write the allergen statement.

Standard to meet. All allergens named and cross-contact controls listed.

Module 3 Recipe development 6 hCreate

Test small, change one thing at a time and record everything.

  • Weigh all ingredients and record steps with time and temperature.
  • Make three batches and compare.
  • Adjust salt, texture and moisture gradually.
  • Record batch numbers and dates.
Worked example. Version 3 uses 18% less salt and holds shape better after a 2-minute longer rest. The three batches vary by 3% in weight.

Activity. Write a recipe card and make three batches.

Standard to meet. Batches within 5% weight and tasting notes recorded.

Module 4 Hygiene and food safety 5 hApply

Prevent harm with controls at the right steps.

  • Identify hazards and control points such as cooking temperature and cooling time.
  • Keep raw and cooked separate; clean tools and surfaces.
  • Use thermometers and record temperatures.
  • Train all staff and keep logs.
Worked example. Cooked patties are cooled from 60 to 21 degrees within 2 hours and then to 5 degrees within a further 4 hours, with times logged.

Activity. Write a hygiene and control plan with measurable limits.

Standard to meet. Each control point has a limit and a record.

Module 5 Labels and claims 5 hEvaluate

The label must tell the truth.

  • Name, ingredients by weight, allergens, net weight, date, storage, maker details.
  • Claims such as "high protein" must meet rules for the amount.
  • Use "meat-free" or "vegan" only if true.
  • Check local rules with an adviser.
Worked example. A label lists "Contains soy" in bold, net weight 250 g, best-before date and storage "keep refrigerated, use within 3 days of opening".

Activity. Draft a label for your product and get adviser comments.

Standard to meet. Label meets local rules per adviser check.

Module 6 Costing and price 5 hAnalyse

Know your cost per pack and your margin.

  • Ingredients, packing, fuel, labour, loss and overhead.
  • Price above cost with a margin; compare with normal products.
  • Account for waste and test batches.
  • Review costs when prices change.
Worked example. A 250 g pack costs 42 INR to make. Sold at 70 INR, margin is 28 INR. A 10% price rise in lentils changes cost to 44 INR.

Activity. Build a cost sheet.

Standard to meet. Totals are correct and currency is shown.

Module 7 Taste test and feedback 4 hEvaluate

Use real tasters and real scores.

  • Ask 10 tasters to score taste, texture, appearance and value from 1 to 5.
  • Ask about allergies and get consent.
  • Record comments without defending.
  • Change one thing and test again.
Worked example. Texture scores 3.2. Mixing time is reduced by one minute and the next test scores 4.1.

Activity. Run a tasting test and record scores.

Standard to meet. Scores and changes logged.

Module 8 Launch plan 5 hCreate

Start small and learn.

  • Choose a channel: market, cafe, online.
  • Plan cold storage and delivery.
  • Plan packaging that can be reused or recycled.
  • Set targets for sales and complaints.
Worked example. A weekend market stall with samples sells 60 packs and collects 25 email sign-ups.

Activity. Write the launch plan.

Standard to meet. Plan includes costs, channels and a review date.

assessment

How you are checked

Your evidence: recipe card, hygiene plan, label draft, cost sheet, tasting record. Two peers and one mentor mark it with this rubric. Pass: no criterion at 'Not yet'.

Marking rubric (Not yet, Meets, Exceeds)
CriterionNot yetMeetsExceeds
Recipe consistencyVariesWithin 5%Within 3% and documented
Food safetyGapsPlan completePlan with logs
Label and claimsErrorsMeets rulesMeets rules with adviser check
Honesty and evidenceClaims without proofEvery claim has a recordEvery claim linked to a dated record and checked by a peer
Safety, ethics and local rulesNot consideredChecked and written downChecked, dated and explained to others

Ten-question check, with model answers

Q1. Why combine protein sources?

Model answer: To get all amino acids. (Covered in module 1: Protein sources. To pass that module: Values come from labels or recognised tables.)

Q2. Name two allergens.

Model answer: Soy and nuts (also gluten, shellfish cross-reaction). (Covered in module 2: Nutrition and allergy. To pass that module: All allergens named and cross-contact controls listed.)

Q3. What goes on a label?

Model answer: Name, ingredients, allergens, weight, date, storage, maker. (Covered in module 3: Recipe development. To pass that module: Batches within 5% weight and tasting notes recorded.)

Q4. Why weigh ingredients?

Model answer: For repeatable batches. (Covered in module 4: Hygiene and food safety. To pass that module: Each control point has a limit and a record.)

Q5. What is a control point?

Model answer: A step like cooking temperature that prevents harm. (Covered in module 5: Labels and claims. To pass that module: Label meets local rules per adviser check.)

Q6. How cool cooked food?

Model answer: Quickly, within the rule. (Covered in module 6: Costing and price. To pass that module: Totals are correct and currency is shown.)

Q7. How many tasters here?

Model answer: At least 10. (Covered in module 7: Taste test and feedback. To pass that module: Scores and changes logged.)

Q8. Name a cost item.

Model answer: Ingredients, packing, fuel, labour, loss, overhead. (Covered in module 8: Launch plan. To pass that module: Plan includes costs, channels and a review date.)

Q9. When say meat-free?

Model answer: Only when true. (Covered in module 8: Launch plan. To pass that module: Plan includes costs, channels and a review date.)

Q10. What do you log?

Model answer: Batches, temperatures, cleaning. (Covered in module 8: Launch plan. To pass that module: Plan includes costs, channels and a review date.)

ten locations and global

Your place

Food laws, labelling and licences differ by place.

India (Bengaluru)

Language: English, Hindi, Kannada. Write numbers in INR. Personal data rule: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Skills frame to name in your evidence: National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) and the national credit framework. Consumer rule: Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Netherlands (Eindhoven area)

Language: Dutch, English. Write numbers in EUR. Personal data rule: EU GDPR (Dutch: AVG), supervised by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Dutch qualification framework (NLQF) linked to the European Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: EU and Dutch consumer rules (14-day cooling-off for online sales).

UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi)

Language: Arabic, English. Write numbers in AED. Personal data rule: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (personal data protection). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the emirate education and training regulators (for example KHDA in Dubai) and the national qualifications framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Brazil (Sao Paulo)

Language: Portuguese (Spanish and English support). Write numbers in BRL. Personal data rule: LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados). Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national technical-education catalogue and the S-system training bodies (SENAI, SENAC). Consumer rule: the Consumer Defence Code (CDC).

Canada (Toronto)

Language: English, French. Write numbers in CAD. Personal data rule: PIPEDA, with provincial rules where they apply. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the provincial colleges framework and the national credential-recognition guidance. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

United States

Language: English, Spanish. Write numbers in USD. Personal data rule: state privacy laws (for example California), and COPPA for children under 13. Skills frame to name in your evidence: state and community-college credit systems. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Singapore

Language: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil. Write numbers in SGD. Personal data rule: Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). Skills frame to name in your evidence: SkillsFuture Singapore frameworks and the national credit system. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Australia

Language: English. Write numbers in AUD. Personal data rule: the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Hong Kong (Eastern Asia)

Language: Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), English. Write numbers in HKD. Personal data rule: the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the Hong Kong Qualifications Framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Georgia and Israel (Western Asia)

Language: Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, English. Write numbers in GEL or ILS. Personal data rule: the personal data law of the country where the learner lives. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national qualifications framework of that country. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Cayman Islands (Caribbean)

Language: English. Write numbers in KYD. Personal data rule: the Data Protection Act. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the local education and training framework. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Mexico (Central America)

Language: Spanish, English. Write numbers in MXN. Personal data rule: the federal law on personal data held by private parties. Skills frame to name in your evidence: the national competency certification system and the education ministry levels. Consumer rule: local consumer-protection law.

Russian Federation

Russian Federation: HELD. A sanctions and licensing check by our lawyer must finish before any course is offered there. No localised version is published.

Global

The global version uses plain English, metric units, and your own currency. Machine translation is available in every language; the English text is the official text. Before you act for real, check your local rules with an adviser. Currency: USD (or your own currency, written next to every number).
