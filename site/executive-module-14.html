Module 14: The accounting equation, linked statements and enterprise assurance

A 2.5 ECTS executive module, 75 hours.

what you take

Module 14

  • Size

    2.5 ECTS, 75 hours of study.

  • Credit

    Counts in 16 countries. See the table.

how it is marked

Assessment

  • Written memo 30%
  • Live interactive simulation 35%
  • Boardroom presentation 35%

Marking bands: A, B or fail. Presentations use at most 10 slides, with a short live defence.

Weights shown follow the shared pattern for modules 3 to 14, and will be confirmed.

task, condition, standard

What you will be able to do

Task

Prepare three linked statements that balance, read them with ratios and test them with reconciliations and sample checks.

Condition

Given a small set of transactions for one year.

Standard

The equation balances at each step, closing cash matches in two statements, ratios are correct and the bank reconciles to the books.

Who it is for: Managers and analysts who need to read, check or sign off accounts.

eight parts

The parts, with worked examples

Part 1 The accounting equation 9 hUnderstand: explain

Every business follows one rule: what it owns equals what it owes plus what the owners have put in. Every record keeps this rule true.

  • Assets = Liabilities + Equity
  • Each event changes at least two items
  • The two sides always stay equal

Worked example: A new firm starts with 100,000 of owner money in the bank. Assets are 100,000 cash, liabilities are 0 and equity is 100,000, so the equation holds. It then borrows 20,000. Cash rises to 120,000 and a loan of 20,000 is added. Assets are 120,000, and liabilities plus equity are 20,000 + 100,000 = 120,000. The equation still balances.

Activity: Start a firm with 80,000 owner money, then borrow 15,000 and buy a machine for 30,000 cash. Show the equation after each step.

Standard: Three equations are shown and each balances: 80,000; 95,000; 95,000 (65,000 cash + 30,000 machine).

Part 2 Double entry and the trial balance 10 hApply: record

Double entry records each event as a debit and a credit of the same amount. A trial balance adds all debits and all credits to prove they match.

  • Debits left, credits right
  • Each entry has equal debits and credits
  • Trial balance checks the whole ledger

Worked example: A sale on credit of 10,000 is recorded as debit Receivables 10,000 and credit Revenue 10,000. A cash expense of 4,000 is debit Expenses 4,000 and credit Cash 4,000. The owner puts in 50,000: debit Cash 50,000 and credit Equity 50,000. Trial balance: debits are 10,000 + 4,000 + 50,000 = 64,000 and credits are 10,000 + 4,000 + 50,000 = 64,000. They match.

Activity: Record three events of your own (one sale on credit, one cash expense, one loan) and total debits and credits.

Standard: Each entry has equal debit and credit and the totals match.

Part 3 The income statement 9 hApply: prepare

The income statement shows revenue minus expenses over a period. The result is profit, which is the change in the owners' share from trading.

  • Revenue when earned, not when paid
  • Expenses matched to that revenue
  • Depreciation spreads the cost of equipment

Worked example: Revenue is 80,000 (70,000 paid in cash and 10,000 not yet paid). Cash expenses are 50,000. Equipment cost 30,000 and lasts 6 years, so depreciation is 30,000 / 6 = 5,000. Profit = 80,000 - 50,000 - 5,000 = 25,000. The unpaid 10,000 still counts as revenue because the work is done.

Activity: Revenue is 120,000 with 15,000 unpaid, cash expenses are 85,000 and equipment of 24,000 lasts 8 years. Find profit.

Standard: Depreciation is 3,000 and profit is 32,000.

Part 4 The balance sheet 10 hApply: prepare

The balance sheet is a picture at one date of assets, liabilities and equity. It must balance.

  • List assets, then liabilities, then equity
  • Equity includes the profit kept in the firm
  • Check that both sides equal

Worked example: At year end: cash 110,000, receivables 10,000, equipment net of depreciation 25,000 (30,000 - 5,000). Total assets are 145,000. The loan is 20,000. Equity is share capital 100,000 plus profit 25,000 = 125,000. Liabilities plus equity are 20,000 + 125,000 = 145,000, so the sheet balances.

Activity: Prepare a balance sheet from cash 60,000, receivables 8,000, equipment net 22,000, a loan of 12,000 and share capital 50,000, and find the profit kept.

Standard: Assets are 90,000, equity is 78,000 and profit kept is 28,000.

Part 5 The cash flow statement 9 hApply: prepare

Profit is not cash. The cash flow statement starts from profit and adjusts for items that did not move cash, then shows investing and financing.

  • Operating: profit plus depreciation minus rise in receivables
  • Investing: equipment bought
  • Financing: loans and owner money

Worked example: Profit is 25,000. Add back depreciation of 5,000 and subtract the rise in receivables of 10,000. Operating cash is 25,000 + 5,000 - 10,000 = 20,000. Investing is -30,000 for the equipment. Financing is +20,000 from the loan. Net change is 20,000 - 30,000 + 20,000 = 10,000. Opening cash 100,000 plus 10,000 gives closing cash of 110,000.

Activity: Profit 18,000, depreciation 3,000, receivables up by 6,000, equipment bought 15,000, loan taken 10,000. Find the net change in cash.

Standard: Operating is 15,000, net change is 10,000 and the working is shown.

Part 6 Linking the three statements 10 hAnalyse: trace

The statements are linked. Profit flows to equity, depreciation reduces equipment, and closing cash in the cash flow must equal cash in the balance sheet.

  • Profit goes to equity
  • Depreciation lowers assets and profit together
  • Closing cash must match

Worked example: Profit of 25,000 from the income statement raises equity from 100,000 to 125,000. Depreciation of 5,000 lowers equipment from 30,000 to 25,000. The cash flow closing balance is 110,000 and the balance sheet cash is also 110,000. Suppose a clerk forgot to lower equipment, so assets show 150,000 against 145,000 on the other side. The 5,000 gap equals the depreciation, which points straight to the missing step.

Activity: Take a balance sheet that is out by 3,000 and find which link has failed using profit, depreciation and cash.

Standard: The gap is matched to a named link and a fix entry is written.

Part 7 Ratios 9 hEvaluate: interpret

Ratios turn the statements into signals about safety, profit and use of money. Each ratio should be compared with last year and with a target.

  • Current ratio for short-term safety
  • Net margin for profit
  • Debt to equity for risk

Worked example: Current assets are 120,000 (cash 110,000 and receivables 10,000) and current liabilities are 20,000 (the loan falls due within a year), so the current ratio is 120,000 / 20,000 = 6.0. Net margin is 25,000 / 80,000 = 31.25%. Debt to equity is 20,000 / 125,000 = 0.16. If the target current ratio is at least 1.5, the firm passes with room. A debt to equity of 0.16 shows low borrowing risk.

Activity: Current assets 90,000, current liabilities 60,000, profit 12,000, revenue 96,000, debt 30,000 and equity 60,000. Find the three ratios.

Standard: Ratios are 1.5, 12.5% and 0.5 with a one-line reading for each.

Part 8 Assurance: controls, reconciliations and audit 9 hEvaluate: test

Assurance gives users confidence that the numbers are right. It uses controls, reconciliations and sample checks.

  • Reconcile bank to books
  • Test a sample of entries
  • Report errors and fixes

Worked example: The bank statement shows 112,500. Deposits in transit are 3,000 and unpaid cheques are 5,500. Adjusted bank = 112,500 + 3,000 - 5,500 = 110,000, which equals the book cash of 110,000. An audit samples 60 of 2,000 invoices and finds 3 errors, a 5% rate. Projected, that is about 100 invoices with errors, so the audit extends the sample.

Activity: Bank balance 48,200, deposits in transit 1,800, unpaid cheques 2,600. Find the adjusted bank balance and compare with a book balance of 47,400.

Standard: Adjusted bank is 47,400, it matches the books and the difference is zero.

ten checks

Check yourself

  1. Assets are 90,000 and liabilities are 35,000. What is equity?
    55,000. (Covered in part 1: The accounting equation. To pass that part: Three equations are shown and each balances: 80,000; 95,000; 95,000 (65,000 cash + 30,000 machine).)
  2. A credit sale of 7,500 is made. Which two accounts are debited and credited?
    Debit Receivables 7,500, credit Revenue 7,500. (Covered in part 2: Double entry and the trial balance. To pass that part: Each entry has equal debit and credit and the totals match.)
  3. Equipment costs 45,000 and lasts 9 years. What is yearly depreciation?
    5,000. (Covered in part 3: The income statement. To pass that part: Depreciation is 3,000 and profit is 32,000.)
  4. Cash 30,000, receivables 5,000, equipment net 15,000, liabilities 10,000. What is equity?
    50,000 - 10,000 = 40,000. (Covered in part 4: The balance sheet. To pass that part: Assets are 90,000, equity is 78,000 and profit kept is 28,000.)
  5. Profit 40,000, depreciation 6,000, receivables up 9,000. What is operating cash?
    37,000. (Covered in part 5: The cash flow statement. To pass that part: Operating is 15,000, net change is 10,000 and the working is shown.)
  6. A balance sheet is out by 4,000. Depreciation of 4,000 was forgotten on equipment. What is the fix?
    Lower equipment by 4,000 and record 4,000 expense so profit and equity fall. (Covered in part 6: Linking the three statements. To pass that part: The gap is matched to a named link and a fix entry is written.)
  7. Current assets 150,000 and current liabilities 100,000. What is the current ratio?
    1.5. (Covered in part 7: Ratios. To pass that part: Ratios are 1.5, 12.5% and 0.5 with a one-line reading for each.)
  8. A firm has revenue of 200,000 and profit of 30,000. What is net margin?
    15%. (Covered in part 7: Ratios. To pass that part: Ratios are 1.5, 12.5% and 0.5 with a one-line reading for each.)
  9. A sample of 80 invoices out of 4,000 finds 2 errors. What is the sample error rate?
    2.5%. (Covered in part 8: Assurance: controls, reconciliations and audit. To pass that part: Adjusted bank is 47,400, it matches the books and the difference is zero.)
  10. Bank 25,000, deposits in transit 2,000, unpaid cheques 3,500. What is the adjusted bank balance?
    23,500. (Covered in part 8: Assurance: controls, reconciliations and audit. To pass that part: Adjusted bank is 47,400, it matches the books and the difference is zero.)

slides L1 to L7

The seven-level ladder, slide by slide

  1. Slide 1: L1 Basic: The equation. A slide shows assets on one side and liabilities and equity on the other. Speaker note: Say: it always balances. Check: ask for equity when assets are 90,000 and liabilities 35,000. Mistake: treating profit as separate from equity.
  2. Slide 2: L2 Basic: Debits and credits. A small ledger shows three events with a trial balance. Speaker note: Say: every entry has two sides. Check: total both columns. Mistake: recording only one side.
  3. Slide 3: L3 Core: Income statement. A list shows revenue, expenses, depreciation and profit. Speaker note: Say: record when earned. Check: calculate depreciation. Mistake: counting cash received as revenue.
  4. Slide 4: L4 Core: Balance sheet. A two-column sheet shows assets against liabilities and equity. Speaker note: Say: equity includes kept profit. Check: add both sides. Mistake: forgetting to net off depreciation.
  5. Slide 5: L5 Applied: Cash flow. A walk from profit to net change in cash. Speaker note: Say: profit is not cash. Check: rework the receivables step. Mistake: adding back a rise in receivables instead of subtracting it.
  6. Slide 6: L6 Applied: Linking and ratios. Arrows from profit to equity, from depreciation to equipment, and from cash flow to cash, with three ratios. Speaker note: Say: follow each link. Check: find a 5,000 gap. Mistake: ignoring a small gap.
  7. Slide 7: L7 Advanced: Assurance. A bank reconciliation and an audit sample with projected errors. Speaker note: Say: prove the cash, test a sample. Check: project 3 errors in 60 to 2,000. Mistake: stopping at the first match.

jobs this leads to

Where this takes you

  • Internal auditor. Tests entries and reconciliations.
  • Financial analyst. Reads ratios and advises management.
  • Assurance associate. Samples transactions and reports errors.
